## Abstract

Purpose -The rapid evolution of artificial intelligence (AI) is revolutionizing organizational operations and altering competitive landscapes. This study examines the influence of organizational resources on AI adoption in recruitment, focusing on their role in achieving competitive advantage through effective implementation.

Design/methodology/approach -This research utilizes a cross-sectional quantitative approach, applying partial least squares structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM) to data from 290 human resource (HR) professionals. It is grounded in the resource-based view (RBV) and dynamic capability framework (DCF).

Findings -The results reveal that HR competencies and open innovation significantly influence dynamic capabilities, which are essential for AI integration, supported by financial support and information technology (IT) infrastructure. These capabilities enable effective AI adoption, leading to a competitive advantage.

Research limitations/implications -The cross-sectional data in this study captures the current landscape of AI adoption in recruitment, providing a snapshot of the present scenario in a rapidly evolving technological environment.

Practical implications -This study offers HR professionals and managers strategic guidance on effectively integrating AI into recruitment processes. By enhancing HR competencies, fostering collaboration and ensuring sufficient financial and infrastructural support, organizations can navigate AI adoption challenges and secure a competitive advantage in a rapidly evolving technological landscape.

Social implications -The adoption of AI in recruitment can reduce biases, enhance diversity and improve fairness through standardized assessments. However, as AI technologies evolve, continuous human oversight is essential to ensure ethical use and to modify AI systems as needed, further reducing biases and addressing societal concerns in AI-driven recruitment processes.

Originality/value -This research introduces a novel framework that underscores the importance of integrating human expertise with advanced technological tools to ensure successful AI implementation. A key contribution is that HR professionals not only facilitate AI integration but also ensure accuracy, accountability and configure the most suitable AI tools for recruitment by collaborating with AI developers to meet the specific needs of the organization.

Keywords Recruitment, AI adoption, Artificial intelligence, Dynamic managerial capability, Competitive advantage, Resource-based view

## 1. Introduction

AI is transforming recruitment by enhancing efficiency and competitive advantage (Weber et al ., 2023). As AI evolves, it continues to drive organizational transformation, boosting productivity and technological integration in Human Resource Management (HRM) (Black and van Esch, 2020; K € ochling and Wehner, 2023; Murugesan et al. , 2023; Prikshat et al ., 2023). Specifically, AI-driven tools are enhancing decision-making, improving efficiencies, and automating hiring processes (Jatob � a et al ., 2023; Kaushal et al. , 2023). Recent research suggests that 24% of businesses worldwide have integrated AI into recruitment, signaling a broader trend toward embracing automated hiring processes (Meshram, 2023).

While extensive research has explored the reasons behind AI adoption in recruitment, emphasizing its benefits and strategic advantages (Allal-Ch � erif et al ., 2021; Black and van Esch, 2020; Chen, 2023; Pan et al ., 2023; Pillai and Sivathanu, 2020; Su et al ., 2021), much of this work has focused on the tools and techniques for enhancing recruitment processes, tools such as gamified interviews (Buil et al ., 2020; Deterding et al ., 2011; Georgiou et al ., 2019), asynchronous video assessments (Basch et al ., 2022; Suen and Hung, 2023), and mobile crowdsourcing platforms (Abououf et al. , 2021). Despite advancements in AI tools and machine learning, the critical challenge of how to adopt AI effectively into recruitment processes remains a hindrance for many organizations. Ensuring seamless integration is essential to maximize utilization and improve recruitment outcomes (Weber et al ., 2023).

Recent work has begun addressing this gap by examining the role of managerial capabilities and non-technical factors in fostering AI adoption (Chowdhury et al ., 2023; Deepa et al. , 2024; Perifanis and Kitsios, 2023). For instance, Deepa et al . (2024), underscore the critical role of managers in driving successful AI Adoption, while Chowdhury et al . (2023), emphasis on developing non-technical ones such as human skills and competencies. Similarly, Chowdhury et al . Prior studies indicate that organizations require specific human skills to navigate AI integration (Anton et al ., 2020; J € ohnk et al ., 2021; Perifanis and Kitsios, 2023; Shet et al ., 2021). Supporting this view, the World Economic Forum (WEF) emphasizes that managerial readiness remains a key barrier to fully leveraging AI's potential in recruitment processes (Meho and Rogers, 2023). Yet, howto adopt AI effectively remains a significant gap, and the role of competent HR professionals is vital in navigating and driving successful AI adoption. Furthermore, strong collaboration between AI and business departments, often achieved through open innovation, is vital for successful integration (Annosi et al. , 2022; J € ohnk et al ., 2021; Murugesan et al. , 2023; Obradovi � c et al ., 2021). Additionally, IT infrastructure (Bhatt, 2000; Shet et al ., 2021), and financial support (Park et al ., 2021), are vital for seamless AI integration. AI adoption not only transforms processes but also deeply impacts a firm's strategic direction (Allal-Ch � erif et al ., 2021).

Tofoster sustained performance, tangible and intangible resources of the organization need to be strategically aligned (Peteraf et al ., 2013; Shet and Pereira, 2021). In the context of AI adoption, RBV is essential in understanding how firms can harness these resources to effectively implement AI-driven recruitment processes. Building on RBV, the Dynamic Capabilities Framework (DCF) emphasizes the importance of a firm's ability to integrate, build, and reconfigure its resources to respond to rapidly changing environments (Graham and Moore, 2021; Teece et al ., 1997). The research seeks to answer the following questions:

RQ1. How do intangible resources (HR competencies and open innovation) and tangible resources (financial support and IT infrastructure) influence managerial and innovation capabilities for achieving AI adoption in recruitment processes?

RQ2. How does AI adoption in recruitment processes, supported by intangible and tangible resources, impact organizational competitive advantage?

This study demonstrates how HR competencies, open innovation, financial support, and IT infrastructure drive AI adoption, leading to improved talent acquisition and competitive advantage. By integrating RBV (Jay, 1991) and DCF (Teece et al ., 1997), this research provides strategic insights into managing the complexities of AI adoption and achieving longterm competitive advantage (Saura et al ., 2022).

In the following sections, we first review the existing literature to establish the foundation of our model in Section 2. Based on this review, we develop hypotheses to address the research questions in Section 3. Section 4 outlines the study's methodology, followed by the data analysis in Section 5. In Section 6, we discuss the findings and their implications, and finally, Section 7 presents the conclusions of the study.

## 2.2 Resource-based view (RBV) theory

RBVemerged in management strategy literature as a framework for evaluating a company's market position by assessing its resource base. Initially proposed by Penrose (1959), the concept emphasized the need to consider a broader range of resources in firm analysis. Jay (1991) further developed RBV theory by introducing the idea that not all resources are equal; their value and strategic significance depend on their being Valuable, Rare, Inimitable, and Non-substitutable (VRIN). In the context of technology adoption, SMEs can leverage VRIN by prioritizing specialized, cost-effective technologies tailored to niche markets, thereby creating value through uniqueness and contextual relevance. Conversely, large firms, equipped with greater resources, fulfill VRIN criteria by investing in scalable and proprietary technologies, seamlessly integrating them across diverse operations to enhance strategic impact (Barney, 2001). Similarly, Grant (1991) categorized resources into tangible, intangible, and personnel-based types, which together build organizational capabilities.

## 2.3 Dynamic capability framework (DCF)

Dynamic capabilities go beyond the VRIN resources emphasized by the RBV (Jay, 1991). They focus on a firm's ability to integrate, build, and reconfigure internal and external competencies to adapt to dynamic environments, thereby ensuring competitive advantage (Teece et al ., 1997). They empower a firm to modify its strategy and allocate resources effectively, ensuring the maintenance and sustainability of a competitive advantage (Eisenhardt and Martin, 2000; Graham and Moore, 2021). The Dynamic Managerial Capability (DMC) theory builds on DCF by offering a framework to understand how managers influence strategic change in uncertain environments (Adner and Helfat, 2003; Helfat and Martin, 2015). DMC is defined as 'the abilities with which managers construct, integrate, and reconfigure organizational resources and competencies' (Adner and Helfat, 2003).

DMC comprises three key components: Managerial Human Capital (MHC), Managerial Social Capital (MSC), and Managerial Cognition (MC) (Adner and Helfat, 2003; Helfat and Martin, 2015; Heubeck, 2023). MHC refers to managers' skills and knowledge essential for effectively implementing AI adoption. Managers with strong MHC can adopt advanced recruitment technologies, improving organizational performance and securing a competitive advantage (Peteraf et al ., 2013). MSC enables managers to access internal and external resources and networks, fostering the exchange of information and best practices related to digital transformation (Greven et al ., 2023; Heubeck, 2023; Kuzior et al ., 2023). Lastly, MC involves the mental models, beliefs, and cognitive processes that shape how managers perceive, interpret, and react to stimuli in their environment (Adner and Helfat, 2003). In the context of HRM, Deepa et al . (2024), emphasize the dynamic interplay of MHC, MSC, and MC in AI adoption. MHC provides the expertise to implement AI systems, MSC facilitates resource access and vendor collaboration for seamless integration, and MC aids strategic decision-making by aligning AI with organizational goals. Together, these capabilities enable adaptive management of complexities, ensuring efficient AI integration. Lack of capabilities hinders the organization growth (Gao et al ., 2019). However, continuous reskilling and upskilling of HR professionals enhances MHC, a core component of DMC (Helfat and Martin, 2015; Heubeck, 2023). ## 3.2 Open innovation

Open innovation plays a critical role in fostering collaborative idea generation and integrating internal and external knowledge, which is pivotal for organizations adopting AI technologies. Recent studies underscore its strategic significance in navigating the rapidly evolving technological landscape. For instance, Jia et al . (2023) emphasize that open innovation expands knowledge sources for developing new products, services, or processes, enabling organizations to transcend traditional R&D boundaries. HR professionals, as key enablers, collaborate with external partners to introduce innovative practices. For instance, collaboration between HR professionals and AI developers fosters mutual learning, combining domain expertise with technical knowledge to enable effective AI adoption (Gilch and Sieweke, 2021; Van den Broek et al ., 2021). Similarly, interdepartmental collaboration between HR, IT, and other key departments ensures the seamless integration of AI solutions, aligning them with organizational goals and achieving impactful outcomes (Chowdhury et al ., 2023). Firms like P&G and NASA effectively leveraged external collaborations for technological advancements and adaptable business models (Obradovi � c et al ., 2021).

As managerial roles adapt to automation, cross-functional collaboration becomes even more vital to align innovation practices with broader organizational strategies (Langer et al ., 2021; Rihayana et al ., 2023). MSC, a key component of DMC, supports open innovation by leveraging networks to access critical external knowledge and technologies. This enhances AI adoption and drives competitive advantage (Greven et al ., 2023; Jia et al. , 2023). These elements illustrate how open innovation and collaboration serve as catalysts for leveraging AI technologies to enhance organizational performance. Based on these insights, we propose the following hypothesis.

H2. Open innovation positively influences Dynamic Managerial Capabilities, supporting AI adoption in recruitment.

## 3.3 Financial support

Financial support refers to the liquid funding that firms allocate to drive innovation and implement technological advancements (Voss et al ., 2008). A survey highlighted that 68% of HR professionals identified lack of financial resources as a primary barrier to adopting AI in hiring processes (Park et al ., 2021). Adequate financial backing enhances a firm's ability to invest in and sustain innovation. Previous studies, particularly in sectors like the automobile industry, have shown that financial resources positively impact Innovation Capability by enabling firms to invest in critical technologies and drive growth (Voss et al ., 2008; Wang et al ., 2016). In the context of AI adoption, financial support is crucial for making investments in advanced AI systems, infrastructure, and upskilling HR professionals to facilitate seamless integration.

H3. Financial support has a positive influence on innovation capabilities, particularly in facilitating AI adoption.

## 3.4 IT infrastructure

ITinfrastructure forms the backbone for integrating hardware, software, and data into a unified system that supports seamless information flow across an organization (Muazu and Abdulmalik, 2021). It is essential for the successful deployment and management of AI technologies, enabling efficient distribution of data and media to stakeholders (Park et al ., 2021; Shet et al ., 2021). A robust IT infrastructure positively influences a firm's innovative capability and competitive advantage (Rahman et al. , 2022). A well-developed IT infrastructure supports organizations in their ability to adopt and implement new technologies by providing the technological foundation required for AI-driven processes (Bharadwaj et al ., 1999; Bhatt, 2000; Muazu and Abdulmalik, 2021). Variations in IT infrastructure can significantly impact how effectively new technologies are adopted, influencing an organization's ability to leverage AI for competitive advantage (Wang et al ., 2016). Based on these understanding, we propose the following hypothesis:

H4. IT infrastructure positively influences innovation capability and the successful integration of AI technologies.

## 3.6 Innovation capability

Innovation capability refers to a firm's ability to develop new technologies or significantly improve existing ones (Cheng and Huizingh, 2014; Wonglimpiyarat, 2010), enabling organizations to quickly adapt to market changes through product and process innovations. Developing IC is crucial for organizational growth and success (Cheng and Huizingh, 2014; Liao et al ., 2012; Su et al ., 2021). IC involves generating or adopting innovations, supported by internal capabilities (Saunila and Ukko, 2014), and enables continuous transformation and value creation (Kelan, 2023; Saunila and Ukko, 2014). According to Mendoza-Silva (2020), IC encompasses multiple organizational dimensions that foster ongoing innovation. In today's fast-paced technological environment, strong IC is essential for integrating and leveraging AI technologies (Bahoo et al ., 2023). Firms with robust IC are better positioned to adopt AI in talent acquisition, improving efficiency and gaining a competitive advantage (Bansal et al. , 2023; Cheng and Huizingh, 2014). Based on these insights, we propose the following hypothesis:

H7. Innovation capability is positively related to the adoption of AI in recruitment.

## 3.7 AI adoption to competitive advantage

The integration of AI in recruitment processes not only boosts an organization's technological reputation but also enhances operational efficiency through automation and cost reduction (Saura et al ., 2022). AI-driven tools simplify candidate assessments, enabling HR managers to process larger talent pools more effectively, promote diversity, and select candidates based on standardized, bias-reducing criteria (Pan et al ., 2023). By facilitating data-driven decisionmaking, AI empowers HR professionals to make more informed hiring choices, resulting in a moreskilled and qualified workforce. Krakowski et al . (2023) investigated the impact of AI on human capabilities using chess players as a model. Their study demonstrated how AI augments human abilities, providing organizations with a competitive edge in talent acquisition and management. Given these insights, we propose the following hypothesis:

H8. The adoption of AI positively influences an organization's competitive advantage.

The survey was distributed individually through a combination of personal interactions and recruitment association networks, ensuring engagement and accurate responses. At the outset, respondents were asked to provide their informed consent before completing the survey questionnaire. A total of 886 questionnaires were distributed to HR individuals, resulting in 354 responses, representing a response rate of 40%. Responses were captured on a 5-point Likert scale, ranging from 1 (Strongly Disagree) to 5 (Strongly Agree).

The study adopted constructs from established and reliable scales to measure the key variables. The specific measurement items for each construct are detailed in the questionnaire in Appendix 1. 4.1.1 Data analysis. Data were analyzed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM 4), chosen for its predictive accuracy and ability to handle complex models with small to medium sample sizes. PLS-SEM allows simultaneous analysis of measurement and structural models, making it ideal for exploratory research. The measurement model was assessed to confirm the reliability and validity of the constructs through Cronbach's Alpha, Composite Reliability (CR), and Average Variance Extracted (AVE). Factor loadings for all measurement items were assessed, with most exceeding the threshold of 0.7, indicating strong indicator reliability. ## 6. Discussion

The findings confirm that both tangible and intangible resources significantly contribute to AI adoption and bolster organizational competitive advantage, aligning with the principles of RBVand DCF. H1 validates a positive relationship between HR competency and DMC (Anton et al ., 2020; Chowdhury et al ., 2023; Deepa et al. , 2024; Perifanis and Kitsios, 2023; Shet et al ., 2021). Similarly, H2 highlights the pivotal role of open innovation in enhancing DMC (Annosi et al. , 2022; Chesbrough, 2006; Greven et al ., 2023; J € ohnk et al ., 2021; Murugesan et al. , 2023). Financial support and IT infrastructure, as shown in H3 and H4, play crucial roles in fostering innovation capabilities (Shet et al ., 2021; Voss et al ., 2008). H5 and H6 highlight that DMC enhances innovation capabilities and drives AI adoption (Alkaraan et al. , 2024; Helfat and Martin, 2015). H7 confirms that innovation capabilities positively impact AI adoption (Wonglimpiyarat, 2010), and H8 confirms that AI adoption positively impacts competitive advantage (Saura et al ., 2022). Model details are in Appendix 2.

In addressing RQ1, the study shows that both intangible resources (HR competencies, open innovation) and tangible resources (financial support, IT infrastructure) play crucial roles in enhancing managerial and innovation capabilities for AI adoption in recruitment processes. HR competencies and open innovation significantly enhance managerial capabilities, while financial support and IT infrastructure provide the backbone for fostering innovation. Addressing RQ2, the study illustrates that AI adoption in recruitment processes, driven by both intangible and tangible resources, significantly strengthens organizational competitive advantage. The findings underscore the importance of continuous evolution and adaptability, key tenets of the DCV, as sources of sustained strategic advantage. The DCV highlights that continuous evolution and adaptability can be sources of sustained competitive advantage. Our results support by showing that firms that actively develop and renew their resources, particularly through AI adoption, are better positioned to stay competitive in rapidly changing markets.

## 6.1 Theoretical implications

This study offers several important theoretical contributions. First, it demonstrates that both tangible and intangible organizational resources are critical for effectively managing AI adoption, particularly in enhancing talent acquisition and decision-making processes. By extending the RBV, this research underscores the significance of integrating resources in technological contexts, highlighting how resource orchestration can influence successful AI implementation. Second, the study emphasizes the essential role of DMC in driving innovation and adapting to rapid regulatory and technological changes. It contributes to the literature by illustrating how managerial competencies, including sensing opportunities, aligning with legal frameworks, and reconfiguring organizational resources, are pivotal for innovation and competitiveness. This expands the application of DMCs in the context of emerging technologies, offering actionable insights into their evolution and practical strategies for enhancement. Third, the research highlights the critical role of internal resources, particularly HR competencies, in organizational transformation. It demonstrates that organizations must proactively develop internal capabilities, such as upskilling HR professionals and fostering collaboration, rather than relying solely on external forces.

By integrating RBV and DMC frameworks, this study provides a comprehensive understanding of how organizations can navigate the complexities of AI-driven recruitment. It offers a novel perspective on resource reconfiguration and dynamic capabilities in response to technological change. Furthermore, it contributes to the literature on human-machine collaboration by illustrating how AI complements existing HR resources, creating a hybrid model where technology and human expertise enhance organizational performance in tandem. Finally, this study presents a new lens on sustaining competitive advantage in dynamic environments. By showcasing the integration of AI-driven strategies with human resources, it broadens the scope of RBV and DMC in the digital age, providing a framework for organizations to align their technological and human capital to drive sustained success.

## 6.2 Managerial implications

This study provides critical insights for HR professionals on how to build competencies required for AI adoption effectively, addressing two key objectives. First, HR competencies, combined with open innovation, enable professionals to effectively leverage AI recruitment technologies, enhancing speed, accuracy, and access to a broader talent pool, fostering seamless AI integration with external partners. Second, successful adoption of AI technologies gives organizations a competitive edge by enhancing operational efficiency, particularly in environments where open resources facilitate rapid technology adoption. Companies like P&Gusing external ideas to enhance product strategies and NASA leveraging crowdsourcing to drive innovation demonstrate the importance of aligning collaborative efforts with strategic goals (Obradovi � c et al ., 2021).

To implement these recommendations effectively, HR teams need to address not only technical and cultural barriers but also significant ethical and legal challenges. For instance, algorithmic bias, which can perpetuate discrimination, must be mitigated through regular audits human interaction and ethical algorithm design to ensure fairness and inclusivity. From a legal perspective, compliance with data protection laws such as GDPR is critical. Organizations must ensure lawful data processing, obtain explicit consent, and safeguard candidate privacy to align with regulatory standards (Hauer et al ., 2021; Kammerer, 2022).

To address these challenges, organizations should foster a culture that embraces AI by providing change management training and encouraging collaboration between HR and IT teams. Upskilling HR professionals with technical and analytical skills is crucial to bridging knowledge gaps in managing AI systems effectively. Competent HR professionals play a pivotal role in mitigating legal and ethical issues through augmented recruitment processes by ensuring compliance with regulations, fostering transparency, and maintaining fairness in AIdriven systems. Managerial competencies must extend beyond technical expertise to include leadership in integrating AI into workflows and decision-making processes. Furthermore, AI adoption enhances HR capabilities and contributes to the organization's intellectual capital, solidifying its competitive position.

## 6.3 Limitations and future research directions

This study acknowledges several limitations that provide valuable opportunities for future research. One significant limitation is the lack of transparency in AI-based systems, which can undermine managerial trust in AI outputs. Addressing this issue requires further exploration of strategies to enhance explainability and trustworthiness in AI applications. Another limitation lies in the cross-sectional design of the study, which captures data at a single point in time and does not account for the evolving nature of AI adoption. Future longitudinal studies could provide deeper insights into the long-term effects of AI adoption across various industries and regions. Such studies would illuminate how organizations adapt to technological advancements and regulatory changes over time, offering more dynamic perspectives on AI integration. Dynamic capabilities within the frameworks of RBV, dynamic capability theory, and adoption intention also warrant further investigation. Future studies could explore constructs such as resistance to cultural change, lack of technical skills in HR professionals, implementation costs, business transformation, and multi-stakeholder collaboration. Understanding how these factors influence AI adoption in diverse organizational contexts could yield actionable insights for both researchers and practitioners. Finally, the study's focus on a developed economy and the creative industries may limit the generalizability of its findings.
