## ABSTRACT

In recent years, artificial intelligence (AI) has become a focal point in academic and business research. With breakthroughs in learning algorithms, AI applications in business operations are increasingly practical and impactful. AI offers tools for market analysis, decision-  making support, and innovations in business models and processes, presenting a significant turning point for firms. Despite this, questions remain about whether AI implementation yields measurable business value or is merely a trend, challenging enterprises and managers. This study provides a significant contribution by empirically examining AI impact on firm-  level performance through three key indicators: financial performance, productivity, and market value. Drawing on internal financial perspectives, this research reveals that while AI adoption enhances financial performance and market value, the advantages for AI first movers and better performers are not uniformly positive across all indicators. This nuanced analysis offers managers and stakeholders a deeper understanding of the tangible value of AI, guiding more informed implementation strategies.

## 1   |   Introduction

In recent years, significant attention has been devoted to companies' investments in information technology (IT) (Carr 2003; Rettig 2007). With the development of IT, it has become possible to turn the capabilities of organizations and industries into competitive  advantages.  Managers and operators have long evaluated the value of IT and hoped that their investments in IT could generate authentic benefit; we call this idea the business value of IT (BVIT). The term BVIT is often used to refer to the impact of IT on organization performance, including productivity, profitability, cost reduction, competitive advantage, inventory reduction, and other performance indicators (Devaraj and Kohli 2003; Hitt  and  Brynjolfsson  1996;  Kauffman  and  Kriebel  1988). Accordingly, BVIT helps managers and operators comprehend

whether investment in IT is worthwhile or not so that they can be responsible for their stakeholders and outside shareholders.

In  order  to  find  out  the  benefits  of  BVIT,  many  scholars  and researchers  have  investigated  BVIT-  related  issues.  For  example, Hitt, Wu, and Zhou (2002) studied the business impact of enterprise  resource  planning  and  its  productivity  measurement.  Bharadwaj  (2000)  employed  a  resource-  based  perspective  to  study  IT  capability  and  firm  performance.  Hitt  and Brynjolfsson (1996) used three measures to investigate the relationship between IT stock and profitability ratios. Dos Santos, Peffers, and Mauer (1993) focused on announcements of IT investments and innovative IT investments. Overall, the eventual goal of these studies has been to understand whether IT can help firms  to  increase  productivity.  The  research  methods  usually use a well-  known production function, Cobb-Douglas, to estimate  the  profitability  of  enterprises  through  some  indicators, and calculate the market value by Tobin's q as well (Brainard and Tobin 1968).

[S. 2]

Nowadays, artificial intelligence (AI) has become the most popular topic in several industries. AI is more than just a buzzword; its  adoption in enterprises represents a growing trend, a wellestablished  strategy,  and  an  innovative  approach  to  business management. John McCarthy in 1956 first proposed the concept of AI and described that an attempt would be made to find how to  lead  machines to use language, form abstractions and concepts,  solve  kinds  of  problems  now  reserved  for  humans,  and improve themselves. Recently,  the  best-  known  and  successful cases of weak AI are AlphaGo and Watson Health (weak AI, in contrast to strong AI, cannot attempt to perform the full range of human cognitive abilities). These AI systems rely on the machine learning algorithm of called 'Deep Learning.' Therefore, by  implementing  new  AI  technologies,  firms  could  improve business productivity, provide continuous innovation for products and services, and expect high growth income in the future. Makridakis (2017) predicted that AI would dominate human life 20 years from now and generate an unavoidable shift in education, management, and employment.

While  substantial  research  has  examined  BVIT,  particularly with respect to traditional IT investments, the specific impacts of  AI  implementation  on  firm  performance  remain  less  explored. Previous studies have generally assessed BVIT in terms of  productivity,  profitability,  cost  reduction,  and  competitive advantage,  often  focusing  on  IT  investments  that  support  established  operational  processes.  The  unique  characteristics  of AI, however, including its ability to enhance decision-  making through machine learning and to foster innovation in business models suggest that AI impact on firms may differ from that of conventional IT.

Despite the growing enthusiasm for AI adoption, gaps exist in our understanding of its real business value. For instance, while AI  is  anticipated  to  boost  productivity  and  financial  performance, empirical evidence of these outcomes remains sparse, particularly  at  the  firm  level.  In  addition,  previous  studies  on first-  mover  advantages  in  IT  adoption  have  shown  mixed  results,  with  some  research  suggesting  that  early  adopters  gain competitive benefits, while others indicate no significant advantage. Finally, the impact of AI on market value, especially over the long term, has been underexplored, leaving managers uncertain about return on investment in AI and its broader financial implications.

To  address  these  gaps,  this  study  presents  three  indicator (dummy)  variables,  including  AI  adopters,  first  movers,  and better  (AI)  performers.  Through  quantitative  analysis  of  our collected data and the secondary data, we thoroughly investigate the impact of AI based on these indicators. IT-  type firms have their own AI abilities and rely heavily on IT. This could lead  to  inaccurate  measurement  of  performance.  Therefore, this study does not include IT-  based companies in our samples (Huang, Lee, and Chou 2020). The arguments presented here are based on the works of Hitt, Wu, and Zhou (2002), Lieberman and Montgomery (1988), and Huang, Lee, and Chou (2020). The results reveal that AI adoption has great impacts on financial performance and market value for AI adopters. Moreover, AI first  movers and better performers are positively impacted according to the three indicators.

## 2   |   Related Works

In  this  section,  we  discuss  two  areas  of  research  literature. One is the business value of information technology (BVIT) in Section 2.1 and the other is AI in Section 2.2.

## 2.1   |   Business Value of Information Technology

As  the  first  step,  we  should  figure  out  the  definition  of  the business value of information technology (BVIT). Devaraj and Kohli (2003) considered empirical BVIT by focusing on 'studies that are at the organizational level and include one or more IT-  related  independent  variables  and  one  or  more  dependent variables related to IT's organizational impact.' There are other related  definitions.  Schryen  (2013)  defined  the  business  value of  information  systems  (IS)  as  'the  impact  of  investments,  in particular, IS assets on the multidimensional performance and capabilities of economic entities at various levels, complemented by the ultimate meaning of performance in the economic environment.' Kohli and Grover (2008) pointed out that IT value manifests  itself  in  many  ways,  including  productivity  gains, process  improvements,  profitability  enhancement,  increased consumer surplus, and improvements in supply chains or innovation at the inter-  organizational level. Melville, Kraemer, and Gurbaxani (2004) defined BVIT as 'the organizational performance impacts of IT at both the intermediate process level and the organization-  wide level and comprising both efficiency impacts and competitive impacts.' Since the definition of Devaraj and Kohli (2003) is simple and explains causal idea, we adopt their definition in this study. Managers are long to evaluate the business or economic value of IT; therefore, the research result of BVIT can be attributed to response to the productivity paradox (Brynjolfsson 1993).

In general, managers expect that the investment in IT should result in payoffs for their companies. However, when it comes to the relationship between productivity and IT, past research has not found a positive relationship between productivity (or profitability) and expenditures. In spite of these uncertain results, Erik Brynjolfsson (2017) found that firms with the highest IT capabilities have significantly higher market values compared to firms with the lowest IT capabilities. In addition, some previous studies demonstrated that IT investment has assuredly advanced organizational performance (Devaraj and Kohli 2003; Hitt and Brynjolfsson 1996; Mukhopadhyay, Kekre, and Kalathur 1995). As explained above, it is clear that most of these studies suggest that the introduction of IT can bring positive benefits.

Regarding the subject of BVIT, two survey streams have investigated two BVIT levels, industrial (or economic) and firm levels. The former is a widely observed level, whereas the latter is a seldom observed level. The reason to study the former is because Robert Solow, the Nobel Prize laureate and economist, stated in the New York Times Book Review (July 12, 1987) that we see the computer age everywhere except in productivity statistics. By the late 1980s, the conventional wisdom was that computers could not contribute significantly to productivity. Therefore, scholars opened the field to address this issue.

[S. 3]

The second facet is observed at a narrow firm level, where 'there is  no  correlation whatsoever between expenditures for IT and any  known  measure  of  profitability'  (Strassmann  1997).  The early studies established a result that either no relationship or a slightly negative relationship exists between firm-  level spending on IT and firm performance (Dehning and Richardson 2002). As a result, IT, accounting, or financial researchers have begun to turn research questions from productivity paradoxes (returning from IT) to economic measures (why or when there is a return from IT). By the end of the 1990s, some studies found that IT investments have a large return (Dehning and Richardson 2002).

Overall, the two levels are always able to obtain data from the observed  or  secondary  datasets  (Masli  et  al.  2011).  The  measurement of dependent and independent variables requires not only general accounting data from firms but also IT-  related statistics from firms, such as IT inventory, computer capital, or IT labor costs. In this study, we adopt the firm level because most researchers have dealt with firm-  level processing for various ITrelated systems or technologies. Furthermore, it easier to collect data for the firm level than for the industry level.

For the most part, researchers often use two methods to survey the impact of IT investments (business performance). The first is to understand the short-  term market reaction of investors by event research methods. The effective market theory provides the theoretical cornerstone for this basic event research method (Malkiel and Fama 1970). Using this method, the manager can understand whether a serious accident affects the stock market. Numerous studies take advantage of this approach in various fields, such as economics and finance (MacKinlay 1997), management (McWilliams and Siegel 1997), and IT operational risk (Goldstein, Chernobai, and Benaroch 2011).

Furthermore,  the  second  method  is  to  measure  accounting performance  by  accounting  (financial)-  based  methods,  such as  gross  margin,  inventory  turnover,  cash  flow,  sales  growth, and market share. This approach is also known as a backwardlooking idea because it reflects the company's past performance. The measurement time and when the evaluation of IT investments occurred must be determined by the researcher (Masli et al. 2011). As mentioned earlier, many researchers have studied  the  firm  level  and  investigated  the  relationship  between  a variety of IT investments and their BVITs.

As  mentioned  above,  most  previous  research  approaches  can be divided into two methods, an event research method and an accounting-  based method. Event research methods are used to objectively  investigate  the  external  validity  of  IT  investments; however, accounting-  based methods are used to objectively investigate the internal efficiency of IT investments. In this study, we focused on investigating the internal efficiency of IT investments. For the issue of the productivity and profitability for IT, the  investigations  of  Dehning  and  Richardson  (2002),  Masli et al. (2011), Devaraj and Kohli (2003), and Tang, Huang, and Wang (2018) are representative and are worthy of reference.

## 2.2   |   Artificial Intelligence (AI)

AI,  the  most  discussed  topic  for  companies  nowadays,  is  one of  indicators  for  companies'  future  development.  The  term 'Artificial Intelligence' was proposed by John  McCarthy (Dartmouth College) in 1956. AI was defined as an attempt made to find how to lead machines to use language, form abstractions and concepts, handle various kinds of problems now reserved for human beings, and improve themselves. AI has appeared in films and novels and has become a frequently discussed subject. In fact, AI services or products have been highly developed and integrated into our daily lives, even though it has been portrayed as part of a seemingly imagined future and fictional story.

As stated above, the current best-  known product of AI implementation  is  AlphaGo  (Google),  which  defeated  Korea's  top Go player Lee Sedol in 2016 and China's top Go player Ke Jie in  2017.  Another  famous AI product is Watson Health (IBM), which can be used to support diagnosing, treating, and predicting outcomes in many medical situations. In fact, most applications  of  AI  are  not  examples  of  strong  AI,  but  rather  weak AI. The latter can be pinpointed as machine learning, of which the idea is to train supervised or unsupervised algorithms to do one special thing well, but not many general things. As a result, through  the  machine  learning  procedure  of  Watson  Health, such as  when studying numerous texts from research papers, electronic health record data and so on, Watson Health is able to  provide  a  suitable  medical  treatment.  For  example,  Watson Health can deal with numerous MRI images of the brain and circle any marks that look like a brain bleed for the doctor to examine (Captain 2017).

How can AI do it? These successes rely on an algorithm of machine learning called 'Deep Learning.' In brief, this AI training process deals with a set of elements X, corresponding to a set of outputs Y. For example, the input element X may be photographs of  a  variety  of  different  animals,  and  then  the  correct  output Y may be the names of these animals, such as dogs, cats, and horses. Next, we use thousands, or even millions of training data to make the system learn. Finally, the correct rate of the successful  AI  system will increase with the amount of training data. A successful AI system can help users to quickly and correctly complete work. Further, AI can predict the next step to improve overall efficiency. For instance, JPMorgan Chase implements a new AI system that can complete the examination of commercial  loan  contracts  in  a  matter  of  seconds,  while  it  previously took lending officers 360,000 h to complete the job.

What is the revolutionary impact of implementing AI for enterprises? We can discuss two impacts. One is the impact on technology, and the other is that on decision-  making. For the former, firms depend on the speed at which AI technologies can succeed in automating non-  repetitive mental tasks currently performed by  humans  and  replacing  them  in  the  process.  AI  programs can develop new computer programs by themselves to expedite the process of technological change. There is no doubt that AI technologies coupled with the Internet will affect the business models of firms and how they are managed, while also influencing  employment  patterns  (Makridakis  2017).  In  addition, Wang et al. (2023) explored how AI and big data technologies are  utilized  in  managing  enterprise  information  security  and conducting risk assessments, providing insights into the role of AI in enhancing organizational security frameworks. However, the  review  examined  potential  negative  implications  of  AI in  electronic  markets,  offering  a  balanced  perspective  on  AI adoption by highlighting associated risks and challenges (Xing et al. 2023).

[S. 4]

For the latter, one way to materialize the synergistic relationship between AI and human beings is to combine the speed of AI in collecting and analyzing information with humans' superior intuitive judgment and insight. AI can furnish some utility computer programs that lead decision makers to overcome  equivocal  situations  and  address  relevant  conflicting needs. For example, AI systems that conduct sentiment analyses  of  internal  and  external  channels  of  social  media  tend to  provide  a  more  precise  reflection  of  possible  reactions  to organizational  decisions  (Jarrahi  2018).  Such  AI  systems can improve the overall value of the business. For instance, Facebook's market cap per employee of $22.1 million is more than nine times that of Johnson and Johnson (J&J), at $2.43 million. In addition, even Facebook revenues per employee of $1.15 million are more than ten times higher than those of J&J, which demonstrates  the  elevated  productivity  of  Facebook's workforce.  We  are,  therefore,  able  to  understand  why  AI  is one  of  imperative  information  technologies  for  numerous managers who are going to implement, implementing, or have implemented AI into their firms. To sum up, there are three reasons that firms are willing to implement AI technologies; they  are  (1)  improving  business  productivity,  (2)  providing continuous innovation for products and services for consumers,  and  (3)  expectations  of  high  growth  income  of  firms  in the future. Accordingly, the three reasons may cause the stock prices of firms implementing AI to rise, explaining why managers are currently paying much attention to AI technologies (Makridakis 2017).

In  this  study,  we  assess  AI  financial  performance,  productivity,  and market value through a financial/accounting-  based approach.  We  use  another  perspective  to  systematically  examine the internal validity of AI and understand the commercial value of AI.

## 3   |   Research Hypotheses

As mentioned in the previous section (Section 2), it is clear that investing  in  IT  has  proven  to  be  superior  in  BVIT.  Similarly, since AI is also an IT investment, including algorithms, big data, and the Internet, we try to confirm that the investment in AI will lead to more benefits through various indicators. Therefore, the assumptions we propose are as follows:

Hypothesis 1a. AI  implementation  in  a  firm  will  provide  greater  efficiency  as  measured  by  financial  performance measurements .

Hypothesis 1b. AI  implementation  in  a  firm  will  provide greater efficiency as measured by productivity .

Hypothesis 1c. AI  implementation  in  a  firm  will  provide greater efficiency as measured by stock market value .

The first firms to develop new products and services can usually occupy most of the previous market and gain a series of advantages, which is determined by the first-  mover advantage in strategic management, with which pioneering companies can obtain positive  economic  profits  (Lieberman  and  Montgomery  1988). According  to  the  above  discussion,  the  key  factor  of  the  first mover advantage is the 'order of entering the market.' On average,  the  first  mover  strategy  brings  a  long-  term  competitive advantage to a company, since it can gain a large share of the product and service market and a higher profit than late competitors. According to Lieberman and Montgomery (1990), in order to obtain the first-  mover advantage, there are three main methods: (1) buyer conversion costs, (2) technology leadership, and (3) asset preemption. Therefore, through technological growth, such as (1) innovative products or services and (2) new processes or process reengineering to consolidate competitive advantage, companies can enter a new market. Many companies implement new IT technologies to design new business models and improve business performance. As stated above, we believe that the implementation of AI technology may also bring the first-  mover advantage  to  enterprises.  Therefore,  we  have  the  following assumptions:

Hypothesis 2a. Firms adopting the first-  mover advantage of AI implementation will present better efficiency according to financial performance measurements .

Hypothesis 2b. Firms adopting the first-  mover advantage of AI implementation will present better efficiency according to productivity measurements .

Hypothesis 2c. Firms  adopting  the  first-  mover  advantage of AI implementation will present a better efficiency according to stock market value .

A survey reporting from SAS of Accenture Applied Intelligence, Intel, and Forbes Insight studied how firms are benefitting from AI. 1   SAS indicated that firms which implement the AI-  related technologies  can  save  business-  related  cost  expenditures,  optimize  business  processes  and  efficiency,  and  finally  improve profitability.

In this report, SAS revealed that AI has helped various companies to digitize their business process and find some amazing results. Three such results showing that these companies present  actual  efficiency  after  implementing  AI  technology include the accuracy of forecasting and decision-  making increased by 60%, developing and liaison with customers efficiency increased by 52%, and productivity increased by 48% (Insights 2018). As mentioned above, a better (AI) performer means that companies in certain industries can introduce AIrelated  technologies,  and  the  performance  of  the  company should be better compared to other companies. Therefore, we set up the following hypotheses:

Hypothesis 3a. Firms  considered  better  performers  will present  better  efficiency  according  to  financial  performance measurements .

Hypothesis 3b. Firms considered better performers will present better efficiency according to productivity measurements .

## 4   |   Research Methodology

Three major research streams are included in this section: data collection and selection in Section 4.1, descriptive statistics in Section 4.2, and research methods in Section 4.3.

## 4.1   |   Data Collection and Selection

The notion of AI was first proffered at the Dartmouth Society in  1956.  However,  the  development  of  AI  technologies  has gained more attention after deep learning was proposed in 2010. Therefore, we try our best to collect all possible news, including companies who invested or implemented AI between 2010 and 2017.  The  data  collection  process  is  described  as  follows.  The data we collected in this study are from 2010 to 2017; however, the baseline for performance analysis is only available in 2017. The  baseline  indicates  that  we  only  use  the  collected  performance data for these companies in the 2017 fiscal year.

In  this  study,  two  programs  were  used  to  collect  data.  We  confirmed  the  list  of  sample  companies,  collecting  data  through Compustat  and  then  selected  some  ratios  to  evaluate  their  performance. First, the sample framework for this study is based on the S&P 500 Index list. The S&P500 Index is the US stock market  index  based  on  the  market  capitalization  of  500  large  companies, listed on the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE), Nasdaq Stock Exchange (NASDAQ), or Cboe BZX Exchange. This is one of the most common stock indices and many researches consider it to be the most representative of the US stock market. Based on this list, we first excluded companies whose industry category belongs to information technology, and then used Google to browse relevant news through a keyword, artificial intelligence, to see if S&P 500 companies announced AI-  related news. In addition, we used the Google search engine to check other keywords relating to AI, which could be employed to find AI related information. We also used them to gather all the stories about AI in the news. Our only concern was the huge impact of IT-  type companies on IT;  we aimed to measure the true influence on AI without this impact. Therefore, in order to ensure that companies with news mentioning AI were definitely implementing AI, we carefully read the news content. Finally, we collected 249 news stories, but we filtered out 152 news stories because they were about IT-  related companies. The remainder (97 news) were included in our experiment.

Again, these two indicators can be used to track the investigation (Hitt, Wu, and Zhou 2002). Before applying these indicators to test the performance of AI adoption, we introduce the three methods, including financial performance analysis, productivity function, and stock market valuation (Tobin's q).

All in all, first movers bared the most relevant cost expenditures in the early stage, and faced the problem of wasting enterprise resources, but they could improve profit and gain a competitive advantage.

## 5.3   |   Comparison Between Better (AI) Performers and Non-  Better (AI) Performers

As discussed in the previous section, AI, which is the most popular new IT technology, has been implemented by many industries  and  improved  operational  efficiencies,  such  as  decision making and efficient contact with customers. We also found that retail, finance, and insurance are the better performing industries, outperforming the other four industries.

We used formula (3-3) to check whether the better performing industries were better than others according to financial indicators. The results are shown in Table 9. We can see that the results of financial indicators return on assets ( -0.152), inventory turnover ( -0.076), assets utilization ( -1.51), collection efficiency ( -0.194), leverage ( -0.021) are all negative, indicating that  there  is  no  significant  impact  on  financial  performance. Hypothesis 3a is thus rejected.

Notably, the Tobin's q ( -0.242) result showed a significant negative impact on the market value, as shown in Table 10. This is the opposite of our hypothesis (5-3). In order to understand the main cause of this conflict, we analyzed the 'retail' and 'financial and insurance' industries separately. The results of the market value indicators for retail were not significant, so we do not discuss them. Table 11 shows that the financial and insurance industries' Tobin's q ( -0.296) is negative and more significant than the previous indicator ( -0.242).

As a result, we tried to figure out what caused the market value to be negative and more significant. We found that the years we collected data were from 2010 to 2017, and there was a financial crisis (2008) during that period, which affected people's confidence in the investments of the bank industry. Furthermore, we found a news stories indicating that more than half of the people in the United States still had no confidence in the future of the economy until 2012. They believed that the economy was still in a slump (Morgan 2011). The study of Zouaoui, Nouyrigat, and Beer (2011) proposed that investor sentiments seem to have a positive impact on the likelihood of a stock market crash. For countries culturally prone to collective behavior and overreaction, investor sentiment has a greater impact on the stock market.

Therefore,  when  companies  spend  a  lot  of  capital  on  new  IT technologies in an uncertain economy, investors will have a negative and significant response. As a result, Hypotheses 3a and 3c are rejected, yet Hypothesis 3b is partially supported.

## 6.2   |   Implications

The latest advancements in AI have enhanced the performance evaluation  of  firms.  AI  implementation  is  diverse  and  has  a revolutionary impact on the firm level. It can not only improve the performance of companies in many ways, but also lead to innovation of business models. Because of the huge changes in new IT implementation, many scholars have also discussed the impact. However, there has been a lack of research on the implementation of AI. Therefore, to provide a comprehensive discussion, we applied a variety of methods to measure the impact of AI implementation on internal performance.

This survey confirms that AI is beneficial and provides a competitive  advantage  to  companies.  Our  findings  offer  practical value for managers and enterprises. However, firms in the financial and insurance industries should exercise greater caution when investing in AI.

## 6.3   |   Limitations and Future Research

The first limitation is data analysis. As mentioned above, we collected data from 2010 to 2017, and the base year for the research was 2017. We did not carry out a longitudinal study for several years. Since the benchmarks are the same, this approach does not provide a comprehensive estimate over time. Each company implemented AI at different times, so we should consider each fiscal year. Therefore, we recommend that researchers can use average performance data from different years as a benchmark for investigating AI performance. This would lead to more accurate results.

Second, the firm data structure of this study comes from the S&P 500 index list. The data we used in this study were based on April 30, 2019. However, the S&P 500 Index list is not fixed and can be adjusted at any time. Therefore, while the lists used by other researchers were lightly different from ours, this did not affect our research results. In addition, to gather information  on  companies'  implementation  of  AI-  related  initiatives, we  sourced  data  from  official  websites,  academic  journals, magazines, and Google, including details on when each company  began  adopting  AI.  Some  companies  implemented  AI but did not show relevant information on the Internet, so that we were unable to collect their information. This may have influenced the accuracy of our experimental results. Google search results also revealed that many companies not listed on the S&P 500 have begun investing in AI-  related operations; however, these companies were not included in our dataset. Therefore, we recommend that future researchers can extend the sample size from the S&P 500 to the S&P 1000 to ensure the external validity of research results.

## 6.4   |   Conclusion

This study has examined the impact of AI implementation on firm performance through a comprehensive analysis of financial performance,  productivity  measurement,  and  market  value (Tobin's q). Focusing on firms outside the IT-  centric sector, we have explored the tangible effects of AI adoption, first-  mover advantage, and better performance metrics on AI-  enabled firms. Our findings reveal that while AI adoption generally enhances financial  performance and market value, the anticipated benefits  for  first  movers  and  better  performers  are  not  uniformly realized across all indicators. This insight offers valuable implications for managers and stakeholders, emphasizing a nuanced approach to AI investment decisions that considers both potential gains and limitations.

Looking forward, this study contributes to a deeper understanding of AI business value and opens up several future prospects. First, future research could expand the sample scope to include additional sectors and international firms, providing a broader perspective on AI global impact. Longitudinal studies would also be valuable to track AI effects over time, particularly to observe any evolving productivity impacts and the persistence of market value gains. In addition, as AI technologies rapidly advance, exploring the specific contributions of emerging AI types, such as generative AI or predictive analytics, to different aspects of firm performance would provide further insights. These directions for future research will help managers and stakeholders better understand  the  long-  term  business  potential  of  AI  and  refine strategic approaches to AI integration.
