AI-infused knowledge (AIK) systems have the potential to transform the way firms operate, providing dynamics for optimizing processes, enhancing decision-making, and fostering innovation. This shift can play a vital role in service-oriented contexts, like accounting firms. In this study, we aim to explore how AIK can drive green intellectual capital (GIC) aspects, spurring accounting firms ' performance related to environmental and sustainable practices. ## 2. Literature review

## 2.1. Theoretical basis: RBV-KBV framework

The RBV, popularised by Barney (1991), posits that firms can achieve a  competitive  advantage  by  possessing  and  utilizing  valuable,  rare, inimitable,  and non-substitutable (VRIN) resources. The collection of resources consists of physical assets with human talents and organizational system management and mental knowledge. RBV explains performance differences between firms through resource heterogeneity and mobility limitations because some organizations hold distinct resources which  competition  finds  difficult  to  duplicate  (Arend  and  L ´ evesque, 2010;  Kozlenkova et  al.,  2014).  Accounting  firms  which  successfully utilize their GIC including sustainability expertise together with environmentally friendly technologies and stakeholder relationships obtain superior  performance  as  well  as  increased  market  competitiveness (Ahmad  et  al.,  2023;  Kamukama & Sulait,  2017).  The  advanced sustainability-related resources found in certain organizations enhance their reporting practices by delivering better efficiency together with compliance  and  innovative  capabilities.  The  RBV  coupled  with  sustainability efforts enables organizations to reach operational excellence and satisfy regulatory and societal standards according to Arda et al. (2023) and Samadhiya and Agrawal (2024). Unique green resources enable  organizations  to  boost  performance  through  compliance  and innovative  practices  in  addition  to  developing  beneficial  reputations (Barney, 2001; Hart, 1995; Khanra et al., 2022).

The KBV extends the RBV by emphasizing that knowledge is the most critical  resource  in  modern  organizations  (Cuthbertson & Furseth, 2022). According to Grant (1996), firms are repositories of knowledge, and their  success  depends  on how effectively they create,  store, and apply knowledge. This view focuses on how organizations manage intellectual capital, particularly through human capital, relational capital, and structural capital, to foster innovation and performance (Low & Ho, 2016; Zheng et al., 2010; Eisenhardt & Santos, 2002). For accounting firms, KBV underlines the importance of leveraging knowledge-driven processes,  particularly  in sustainability  practices  (Azmi  et  al.,  2024). Firms that manage their green human capital (employee skills in sustainability), green structural capital (systems and processes that support sustainability), and green relational capital (stakeholder relationships focused on environmental concerns) are more likely to achieve superior accounting  performance  (Asiaei  et  al.,  2022).  Integrating  AI-infused knowledge systems can further enhance these knowledge capabilities, allowing firms to generate  insights,  optimize  processes,  and  increase compliance and reporting efficiency (Nonaka, 2009; Teece, 2007). By combining insights from both RBV and KBV, we can argue that firms that leverage both their unique green resources and advanced knowledge capabilities are better equipped to achieve superior accounting performance, especially in areas such as compliance, accuracy, and innovation in reporting.

## 2.2. AI-infused knowledge and green intellectual capital

AI-infused  knowledge  (AIK)  plays  a  transformative  role  across various  industries,  enhancing  sustainability  and  improving  firm  performance.  In  manufacturing,  AIK  optimizes  production  processes  by reducing waste and improving energy efficiency (Jing & Zhang, 2024). In  healthcare,  AIK  supports  diagnosis  and  enhances  operational  efficiency,  leading  to  better  patient  outcomes  and  reduced  costs  (Dutt, 2020). In finance, AIK improves risk assessment, automates financial analysis, and ensures regulatory compliance (Milana & Ashta, 2021). AIK enables the retail industry to enhance demand forecasting as well as inventory management and personalized customer experiences which saves resources by utilizing them more efficiently (Oosthuizen et al., 2021).  Digitized  metrological  knowledge  through  AIK  helps  farmers practice  precise  agriculture  which  optimizes  water  conservation  and minimizes  chemical  product  waste  for  sustainable  food  management

(Javaid  et  al.,  2023).  AIK  demonstrates  usefulness  for  improving educational environments and management systems in the educational sector according to Bouteraa et al. (2024) and Sadallah et al. (2025). The use  cases  outline  AIK ' s  wide  applicability  in  various  sectors  for  supporting sustainable operations as well as process optimization (Cubric, 2020).

AIK systems have emerged as fundamental organizational tools to upgrade GHC performance according to Almansour (2024) and Kusi-Sarpong, Mubarik, Khan, Brown, and Mubarak (2022). Firms that utilize AI technologies can create training programs which deliver customized sustainability development experiences to employees. Such employee training  builds  essential  green  initiative  competence  which  leads  to sustainability  culture  development  according  to  AL-Khatib  and  Shuhaiber (2022) and Gashenko et al. (2020). Employees ' learning patterns undergo  AI  analysis  so  that  customized  materials  become  available which follow sustainability-oriented organizational objectives (Cicerone et  al.,  2023).  Through  targeted  training  programs  organizations  can boost  environmental  consciousness  and  create  possibilities  for  their employees to implement innovative practices which drive sustainable development in the company (Abdelfattah et al., 2024).

GSC includes all operations ' systems and processes together with infrastructure  that  supports  sustainable  operations  (Hoang  Thanh & Truong Cong, 2024; Shehzad et al., 2023). Knowledge augmented by AI capabilities boosts GSC through the optimal management of operations and resources (Ruzza et al., 2020). AI evaluation of extensive records enables managers to locate operational inefficiencies that lead to better resource decisions for waste reduction (Li et al., 2023; Nishant et al., 2020). Organizations using structural capital with AI-powered systems can develop superior environmental management systems to precisely monitor and present sustainable initiative progress (Cheng et al., 2024). The connected operations prove beneficial for operational effectiveness and sustainability goals by improving organizational performance.

The  interplay  between  AIK  systems  and  GRC  presents  a  more nuanced  picture.  The  sustainability  practices  through  GRC  rely  on collaborative networks between stakeholders for their achievement (Wu & Yu,  2024).  The  implementation  of  AIK  systems  within  accounting firms  generates  substantial  GRC  improvement  through  stronger  connections with environmentally oriented stakeholders and both clients and legal bodies. The external social networks through which a firm supports  its  sustainability  initiatives  are  known  as  GRC.  The  implementation  of  AI  tools  including  data  analytics  and  environmental reporting automation and advanced client management  systems through accounting firms lets them serve sustainability-conscious clients interested  in  corporate  social  responsibility  (CSR)  (Bharadwaj  et  al., 2013; Fukuda-Parr & Gibbons, 2021 Fukuda-Parr & Gibbons, 2021). AI improves  sustainability  data  reporting  accuracy  through  automated environmental process management which enhances stakeholders ' trust about  environmental  concerns  and  client  faith  in  accounting  firms ' sustainability practices (Lourenço, Branco, Curto, & Eug ´ enio, 2012). AI systems  evaluate  extensive  information  about  clients  to  find  sustainability  possibilities  and  risks  which  enables  accounting  firms  to  give advanced  environmentally-oriented  counsel  that  creates  strong  partnerships through shared sustainability aims. The implementation of AI systems helps accounting firms develop strong relations with sustainability-related regulatory bodies and industry organizations. The integration of AI technology with blockchain features delivers improved data  visibility  allowing  firms  to  validate  their  adherence  to  growing sustainable regulations (Grewal, Riedl, & Serafeim, 2019). AI can also assist in monitoring clients ' adherence to environmental standards and integrating  sustainability  metrics  into  financial  performance  assessments. As accounting firms increasingly incorporate sustainability into their service offerings, AI helps build and maintain GIC by aligning the firm ' s capabilities with clients ' environmental goals and expectations (Goralski,  2020;  Goralski & Tan, 2020). ## 2.3. GIC and accounting performance

Literature has mainly paid attention to the traditional aspects of intellectual  capital  and  firm  performance  (Bayraktaroglu  et  al.,  2019; Truong et al., 2024; Xu & Li, 2022), the role of intellectual capital in firms ' competitive advantage (Ahmad et al., 2023; Kamukama & Sulait, 2017), green intellectual capital and firm performance (Hoang Thanh & Truong Cong, 2024; Shehzad et al., 2023; Umar et al., 2024). As the first dimension  of  GIC,  GHC  refers  to  employees ' skills,  knowledge,  and competencies regarding environmental sustainability. Employees equipped  with  sustainability-related  skills  contribute  to  improved decision-making processes and enhance the firm ' s  capability  to  meet regulatory requirements. Additionally, training programms that focus on green practices not only increase employee awareness but also foster a culture of sustainability within firms, leading to better financial outcomes  (Munawar  et  al.,  2022;  Zhou  et  al.,  2023).  Previous  studies indicated  that  GHC  contributes  to  enhanced  firm  performance  by fostering  a  culture  of  environmental  awareness  and  accountability among employees (Hoang Thanh & Truong Cong, 2024; Shehzad et al., 2023). The ongoing development of GHC by firms demonstrates their environmental  responsibility  at  market  levels  and  enables  better  interactions  concerning  stakeholders  such  as  clients  and  investors  and regulatory  bodies.  Accounting  firms  can  strengthen  multiple  performance aspects through the implementation of GHC because it enhances operational efficiency as well as reporting accuracy and innovation and delivers  prompt  service  while  achieving  environmental  standards compliance (Abernathy et al., 2014; Chan & Vasarhelyi, 2018; DeFond & Zhang, 2014; Mancini et al., 2017). Positive sustainability reputation creates trust while boosting credibility which serves as essential drivers for enduring business success (Pham et al., 2020).

## H4 . GHC positively influences accounting firms ' performance.

GSC systems  in  companies  embody  environmental  programs  that require process establishment and structural development and continuous  integration  maintenance  of  all  participating  elements.  Prior research validated that firms possessing exceptional GSC demonstrate enhanced accounting performance based on Li et al. (2023) and Yusliza et al. (2020). When organizations implement thorough environmental management  systems  they  gain  enhanced  sustainability  monitoring abilities leading to workflow enhancement that minimizes operational costs. Accounting performance increases through GSC because organizations  manage  their  resources  exactly  and  decrease  waste  spending (Umar et al., 2024). Financial results improve when stakeholders trust GSC due to its transparent practices (Yu et al., 2021). GSC enables accounting  firms  to  deploy  systems  of  organizational  structure  with technology  frameworks  and  sustainability  policies  to  establish  environmental sustainability in their operations. Research has established that companies dedicating attention to GSC accomplish enhanced performance related to regulatory acceptance and innovative sustainable reporting practices and operational success (Shehzad al., 2023; Ullah et  al.,  2022;  Ali  et  al.,  2022;  Yusliza  et  al.,  2020).  Accounting  firms establish readiness for legal and stakeholder compliance needs through effective implementation of environmental management systems which links compliance tasks with sustainability metrics. Environmental rule compliance  improves  and  sustainability  disclosures  become  stronger when firms establish proper alignment according to research. Understanding and accountability development through transparency creates trustworthy relationships with dedicated customers.

## H5 . GSC positively influences accounting firms ' performance.

GRC is about relationships a firm maintains with its stakeholders ' concerning sustainability. The literature indicates that strong GRC can significantly enhance firms ' performance by fostering collaboration and knowledge sharing among stakeholders (Nirino et al., 2022; Yu et al., 2021).  Companies  that  invest  in  green  rational  capital  by  actively engaging with their stakeholders, e.g., clients, suppliers, and community organizations, on sustainability issues often experience improved brand reputation and client loyalty. This can also help differentiate themselves in a competitive market and ultimately enhance performance (Yadiati et  al.,  2019).  By  establishing  themselves  as  trusted  advisors  on  sustainability reporting and environmental compliance, accounting firms with high GRC can enhance their reputation and strengthen client relationships, ultimately leading to improved financial performance and client retention. Green rationality drives innovation and value creation within accounting firms, which translates into higher performance (Ali et al., 2022; Ullah et al., 2022; Umar et al., 2024; Shehzad al., 2023; Yusliza et al., 2020). When firms engage deeply with stakeholders who value  sustainability,  they  gain  insights  into  emerging  sustainability trends, standards, and client needs (Zhou et al., 2023). This ongoing collaboration encourages accounting firms to innovate in areas such as green  auditing,  sustainability  reporting,  and  compliance  consulting services.  However,  the  impact  of  GRC  on  accounting  performance  is complex, as it may also depend on the firm ' s ability to leverage these relationships for innovative solutions and sustainable practices (Juo & Wang, 2022). Thus, firms need GRC to achieve better financial outcomes from their sustainable initiatives.

## 2.4. Sustainability culture (SUC) as a moderator

Sustainability culture (SUC) embodies a set of shared values, practices, and beliefs to prioritise sustainable practices and environmental responsibilities within companies and among their employees (Al-Emran,  2023;  Al-Emran & Griffy-Brown,  2023;  Marshall  et  al., 2015). Companies implement sustainability initiatives and their overall performance results from their cultural orientation (Galpin et al., 2015; Haladu & Bin-Nashwan, 2024; Haladu et al., 2025a, 2025b; Haladu & Bin-Nashwan, 2022). The research demonstrates that businesses with robust sustainability culture frameworks achieve better financial outcomes from their global information centers (GIC) (Sch ¨ onborn et al., 2019; Siyal et al., 2022). Organizations adopting a sustainable strategic approach  prioritise  sustainability  in  their  decisions  which  produces more efficient reporting processes.

Likewise, SUC can play a critical role in bridging the gap between GIC components and organizational performance outcomes. Although the existing literature that explored SUC  appears very limited, Sch ¨ onborn et al. (2019) suggested that a strong sustainability culture can amplify the positive effects of GHC on innovation and operational effectiveness, as employees are more likely to engage in environmentally friendly  practices  when  supported  by  an  organizational  culture  that values  sustainability  (Ketprapakorn & Kantabutra,  2022;  Salvioni & Almici, 2020). Scholars, such as Yong et al. (2019), have shown that employees ' skills  and  motivations  around  environmental  initiatives have a greater effect on performance when backed by a sustainability-driven organisational culture, reinforcing environmental goals, and leading to improved productivity, innovation, and compliance. On the other hand, in firms with a poor sustainability culture, GHC benefits may not materialise as intended, resulting in missed opportunities for improving performance.

Firms with high sustainability cultural values enhance the impact of eco-oriented policies through daily operation integration while increasing  strategic  decision  effectiveness  (Salvioni & Almici,  2020). According to past studies (Liu et al., 2021; Sch ¨ onborn et al., 2019), firms with an established sustainability culture not only improve compliance and reporting accuracy but also increase operational efficiency through S.A. This  alignment  strengthens  organisational capabilities and optimizes performance metrics, especially in areas like cost  savings  and  resource  management.  Likewise,  GRC  finds  value through SUC because the approach helps organizations uphold enduring relationships  with  stakeholders  through  mutual  environmental  commitments (Siyal et al., 2022). When SUC supports GRC, firms tend to experience greater stakeholder loyalty and trust, leading to enhanced market reputation and brand differentiation (Yu et al., 2021). As such, firms with a strong culture of sustainability can better leverage their relational  capital,  translating  stakeholder  goodwill  into  competitive advantages and improved operational and financial outcomes. ## 3. Research methods

## 3.1. Sampling and data collection

The  online  survey  remains  popular  because  it  offers  researchers benefits  which  include  formulating  research  models  and  hypotheses together with affordability and direct access to the audience as well as fast response options (Fricker & Schonlau, 2002). The researchers have selected an online survey as the optimal research method for their study on AI tools (Bin-Nashwan, Sadallah, & Bouteraa, 2023). Our research determines  the  ways  ChatGPT  integration  impacts  GIC  operations  to achieve  better  accounting  outcomes  from  an  accounting  professional viewpoint. The selection of accounting professionals for this study was attributed to the fact that they actively work with extensive data while managing compliance needs and making decisions where AIK significantly affects their activities (Bin-Nashwan et al., 2025). Professional accountants  supply  essential  knowledge  about  AIK ' s  potential  to  increase operational effectiveness as well as sustainability for this industry sector.  The  findings  can  produce beneficial  implications for  different service-based sectors which possess similar traits including strict regulatory oversight and data-driven business approaches. The online survey took place from December 21, 2023 to January 7, 2024. Over 5000 invitations  via  online  platforms  were  sent  to  Chinese  panelists  to participate in the study. The sample was chosen randomly and fulfilled specific criteria, including accounting firms, and to be responded to by accounting professionals, such as chief financial officers (CFOs), audit managers, senior accountants, or senior auditors. The chosen participants can provide comprehensive practical, technical, and strategic insights. To effectively filter participants and ensure a qualified sample that aligns with the research objectives (Short et al., 2002), we included a screening question: "Has your firm already adopted AI in accounting services?" This question was designed to ensure that participants have direct experience with AI implementation, as their insights are essential to understanding the impact of AIK on accounting firm performance. By using this criterion, we focused on firms where AI adoption is actively influencing operations.

The study participants received a detailed description of the study ' s objectives and scope together with confirmation their responses would remain private and would only be used for academic investigations. The survey questions were arranged differently to prevent possible bias from influencing responses. The research eliminated participants  who finished each question within 5 s or less because such rapid answers point  toward  unreliable  data  in  digital  surveys  (Bin-Nashwan & Muneeza, 2023). automated systems removed participants who finished the survey questions in unusually short amounts of time or repeated answers without hesitation as they likely did not fully consider their responses. The survey included 461 respondents who managed to pass the screening criterion among all who took the assessment. An analysis was performed using 433 valid responses because the remaining data underwent an outlier exclusion process.

An English back-translation  of  the Chinese  questionnaire  was  performed using the approach mentioned in Douglas and Craig (2007).

## 3.3. Data analysis tool

The researchers used SmartPLS variance-based partial least squares structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM) software for assessing the integrated model. The recommendation of Hair et al. (2017) prompted us to  select  PLS-SEM  because  the  method  suits  non-normal  data  and exploratory investigation. The data collected exhibited non-normality as we obtained a p-value lower than 0.05 from the Mardia ' s multivariate skewness test using WebPower ' s online tool. The research model needed PLS-SEM  as  its  estimation  method  since  it  proved  suitable  for  this purpose.

The  research  evaluated  common  method  bias  (CMB)  by  studying endogenous and exogenous constructs together with the same participants  through  equivalent  procedures  (Podsakoff  et  al.,  2003).  The evaluation  of  CMB  utilized  Harmon ' s Single-Factor test  through exploratory factor analysis according to the methodology. A study by Podsakoff et al. (2003) states that CMB does not exist when different factors emerge in analysis and the first factor only explains less than 50 % of the variance. The proposed model results showed the CMB value stood below 50 % which verified the study did not display a substantial CMB issue (Bin-Nashwan, Shah, et al., 2023).

## 4. Results analysis

## 4.1. Sample details

Accounting professionals consisting of 54 % males and 46 % females participated in the survey which demonstrates almost perfect equality between genders. The survey shows a balanced representation of data since it obtained reactions from equally split genders. Within the surveyed professional roles 48 % served as audit managers while senior auditors made up 25 % and senior accountants were 20 % with 7 % working as CFOs within the firms. In terms of professional qualifications, 40 % held CPA certifications, 35 % were ACCA members, and 19 % had CMA credentials. This variety in professional certifications contributes to  a  comprehensive  analysis  of  how  AI-infused  knowledge-GIC  influences accounting firm performance. As for educational attainment, 61 % had a bachelor ' s degree, 36 % held postgraduate degrees (master ' s or PhD), and 3 % had diplomas or other certifications. Regarding work experience, 80 % had over ten years in the field, indicating that the participants are highly experienced and well-educated, providing valuable insights into the research topic.

## 4.2. Measurement model

The outer model results which consist of the measurement model appear in Tables 2 and 3. The proposed model achieved satisfactory internal  reliability  because  all  latent  constructs  maintained  values greater  than  0.70  for  Cronbach ' s α and  Dijkstra-Henseler ' s  rho  and composite reliability (Table 2) (Hair et al., 2017). The assessment of convergent  validity  (CV)  relied  on  checking  factor  loadings  together with  the  average  variance  extracted  (AVE).  The  majority  of  items revealed loadings surpassing 0.70 yet two items (SUC5 and ACP11) had values below 0.50 and were excluded (Table 2). The evaluation of the proposed model indicates its robust convergent validity in accordance with Hair et al. (2017).

Henseler et al. (2015) indicated that the heterotrait-monotrait ratio (HTMT) of correlation  should  be  used  for  model  evaluation.  All  the HTMT values in the study remained under the recommended threshold of 0.85 with variations between 0.540 and 0.833 according to Henseler et al. (2015). ## 4.3. Structural model

Several preliminary standards needed to be evaluated before we can conduct  our  analysis  of  path  coefficients  and  test  the  proposed  hypotheses.  The  VIF  test  showed  no  multicollinearity  since  all  values remained  below  3.3  at  the  recommendation  of  Diamantopoulos  and Siguaw (2006). We then examined the endogenous variable, accounting performance R 2 (variance  explained).  The  analysis  shows  that  the explanatory  power  of  the  collectively  direct  impact  of  the  proposed model, i.e., GHC, GSC, and GRC, as well as the moderating role of SUC, in ADP was 42 %. On the other. This outcome indicates the significant level  and  good  fit  of  the  proposed  model,  capturing  crucial  drives shaping  AI-infused  knowledge-GIC  and  accounting  performance.  The predictive capability of the research model was further evaluated using the  blindfolding  technique  in  SmartPLS,  with  a  predefined  omission distance of seven, to calculate Stone-Geisser ' s Q 2 value. The findings in Table 4 indicate that the Q 2 values for the endogenous construct were scored as 0.489 above zero. This result demonstrates the satisfactory predictive relevance of the research model (Hair et al., 2017).

The proposed hypotheses were tested using PLS bootstrapping with 5000 resamples, analyzing the corresponding t-statistics and path coefficients (Chin et al., 2003). The results displayed in Table 4 reveal that GHC ( β = 0.221; p = 0.000) and GSC β = 0.225; p = 0.004) within accounting firms were influenced by AI-infused knowledge (AIK) systems. Hence, we found that hypotheses H1  and H2  are statistically accepted. However, the impact of AIK on GRC ( β = 0.016; p = 0.865) within accounting firms was insignificant. Thus, H3 is rejected.

Additionally, we found that accounting firms ' performance is influenced by two components of GIC, i.e., GHC ( β = 0.390; p = 0.000), and GSC ( β = 0.194; p = 0.005). In contrast, GRC failed to report a significant effect on accounting performance ( β = 0.074; p = 0.616). Hence, H4 and H5  are  accepted, while H6  is rejected. With regard to interaction relationships, the analysis indicates that all interaction effects of GHC x sustainability culture ( β = 0.295; p = 0.001), GSC x sustainability culture  ( β = 0.210; p = 0.004),  and  GRC  x  sustainability  culture  ( β = 0.199; p = 0.000) on accounting firms ' performance were significant. Thus, H7, H8, and H9 are accepted.

## 5. Discussion and implications

## 5.1. Discussion

Given the timely and relevance of AI-green intellectual capital integration and its impact on accounting professionals ' performance, this study provides empirical evidence drawn from a viable model of RBVKBV  model  and  sustainability  culture.  While  prior  research has explored the role of AI tools in enhancing organizational performance (Mikalef et al., 2023; Olan et al., 2022; Wamba-Taguimdje et al., 2020) and the contribution of IC to competitive advantage (Ahmad et al., 2023; Kamukama & Sulait, 2017), there is a distinct lack of research examining the specific interplay between AI-infused knowledge-GIC and accounting  performance  in  a  sustainability-infused  context.  The  literature extensively focused on traditional IC components without delving into how such elements can be adapted to the growing demand for sustainability.  Furthermore,  little  is  known  about  how  AI  impacts  each dimension  of  GIC  within  accounting  firms,  particularly  in  regard  to sustainability-focused innovations.

It  means  that  AI-infused  knowledge systems enhance internal capabilities through the automation of tasks and the improvement of decision-making processes within accounting firms. The obtained result showing AIK positively related to GHC indicates that cutting-edge technology like AIK plays an essential role in developing  the  sustainability-related  skills  and  competencies  of  accounting professionals.  AIK  platforms  have  the  potential  to  facilitate personalized  learning  experiences,  providing  real-time  access  to  sustainability resources and enhancing training programs. Such a function is  more  relevant  to  accounting  firms,  where  professionals  must  stay updated  on  evolving  sustainability-related  practices  and  compliance regulations. By equipping accounting firms with the needful knowledge and  tools,  AI  technologies  foster  accounting  workforces  to  be  better prepared  to  participate  in  green  initiatives  and  contribute  to  firms ' sustainability goals. While this relationship has sparsely examined in the literature, our conclusion could be relatively aligned with a prior study ' results by Saxena (2024), who explored the impact of industry 4.0 on green intellectual capital.

We also found that green structural capital (GSC) is positively and significantly influenced by AI-infused knowledge. This indicates that the strong  effect  of  IAK  on  accounting  firms ' GSC  further  reinforces  its importance  in  sustainable  practices.  AIK  provides  the  capability  to structure  operational  frameworks  more  efficiently  while  advancing resource management best practices as well as data systems for higher operational  effectiveness.  The  combination  of  AI-based  systems  enhances  accounting  firms ' ability  to  address  inefficiencies  while  conforming to environment standards and practicing sustainability more effectively through automated tasks and data analysis. Accounting firms should allocate priority to structural enhancements which will enable them  to  integrate  sustainability-related  practices  across  their  fundamental business operations. The studies about similar findings remain S.A. Bin-Nashwan and J.Z. Li scarce yet Goralski and Tan (2020) established that business internal practices benefit greatly from AI technologies for environmental sustainability  work.  The  analysis  discovered  that  AI-based  knowledge created no change in green rational capital performance. Understanding the AI limitations for external relationship development becomes crucial based on these findings. Green rational capital involves networks and relationships with stakeholders like clients, venders, and the community at large, as are critical for collaborative sustainability practices. This insignificant result indicates that although AIK can streamline internal processes and individual competencies, it might not be able to effectively support interpersonal dynamics necessary to foster cooperation and trust with external stakeholders. The unsupported outcome could be attributed to the transactional nature of AI technologies, which prioritizes data processing, compliance, and financial analysis over encouraging the kind of cooperation and communication necessary to forge solid client and stakeholder relationships. Moreover, accounting clients and  stakeholders  may  be  skeptical  of  AI  technologies  (Bosse  et  al., 2023), viewing them as impersonal and less trustworthy than human interactions for complex financial matters. AI-infused knowledge systems may not be able to fully handle the complex and intricate stakeholder interactions in accounting that call for a thorough awareness of client-specific  requirements  and  regulatory  settings.  As  a  result,  the interplay  between  green  relational  capital  and  AI  within  accounting firms may be limited.

The study also reveals that two aspects of green intellectual capital (i. e.,  GHC  and  GSC)  were  positively  associated  with  accounting  firms ' performance  given  to  the  various  roles  they  play  within  accounting firms,  while  GRC  was  not.  Green  human  capital -professionals ' sustainability  knowledge  and  skill -directly  drives  accounting  firm  performance through higher efficiency in sustainable practices, improved compliance with environmental regulations, and fostering innovation in financial disclosures. Despite the literature having rarely explored the GHC -accounting performance relationship, from a broad perspective prior studies revealed that firms investing in GHC can gain better performance (Mansoor et al., 2021; Yusliza et al., 2020). Likewise, green structural capital (i.e., encompasses systems, processes, and infrastructure supporting sustainability) helps to streamline operations, guaranteeing accurate and timely reporting, creative reporting techniques, and improved adherence to regulatory standards. Scholars from different contexts (e.g., Asiaei et al., 2022) also affirmed that GRC plays a substantial role in fostering firms ' performance related to environmental and sustainable practices. Alternatively, we found that green rational capital -which describes firm networks and relationships with stakeholders -did not affect accounting firms ' performance. This conclusion could be justified that organizational performance is infused more by internal factors, including professionals ' skills related to GHC and sustainable systems related to GSC. Although green rational capital has the potential to enhance trust and long-term relationships, its effects are gradual and might not have an instant impact on the operational components  of  accounting  firms ' performance  explored  in  our  research. Studies, such as Asiaei et al. (2022), also found no effect of GRC on organizational performance.

With the introduction of sustainability culture as a moderator in our model, the analysis revealed interesting outcomes. With sustainability culture in place, all dynamics of green intellectual capital are significantly fostering accounting firms ' performance. When accounting firms have a considerable sustainability culture, the value of GHC, GSC, and GRC is  amplified  as  accounting  professionals,  staff,  and  systems  are likely  to  be  more  aligned  with  sustainable  practices.  This  alignment stimulates improved adherence to environmental regulations, increases productivity  through  the  simplification  of  environmentally  friendly processes, improves reporting accuracy through sustainable data management practices, encourages creativity in the preparation and delivery of reports, and aids in consistently meeting deadlines. We can argue that the  impact  of  GIC  on  performance  increases  when  sustainability  is ingrained  in  the  company ' s  culture,  particularly  in  areas  where operational excellence and sustainability overlap. From the analysis, it is demonstrated  that  the  integration  of  sustainability  culture  into  the model altered the insignificant association between green rational capital and accounting performance to be significant. This result is in line with the literature (Eccles et al., 2014) showing that firms with a robust sustainability  culture  are  more  likely  to  engage  meaningfully  with stakeholders, driving improved performance outcomes.

## 5.2. Policy implications

The results of this research offer actionable insights for stakeholders and decision-makers, including accounting firms, clients, and the community at large. Accounting firms are highly recommended to adopt AIinfused platforms to foster GIC dynamics, specifically GHC and GSC. AIinfused knowledge tools have the potential to improve efficiency, accuracy,  innovation  in  reporting,  compliance  with  sustainability  standards and timeliness, thus leading to better overall performance. It is suggested that firms be invested in training their staff and professionals to use AI technologies effectively, thus leveraging knowledge, skills, and competences infused within accounting firms in sustainability reporting, carbon  accounting,  and  environmental  regulations.  Additionally,  the impact of AI-infused knowledge on green structural capital promotes the development of advanced, AI-powered systems that support innovative methods for sustainability reporting. Firms that use AI to improve their GSC are better able to meet stakeholder expectations and regulatory requirements by providing accurate and timely sustainability reports. Accounting firms could put in place specialized training courses that cover sustainability principles and AI tools. Accounting professional skill development  in  utilizing  AI  systems  for  environmental  reporting  is facilitated by this approach, which also guarantees that green human capital is maximised for improving performance indicators such as accuracy, compliance, and innovation.

Developing  a  robust  sustainability  culture  in  accounting  firms  is essential  to  optimizing  the  influence  of  GIC  aspects  on  their  performance. Sustainability culture acts as a catalyst, GHC, GSC, and GRC all have a stronger impact on accounting firms ' performance. Incorporating sustainability  into  organisational  principles,  plans,  and  accounting professionals and staff engagement initiatives is imperative for firms to optimize the advantages of AI systems and enhance compliance, efficiency,  and  creativity  in  sustainability  reporting.  Additionally,  it  is suggested that accounting firms need to pay attention to enhancing internal architecture, procedures, and systems supporting sustainability practices. This can be done by implementing AI technologies and ecofriendly platforms that facilitate process optimization, promote innovation, and minimize resources ' consumption. Although green rational capital did not directly relate to performance, accounting firms could enhance their value through sustainability initiatives, building trust and collaboration with outsider stakeholders, such as clients, suppliers and regulators. Firms can align with broader social goals through strategies including involving clients in sustainability efforts and clear communication about environmental activities. These strategies can eventually translate into long-term performance gains. The study also recommends that  AI  solutions  might  be  tailored  to  meet  reporting  requirements pertaining to sustainability, such as observing environmental laws. Accounting firms may enhance the speed and accuracy of sustainability disclosures  and  successfully  address  market  and  regulatory  needs  by integrating AI technology into their reporting frameworks.

While our study focuses  on  accounting  firms,  the  insights  gained from  AIK  and  GIC  may  be  applicable  to  sectors,  such  as  finance, consulting, and legal services. For example, like accounting firms, these industries rely heavily on knowledge management and precision, and AI tools can enhance efficiency and sustainability efforts. However, variations  in  industry-specific  regulations  and  operational  structures  may lead to differences in how AIK drives GIC and influences performance. Future research could explore these differences to further validate the broader relevance of AI-driven sustainability practices across various S.A. Bin-Nashwan and J.Z. Li service sectors.

## 5.3. Theoretical implications

Upon a thorough review of the literature, this research is a pioneer and  provides  profound  implications  to  the  theory  and  theoretical framework of the interplay between AI-infused knowledge and green intellectual  capital,  and  eventually  how  to  shape  accounting  firms ' performance. The contributions of this study are summarized as follows: First, the literature has mainly paid attention to the traditional aspects of intellectual capital and firm performance (Bayraktaroglu et al., 2019; Truong et al., 2024; Xu & Li, 2022), green intellectual capital and firm performance (Hoang Thanh & Truong Cong, 2024; Shehzad et al., 2023; Umar  et  al.,  2024),  and  the  role  of  intellectual  capital  in  firms ' competitive advantage (Ahmad et al., 2023; Kamukama & Sulait, 2017). However, there is a dearth of research exploring the crucial impact of AI-infused knowledge on green intellectual capital, and thus fostering firms ' performance. Therefore, the present study is likely the first of its kind to delve empirically into the interplay between AI-infused knowledge on green intellectual capital. Second, the green intellectual capital -fi rm performance relationship was examined in the literature to a reasonable  extent,  it  is  not  overly  saturated  compared  to  traditional intellectual capital. Prior studies have investigated the effect of GIC on performance in certain domains, such as manufacturing (Umar et al., 2024; Shehzad al., 2023; Yusliza et al., 2020; Mansoor et al., 2019), hospitality  (Elzek  et  al.,  2024;  Haldorai  et  al.,  2022;  Shahbaz  et  al., 2024), high-tech industry (Wang & Juo, 2021), SMEs (Khanlarov et al., 2020, pp. 100 -109), healthcare (Zahlan et al., 2023), energy (Liu et al., 2021), education (Ahmed et al., 2023), and waste recycling (Haddad et al., 2024). Yet, although service-oriented contexts, like accounting, also have the potential to benefit from green intellectual capital practices,  research  in  such  a  significant  discipline  has  been  remarkably scarce. Green strategies adopted in accounting firms could spur environmental reporting,  improving stakeholder  engagement around sustainability and optimizing internal processes to be eco-friendlier. Hence, our  study  enriches  the  literature  by  examining  the  interplay  of AI-infused knowledge and green intellectual capital within accounting firms and how this can spur their performance. Third, our study also contributes to the body of knowledge by incorporating sustainability culture  as  a  moderator,  strengthening  the  association  between  green intellectual capital and accounting firm performance. Fourthly, the integrated model of RBV-KBV could provide a viable and comprehensive framework  for  AI-infused  green  intellectual  capital  and  firm  performance. This model could serve as a catalyst for further literature in the field.

## 6. Conclusions, recommendations and future directions

Although a growing body of literature has been drawn to the nexus between green intellectual capital (GIC) and firm performance, little is known about how AI technologies can enhance green intellectual capital and eventually firm performance. This study aims to explore the impact of  AI-infused  knowledge  on  GIC  within  accounting  firms,  and  thus potentially  fostering  their  performance.  Relying  on  a  quantitative research approach employed to collect data from accounting firms, the analysis results show that AI-infused knowledge had a significant impact on  the  internal  two  aspects  of  GIC,  green  human  capital  (GHC)  and structural capital (GSC), while it had no impact on green rational capital (GRC). Internal dimensions, i.e., GHC and GSC, had a significant impact on accounting performance across various aspects, including efficiency, accuracy,  compliance  with  environmental  standards,  innovation  in reporting, and timeliness. Sustainability culture was found to significantly moderate the association between GIC dimensions and accounting firm performance.

Drawn from our results, the study suggests some actionable recommendations to stakeholders: With the integration of AI-infused knowledge, accounting firms are recommended to pay attention to investment  in  extensive  training  programs  focusing  on  sustainability practices  and  green  accounting  principles.  Integrating  AI-enhanced knowledge  frameworks  can  substantially  foster  GHC  by  giving  staff and professionals access to cutting-edge resources and real-time data that can infuse their understanding of sustainability practices. With AIpowered insights adoption, firms can efficiently upskill their employees in  green accounting concepts, enabling them to integrate sustainable initiatives into their business model. Thus, this can lead to innovative sustainability-related accounting activities, enhancing the firm ' s overall performance. Additionally, efforts must be geared toward the vital role of AI-infused knowledge in fostering GSC by automating sustainability processes, enhancing reporting accuracy, and embedding sustainability metrics  into  accounting  systems.  AI  solutions,  for  example,  have  the potential  to  make  environmental  data  collection  and  analysis  more efficient  and  accurate,  allowing  accounting  firms  to  embrace  more effective and precise sustainability reporting procedures. By integrating AI-fueled  knowledge  into  green  structures  and  processes  within  accounting firms, robust sustainability structures and policies can be built, and green initiatives can be consistently integrated. Effective analysis of vast datasets using AI technologies can facilitate long-term planning and strategic decision-making in line with sustainability goals. Another area that warrants further consideration is green rational capital and how accounting firms can employ AI-infused knowledge systems to improve their stakeholder engagement. This could be done by transparent, datadriven communication about sustainability. AI solutions can assist these companies  advance  trust  and  collaborate  with  stakeholders  more effectively. It is suggested that companies that have a robust sustainability  culture  backed  by  AI-driven  insights  are  better  able  to  meet stakeholder  needs  and  match  their  green  initiatives  with  market  expectations.  By  leveraging  AI  capabilities  in  amplifying  sustainability activities, firms have the potential to demonstrate strong sustainability practices and bolster their rational capital. This, in turn, can lead to improved  compliance,  efficiency,  and  innovation  in  accounting  firm performance.

However,  despite  the  promising  implications  of  the  study,  some limitations are to be underlined. The study employed a cross-sectional survey,  capturing  the  nexuses  between  AI  and  GIC,  and  accounting firm performance at a single point in time. Given the limitation of this approach  in  assessing  long-term  causal  effects,  longitudinal  future research  could  provide  deeper  insights  into  the  evolution  of  such  a model. Moreover, as accounting firms operate in specific regulatory and market environments, this is likely to influence AI adoption and sustainability practices. Accordingly, some  crucial extrinsic factors, including market competition, regulatory pressures, and client expectations, which may have an impact on the development of green intellectual capital and performance were not incorporated into the model. Finally,  our  study  relied  on  the  service-oriented  sector  (accounting firms), as this may restrict the generalization of the outcomes to other sectors.  It  is  suggested  to  expand  the  sample  in  future  research  by including diverse industries.
