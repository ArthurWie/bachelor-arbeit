## Abstract

Purpose -This study aims to analyse the factors influencing AI adoption in the hospitality sector, utilising the technology acceptance model (TAM) and the technology-organisational-environmental (TOE) framework to understand how Industry 4.0 pressures drive the integration of emerging technologies, such as artificial intelligence (AI).

Design/methodology/approach -Data were collected via structured questionnaires from 312 valid responses from luxury hotels offering lodging and food services, and the hypotheses were tested using structural equation modelling.

Findings -This study demonstrates that perceived ease of use, competitive pressure, robust IT infrastructure and supportive government policies significantly drive AI adoption, thereby enhancing service performance. These factors also supplement each other: technological innovations enable new functionality, organisational preparation prepares for implementation and exogenous factors accelerate adoption processes, all together building significant momentum for the digital transformation of the hospitality industry.

Practical implications -The results give a hotel management, policy, and tourism stakeholder AI technology deployment strategic framework for boosting service performance, operation efficiency improvement and competitive advantage entrenchment during an industry being accelerated digitalised at a greater speed.

Originality/value -This study advances the literature by offering a novel, integrative perspective on AI adoption in the hospitality sector through the combined application of the TAM and the TOE framework. Positioned within a developing economy, it adopts a rigorous empirical lens to foreground organisational decision-making, thereby moving beyond the dominant customer-centric discourse. Further, by empirically linking AI adoption with service performance outcomes, the study bridges the critical gap between adoption intent and operational realisation, with particular relevance to the underexplored context of luxury hotels in India.

## 2. Literature review and hypothesis development

## 2.1 AI adoption and SP

Information technology is causing a tremendous revolution in the hospitality industry as it reinvents the concept of design, service delivery and service evaluation. In such a business environment, as competition and uncertainty continue to grow, technological changes are more than crucial to tourist attractions, improved customer satisfaction and long-term growth (IBEF, 2021; Pizam et al. , 2024). The trend to become digital allows hospitality businesses to simplify operations, minimise expenses, optimise the use of resources, and personalise services, which creates an overall impact on the improved performance of the services (Mishra et al. , 2022; Olan et al. , 2021). Within this wider technological transformation, AI has become a critical factor in service innovation, operational efficiency and client-focused service design.

The AI technologies, such as chatbots, virtual assistants, service robots, voice-controlled devices, and predictive analytics, are being increasingly implemented in the work of the hospitality industry (Ozekici et al. , 2025; Bhuiyan et al ., 2024). These innovations enhance the quality of services, as they contribute to real-time responsiveness, providing guests with individually tailored recommendations and ensuring smooth interactions between the guest and the hotel. Additionally, they improve the performance of operations by automating operations, improving the accuracy of demand prediction, and managing staffing and resource allocation (Naz et al. , 2024). As a result, the operating costs of organisations that implement AI will decrease by a significant margin of up to 15%, besides noteworthy increases in revenue and efficiency of services delivered (Pinheiro et al. , 2021). According to industry projections, by 2030, robotisation and AI have a possibility to replace close to a quarter of the hospitality workforce and, at the same time, increase service productivity and customer experience (Law et al ., 2022). The COVID-19 crisis increased the interest in AI, as hospitality companies began searching for contactless technologies to boost their safety, customer trust, and profitability (G € ossling et al. , 2020; Shin and Kang, 2020). The concept of AI-based automation targets the labour-related issues, as well as providing the continuity of the services in case of crisis, which consolidates the strategic value of the concept. Also, the major brand hotel chains, including Hilton, Marriott, and Uber, have already implemented AI to predict the preferences of consumers, streamline the process of booking, and provide very personal services (Alsetoohy et al ., 2019).

In spite of these developments, the empirical evidence on the connection between AI adoption and performance of the service is limited, especially in developing economies such as India (Bhushan, 2021; Goel et al. , 2022). A large portion of the available literature is focused on consumer acceptance, the policy implications, or theoretical frameworks (Chatterjee, 2020; Lin et al ., 2020; Talwar et al ., 2022), and the gap in knowledge about the transformation of managerial AI adoption decisions into improved SP remains significant. ## 2.2 Theoretical underpinning

This study uses the TAM and TOE framework. This study uses TAM as one of its models because understanding technology acceptance enables more accurate forecasting of new information resources. This study uses the TOE framework as one of its models because it explains the latest technological innovation adoption in organisations and how the TOE influences its adoption and implementation.

Davis (1989) developed the TAM to understand the intention to adopt any technology. In previous studies, TAM has been used to investigate individuals' acceptance towards various technologies. The TOE framework was developed to investigate the factors impacting behavioural intentions towards technology adoption. Presently, this study uses both the framework (TAM and TOE) to evaluate the acceptance of AI in hotels. It is compulsory to provide a valid explanation for the technology adoption where these two frameworks will best suit. TOE frameworks explain technology adoption using three constructs: technology, organisational, and environmental (Abdurrahman et al. , 2024). On the other hand, TAM uses two main variables: PEOU and PU (Burgess et al. , 2023). TAM-TOE integration is best for investigating the socio-environmental and technological factors that contribute to AI acceptance.

(Alsetoohy et al. , 2019) explored the attitude of managers in adopting intelligent agent technology in hotel food SC using the TOE framework. The hospitality sector is primarily focused on delivering exceptional customer service, and AI can fundamentally transform the guest experience. AI has the potential to enhance customer satisfaction, increase efficiency, and drive revenue through various means, such as personalised recommendations, dynamic pricing that responds to customer needs, and chatbots that assist guests with their requirements (Cai et al. , 2022). Hotels can automate and optimise these operations using AI-powered solutions, increasing efficiency and effectiveness.

From a TAM perspective, PEOU and PU reflect managers' perceptions of AI technologies, which inform their adoption intentions. From a TOE perspective, GS and CP capture external environmental drivers, while TMS and ITI reflect internal organisational capability and preparedness. The six antecedents hypothesised here are expected to positively affect IAAI, which, in turn, should improve SP, as measured by service quality, responsiveness, operational efficiency, and customer satisfaction. ## 3. Research methodology

This section presents the research methodology, including the research design, instruments, sampling techniques, data collection procedures, and the study sample size, as it examines AI adoption within India's luxury hospitality sector.

## 3.1 Survey instrument development

This study analyses the managers' intentions regarding the use of AI in luxury hotels. A luxury hotel offers luxurious accommodations for its guests. There are currently no standards for luxury hotels (e.g. star ratings). Hotels with four or five stars are considered 'luxury.' Additionally, this suggests that any accommodations can be considered luxurious (Alsetoohy et al. , 2019). Most of the current questionnaire's items were adapted from previously established scales. The constructs PEOU and PU were adopted (Davis, 1989). The constructs TMS, ITI, GS and CP were adopted (DePietro et al ., 1990). Academic and corporate experts helped to design the questionnaire (refer Table A1 in Appendix). After preparing the questionnaire, it was sent to six academics and five industry experts in the hospitality sector for validation.

## 3.2 Research design, sampling and data collection

The present study focuses on managers working in luxury hotels across India that offer both lodging and dining facilities. This research uses an explanatory design since it intends to verify the hypotheses that are derived from theory and identify the causal relationships between the technological, organisational, and environmental factors influencing AI adoption through SEM. India may be one of the most topical locations to study how AI is adopted in the hospitality industry since the country is technologically developing rapidly, and the elements of digital transformation are getting more attention in the tourism and service sectors (Ministry of Tourism, 2023). The key aim of the research is to define and discuss the factors that affect the willingness of hotel managers to implement AI technologies in the operation of a luxury hotel. The research design that was used was quantitative, cross-sectional, to examine the interrelationships between the latent constructs developed in this study in a systematic manner, as suggested by Kumar et al . (2024).

In line with previous studies (e.g. Chahal and Devi, 2024; Douglas et al. , 2026), the unit of analysis was hotel managers who were directly involved in the strategic planning and the adoption of technologies. These interviewees were specifically chosen on the basis of their experience in operations, management supervision, and the level of understanding of AI-based applications in hospitality management. Only the respondents that proved to have enough knowledge on AI technologies and the ways of their use in business were included in the final sample to guarantee the relevance of the data and its accuracy.

Purposive sampling is the appropriate choice in the hotel industry as suggested in the recent research on the online hotels booking intentions of travellers by Biswas (2023). ## 5. Implications of the study

## 5.1 Theoretical/academic implications

This study makes several meaningful theoretical contributions to the existing literature on technology adoption, service innovation, and hospitality management by extending, refining, and applying established theories in relevant contexts. First, it refines the application of the TAM (Davis, 1989) and the TOE framework (DePietro et al. , 1990) by showing the merits of a combined approach. While TAM has conventionally focused on individual-level belief factors, such as PEOU or PU, and TOE stresses organisational and environmental factors, this study combines both theories to provide a more comprehensive view of organisational decision support adoption within organisational decision support processes. This dual-theory approach allows for a deeper examination of the interplay between cognitive perceptions and institutional pressure, managerial support, and technological capabilities, which, according to earlier research, have often been examined independently (Chatterjee et al. , 2021; Nam et al. , 2021).

Second, the paper contributes to the contextual extension of adoption theories by investigating AI adoption in an emerging economy setting. Although the majority of earlier studies have drawn on developed markets (Belanche et al. , 2021; Tung and Au, 2018), our results demonstrate that environmental factors, for instance, governmental support and competition pressure, have a more decisive impact on managerial adoption decisions based on developing markets, for which institutional maturity and information infrastructure are continuously evolving. This contributes to the widening of the external validity of TAM-TOEbased models, allowing for theoretical refinement of adoption behaviour based on the shaping impact of setting contexts. Third, this study broadens the end-focused dimension of the literature on technology adoption by relating the intention to adopt AI to SP. Most prior work has confined its scope primarily to measuring adoption intentions, without examining subsequent effects (Seo and Lee, 2021; Pillai and Sivathanu, 2020a, b). This study demonstrates that adopting AI significantly enhances SP, reflected in service quality advancements, personalisation, responsiveness, and efficiency, thereby bridging the gap between adoption decisions and organisational outcomes. Managers need to ensure that hotels are ready for AI adoption by developing effective strategies and planning. Southeast Asian eco-lodge parallels have used AI-based resource-management and predictive maintenance platforms to reduce water and energy use and at the same time enhance guest experience, thus pointing to the reality of technological adoption as sustainable tourism and climate change. These practical findings indicate that hotel administrators and policy-makers need to understand the introduction of AI as a vital component of an overall sustainability transition plan and not a mere technological addition. ## 5.3 Practical implications

The results of this research make a number of substantive pragmatic contributions on practitioners in the hospitality and tourism industry, policy makers, and industry stakeholders in the value chain. To begin with, they argue that the adoption of AI is not an act of technical supplementation but a strategic pathway to achieving service predominance, strengthening the business, and securing competitive differentiation. When hospitality enterprises plan to implement AI at every stage of their value chains, they will have an opportunity to afford hyper-personalised guest experiences, increase service quality and responsiveness, and improve the work efficiency. These advantages could be achieved by implementing AIpowered chatbots to provide 24/7 communication with guests, using predictive analytics to predict demand, and implementing robotic automation in order to optimise housekeeping services and front-office operations.

Second, the paper highlights the importance of capacity building and redesigning of the workforce. The implementation of the AI requires the retraining of staff, the development of online skills, and the adoption of the culture of decision-making based on data to stimulate innovative working practices. Training and partnerships with information-technology suppliers can provide the existing gaps in digital literacy and enhance the ability of the workforce to process, analyse, and operationalise insight based on AI systems with effectiveness.

Third, the study provides practical recommendations to policy making and planning of the industry. Governments are being requested to implement adequate regulatory mechanisms, invest and provide funding opportunities and infrastructural development ventures that will encourage AI utilisation, especially among small and medium-sized hospitality businesses. Also, the combination of AI and green projects, including energy efficiency and waste reduction, and supply-chain transparency has potential to support the development of environmental transformation goals and meanwhile improve the efficiency of the economy. Lastly, the research outlines how innovation ecosystems between technology companies, academic institutions, and tourism agencies become key to the scale-up of AI solutions in the hospitality industry. These platforms have the potential to hasten the spread of innovation and capitalise on its social, economic, and environmental gains and use them as the foundation of overall developmental returns.

## 5.5 Limitations of the study

Although the research has been helpful, it still has some limitations. For example, the research is only about the managers of luxury hotels in India, so the results might not be the same for non-luxury hotels or other places. Also, the study didn't take into account the size of the organisation, how digitally advanced it is, or the manager's experience, and this could affect the results.

Another limitation of this study is that it does not have control variables in the conceptual framework. These include organisational size, digital maturity level, technological experience of managers, and type of hotel (4-star vs 5-star), which may affect the decisions of AI adoption. Omitting these controls may introduce omitted variable bias in the model estimates. Subsequent studies should incorporate appropriate control variables not only to improve the reliability of AI adoption models but also to enhance their explanatory capacity in the hospitality sector. The survey was done at one point in time, so it doesn't show how the use of AI has changed over time.

## 6. Conclusions and future research

The purpose of the proposed study was to uncover the factors that determine the use of AI in the hospitality industry and to evaluate its effect on the service performance by combining the TAM and TOE frameworks. The qualitative, hypothesis-oriented methodology was used whereby data were gathered in relation to 312 managers in luxury hotels in major Indian cities and examined through SEM. The research gives an in-depth insight into the ways technological, organisational, and environmental factors interact to predict managers' and their intention to use AI and performance.

The results show that PEOU, CP, GS, and ITI have a major effect on the intention to use AI, and perceived usefulness and top management support did not prove to be of great importance in this situation. Moreover, the use of AI shows a significant benefit of a positive impact on the service performance, increasing efficiency, responsiveness, and customer satisfaction. These findings emphasise the increasing strategic value of AI-based technologies in enhancing operational efficiency and providing a high-quality service experience in the hospitality sector.

The research will add value to the theory by incorporating TAM and TOE as one framework, which provides a more comprehensive picture of the organisational technology adoption in the context of an emerging economy. It is also built upon the literature, as it connects AI adoption intention to real service outcome performance. In practical terms, the results can inform the hotel managers and policymakers to focus on easy-to-use AI systems, enhance digital infrastructure, and use external pressures and policy facilitation to hasten its adoption. In general, the paper highlights AI as a key facilitator of service quality and competitiveness in changing hospitality industry.

The researchers may broaden the spectrum of the hospitality businesses to include boutique hotels, budget hotels, and restaurants for the generalisability of their findings. They may as well include some relevant control variables to depict the characteristics of an organisation and lessen the possible bias of the model. Moreover, customer acceptance of AI technology in services is an alternative angle to adoption and SP which future studies can consider. Lastly, the exploration of the combination of AI with other emerging technologies such as blockchain, big data analytics, and robotics would reveal more about the digital transformation process in the hospitality sector. Researchers in the future can use a longitudinal design to see changes in adoption behaviour and SP outcomes over time.
