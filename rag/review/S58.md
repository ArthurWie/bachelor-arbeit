## 1. Introduction

Today, AI plays a leading role in industrial transformation,  technological  innovation,  and  economic  modernization, profoundly impacting the economy, society, and everyday life. By the end of 2024, China had more than 4500 AI companies, with the core AI industry valued at nearly 600 billion RMB. Second, artificial intelligence (AI) enhances investment efficiency by improving a firm ' s financial performance. Leveraging big data analytics and intelligent algorithms, AI enables real-time monitoring and forecasting of financial conditions, helping managers overcome the limitations of traditional financial analysis and make more precise investment decisions. AI offers three key advantages in financial optimization: it can comprehensively  integrate  multidimensional  financial  data,  conduct dynamic risk assessments with early warnings, and autonomously adjust financial strategies in response to market fluctuations. As a result, the use of AI in financial management enhances a firm ' s ability to process complex financial information, increases the precision of capital allocation,  and  boosts  investment  returns.  Moreover,  as  AI  technologies continue to evolve and become more deeply embedded in business operations, collaboration between AI systems and financial professionals is expected to yield new models of financial analysis. These innovative approaches will allow firms to assess investment projects more scientifically and mitigate financial risks, leading to a systematic improvement in investment efficiency.

The data sources for the main variables used in this study are as follows: The corporate investment efficiency is calculated using data on listed companies from the CSMAR database. Following the investment model proposed by Richardson (2006), we estimate the residuals from the model and take the negative absolute value of these residuals to measure  investment  efficiency -where  smaller  deviations  from  expected  investment  indicate  higher  efficiency.  The  corporate  artificial intelligence (AI) adoption is measured through text analysis of company annual reports. First, we collect firms ' annual reports from 2008 to 2023 from CNINFO (China Securities Market Financial Disclosure Network) by python web-scraping tools. Drawing on methodologies from Chen and  Srinivasan  (2020) and  Li,  Mai,  Shen,  and  Yan  (2021),  we  constructed an AI-related keyword dictionary based on resources from the World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO). This dictionary includes 73 terms such as ' artificial intelligence, ' ' AI products, ' and ' AI chips. ' Using Python ' s open-source word segmentation package Jieba, we performed text matching on the collected annual report data. The frequency of AI-related keywords appearing in each report was then counted, with the final measure of AI usage calculated as the natural logarithm of one plus the keyword frequency. After excluding ST and ST* firms, as well as observations with missing or incomplete data, the final dataset includes 24,768 firm-year observations.

However, the empirical results of this study may be subject to potential endogeneity issues. It is also important to consider the potential for reverse causality, as enterprises demonstrating greater investment efficiency might exhibit a higher propensity to implement AI technologies, thereby complicating the causal inference between AI adoption and investment performance. Although we have controlled for year and industry fixed effects and included a rich set of control variables, the results  may  still  be  influenced  by  omitted  observable  or  unobservable factors, thus raising endogeneity concerns. To address these potential issues, we employ two strategies: First, we use the issuance of the ' Smart Manufacturing Development Plan (2016 -2020) ' as an exogenous policy shock and conduct DID regressions to identify the impact of AI adoption on  investment  efficiency.  Second,  we  construct  a  Bartik-style  instrumental  variable  based  on  the  shift-share  method  to  further  address endogeneity concerns and perform two-stage regressions. The results from  both  methods  support  the  validity  of  the  baseline  model,  confirming  the  robustness  of  our  main  finding:  artificial  intelligence significantly enhances corporate investment efficiency. The results of the heterogeneity analysis indicate that the enhancement in investment efficiency associated with artificial intelligence is particularly evident among firms characterized by higher knowledge intensity, greater R & D commitment, and more robust internal control systems. Furthermore, the mechanism analysis shows that AI enhances investment efficiency primarily by boosting firms ' innovation capabilities and improving their financial conditions.

This  study  makes  several  contributions  to  the  existing  literature. First,  it  enriches  research  on  the  impact  of  artificial  intelligence  on corporate  economic  behavior.  While  previous  studies  have  mainly examined  AI ' s  influence  on  financial  decision-making,  operations management, risk control, and financial performance (Lee, Yang, & Kim, 2019; Li, Xu, Zheng, et al., 2023; Mishra, Chen, et al., 2022; Bhatia, 2019; Liu, Dong, Mei, & Shen, 2023), this paper extends the discussion by focusing specifically on how AI enhances corporate investment efficiency. In doing so, it contributes to the broader fields of digital finance and  financial  technology  (FinTech).  Furthermore,  by  constructing  a tailored  AI-related  keyword  dictionary  and  employing  a  novel  text analysis approach, this study not only introduces an innovative method for measuring corporate AI adoption but also expands the theoretical understanding of AI ' s role in firm-level economic activity.

Second,  while  prior  studies  on  the  determinants  of  corporate  investment efficiency have primarily focused on factors such as internal control deficiencies, information asymmetry, financing constraints, and agency problems (Denis & Sibilkov, 2010; Liu & Yu, 2023; McLean et al., 2012; Stein, 2003; Zheng & Xiao, 2020), this study shifts the focus to artificial  intelligence -a  transformative  force  at  the  forefront  of  the current technological and industrial revolution. By empirically demonstrating the role of AI in improving investment efficiency, this paper offers robust evidence of AI ' s productivity-enhancing potential at the firm level. In doing so, it contributes to the literature on corporate investment efficiency by introducing a novel, technology-oriented perspective.

## 2. Literature review and research hypotheses

## 2.1. Literature on AI ' s impact on corporate financial behavior and performance

As the core of the contemporary technological revolution, artificial intelligence (AI) is profoundly influencing corporate financial behavior and  performance.  AI,  a  branch  of  computer  science  that  simulates human intelligence,  refers  to  technologies  and  methods  that  employ computer  programs,  machines,  and  other  means -such  as  machine learning, deep learning, and natural language processing -to emulate, extend, and expand human intelligence. AI can rapidly integrate and process vast amounts of data, providing technical support for the practice and innovation of corporate financial management. In recent years, a  substantial  body  of  literature  has  emerged  on  AI ' s  applications  in corporate financial behavior and performance, with research primarily focusing  on  financial  decision-making,  operational  management,  risk management, and financial performance.

In  terms  of  corporate  financial  decision-making,  AI  alleviates financing constraints faced by firms and enhances the rationality of asset pricing, thereby improving the efficiency of investment decisions. The development of the internet has enabled corporate activities such as production,  sales,  and  payments  to  leave ' digital  footprints ' online. Confronted with this complex data, banks can leverage AI to quickly identify and process credit-related information, accurately constructing a ' digital profile ' of firms. This improves the accuracy and timeliness of information processing (Gambacorta, Huang, Qiu, & Wang, 2019; Liberti & Petersen, 2019), thereby mitigating financing constraints caused by  insufficient  credit  information  for  small  and  medium-sized  enterprises  (SMEs).  Lee  et  al.  (2019) also  find  that  fintech  technologies, represented  by  AI,  significantly  reduce  financing  gaps  and  optimize credit allocation. Additionally, AI can improve firms '  fi nancing environments (Goldstein et al., 2019), help identify and evaluate investment opportunities,  and  enhance  capital  allocation  efficiency  (Vial,  2019; Acemoglu and Restrepo, 2019).

[S. 3]

In the context of operational management, Li et al. (2023) find that AI significantly enhances resource allocation efficiency in their study of how resource reallocation impacts innovation efficiency among Chinese listed companies. Similarly, Mishra et al. (2022), analyzing reports from U.S. public firms, demonstrate that AI improves operational efficiency and return on investment while lowering marketing costs. Bharadwaj, Sawy, Pavlou, and Venkatraman (2013) argue that digital technological innovations -exemplified by AI -empower organizational management and help reduce internal control costs.

In risk management, numerous scholars have employed AI to predict corporate  risks.  For  example,  risk  perception  models  and  dynamic financial risk early-warning models have been constructed using natural language materials and technologies, integrating convolutional neural networks and long short-term memory networks (Bhatia, 2019). Ghodselahi  and  Amirmadhi  (2011) design  a  hybrid  credit  scoring  model applying  ensemble learning  to  credit  decisions,  validating  AI ' s  feasibility in credit risk management. Hasan, Taylor, and Richardson (2022) use gradient boosting tree algorithms to improve the accuracy of predicting future risks while studying the impact of brand capital on stock price crash risk in listed companies.

Regarding financial performance, digital technological innovations represented by AI can reduce information asymmetry among corporate divisions, thereby improving financial performance (Liu et al., 2023). Vrontis, Christofi, Pereira, et al. (2022) explore the impact of digital intelligence  on  human  resource  management  and  corporate  performance,  noting  that  AI  and  other  intelligent  technologies  enhance resource  management efficiency  and reduce costs,  leading  to  overall financial performance growth. Since humans and AI each have unique advantages, corporate management can coordinate their complementary  strengths  to  improve  performance  (Davenport & Kirby,  2016). Additionally,  many scholars have studied AI ' s  impact  on  total  factor productivity,  labor  demand,  new  quality  productivity,  and  corporate innovation. For instance, Babina, Fedyk, He, and Hodson (2024) propose a metric using employee resumes to measure AI workforce size at the company level, serving as a new indicator of AI investment. Using this metric, they found that AI promotes product innovation and drives corporate growth.

## 2.2. Literature on factors influencing corporate investment efficiency

Corporate investment efficiency refers to the gap between actual and ideal investment levels. A smaller gap indicates higher efficiency, while a larger gap reflects inefficiency, with the difference representing nonefficient  investment.  Existing  research  on  factors  influencing  investment  efficiency  primarily  focuses  on  internal  controls,  information asymmetry, financing constraints, principal-agent problems, ownership structure, and external environmental and policy issues (Zheng et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024).

In terms of internal controls, Liu and Yu (2023) studied A-share listed companies in China, finding that internal control quality significantly reduces non-efficient investment. Deumes and Knechel (2008) argued that improving internal control quality enhances external confidence in accounting  information,  reducing  financing  costs  and  non-efficient investment.

Regarding  information  asymmetry,  Stein  (2003) systematically explained  its  impact  on  investment  decisions,  noting  that  it  disrupts capital  allocation  efficiency.  According  to  Biddle  and  Hilary  (2006), enhanced  accounting  quality  plays  a  role  in  mitigating  information asymmetry,  thereby  facilitating  more  efficient  investment  decisions. Similarly,  Chen,  Hope,  Li,  and  Wang  (2011) provide  evidence  of  a positive association between the quality of financial reporting and firms ' investment  efficiency.  McNichols  and  Stubben  (2008) showed  that earnings management distorts accounting information, leading to inefficient  investment  decisions.  Gomariz  and  Ballesta  (2014) studied Spanish  listed  companies  (1998 -2008),  concluding  that financial reporting  quality  alleviates  overinvestment.  Other  studies  found  that digital finance, ESG, and key disclosure items also reduce information asymmetry, enhancing investment efficiency.

On  financing  constraints,  McLean  et  al.  (2012) found  that  firms facing  higher  constraints  exhibit  greater  sensitivity  of  investment  to internal cash flows but weaker responsiveness to investment opportunities. Market imperfections exacerbate financing constraints, leading to inefficient  investment  (Campello,  Graham, & Harvey,  2010).  Local government debt expansion crowds out access to quality bank loans, causing firms to miss investment opportunities (Cao, Duan, & Ibrahim, 2023; Huang, Pagano, & Panizza, 2020; Ma & Hou, 2023). Regarding principal-agent problems, Zheng and Xiao (2020) used agency theory to show  that  managerial  corruption  suppresses  investment.  Denis  and Sibilkov  (2010) argued  that  financing  constraints  can  reduce  agency costs and curb overinvestment. Due to misaligned interests, agents may overinvest  to  build ' empires, ' seeking  greater  control  benefits  and professional  reputation  (Richardson,  2006).  In  ownership  structure, Chen,  Ghoul,  Guedhami,  and  Wang  (2017) examined  how  state  and foreign  ownership  affect  investment  efficiency  in  privatized  firms globally. On external factors, Li, Li, and Wu (2019) found that exchange rate uncertainty stimulates investment in labor-intensive and low irreversibility firms using Chinese industrial data. Chen, Sun, Tang, and Wu (2011) showed  that  government  intervention  exacerbates  inefficient investment in Chinese listed firms. Dai, Hou, and Li (2021) argued that policy incentives may induce irrational overinvestment.

Our paper investigates the influence of AI on corporate investment efficiency. Specifically, it constructs an indicator of corporate AI adoption using the word frequency method, measures corporate investment efficiency levels based on Richardson ' s (2006) actual investment level model, and conducts empirical analysis. Secondly, the ' Smart Manufacturing  Development  Plan  (2016 -2020) ' policy  enactment  is used as an exogenous event shock in the DID method, along with a Bartik instrumental variable constructed via the shift-share method for endogeneity testing. Furthermore, robustness tests are performed by replacing the explained and explanatory variables. Additionally, this paper conducts heterogeneity analyses based on corporate location, firm type, and  firm  size,  distinguishing  between  over-investment  and  underinvestment  scenarios  for  categorical  testing  of  corporate  investment efficiency. Finally, the paper explores transmission mechanisms through corporate innovation levels and financial conditions, followed by economic consequence testing. Current research on AI primarily focuses on the macro level, and due to the lack of effective firm-level measurement indicators for AI technology, how AI affects the investment efficiency of micro-level firms remains inconclusive. This paper builds upon existing research by constructing a new indicator for measuring corporate AI adoption, thereby expanding the literature on AI ' s effect on firm economic behavior and enriching research on the determinants of firm investment efficiency.

## 2.3. Hypotheses development

Artificial intelligence refers to a class of technologies and methods that simulate, extend, and expand human intelligence. Artificial intelligence  can  influence  corporate  investment  efficiency  by  improving firms ' fi nancing environment (Goldstein et al., 2019), enhancing innovation capabilities (Babina et al., 2024), and optimizing financial conditions (Liu et al., 2023), among other pathways.

[S. 4]

First,  regarding  corporate  innovation  capability.  Issues  such  as lengthy R & D cycles, high trial-and-error costs, and uncertain technological pathways often constrain the efficient allocation of innovation resources, leading to low investment efficiency. Artificial intelligence can  optimize  R & D direction  through  big  data  analysis,  predict  technology trends, and reduce ineffective investments. Meanwhile, machine learning  algorithms  can  accelerate  experimental  simulations,  shorten innovation  cycles,  and  lower  trial-and-error  costs.  Additionally,  AIdriven intelligent decision-making systems can optimize R & D resource allocation, thereby improve the conversion rate of innovation outcomes and enhancing corporate investment efficiency.

Second, in terms of corporate financial management. AI with big data  analytics  and  machine  learning  technologies  at  its  core,  breaks through  the  temporal  and  spatial  limitations  of  traditional  financial management, thereby improving firms ' data collaboration and intelligent decision-making capabilities. After obtaining and analyzing operational data, industry trends, and financial market information in realtime through AI systems, firms can more accurately assess and forecast  their  financial  conditions,  leading  to  more  scientific  investment decisions  and  higher  resource  allocation  efficiency.  In  summary,  our paper proposes the following hypothesis:

Hypothesis H1 . Artificial  intelligence  can  improve  firm  investment efficiency.

## 3. Research design

## 3.1. Data sources

This paper selects A-share listed companies from the Shanghai Stock Exchange and Shenzhen Stock Exchange from 2008 to 2023 as sample data. The following treatments are applied to the sample data: First, ST and  ST*  listed  companies  are  excluded;  second,  financial-type  listed companies are removed; third, observations with missing financial data are eliminated. The annual report data of enterprises used in this study are sourced from the CSMAR database and the China Securities Market Financial Disclosure Network (CNINFO); patent data are obtained from the International Intellectual Property Rights Database (IRPDB); basic corporate information and financial data come from the CSMAR database;  GDP  data  are  sourced  from  the  National  Bureau  of  Statistics; regional  marketization  level  data  are  extracted  from  the  China  Marketization  Index  Database.  Furthermore,  all  continuous  variables  are subjected  to  1  %  winsorization  to  mitigate  the  influence  of  outliers. Following this data preprocessing, the final dataset comprises 24,768 firm-year observations.

## 3.2.2. Explanatory variables

The  explanatory  variable  in  this  paper  is  the  status  of  corporate artificial intelligence (AI) usage (AI). Whether a company develops AI depends  on  management  decisions,  and  the  annual  reports  of  listed companies reflect the importance they place on AI. Generally, the higher the frequency of AI-related terms in a listed company ' s annual report, the  greater  the  emphasis  the  company  places  on  AI  development. Therefore, the AI-related vocabulary disclosed in annual reports can, to some extent, represent the status of corporate AI usage. Drawing on the AI dictionary construction methods of Chen and Srinivasan (2020) and Li et al. (2021), to quantify corporate engagement with AI, this study identifies a set of AI-related keywords -such as artificial intelligence, AI products,  AI  chips,  machine  translation,  machine  learning,  computer vision,  human-computer  interaction,  deep  learning,  neural  networks, biometrics,  and  image  recognition -and  matches  them  with  textual content from firms ' annual reports. The natural logarithm of one plus the frequency of these terms ( lnword ) serves as the primary proxy for AI adoption.  For  robustness  checks,  the  same  set  of  AI-related  terms  is cross-referenced with firms ' patent data to determine the number of relevant AI patents granted. The natural logarithm of one plus the patent count ( lnpatent ) is used as an alternative indicator of AI involvement.

Specifically, the construction process of the explanatory variables in this paper is as follows:

I. Data Collection.

Python web scraping technology is employed to collect annual reports of listed companies from the China Securities Market Financial Disclosure Network (CNINFO) for the years 2008 -2023, which are then converted into txt-format data. Similarly, the API interface provided by the International Intellectual Property Rights Database (IRPDB) is used to scrape the titles and abstracts of patents held by listed companies from 2008 to 2023, which are also converted into txt-format data.

II. Construction of the AI Dictionary.

The AI vocabulary selected in this paper is based on the research of Chen and Srinivasan (2020) and Li et al. (2021). The construction of the AI-related term dictionary follows a three-step procedure: (1) An initial list of 52 seed terms is compiled by drawing on sources such as the World Intellectual  Property  Organization ' s  AI  vocabulary  and  industry  analyses,  including  the  'STAR Market Series -Panoramic View of the AI Industry Chain ' by Ping An Securities. (2) Using the Skip-gram model, word embeddings are trained on corpora composed of annual reports and patent documents. For each seed term, the ten most semantically similar words are identified based on cosine similarity. (3) After eliminating  duplicates,  contextually  irrelevant  terms,  and  low-frequency entries,  a  refined  set  of  73  AI-related  terms  is  obtained  as  the  final dictionary.

[S. 5]

III. Generation of AI Variables.

The finalized AI dictionary is incorporated into the open-source Python  package  'jieba ' for  word  segmentation.  Subsequently,  the  frequency of AI-related terms within annual reports is calculated, and the natural logarithm of one plus this frequency serves as the primary metric for measuring corporate AI adoption. In parallel, AI-related patents are identified and used as an alternative measure to verify the robustness of the results.

## 3.2.3. Control variables

With reference to existing literature  on  the  influencing  factors  of corporate investment efficiency, this paper sets corporate-level control variables. Specifically, the control variables include: listing age, assetliability ratio, return on assets, Tobin ' s Q, firm size, the proportion of shares held by the largest shareholder, CEO duality, net cash flow from operating activities,  board size,  audit  opinion,  and  the  proportion  of independent directors. Additionally, this paper controls for fixed effects at the industry and year levels.

## 5. Heterogeneity analysis

## 5.1. Knowledge density heterogeneity

This study further explores the heterogeneity in the impact of AI on corporate investment efficiency by examining differences across firms with varying levels of knowledge intensity, R & D intensity, and internal control quality. To begin, companies are categorized into high and low knowledge  density  groups  based  on  whether  the  proportion  of  employees holding at least a bachelor ' s degree in a given year is above or below the median. A binary variable is created each year to indicate whether a firm is above or below this median. These annual binary indicators are then aggregated to generate a new variable, from which a new median is calculated. Based on this, firms are reclassified into high and low knowledge density groups for heterogeneity analysis. Table 10 shows these results. The results indicate that the impact of AI adoption on firm investment efficiency is more pronounced among high knowledge  density  firms.  This  is  likely  because,  compared  to  their  low knowledge  density  counterparts,  high  knowledge  density  firms  are better equipped to integrate AI tools efficiently and optimize workflows, thereby  enhancing  human-machine  collaboration.  As  a  result,  these firms  are  more  capable  of  mitigating  inefficiencies  in  investment decisions.

## 5.2. R & D density heterogeneity

Firms are classified into high and low R & D density groups based on the median proportion of R & D and technical personnel in a given year. A binary variable is created annually to indicate whether a firm is above or below this median. These annual binary indicators are then summed to generate a new variable, and firms are reclassified into high and low R & D density groups based on the median of this aggregated variable. As shown in Table 10, the impact of AI adoption on corporate investment efficiency is more significant among high R & D density firms. This is because firms with higher R & D density tend to have stronger capabilities  in  technology  development  and  application,  enabling  them  to effectively deploy, optimize, and deeply integrate AI systems into their core business processes. As a result, the marginal improvement in investment efficiency is greater for these firms compared to those with lower R & D density.

## 5.3. Internal control heterogeneity

Firms are classified into two groups -those with and without internal  control  deficiencies -for  heterogeneity  analysis.  The  findings  in Table 10 indicate that the impact of AI adoption on investment efficiency  is  more  significant  among  firms  without  internal  control  deficiencies. A sound internal control system provides a strong foundation for AI application by ensuring high-quality data, standardized processes, and an effective risk management framework, all of which contribute to improvements in investment efficiency. In contrast, firms with internal control deficiencies often suffer from poor data quality, disorganized processes, and weak oversight, which significantly hinder the effective implementation and performance of AI technologies.

## 8. Conclusion

The findings of this study reveal that artificial intelligence (AI) can significantly enhance corporate investment efficiency, and the results have passed endogeneity and robustness tests. The heterogeneity analysis reveals that the beneficial impact of AI on investment efficiency is stronger  among  firms  with  high  knowledge  intensity,  elevated  R & D investment, and robust internal control systems. Moreover, AI enhances corporate  investment  efficiency  by  boosting  innovation  capacity  and improving  financial  health.  Lastly,  tests  of  economic  consequences confirm that both increased investment efficiency and AI adoption play important roles in driving corporate value creation.

This study offers several actionable insights for policymakers seeking to enhance corporate investment efficiency through the adoption of AI. First, governments should provide clear strategic guidance and direct financial  support  to  facilitate  firms ' digital  and  intelligent  transformation. This could be achieved through the creation of a dedicated AI Transformation Fund or a voucher-based subsidy system to help offset the initial costs of AI adoption, particularly for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) and firms in traditional manufacturing sectors. By lowering financial barriers to entry, such initiatives directly address the resource constraints identified in our analysis. Second, policy support should be prioritized and targeted. Resources from the proposed fund should be directed toward firms with low knowledge intensity, limited R & D spending, or weaker internal control systems -characteristics that, as our findings show, limit the natural benefits of AI adoption. Importantly, financial assistance to these firms should be contingent on parallel efforts to upskill their workforce or strengthen internal governance structures, thus fostering the complementary capabilities necessary for AI to be effective. Third, to mitigate challenges related to knowledgeintensity  heterogeneity,  the  government  should  collaborate  with  industry and academic institutions to establish a national AI Skills Certification and Training System. This system would focus on reskilling and upskilling  the  existing  workforce,  cultivating  a  broader  talent  pool capable of working effectively alongside AI systems. Such efforts are crucial for enabling more firms to integrate AI tools successfully and optimize human -machine collaboration.

[S. 10]

This study also opens several avenues for future research. First, the measure  of  AI  adoption  could  be  refined  by  distinguishing  between different types of AI applications (e.g., machine learning vs. robotics) to examine their varying impacts on investment and other corporate outcomes. Second, future studies could investigate how AI influences labor composition within firms and how this reorganization, in turn, affects decision-making  efficiency.  Third,  as  more  data  becomes  available, research could explore the long-term and general equilibrium effects of widespread AI adoption on industry dynamics and market competition. Finally,  examining  the  relationship  between  AI  governance,  ethical guidelines, and corporate financial decisions presents another important area for future study.
