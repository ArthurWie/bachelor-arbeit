## 1. Introduction

Small and medium enterprises (SMEs) account for the majority of businesses  globally  (World  Bank,  2020)  and  they  have ' become  the centrepiece of strategies to achieve inclusive growth in the Middle East, North  Africa,  Afghanistan  and  Pakistan  (MENAP)  region ' (Lukonga, 2020,  p.5).  SMEs  have  been  increasingly  extending  their  activities within business markets in order to create more opportunities for growth and expansion (Mogaji, Soetan, & Kieu, 2020). B2B SME success largely depends on the extent to which there is an effective level of coordination and contact between SMEs and their business customers (Osterwalder & Pigneur, 2010; Rauch et al., 2018). ## A B S T R A C T

Development of small and medium enterprises (SMEs) is a key approach to achieving economic growth in the Middle East and successful adoption of technology is vital for SMEs' success and continuity. Artificial intelligence (AI) is part of a new generation of technologies that can facilitate competitive advantage but currently there is a lack of evidence regarding AI applications in relation to B2B SMEs in Middle East countries. Therefore, this study empirically examines antecedents to, and consequences of, successful acceptance of AI practices by B2B SMEs in Saudi Arabia. A conceptual model based on the technology-organisation-environment framework is developed which considers the impact of AI enablers and AI readiness on the acceptance of AI practices, and the impact of this on relational governance, performance, and SMEs' AI-based business customer interaction. The conceptual model was tested using structural equation modelling of survey data collected from B2B SMEs ( n = 392). The results showed that, of the AI enablers, acceptance of AI practices was significantly influenced by both technology roadmapping and attitude but not professional expertise. Of the AI readiness variables, acceptance of AI practices was significantly influenced by infrastructure and awareness but not technicality. The acceptance of AI practices was found to significantly affect AI-enabled relational governance and performance, and SME's business customer AI-based interaction. This study provides a broader base for theoretical and practical understanding of issues related to AI practices in SMEs and the B2B sector in general.

B2B interactions seem to be more complex and intertwined than in the consumer market (Agnihotri, Dingus, Hu, & Krush, 2016), thus creating a need for sophisticated solutions.

Adoption of emerging technologies has contributed considerably to SMEs'  ability  to  effectively  overcome  challenges,  collaborate  and interact with their business customers, and improve firm performance (Agnihotri et al., 2016; Brink, 2017; Dwivedi & Mustafee, 2010; Ghobakhloo & Ching, 2019; Sila & Dobni, 2012; Tan, Chong, Lin, & Eze, 2010). Artificial intelligence (AI) is a system's ability to interpret and learn from data (Akter et al., 2021; Alter, 2021; Haenlein & Kaplan, 2019),  mimicking  human  intelligence  (Rahman,  Hossain, & Fattah, 2021), and it is part of a new generation of technologies that introduce

AI  applications,  such  as  process  automation and optimisation, analytics, dynamic pricing, and prediction, can be used in diverse ways across business functions to enable organisations to obtain a  variety  of  benefits  in  terms  of  greater  revenue,  efficiency,  agility, productivity,  and  reliability,  as  well  as  better  decision  making  and customer experience (Alsheibani, Cheung, & Messom, 2020; Dwivedi, Hughes, et al., 2021; McKinsey, 2020; Min, 2010; Rauch, Dallasega, & Unterhofer, 2019; Syam & Sharma, 2018). In a survey of executives, Ransbotham,  Khodabandeh,  Fehling,  LaFountain,  and  Kiron  (2019) found that 90% of respondents agree that AI presents an opportunity for their organisation and Basri (2020) has also argued that adoption of AI by SMEs can lead to increased market share and higher revenues.

(2018), there are multiple issues associated with the acceptability and transparency of AI. There may also be,  as  Kozinets & Gretzel  (2021,  p.159)  describe, ' interrelated  challenges of incomprehensibility, disconnection, and vulnerability ' which may be difficult to navigate for B2B SMEs. A key constraint for SMEs is their lack of financial resources, which constitute the main difference between large- and small-scale organisations; large organisations will likely have greater financial resources and are thus able to invest capital to adopt technological advancements and take on greater challenges and risks  (Ghobakhloo & Ching,  2019;  Masood & Sonntag,  2020;  Rauch et al., 2019; Vrchota, Volek, & Novotn ´ a, 2019). Moreover, it has been reported that limited access to finance is an even greater constraint for SME development in the Arab region (Stepanyan, Abajyan, Alnasaa, & Ndoye, 2019).

Given the importance placed on SMEs for economic growth in the Middle East, it is imperative to identify drivers and outcomes of adoption  of  new  technological  advancements  so  that  these  organisations succeed and remain competitive. However, there is currently a gap in the literature exploring this in the context of B2B SME acceptance of AI practices. Therefore, the questions this research aims to answer are:

RQ1: What are the main antecedents and consequences of acceptance of AI practices by B2B SMEs in Saudi Arabia?

RQ2: How could AI practices help B2B SMEs improve their business outcomes in aspects such as customer interaction, relational governance, and business performance?

To answer these questions, the next section of the paper reviews existing literature related to antecedents and consequences of AI adoption. Then, a conceptual model and hypotheses are developed identifying  B2B  SME  AI  enablers  and  readiness  factors  as  antecedents  of acceptance of AI practices, and consequences of acceptance in terms of SMEs' customer engagement and service experience, relational governance, and business performance. Following this, the paper explains the methods adopted to test the conceptual model empirically and, subsequently, the results of structural equation modelling using survey data collected from B2B SMEs in Saudi Arabia are reported. Finally, the paper goes on to discuss the results in the context of existing literature and the practical and theoretical implications of the findings, as well as limitations and future research directions.

## 2. Literature review

## 2.1. Technology adoption by SMEs

E -commerce  has  become  an  important  medium  for  conducting business and numerous studies have focused on e-commerce acceptance by B2B SMEs (e.g., Asare, Gopolang, & Mogotlhwane, 2012; Kurnia, Choudrie, Mahbubur, & Alzougool, 2015; Sila & Dobni, 2012). Kurnia et al. (2015) proposed and empirically tested the impact of organisational, industry, and national readiness on the adoption of e-commerce applications, such as e-mail, electronic funds transfer, and barcodes, by B2B SMEs. They found that perceived benefits, perceived organisation resources and governance, perceived supporting services, and perceived environmental  pressure  had  varying  influences  on  the  adoption  of different e-commerce applications, demonstrating the need to examine adoption of individual applications rather than treating them as a homogenous group. Asare et al. (2012) found differences in the way B2B and B2C SMEs perceive problems in two different developing countries, but their results supported the role of power disruption and technicality in predicting the adoption of ICT applications by B2B SMEs. Based on the technology-organisation-environment  (TOE)  theory,  Sila  and  Dobni (2012)  explored  the  contextual  factors  influencing  B2B  SME  e-commerce usage patterns. They found that top management support and pressure from competition were two key differences between organisations  with  the  highest  levels  of  e-commerce  usage  and  integration compared  with  those  more  reluctant  to  adopt  these  technologies. Moreover, they found that firms utilising higher levels of B2B e-commerce  technologies  had  stronger  business  performance,  providing further support for SMEs' continued adoption of innovative technologies in order to remain competitive.

The  fourth  industrial  revolution,  or  Industry  4.0,  enhances  the adoption  of  computers  and  automation  with  smart  and  autonomous systems, and includes technologies such as AI, Internet of Things (IoT), and blockchain (Collins, Dennehy, Conboy, & Mikalef, 2021; Hansen & B ø gh,  2020; Syam & Sharma, 2018; Verma, Sharma, Deb, & Maitra, 2021). Numerous studies have explored implementation of such technologies  by  SMEs  although,  compared  to  e-commerce  adoption,  few have focused specifically on B2B SMEs. Examining the driving forces and barriers to adopting Industry 4.0 through interviews with 26 company  executives,  Horv ´ ath  and  Szab ´ o  (2019)  found  that  SMEs  have different driving forces and barriers compared to multi-national enterprises (MNEs). For SMEs, having more limited recruitment opportunities means human resource factors are a high driving force of using Industry 4.0 as a solution. On the other hand, financial resources and profitability pose a higher barrier for SMEs since they often have fewer financial resources to invest in new technologies compared to MNEs. In a survey of  UK  manufacturing  SMEs  ( n = 271),  Masood  and  Sonntag  (2020) found that perceived benefits of implementing Industry 4.0 technologies was  affected  by  company  size  and  attitude  towards  Industry  4.0, whereas perceived challenges of implementing Industry 4.0 was affected by manufacturing complexity. Open text responses regarding how SMEs could prepare for future manufacturing systems and technologies suggested training, financial support, and investment in people were key. In a survey of manufacturing SMEs in Iran ( n = 183) and Malaysia ( n = 177), Ghobakhloo and Ching (2019) found that strategic roadmapping for manufacturing digitalization is the most significant determinant of Smart  Manufacturing-related  Information  and  Digital  Technologies (SMIDT) adoption. They found that AI SMIDT adoption specifically is affected by perceived costs, perceived value, perceived compatibility, and IDT knowledge competency.

However, in their systematic review, Verma et al. (2021) pointed out that there is a lack of studies that have tested the related issues of AI acceptance in the marketing field. Therefore, practitioners suffer from a lack of full knowledge of the important aspects for the success of using this technology, which in turn, consumes more time, effort and investment (Kushwaha et al., 2021).

While progress has been made in examining the antecedents and consequences  of  AI  acceptance,  there  are  still  some  limitations.  For example, research participants tend to be from primarily large organisations (e.g., Alsheibani et al., 2020; Cao et al., 2021; (Rahman et al., 2021), few studies have explored both antecedents and consequences of AI  adoption  simultaneously  (e.g.,  Bag,  Pretorius,  Gupta, & Dwivedi, 2021), and there is little explicit consideration of B2B SMEs specifically. This, in turn, presents research gaps worthy to be examined based on a solid theoretical foundation covering the most important antecedents and  consequences  of  AI-practices  and  suitable  to  the  nature  of  B2B SMEs.  Therefore,  this  study  seeks  to  contribute  to  these  gaps  by considering both antecedents and consequences of AI practices for B2B SMEs in the context of Saudi Arabia.

## 3. Theoretical background, proposed conceptual model and hypotheses

A conceptual model is primarily used to create a relationship among all  the  variables  associated  with  a  research  approach.  Tornatzky, Fleischer, and Chakrabarti (1990) evaluated the technologyorganisation-environment (TOE) model for suggesting significant steps when adopting any technological aspect in the business process. As it provides a comprehensive picture regarding the main factors that could determine  the  adoption  of  new  systems  from  the  organisational perspective,  TOE  has  been  extensively  used  as  a  theoretical  base  by several  studies  over  different  contexts  such  as  smart  cities  (Ullah, Qayyum, Thaheem, Al-Turjman, & Sepasgozar, 2021), green innovation (Zhang,  Sun,  Yang, & Wang,  2020),  RFID  (Aboelmaged & Hashem, 2018), social commerce (Abed, 2020), big data analytics (El-Haddadeh, Osmani, Hindi, & Fadlalla, 2020), IoT (Schmitt, Mladenow, Strauss, & Schaffhauser-Linzatti, 2019), and big data in the B2B context (Sun, Hall, & Cegielski, 2020).

According  to  the  TOE  model,  factors  predicting  the  successful implementation and adoption of emerging systems fall into three key dimensions:  technology,  organisation,  and  environment  (Tornatzky et al., 1990). Technological dimensions pertain to these related technologies (software and hardware) that are available inside or outside the organisation and can facilitate the adoption of AI practices. In the current study, a number of aspects fall under the technological component of  the  TOE  model:  AI-based  technology  roadmapping,  infrastructure, and technicality. The second component in the TOE model is organisation which incorporates elements such as the size of the organisation, organisational  chart,  human  resources,  organisational  culture,  and departmental interconnections (Cruz-Jesus, Pinheiro, & Oliveira, 2019; Junior,  Oliveira, & Yanaze,  2019).  These  aspects  have  been  largely approved as key determinants of successful adoption of new systems (e. g., Junior et al., 2019; Venkatesh & Bala, 2012). AI-based professional expertise, attitude, and awareness were proposed in the current study to capture the organisational component of TOE. The third TOE component relates to the environment in which all business activities are conducted and includes industry, suppliers, competitors, and stakeholders (Martins, Oliveira, Thomas, & Tom ´ as, 2019; Tornatzky et al., 1990). As the main context of this study is B2B, business customers were included under the environment component which is reflected by two main aspects  in  the  current  study:  service  experience  with  AI  and  AI-based customer engagement.

In this paper, multiple variables measuring antecedents and consequences of AI are adopted so that thorough conclusions can be drawn. Dwivedi, Hughes, et al. (2021) researched the opportunities provided by AI for business organisations and argued that the nature or organisational culture of SMEs facilitates adoption of technological elements in business premises. Ayyagari, Demirguc-Kunt, and Maksimovic (2014) claimed that a significant orientation among SMEs would be required to enhance their existing AI framework. They strongly recommended that SMEs should run a continuous system for AI because a steady system can be updated to a better version of a previous one. According to Baker (2012), a company's adoption of any sort of technological tool is highly dependent  on  its  readiness,  organisational  behaviour,  and  organisational environment. These three aspects are essential for understanding whether  organisations  are  ready  to  adopt  technology  (Baker,  2012; Tornatzky et al., 1990). In the present research context, this conceptual model would enable a strong interrelation to be made among all the B2B and AI variables of Saudi SMEs (Baker, 2012; Tornatzky et al., 1990). The conceptual model for analysing this research topic is presented in Fig. 1. Definitions of the main constructs are also provided in Table 2.

Accordingly,  this  study proposes the following hypothesis:

H1a . Technology roadmapping has a positive influence on B2B SMEs' AI practices.

In a qualitative study with 25 AI experts, J ¨ ohnk et al. (2021) noted the  importance  of  knowledge  and  culture  as  prerequisite  factors  to successful  AI  implementation.  Similarly,  in  a  quantitative  study,  Bag et al. (2021) found that workforce skills positively affect adoption of big data analytics powered by AI. Within the B2B SME sector, it has been supported that having staff with adequate levels of professional expertise is important for ensuring the success of technological transformation and  technology  adoption  (Alsudairi & Dwivedi,  2010;  Kurnia  et  al., 2015; St-Pierre & Audet, 2011). Cantarello, Martini, and Nosella (2012) demonstrated that the performance of the development process largely depends on the extent to which employees enjoy professional expertise in terms of the skills and cognitive knowledge required. Furthermore, professional expertise has been widely shown to play a significant role in shaping the innovation diffusion process across the business sector (Lin & Lee, 2005; Lin & Lin, 2008; Zhu, Kraemer, & Xu, 2003). Accordingly, B2B SMEs that have staff with high technical skills and capabilities are more likely to successfully implement AI practices, which then positively  impacts  their  business  customer  interactions  and  business  performance  (Agostini & Nosella,  2016;  Ghobakhloo & Ching,  2019). Accordingly, this study suggests the following hypothesis:

## H1b . Professional expertise has a positive influence on AI practices in B2B SMEs.

In the context of AI, attitude explains the positive or negative feelings individuals have towards the technology (Cao et al., 2021). To implement a technology like AI, organisational leaders have to be sufficiently positive about it (Anandarajan, 2002). Lee and Runge (2001) studied the perception and behaviour of leaders towards the adoption of new IT in the business process. They found that leaders of small firms must have a positive attitude towards the technology for its successful implementation. A study by Masood and Sonntag (2020) found that attitude towards Industry  4.0  had  the  strongest  effect  on  perceived  benefits.  More recently,  Cao  et  al.  (2021)  found  that  managers'  attitudes  are  the strongest predictor of intention to use AI for organisational decisionmaking. Hence, the following hypothesis is proposed:

H1c . Attitude towards AI has a positive influence on B2B SMEs' AI practices.

## 3.2. SMEs' AI readiness and AI practices

SMEs' AI readiness refers to organisations' capabilities to support AI practices (Alsheibani et al., 2020). In the current study three aspects are associated with AI readiness: AI infrastructure, awareness, and technicality.  It  has  been  widely  argued  that  technical  infrastructure  is  a fundamental prerequisite for a successful digital transformation process (e.g., Kowtha & Choon, 2001; Lin & Lee, 2005; Lin & Lin, 2008). Recent research has found that IT infrastructure is one of the key AI readiness factors, specifically three underlying elements of IT infrastructure: data storage, networking, and scalable computing power capabilities (J ¨ ohnk et al., 2021). Moreover, Alsheibani et al. (2020) found that technology infrastructure and data structure were critical components of organisational readiness which positively influenced AI adoption. Based on these findings, this study hypothesises that:

H2a . AI  infrastructure  has  a  positive  influence  on  B2B  SMEs'  AI practices.

In  addition  to  AI  infrastructure,  AI  technicality  is  also  essential. Technicality can be operationalised as the extent to which the targeted system (AI) is perceived to be uncomplicated for users (Sigala, 2006). If users perceive that a system is free of physical and mental effort then they are more likely to be inclined to use it; likewise, a system that is perceived to be technically difficult will be less likely to be used (Sohn & Kwon, 2020). In the context of AI adoption and implementation by organisations, several studies have found factors relating to user friendliness (e.g. ease of use, effort expectancy) to have a significant positive effect on adoption (e.g. Cao et al., 2021; Chatterjee, Rana, Dwivedi, & Baabdullah,  2021,  Chatterjee,  Rana,  Tamilmani, & Sharma,  2021). Therefore, this study hypothesises that:

## 3.3. SMEs' AI practices and business customer AI-based interaction

AI  is  an  innovation  that  can  contribute  to  all  aspects  of  business customer interactions (Dwivedi, Hughes, et al., 2021). Practically, AI has changed the business dimension and allowed organisations to be competitive  by  establishing  a  special  service  experience,  customer engagement, and, accordingly, brand value in the marketplace (Ameen, Tarhini,  Reppel, & Anand,  2021;  Chen,  Guo,  Gao, & Liang,  2020; Haenlein & Kaplan, 2019; Prentice & Nguyen, 2020; Prentice, Weaven, & Wong, 2020; Wang, Teo, & Janssen, 2021). According to Prentice and Nguyen (2020), implementation of AI has changed how organisations serve and interact with their customers. In this respect, the critical role of AI practices in shaping B2C customer experience has recently received considerable  attention  by  marketing  researchers  (e.g.,  Ameen  et  al., 2021; Prentice & Nguyen, 2020). With the help of AI-based practices, organisations are more able to efficiently serve their customers anytime and anywhere. This, in turn, not only changes the customer experience but also accelerates the organisation's performance (Prentice & Nguyen, 2020). Morosan and Bowen (2018) demonstrated how AI applications can process a huge amount of customer data in the service sector, and, accordingly, how they can enrich the service experience by providing a high  level  of  customisation.  AI  provides  companies  with  a  great  opportunity  to  know  their  customers'  needs,  wants,  expectations,  and buying behaviour more accurately. Thus, companies can enhance and create added value in the customer experience more than ever before (Ameen et al., 2021). Accordingly, it is hypothesised that:

H3a . B2B  SMEs'  AI  practices  have  a  positive  influence  on  service experience with AI.

As well as benefitting the service experience, AI applications can also contribute to how much the customer engages with suppliers (Prentice et al., 2020). Customers are more likely to be engaged with their suppliers when there is an interactive experience (Vivek, Beatty, & Morgan, 2012). Given the innovative features of AI that could provide customers with more interactivity with suppliers, customer engagement is more attainable  in  comparison  with  traditional  mechanisms  used  in  this respect (Prentice & Nguyen, 2020). The crucial role of AI in customers' engagement  has  been  recently  shown  by  Prentice  et  al.  (2020)  who revealed that both AI information satisfaction and AI system satisfaction significantly contribute to the level of customer engagement. Prentice and  Nguyen  (2020)  also  provided  further  evidence  supporting  the crucial  role  of  AI-based  service  experience  in  customers'  tendency  to engage  with  service  providers.  Ayyagari  et  al.  (2014)  argued  that  if SMEs  can  implement  AI  technology  well  then  interaction  between organisational stakeholders would be smooth. They also argued that AI implementation  would  be  the  best  approach  to  engage  customers directly with organisational activities. Based on the above discussion, it is proposed that:

H3b . B2B SMEs' AI practices have a positive influence on AI-based customer engagement.

## 3.4. SMEs' AI practices and relational governance

It has been commonly argued that relational governance largely relies on the extent to which business partners collaborate, share knowledge, co-create value, and depend on each other (Janssen, van der Voort, & Wahyudi, 2017). This would not be possible without sophisticated systems  like  AI  (McAfee,  Brynjolfsson,  Davenport,  Patil, & Barton, 2012). Practically, AI helps business partners to jointly collect, organise, and analyse high quality big data. This, in turn, improves governance and the decision-making process (Janssen et al., 2017; Nasseef, Baabdullah, Alalwan, Lal, & Dwivedi, 2021). Haenlein and Kaplan (2019) argued that AI has the potential to collect various data and analyse them to the benefit of the organisation. Business organisations traditionally had to carry out a manual survey to understand consumer demands and opinions in the target market but now they can easily analyse data and understand customer perceptions with the help of AI; accordingly, they can mutually solve problems and prepare future plans with their business customers (Mogaji et al., 2020; Prentice & Nguyen, 2020). Dwivedi, Hughes, et al.  (2021)  argued  that  AI  has  given  enough  flexibility  to business partners to validate large data sets on any business strategy. This then facilitates business partners in delivering the highest quality experience to their target business customers in terms of joint planning and problem solving (Haenlein & Kaplan, 2019). Dubey et al. (2020) recently reported that AI practices will sustain the business partners' alignment by reconfiguring their resources to meet unexpected situations. Accordingly, this study proposes that:

H4a . B2B  SMEs'  AI  practices  have  a  positive  influence  on  joint planning.

H4b . B2B SMEs' AI practices have a positive influence on joint problem solving.

Dwivedi, Hughes, et al. (2021) and Grover, Kar, and Dwivedi (2020) claimed that AI has the potential to increase business performance and operations. Therefore, this study has adopted the proposition that acceptance of AI practices within innovative systems will positively impact the business performance of B2B SMEs.

Highly complex and diversified information is much easier to process and interpret by facilitating AI practices (Chen et al., 2020; Dubey et al., 2020;  Dubey,  Gunasekaran,  Childe,  Fosso  Wamba,  et  al.,  2019). Accordingly, business organisations are better able to be sure about their decisions relating to market demand, resource flow, and capacities. ## 4. Methodology

To  empirically  test  the  proposed  hypotheses,  and  since  validated measurement scales were already available to be adapted from previous studies,  a  survey  method  was employed using a structured questionnaire.  Measurement  items  (Appendix)  were  selected  based  on  their alignment with the definitions of constructs outlined in Table 2. Items were  measured  using  a  five-point  Likert  scale  anchored  1 strongly disagree to 5 strongly agree . The questionnaire was translated into Arabic based on the back-translation approach proposed by Brislin (1976). The Arabic version of the questionnaire was also inspected by several experts in the area of information systems, digital marketing, industrial marketing, and SMEs. These experts approved the validity of the scale items and the accuracy of the translation process.

The target population was B2B SME CEOs, CIOs, general managers, senior managers, marketing managers, and owners in Saudi Arabia. The targeted  participants  worked  in  wholesaling,  manufacturing,  agriculture,  information  technology  and  social  media,  and  construction  industries that provide services and products to business customers. These sectors were selected based on what has been suggested by Merrilees et al. (2011). Firstly, a pilot study with 25 such participants was undertaken  to  ensure  scale  reliability.  The  Cronbach's  coefficient  alpha values for all constructs were above 0.70, as recommended by Nunnally (1978). The questionnaire was then distributed to 500 target participants from July 2020 to the end of September 2020. Different methods were used to reach the targeted respondents. For example, contact was initially made with public relations offices in these companies to reach the target respondents. Also, respondents were contacted through their publicly available email address or through other means, such as professional social network sites. Moreover, field visits were made to suitable SMEs to request their participation.

SPSS v23 and AMOS were used for data analysis. Structural equation modelling (SEM) was used to analyse the proposed conceptual model, combining multiple regression with factor analysis, while also allowing for both observed and latent variables to be analysed simultaneously, and providing overall model fit statistics (Tabachnick & Fidell, 2007). In accordance  with  the  recommendation  of  a  two-stage  analytical  procedure (Anderson & Gerbing, 1988) confirmatory factor analysis was conducted in AMOS using Maximum Likelihood Estimation, which was then followed by path analysis of the structural relationships.

## 5.3. Common method bias

To rule out issues of common method bias, this study used Harman's single factor test, which eradicates concern about common method bias if  the  variance  accounted  by  the  first  factor  is  not  higher  than  50% (Podsakoff, MacKenzie, & Lee, 2003). Fifty-one scale items for thirteen latent constructs (i.e., FIN, TRM, AWR, ATT, INFR, PRE, T.C, EX, R.GP, P.AI, R.GPS, ENG, and N ⋅ F) were loaded for exploratory factor analysis. Cumulative variance extracted was 42.829% for the first factor, hence, the  current  study  data  does  not  have  concerns  regarding  common method bias.

## 5.4.2. Structural model results

The second stage of the SEM analysis of the model largely supported the proposed conceptual model. All fit indices of the structural model were within their suggested values (CMIN/DF = 2.901; GFI = 0.903; AGFI = 0.821, RMSEA = 0.067; NFI = 0.919; CFI = 0.951). Furthermore,  the  model  accounts  for  adequate  variance  ( R 2 )  in  the  main endogenous factors (SMEs' AI practices = 0.69; service experience = 0.59; customer engagement = 0.56; joint problem solving = 0.70; joint planning = 0.45; financial performance = 0.50; non-financial performance = 0.48). The model hypotheses were tested based on the path coefficient results. As Table 9 shows, apart from two hypothesised paths (T.C → P.AI; PRE → P.AI), all paths were found to be significant. As for the main P.AI antecedents, awareness was the most influential factor contributing to SMEs' AI practices ( γ = 0.452, p < 0.000), followed by technology roadmapping ( γ = 0.228, p < 0.000). Attitude ( γ = 0.188, p < 0.000) and infrastructure ( γ = 0.167, p < 0.000) were also found to have a significant positive effect on SMEs' AI practices. However, neither technicality nor professional expertise were able to significantly predict variance in SMEs' AI practices. Path coefficient results support the significant impact of SMEs' AI practices on all measured consequences. In detail, joint problem solving ( γ = 0.784, p < 0.000), service experience ( γ = 0.768, p < 0.000), and customer engagement ( γ = 0.759, p < 0.000) are most influenced by SMEs' AI practices. Another strong hypothesised path exists between SMEs' AI practices and joint planning ( γ = 0.669, p < 0.000).  Both  dimensions  of  business  performance,  financial  ( γ = 0.709, p < 0.000) and non-financial ( γ = 0.681, p < 0.000) performance, were also found to be significantly predicted by the role of SMEs' AI practices.

The study has successfully provided scientific evidence  for  understanding  the  antecedents  and  consequences  of  AI acceptance by B2B SMEs. According to the structural model results, the main  antecedents  of  AI  practices  have  a  high  predictive  power:  the significant  AI  enablers  and  AI  readiness  factors  account  for  69%  of variance  in  B2B  SMEs'  acceptance  of  AI  practices.  Furthermore, considerable  variance  was  also  found  in  the  main  consequences  of acceptance  of  AI  practices  (service  experience = 59%;  customer engagement = 59%; joint planning = 45%; and joint problem solving = 70%; financial performance = 50%; non-financial performance = 48%).

According to the coefficient results, two dimensions of technology readiness (awareness and technical infrastructure) were found to be key determinants of B2B SMEs' acceptance of AI practices. This result indicates that AI practices have more potential to be successfully utilised if adequate knowledge and information about the features and benefits of AI applications have been previously captured by B2B SMEs (Delponte & Tamburrini,  2018).  Technical  infrastructure  as  another  dimension  of technology  readiness  was  also  found  to  be  a  key  prerequisite  for acceptance of AI practices. Thus, B2B SMEs are more likely to successfully implement AI practices if the required technical resources and facilities are available. This could be attributed to the particular nature of AI practices, which depend on the availability of highly advanced networks and systems (Gusev, 2020). This challenge would play a more crucial role in B2B SMEs due to the limited financial resources that SMEs have  to  buy  or  rent  such  infrastructure  (Amaral & Peças,  2020). Nevertheless, technicality was not able to predict any variance in the acceptance of AI practices, which means that B2B SMEs in Saudi Arabia, when making their decision to implement AI practices, are not concerned about the ease and time required to become familiar with AI practices. This is similar to findings by Asare et al. (2012) that SMEs did not consider lack of technical know-how to be a hindrance to adoption of ICT. People are more engaged with smart technologies, so they have more familiarity with and knowledge of how to use the technology than ever before. Moreover, people are more likely to override any limitations regarding the level of complexity if they perceive that the new system is more valuable and can contribute to improved performance and productivity. This assumption was recently confirmed by Sohn and Kwon  (2020)  who  noticed  that  technicality  perceived  in  AI-based intelligent products is less important than the utilitarian and hedonic value of these products.

SMEs' AI enablers (i.e., attitude and technology roadmapping) were also confirmed to play a crucial role in facilitating the acceptance of AI practices by B2B SMEs. Such results indicate the importance of a wellinformed and clear roadmap for any process of technology transformation, which would not succeed based on a random decisionmaking  process.  Smart  technologies  such  as  AI  practices  require  a strategy to guide decision-makers in SMEs to carry out appropriate actions that serve this aspiration (Borges, Laurindo, Spínola, Gonçalves, & Mattos, 2020). This, in turn, calls for the presence of technology roadmapping that clearly identifies the main goals, objectives, and decisions required for any implementation programme of AI practices. Practically, such a roadmap provides guidelines to help B2B SMEs make the right decisions about AI utilisation, and it also represents a solid basis for addressing the main issues that could arise prior to or during the technology implementation process (Jeffrey, Sedgwick, & Robinson, 2013). The results regarding the significant role of technology roadmapping yielded in the current study are similar to these reported by Ghobakhloo and Ching (2019). By the same token, AI practices would not be successful  without  managers,  owners,  and  employees  having  positive thoughts, beliefs and feelings about AI practices. Attitudes have been largely identified as key determinants of people's intention and reactions towards new systems. The current study's result regarding the significant impact of attitude is very close to those reported by Cao et al. (2021).

The results of this study disprove the significant impact of professional expertise in the acceptance of AI practices by B2B SMEs in Saudi Arabia. This means that the levels of skills and technical knowledge do not  present  critical  issues  from  the  perspective  of  B2B  SMEs  when forming their decision to adopt AI practices. This could be due to the increasing level of awareness of AI systems, both globally and in Saudi Arabia,  which  is  suggested  by  the  results  relating  to  the  awareness construct  in  the  current  study.  Specifically,  awareness  items  in  the questionnaire  were  all  highly  rated  by  participants,  with  an  average mean  of  3.72.  This  suggests  that  there  is  extensive  awareness  of  AI practices in Saudi Arabia. Another explanation for the non-significant influence of professional expertise could be that AI practices are less complicated  and  do  not  require  much  effort  or  human  intervention. Consequently, there is less need for training by experts.

The results for the path coefficients largely support the significant role of acceptance of AI practices in contributing to both dimensions of customer  AI-based  interactions  (business  customer  engagement  and service experience with AI). Due to its interactive and smart nature, AI can capture, analyse, and organise a huge amount of data and make it quickly available at the time a customer needs or requests. Moreover, as discussed  by  Prentice  and  Nguyen  (2020)  and  Morosan  and  Bowen (2018),  AI  tools  are  more  able  to  work  reliably  and  provide  an outstanding customer service experience in a consistent manner. This, in turn, will be reflected in the level of confidence and trust that business customers have in an AI-based customer service. In addition, AI practices  enable  business  customers  to  have  a  highly  customised  service experience  that  matches  their  needs  and  expectations  because  such practices enjoy more flexibility that facilitates SMEs in modifying their marketing tactics and actions according to customers' preferences. The results regarding the impact of AI practices on service experience with AI are close to those of previous studies (Ameen et al., 2021; Chen et al., 2020;  Haenlein & Kaplan,  2019;  Morosan & Bowen,  2018;  Prentice et  al.,  2020;  Prentice & Nguyen,  2020).  As  for  business  customer engagement, a very strong causal path was noticed with acceptance of AI practices.  This  means  that  the  adoption  of  AI  practices  creates  more opportunities for B2B SMEs to be actively engaged with their targeted business customers. As discussed regarding AI-based customer service, AI practices can create an interactive and communicative environment capable of linking the company with its customers and promoting synchronous contact between them, regardless of any temporal or spatial obstacles.  Furthermore,  AI  practices  can  positively  contribute  to  the customer  service  experience  with  SMEs,  which,  in  turn,  creates  an appropriate  environment  that  enhances  opportunities  for  integration and engagement  among  business  partners. Several studies have demonstrated the impact of AI practices on customer engagement (e.g., Prentice et al., 2020; Prentice & Nguyen, 2020).

## 6.1. Theoretical implications

This study has been conducted due to the absence of a clear and comprehensive picture regarding the antecedents and consequences of AI  practices  from  the  perspective  of  B2B  SMEs.  It  has  theoretically proposed an inclusive model and empirically tested the main hypothesised paths among the antecedents and consequences of AI practice acceptance based on data obtained from 392 respondents from B2B SME organisations  in  Saudi  Arabia.  Accordingly,  this  study  provides  a broader  base  for  theoretical  and  practical  understanding  of  issues relating to AI practices in SMEs and the B2B sector in general.

This study operationalised new mechanisms from the TOE theory by considering SMEs' AI enablers; accordingly, it has extended the theoretical  horizon  of  TOE.  For  example,  it  presents  more  evidence  of technology roadmapping as one of the main AI enablers of a successful implementation of AI practices by SMEs. Another theoretical contribution is the validation of the role of SMEs' AI readiness. In this regard, this research has shed light on variables (AI infrastructure and AI awareness) that have long been in need of further study and investigation.

With regard to the consequences of AI practices, this study has successfully gone further than the acceptance stage by addressing customer AI-based interaction in terms of service experience with AI and customer engagement. This clearly shows that the success of AI practices concerns not  only  their  implementation  but  also  their  ability  to  enhance opportunities to improve customer service and engagement. A significant contribution of this study is its validation of a strong relationship between AI practices and factors of relational governance, which has rarely been discussed in the area of AI for SMEs. Thus, this study has broadened the scope of AI acceptance in terms of business customer engagement and the effectiveness of the service delivery process, both of which contribute to business  performance  and relational  governance (Basri, 2020). As discussed in the literature review section, there is a dearth of literature on the AI practices of B2B SMEs, particularly SMEs in Arab Middle Eastern countries such as Saudi Arabia. Accordingly, this research enhances the scholarly understanding of AI practices in Saudi B2B SMEs.

## 6.2. Managerial implications

As discussed above, this study was conducted with the aim of helping B2B SMEs in Saudi Arabia to better understand how they can successfully implement AI practices. Therefore, the empirical results constitute a frame of reference for the most important dimensions that must be considered to ensure the successful implementation of AI practices. One of the most important dimensions is the necessity of B2B SMEs having technology roadmapping. This initially requires B2B SMEs to consciously  and  accurately  prepare  a  clear  roadmap  for  the  implementation and use of contemporary technologies. It is strongly recommended that business partners be involved in all stages of designing and developing  technology  roadmapping.  Such  a  technology  roadmap should carefully identify the short- and long-term objectives of a digital transformation  process  (Jeffrey  et  al.,  2013).  More  importantly,  the technology roadmapping should be moved from its theoretical framework to its practical framework so that it can serve as a guide for all decisions and actions pertaining to AI practices.

The results also provide clues about the importance of attitudes for the successful acceptance of AI practices. This requires SMEs to further explore the nature of such attitudes to AI practices and then to shape such attitudes. Therefore, a survey could be conducted to discover the common opinions and attitudes of stakeholders towards AI practices. In parallel, more internal marketing efforts should be made to improve and sustain positive feelings and thoughts that AI practices can provide SMEs with more control, convenience, and safety in their operations.

In  relation  to  AI  readiness,  awareness  and  infrastructure  should receive attention from B2B SMEs in their endeavours to implement AI practices.  It  is  important  to  extensively  familiarise  people  with  AI practices and increase awareness of their benefits and characteristics. More education and training programmes can help to raise the current level of awareness and the value generated from AI practices. According to Lin and Lin (2008), B2B SMEs should ensure that AI vendors provide more assistance by educating and training their employees in the skills and technical knowledge required for effective use of AI practices. Social media platforms (e.g., LinkedIn, Twitter, YouTube, and TEDx) could also be useful when creating public awareness among stakeholders in the B2B SME sector (Dwivedi, Ismagilova, Hughes, Carlson, et al., 2021).

With regard to infrastructure, it is important that B2B SMEs either have or invest in the facilities and technical resources needed to successfully  implement  AI  practices.  Therefore,  B2B  SMEs  that  hope  to utilise AI practices in their business model should initially evaluate their existing technical capabilities and resources, thereby enabling them to identify  which  areas  of  technical  infrastructure  need  investment  and improvement (Lin & Lin, 2008). Further, B2B SMEs should concentrate on their ability to integrate AI practices with current digital applications and infrastructure.

## 6.3. Limitations and future research directions

Although this study has provided several theoretical and practical implications, some limitations could be addressed in future research. First, this study is cross-sectional, where  both  antecedents  and A.M. Baabdullah et al.

consequences of AI practices were tested at one point in time. However, this does not provide a full picture of the long-term applicability of the current study's results. Therefore, a longitudinal study is recommended to investigate the impact of the current study's factors prior to, during, and after implementation of AI practices. Second, this study examined the related issues of AI practices only from the supplier's perspective within the B2B SME sector, thus restricting the ability to expand the generalisability  of  the  results  to  the  business  customer's  perspective. Therefore,  future  studies  could  consider  this  phenomenon  from  both perspectives:  SMEs  as  both  venders  and  business  customers.  Third, despite the current study model being based on the TOE model, other macro-environmental  factors  (e.g.,  culture,  government  regulations, national technological advancement level, and economic situation) were not considered in the current study. Thus, it would be useful for future researchers to investigate whether macro-environmental factors positively or negatively impact the successful implementation of AI practices. Finally, the dark side of AI (Cao et al., 2021), for example issues of privacy, security, and ethics were not addressed in the current study, so future  studies  should  consider  how  these  affect  the  tendency  of  B2B SMEs towards AI practices.

## 7. Conclusion

The goal of this paper has been to evaluate the main antecedents and consequences of acceptance of AI practices by B2B SMEs in Saudi Arabia. By reviewing previous work on AI within the SME sector, it was found that there is a lack of understanding regarding this topic and that only a few studies have addressed it. The TOE model was considered as a theoretical  basis  for  the  model  proposed  by  this  study.  Specifically, SMEs' AI-enablers (i.e., AI-based technology roadmapping, professional expertise,  and  attitude)  and  SMEs'  AI  readiness  (i.e.,  infrastructure, technicality,  and  awareness)  were  proposed  as  key  antecedents  of acceptance  of  AI  practices.  The  conceptual  model  also  proposed  the consequences  of  AI  practices  in  three  main  categories:  relational governance,  customers'  AI-based  interactions,  and  SMEs'  AI-enabled performance.  Empirical  data  were  collected  from  participants  who own or work in B2B SMEs. The SEM results support the current study model  fitness  and  predictive  validity,  as  well  as  the  majority  of  the proposed research hypotheses, with the exceptions being the impact of both technicality and professional expertise on acceptance of AI practices. Solid theoretical and logical justifications for the results have been discussed. In addition, the main theoretical and practical implications of this study have been outlined.
