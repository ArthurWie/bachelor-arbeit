## 1. Introduction

Artificial intelligence (AI) has emerged as a critical driver of organizational  performance  in  the  era  of  Industry  4.0,  fundamentally reshaping  how  firms  design  operations,  manage  teams,  and  sustain competitiveness  in  technology-intensive  environments  (Gabsi,  2024; Khogali & Mekid, 2023). Historically, AI adoption in organizations was primarily associated with task automation and efficiency gains, focusing on cost reduction and process optimization (Ryalat, Franco, Elmoaqet, Almtireen, & Al-Refai,  2024).  More  recently,  advances  in  machine learning, data analytics, and intelligent systems have expanded AI ' s role from operational support to strategic value creation, influencing innovation, decision-making quality, and organizational adaptability (Ali, Yang,  Ali,  Ali, & Noman,  2025a;  Singh,  Joshi,  Dissanayake,  Nainanayake, & Kumar, 2025). For managers and policymakers, the practical challenge  is  no  longer  whether  to  adopt  AI,  but  how  to  leverage  it effectively  to  generate tangible performance gains while maintaining workforce engagement and organizational commitment. A B S T R A C T

As artificial intelligence (AI) adoption accelerates across industries, the role of organizational mechanisms that enable its effective utilization becomes increasingly critical. Drawing on innovation diffusion theory, this study investigates how team dynamics, high-commitment workforces (HCW), and corporate innovation and innovation capabilities (CIIC) mediate the relationship between AI adoption and organizational performance. Using survey data from 419 Chinese manufacturing firms, the study employs structural equation modeling alongside long short-term  memory  techniques  to  validate  the  proposed  framework.  The  findings  reveal  that  cohesive  team dynamics enhance collaboration and knowledge sharing, while strong CIIC promotes strategic alignment and innovation outcomes. Moreover, a highly committed workforce amplifies the performance benefits of AI adoption by fostering deeper employee engagement, greater adaptability, and enhanced organizational flexibility. This  study  contributes  to  the  literature  on  organizational  behavior  and  technology  management  by  demonstrating how human and organizational enablers unlock the performance-enhancing potential of AI. The results also  provide  practical  guidance  for  managers  aiming  to  design  supportive  organizational  environments  and leverage AI to achieve sustainable competitive advantage.

Beyond efficiency, AI facilitates mass customization and resilience in global value chains by allowing firms to respond rapidly to demand volatility and operational disruptions (Wan et al., 2020; Modgil, Singh, & Hannibal, 2022). As competitive pressures intensify, AI adoption has thus evolved from a purely technological upgrade into a strategic imperative with direct implications for organizational performance, innovation capacity, and long-term sustainability (Tariq, Poulin, & Abonamah, 2021).

Despite these practical promises, firms frequently struggle to translate  AI  investments  into  sustained  performance  improvements,  highlighting  a  critical  gap  between  adoption  and  value  creation.  Prior research  offers  limited  insight  into  the  organizational  mechanisms through  which  AI-driven  technologies  enhance  performance,  often treating  AI  as  a  standalone  technological  input  rather  than  a  sociotechnical system embedded within teams and organizational processes (Mikalef, Islam, Parida, Singh, & Altwaijry,  2023).  Drawing  on innovation  diffusion  theory  (IDT),  this  study  argues  that  the  performance effects of AI depend not only on its technical capabilities but also on how it is diffused, interpreted, and institutionalized within organizations (Kelley & Brooks, 1991). In practice, manufacturing firms face several barriers to this diffusion process, including inadequate digital infrastructure, limited real-time data capabilities, and skepticism arising from  the ' black-box ' nature  of  machine  learning  models,  which  undermines managerial trust and acceptance (Antosz, Pasko, & Gola, 2020; and  Carletti,  Masiero,  Beghi, & Susto,  2019).  Moreover,  effective  AI implementation  requires  alignment  with  organizational  strategies, workforce  reskilling,  and  adaptive  team  dynamics,  factors  that  are frequently overlooked in technology-centric studies (Singh et al., 2025). These challenges are particularly pronounced in emerging economies, where resource constraints and coordination difficulties further complicate  AI  integration  (Moharrak,  Nguyen, & Mogaji,  2024). Collectively, these issues underscore the need to examine how organizational  factors  such  as  team  dynamics,  innovation  processes,  and employee commitment enable firms to convert AI adoption into meaningful performance outcomes.

To address the gap regarding the underlying mechanisms through which AI influences organizational performance, this study applies IDT to examine the role of team dynamics, CIIC, and a HCW as mediators. Importantly, we distinguish between service-purpose AI (SPAI), which is designed to perform specific operational tasks such as quality inspection, scheduling, or customer service, and general-purpose AI (GPAI), which provides broader and more flexible applications, including predictive analytics, strategic decision-making, and cross-functional optimization. IDT suggests that the successful diffusion of these technologies depends on how organizations embed them into routines, processes, and cultural practices (Bertels, Howard-Grenville, & Pek, 2016). For SPAI, strong  team  dynamics  enable  employees  to  coordinate  effectively around task-specific applications, ensuring that the benefits are realized in day-to-day operations (Patel, Pettitt, & Wilson, 2012). For GPAI, team dynamics play a broader role in fostering cross-departmental collaboration  and  knowledge  sharing,  which  are  essential  for  strategic  integration (Lindblom & Martins, 2022). CIIC also mediates differently: in the case of SPAI, firms innovate around specific processes to improve efficiency  (Sun & Abdullahi  Usman,  2025),  while  with  GPAI,  firms leverage broader innovation capabilities to transform business models and invest in long-term technological advancement. Finally, a high-commitment workforce reduces resistance and builds trust in AI adoption (Silvestre, Begnini, & Abreu, 2025). Employees committed to the organization are more willing to integrate SPAI into routine activities and adapt to GPAI-driven systemic changes that require ongoing learning and cultural adjustment.

Given the rapid digital transformation under Industry 4.0, the integration of AI into manufacturing organizations holds profound implications for competitiveness, efficiency, and sustainable growth (Emon & Khan, 2025). However, without understanding the organizational and human mechanisms that translate AI adoption into tangible outcomes, firms  risk  underutilizing  these  technologies  or  even  experiencing counterproductive  effects  (Amankwah-Amoah & Appiah,  2025).  By distinguishing  between  SPAI  and  GPAI,  this  study  advances  a  more nuanced  perspective  on  AI  adoption  and  its  performance  outcomes. Drawing on IDT, we argue that the impact of AI on organizational performance does not occur in isolation but is contingent upon mediating mechanisms  that  enable  the  diffusion  and  institutionalization of AI-enabled practices. Specifically, this study aims to (1) investigate how SPAI and GPAI affect organizational performance through the mediating roles of team dynamics, CIIC, and a high-commitment workforce, and (2)  clarify  the  conditions  under  which  these  mediating  mechanisms become most effective in unlocking AI ' s potential.

Accordingly, the study is guided by the following research questions (RQ):

RQ1. How does AI influence organizational performance through team dynamics, CIIC, and a high-commitment workforce?

RQ2. How  do  SPAI  and  GPAI  influence  organizational  performance through team dynamics,

CIIC, and a high-commitment workforce?

This study makes several key contributions to the business strategy, technology, and innovation literature by advancing our understanding of AI adoption and its organizational impact. First, this study extends IDT (Rogers, 2003b) by theorizing that the performance implications of AI  adoption  depend  on  organizational  and  human-centered  enabling mechanisms, rather than on technological adoption alone. Specifically, we advance IDT by introducing team dynamics, high-commitment work systems, and CIIC as key mediating mechanisms through which AI is diffused, embedded, and translated into organizational performance. In doing  so,  the  study  refines  IDT  within  the  context  of  digital  transformation by conceptualizing AI diffusion as a socio-technical process shaped by organizational structures and workforce capabilities (Wonglimpiyarat & Yuberk,  2005).  Second,  it  distinguishes  between SPAI and GPAI, showing that AI ' s effects on performance vary by type: GPAI  enhances  adaptability,  cross-functional  knowledge  integration, and strategic  innovation,  while  SPAI  improves  operational  efficiency and addresses domain-specific challenges (Marzi & Balzano, 2025).

Third, it highlights the mediating role of team dynamics, demonstrating  that  performance  gains  depend  on  team-level  processes  that assimilate  and  apply  AI  knowledge,  with  GPAI  requiring  broader collaboration and SPAI benefiting primarily domain-specific teams (Sik, 2016). Fourth, the study shows that CIIC mediates AI ' s impact on performance, enabling firms to transform AI knowledge into tangible innovations, with GPAI supporting firm-wide integration and SPAI driving targeted  efficiency  gains.  Finally,  it  emphasizes  the  importance  of  a HCW, revealing that employee engagement, adaptability, and initiative are critical for translating AI adoption into performance (Rubel, Kee, & Rimi, 2023), with different requirements for GPAI and SPAI.

## 2. Theoretical background and hypothesis development

## 2.1. Innovation diffusion theory and organizational performance

IDT posits that the adoption and effective utilization of innovations depend not only on their technical merits but also on how well they are integrated into organizational and social contexts (Rogers, 2003a) . According to IDT, the diffusion of new technologies such as AI is shaped by attributes  including  relative  advantage,  compatibility  with  existing processes, complexity, trialability, and observability (Wonglimpiyarat & Yuberk, 2005) In organizational settings, these factors determine how innovations move from mere adoption to full assimilation, becoming embedded  in  everyday  practices  and  decision-making  (Gustafsson & Lazzaro, 2021). AI -both GPAI, which can be applied across multiple functions, and SPAI, which is tailored to specific operational or service domains -offers  significant  potential  to  enhance  efficiency,  decision quality,  and  strategic  outcomes  (Patnaik & Bakkar, 2024). However, simply acquiring AI technologies does not guarantee improved performance. Organizations must cultivate the structures, culture, and capabilities that enable AI to be effectively diffused, absorbed, and exploited across different levels of operation. From an IDT perspective, AI ' s impact is therefore contingent upon mediating mechanisms that reduce uncertainty, enhance compatibility with organizational routines, and generate observable benefits that reinforce adoption and sustained use (Bin-Nashwan & Li, 2025; Espina-Romero et al., 2024).

Building  on  this  reasoning,  we  argue  that  the  influence  of  AI  on organizational  performance  is  primarily  indirect,  unfolding  through three critical mediating pathways. First, team dynamics serve as a bridge between AI capabilities and outcomes by fostering knowledge sharing, collaboration,  and  adaptive  problem-solving,  which  are  essential  for translating AI-generated insights into coordinated organizational action. Second, CIIC mediates AI ' s role by enabling firms to transform technological  inputs  into  new  products,  processes,  and  business  models, ensuring that both GPAI and SPAI applications yield tangible competitive  advantages  rather  than  remaining  isolated  tools.  Third,  a  HCW provides the cultural and motivational foundation for AI assimilation, as employees who are engaged and aligned with organizational goals are more likely to embrace change, experiment with new technologies, and integrate  AI  into  their  work  routines.  Collectively,  these  mediators demonstrate that AI adoption alone is insufficient; it is the interplay of team-based  collaboration,  organizational  innovation  capacity,  and workforce commitment that unlocks AI ' s full potential. Without these mechanisms, AI risks underutilization, symbolic adoption, or technological hype without substantive improvements in organizational performance. ## 2.3. The mediating role of team dynamics

Strong  team  dynamics  help  explain  AI-driven  efficiencies  into perceptible  outcomes  (Keshireddy,  2023).  Elements  such  as  clear communication, trust, and collaboration maximize the benefits of AI tools, like predictive analytics, to ensure visions are well applied (Zong & Guan,  2024).  Without  these  strong  team  dynamics,  AI ' s  potential remains  underutilized.  Evidence  also  illustrates  that  IT  systems  can assist leaders in structuring processes that strengthen AI connection (Ali, Xue,  Wang,  Yin, & Tariq,  2024).  Team  dynamics  also  mediate  the relationship between leadership style and team performance, present valued  awareness  into  the  way  AI-based  interaction  systems  can  be planned  for  organizational  accomplishment  (Ning,  2024).  Team  dynamics, which mediate the role between SPAI and organizational performance, together with diversity, communication, and collaboration, increase the successful adoption and integration of service-oriented AI. Teams with strong dynamics can better employ service-oriented AI to deal with operational challenges and improve outcomes (Khakifirooz et  al.,  2024).  When  communication  flows  smoothly,  teams  are  more expected  to  align  AI  initiatives  with  organizational  aims  (Van  Knippenberg,  2017).  These  technologies  facilitate  continuous  interactions within teams for better task coordination and engagement among team members (Somanathan, 2023).

AI tools such as intelligent assistants, real-time analytics platforms, and  collaborative  AI  purpose  systems  facilitate  better  information sharing,  coordination  to  improve  transparency  and  help  teams  work more efficiently. AI-purpose assists teams with perceptions and pattern recognition from complex datasets, aiding quicker and more accurate decisions, to strengthen team trust and alignment (Raisch & Krakowski, 2021). AI purposes are often embedded across functions like HR, operations, and  customer  service, allowing  cross-functional teams  to collaborate on shared AI platforms (Jarrahi, Askay, Eshraghi, & Smith, 2023).  These  systems  encourage  adaptive  behaviors  of  the  team,  as teams  continually  refine  workflows  in  response  to  AI-driven  understandings, promoting agility and innovation (Chui, Harryson, Valley, Manyika, & Roberts, 2018).

## 2.4. The mediating role of CIIC

AI  prospers  in  organizations  with  robust  innovation  and  R & D frameworks, enabling them to explore several applications and drive performance  improvements  across  various  domains  (Mariani  et  al., 2023).  CIIC  is  an  important  mechanism  to  link  AI  to  organizational success. AI-powered predictive analytics comfort businesses to anticipate trends  and adapt professionally, improve  profitability,  and customer satisfaction. When used in R & D, AI quickens product development, refines innovation strategies, and gain a competitive edge. The relationship between AI and enactment strengthens when CIIC mediates it, as AI cares for a culture of innovation that pushes business success. Through advanced data analysis, predictive intuitions, and automation, AI  reinforces  CIIC,  which  in  turn  supports  performance  perfections (Sj ¨ odin, Parida, & Kohtam ¨ aki, 2023). CIIC mediates the role of SPAI and organizational performance. Corporate CIIC, such as innovation strategy and R & D capacity, fortifies the impact of service-oriented AI, given the necessary  infrastructure  and  strategic  direction  for  its  enactment. Studies suggest that organizations with strong innovation capabilities can better acclimate service-oriented AI for operational improvements and competitive advantage (Akter et al., 2023). R & D initiatives support the customization and optimization of AI solutions. IDT also describes in what manner organizations with high CIIC are more likely to adopt and assimilate service-oriented AI excellently. AI services such as machine learning  algorithms,  natural  language  processing,  and  data  analytics platforms  openly  enrich  CIIC  in  assisting  innovation  in  processes, products, and business models (Marinakis, 2020).

In short, AI supplies advanced inputs (data, automation, prediction), CIIC  transforms  those  inputs  into  novel  products/processes/business models, and CIIC-driven innovations then ultimately improve organizational performance (productivity, growth, competitive position). This logic  is  consistent  with  absorptive  capacity  and  dynamic  capabilities perspectives:  technology  alone  is  insufficient  without  organizational capability to assimilate and exploit it (Cohen & Levinthal, 1990; Teece, 2007). Empirical studies likewise find that technological investments raise  performance  primarily  when  accompanied  by  innovation  capabilities (Davenport & Ronanki, 2018).

## 2.5. The mediating role of HCW

This hypothesis submits that HCW mediate the relationship between Artificial  Intelligence  and  organizational  performance.  HCW  systems represent a package of human resources and managerial performance, just  like  employee  authorization,  participative  decision-making,  and development  of  skills,  that  raises  loyalty,  discretionary  trust  effort among  workers  (Berg,  Appelbaum,  Bailey, & Kalleberg,  2004).  The positive incorporation of AI into processes of organizational processes regularly pivots on workers ' inclination and capacity to adapt to new systems, learn AI-driven applications, and contribute innovative concepts (Priyadarshini, Dubey, Kumar, & Jha, 2020). Previous literature advocates  that  high-commitment  environments  empower  workers  to better leverage the capabilities of technology, thereby intensifying the performance  outcomes  of  AI  adoption  (Meijerink & Keegan,  2019). Consequently, HCW are posited to serve as a serious mediating device over which AI adoption, AI services, and AI general-purpose applications translate into improved organizational performance.

AI is seen as a catalyst that increases organizational performance, but its  true  potential is realized through the engagement of HCW (Moin, Behl, Zhang, & Shankar, 2024). HCW are emotionally invested in both their roles and the organization's achievements, enthusiastically engage with AI tools, and leverage them to improve their productivity, expertise, and decision-making (Biliaev, 2024). AI's ability to process large datasets and produce understandings directly for performance metrics like  productivity,  quality,  and  efficacy  (Kumar,  Mohan, & Shivalini, 2024). AI also adds to job satisfaction and motivation for HCW by way of improving efficiency and continuous skill growth (Alothmany, Jiang, & Manoharan,  2023).  Personalized  AI  tools,  such  as  adaptive  learning platforms and automated task assistance, aid employees in refining their skills  and  performing  tasks  more  accurately.  AI-driven  training  programs deal with customized learning paths, permit employees to grow professionally, and align with organizational objectives too (Dwivedi et al., 2023).

## 3. Methodology

## 3.1. Sample and data collection

We collected data from Chinese manufacturing firms to examine the proposed model driven by two main considerations: first, AI adoption, and  organizational  performance  in  manufacturing  firms.  China,  as  a global  AI  manufacturing  leader,  receives  reimbursement  from  strong government support under the "Made in China 2025" initiative (Luthra, Kumar, Zavadskas, Mangla, & Garza-Reyes, 2020). This environment presents a unique opportunity to analyze how AI adoption influences organizational performance within a critical sector of China ' s economy, despite challenges like high costs, talent shortages, and data security issues persisting, specifically for SMEs (Badghish & Soomro, 2024). Industrial firms are basic to China's economy (Wang, Lu, Fan, Hu, & Wang, 2022).  Thus,  strategic  AI  implementation  is  essential  for  sustainable progression and transparent explanations of issues (Kokina, Blanchette, Davenport, & Pachamanova,  2025).  Second,  China ' s  manufacturing sector is central to the country ' s economic growth, making it essential to understand how strategic AI adoption can drive sustainable development and operational agility in this rapidly evolving context (Jaafar, Khan, & Salman, 2025).

[S. 5]

A  structured  survey  methodology  was  employed  to  collect  data, supported  by  rigorous  language  and  validation  procedures  to  ensure cross-cultural equivalence in the Chinese context. The survey instrument was initially developed in English and then translated into Chinese (Ali, Qin, Raza-Ullah, Ali, & Sherwani, 2025b). An independent back-translation was subsequently conducted to verify conceptual consistency and translation accuracy. To further strengthen interpretability and content validity, we conducted a pilot test with 30 executives and directors, based on which minor wording adjustments were made. The finalized  Chinese  questionnaire  was  then  used  for  the  main  data collection.

To ensure balanced regional representation, firms were selected from Zhangzhou, Guangzhou, and Xi ' an. Data collection took place between July and December 2024. These cities were chosen for their accessibility and  industrial  significance,  as  they  host  major  manufacturing  zones actively leveraging AI technologies. Notably, Xi ' an and Guangzhou are recognized  as  progressive  economic  hubs  with  strong  technological foundations, enabling us to capture insights from organizations at the forefront of AI adoption.

Using business directories from each city, we identified 1000 firms. Five  trained  interviewers  contacted  these  firms ' directors  and  senior managers, explained the study ' s  objectives  and  confidentiality  assurances, and secured participation from 350 firms. To maximize response quality,  we  specifically  targeted  top  executives  and  senior  managers involved  in  decision-making.  Personalized  invitation  letters  further encouraged participation. The face-to-face approach allowed us to assess respondents ' suitability, increase response rates, and provide clarifications  when  needed.  To  mitigate  common  method  bias,  data  were collected  from  multiple  informants  (directors  and  senior  managers) within each firm.

In  total,  419  valid  questionnaires  were  obtained  from  290  firms, yielding  a  response  rate  of  38%,  which  was  deemed  satisfactory  for analysis.  The  sample  firms  represented  diverse  sectors  within  the manufacturing  industry,  including  automotive,  electronics,  textiles, machinery, chemicals, and IT. Respondent demographics are summarized in Appendix A.

## 3.2. Measurements of variables

All the items were adapted from previous literature. We used a fivepoint  Likert  scale  ranging  from  1  (strongly  disagree)  to  5  (strongly agree). The questionnaire included three sections with 41 items.

## 3.2.1. Independent variable

Our measures of AI were divided into two categories: SPAI and GPAI, newly adapted from Al-Rousan, Ayasrah, Salih Yahya, and Khasawneh (2025),  Holzinger,  Langs,  Denk,  Zatloukal,  and  Müller  (2019),  and Ro ˇ zman, Tominc, and ˇ Strukelj (2023). SPAI refers to AI systems specifically designed for specific functions, such as customer support and medical diagnosis are measured by task accuracy, user satisfaction, and speed. This construct was measured using six items. On the other hand, GPAI can achieve a wide choice of tasks across different fields, and is evaluated based on reasoning ability, adaptability, and performance on standard benchmarks. This construct was measured using six items.

## 3.2.2. Mediating variables

We  measured  team  dynamics  with  eight  items  adapted  from Croucher et al. (2019), which refers to the interplay of communication, collaboration, and problem-solving within groups, particularly as they adapt to AI technologies. AI tools such as task management platforms or collaborative software like Slack and Microsoft Teams help streamline communication and foster transparency.

We  measured  CIIC  using  eight  items  adapted  from  Elenkov  and Manev (2005) and Lin, Chen, and Kuan-Shun Chiu (2010). CIIC captures an organization ' s ability to leverage AI for creating new products, services,  and  processes.  AI  drives  innovation  by  accelerating  product development cycles, enabling real-time market testing, and fostering a culture of experimentation.

HCW  are  measured  using  eight  items  adapted  from  Chang,  Jia, Takeuchi, and Cai (2014) and Riaz (2019), which refers to employees ' motivation and engagement with AI technologies to enhance organizational performance. This includes their willingness to learn and adopt AI tools, as well as their perception of the benefits AI brings to their roles and career development. AI can boost workforce morale by automating ordinary tasks, allowing employees to focus on higher-value activities.

## 3.2.3. Dependent variable

We measured organizational performance using seven items adapted from Boeker and Goodstein (1991) and Le and Ngoc-Khuong (2025). Organizational performance refers to the impact of AI on an organization ' s productivity, efficiency, financial outcomes, and customer satisfaction. AI technologies improve operational efficiency by automating processes,  enhancing  decision-making,  and  driving  innovation. ## 4.3. Structural equation modelling

This study examines both direct and indirect effects using structural equation  modeling (SEM). SEM is a powerful multivariate technique that  integrates  factor  analysis  and  regression  to  investigate  the  relationships between observed variables and latent constructs, such as attitudes  and  perceptions,  that  cannot  be  measured  directly  (Bollen, 1989).  The  SEM  method  requires  sufficiently  large  sample  sizes  to ensure reliable estimates, as well as adherence to key assumptions such as multivariate normality and the absence of multicollinearity. Due to its ability to rigorously evaluate theoretical models and capture complex interactions,  SEM  has  become  widely  applied  in  disciplines  such  as marketing, management, psychology, and the social sciences (Sarstedt, Ringle, Smith, Reams, & Hair Jr, 2014). In this study, we employ two approaches: one combining AI constructs and another separating them into service-purpose AI and general-purpose AI.

## 5.2. Theoretical contribution

This  study  contributes  to  the  business  strategy  and  technology literature  in  three  distinct  ways.  First,  this  study  makes  a  significant contribution to IDT by highlighting that the adoption of AI is not merely a  technological  process  but  a  complex  organizational  phenomenon shaped by social and managerial mechanisms (Al-Husain et al., 2025; Hartley, Sawaya, & Dobrzykowski, 2022). Specifically, we demonstrate that team dynamics, high-commitment work systems, and CIIC serve as key mediators that translate AI adoption into tangible organizational performance  outcomes.  By  integrating  these  organizational  enablers, the  study  extends  IDT  beyond  the  traditional  focus  on  technology characteristics, emphasizing that the effectiveness of innovation diffusion depends critically on how firms mobilize human, relational, and structural resources. This perspective provides a more nuanced understanding of AI diffusion, showing that successful adoption is contingent on the alignment between advanced technologies and internal organizational capabilities, thus offering both theoretical and practical insights for managers seeking to maximize AI-driven value creation.

Second, it theoretically and empirically distinguishes between SPAI and GPAI and examines their differential effects on organizational performance. While much of the existing research conceptualizes AI as a single, unified construct (Zhang, Zhang, Daim, & Yalçın, 2025). It often overlooks the nuanced differences between broadly applicable, versatile GPAI systems and task- or SPAI capabilities. By addressing this gap, our study advances the literature by showing that AI ' s impact on organizational performance is not uniform but depends on the type of capability  deployed.  This  distinction  is  important  because  GPAI  supports broader  organizational  adaptability,  cross-functional  knowledge  integration,  and  strategic  innovation,  whereas  SPAI  is  more  effective  in improving  operational  efficiency,  automating  specific  services,  and addressing domain-specific challenges. By offering this refined conceptualization, our work enriches technology management scholarship by providing a more granular understanding of how different AI capabilities create organizational value.

Third, this study advances the human capital and innovation literature by uncovering the mediating role of team dynamics in the relationship between AI adoption and organizational performance, framed through the lens of IDT. Prior research has highlighted the importance of team dynamics in contexts such as project management (O ' Connor & Basri, 2012), organizational support (Gelbard & Carmeli, 2009), team creativity (Furukawa, 2016), and organizational performance (Elyousfi, Anand, & Dalmasso, 2021). Little attention has been paid to how team interactions facilitate the effective diffusion and utilization of AI technologies  within  organizations.  Drawing  on  IDT,  we  argue  that  AI adoption alone is insufficient to improve organizational performance; instead, performance gains depend on team-level processes that enable the assimilation, sharing, and application of AI-enabled knowledge. By empirically demonstrating this mediating mechanism, our study contributes to the literature by linking technological innovation to human and social capital processes, showing that team dynamics are a critical channel through which AI adoption translates into tangible performance outcomes. Furthermore, we differentiate the effects of SPAI and GPAI, revealing that while GPAI requires more cross-functional collaboration and knowledge integration, SPAI ' s  performance  benefits  are  realized primarily within domain-specific teams, highlighting nuanced pathways through which different AI types influence organizational outcomes.

Fourth, this study contributes to the literature by demonstrating that CIIC mediates the relationship between AI adoption and organizational performance, also drawing on IDT. While prior research acknowledges the role of innovation capabilities in driving firm competitiveness (Yuen & Lam, 2024). Little is known about how these capabilities translate AI investments  into  tangible  outcomes.  Our  findings  indicate  that  AI, whether considered broadly or in terms of SPAI and GPAI, enhances organizational performance most effectively when firms possess strong CIIC  that  enable  the  assimilation,  adaptation,  and  diffusion  of  new technologies across processes and functions. Specifically, GPAI drives value by supporting firm-wide innovation integration and knowledge sharing, whereas SPAI contributes to incremental innovations and efficiency gains in targeted functional areas. This insight deepens our understanding of IDT, showing that AI adoption alone does not guarantee superior performance; rather, it is the organization ' s innovation capabilities  that  determine  whether  AI  knowledge  is  successfully  transformed into innovative solutions and measurable performance improvements.

Finally, this study advances the literature by highlighting the role of a HCW as a mediating mechanism between AI adoption and organizational  performance,  providing  new  insights  through  the  lens  of  IDT. While existing research acknowledges the importance of human capital in  leveraging  technological  change  (Bambi & Pea-Assounga,  2025). Limited attention has been paid to how workforce commitment enables organizations to fully exploit AI ' s potential. Building on IDT, we show that the diffusion of AI-driven practices depends not only on technological availability but also on employees ' willingness, adaptability, and engagement in integrating AI into daily routines. Our findings reveal that  AI  adoption  enhances  performance  outcomes  when  employees demonstrate strong commitment, adaptability, and initiative. This effect is  nuanced  across  AI  types:  GPAI  requires  employees  to  coordinate across  functions  and  think  strategically,  whereas  SPAI  relies  on  employees ' capability to optimize domain-specific processes. By identifying high-commitment employees as a critical mediating mechanism, this study extends human capital and technology management literatures and enriches IDT by emphasizing the central role of workforce dedication in translating AI capabilities into organizational performance.

## 5.3. Managerial implications

This  study  offers  key  managerial  implications  for  managers.  This study offers several key managerial implications for managers. First, it emphasizes the importance of distinguishing between SPAI and GPAI, as these technologies embody  different  characteristics and  generate distinct effects on firm performance. SPAI is typically designed to support specific organizational services or tasks, such as customer support, automated reporting, or process-specific decision-making. In contrast, GPAI is highly versatile and can integrate across multiple organizational functions, enabling broader strategic transformations such as enterprisewide  data  analytics,  innovation  in  business  models,  and  enhanced collaboration  across  ecosystems.  Managers  should  therefore  avoid  a ' one-size-fits-all ' approach to AI adoption and strategically align SPAI and GPAI investments with firm priorities, resources, and competitive positioning. Failing to recognize this distinction can lead to inefficient resource  allocation,  poor  technology -strategy  alignment,  and  missed opportunities for value creation. Second, managers must recognize team dynamics as a critical mediating mechanism in leveraging AI. Even the most sophisticated SPAI or GPAI tools cannot deliver meaningful performance gains if employees operate in silos or are resistant to incorporating AI into their workflows. Fostering strong collaboration, open communication, and shared learning practices enables teams to better absorb, adapt, and exploit AI-driven insights. thereby amplifying both operational efficiency and strategic impact.

Third, the study highlights CIIC as a crucial pathway through which AI  enhances  organizational  outcomes.  Firms  with  strong  R & D  investments, dynamic learning processes, and high absorptive capacity are better positioned to convert SPAI and GPAI applications into tangible innovations -whether  through  novel  products,  improved  services,  or more agile business processes. Without these capabilities, AI adoption risks remaining a superficial initiative with limited long-term impact. Finally,  the  role  of  high-commitment  employees emerges as vital for successful  AI  integration.  Workers  who  are  engaged,  adaptable,  and motivated to embrace technological change act as catalysts in bridging the gap between AI ' s potential and realized organizational performance. Their commitment reduces resistance, accelerates learning, and strengthens resilience during digital transformation, ensuring that SPAI and GPAI investments translate into sustainable value creation.

## 5.4. Limitations and future research directions

This study has several limitations that provide avenues for future research. First, the cross-sectional design and reliance on self-reported survey  data  may  introduce  common  method  bias  and  limit  causal inference. Although methodological safeguards were applied, longitudinal or multi-source data could provide a more robust understanding of the  causal  pathways  between AI adoption and organizational performance. Second, the empirical setting is restricted to Chinese manufacturing firms, which may constrain the generalizability of the findings  to  other  industries  or  emerging  economies  with  different institutional, cultural, and technological contexts. Future studies could extend  the  analysis  to  service  sectors  or  conduct  cross-country  comparisons to assess whether the observed relationships hold in diverse socio-economic environments.

Third, this study primarily examined the mediating roles of team dynamics, CIIC, and HCW in linking SPAI and GPAI with organizational performance.  While  these  factors  are  crucial,  future  research  should incorporate  additional  mediators  such  as  organizational  learning, employee creativity, and resource efficiency to capture a broader spectrum of mechanisms through which AI drives performance outcomes. Fourth,  future  studies  could  also  explore  potential  moderators  that condition  the  strength  of  these  relationships.  Institutional  support, regulatory enforcement, and R & D investment may significantly influence the effectiveness of AI adoption, shaping how firms leverage SPAI
