## 1. Introduction

The digital revolution is no longer a distant horizon but an immediate operational reality reshaping how firms generate, appropriate, and sustain value. Artificial intelligence (AI) now occupies the epicentre of this upheaval, serving simultaneously as an innovation enabler, a process optimiser, and a strategic decision partner across virtually every sector, from heavy manufacturing and logistics to financial services and creative industries (Bloom, Prettner, Saadaoui, & Veruete, 2025; Bonaparte, 2024; Xin, 2025; Yang, 2025). ## A B S T R A C T

The integration of artificial intelligence in corporate operations has emerged as a transformative force in business landscapes, yet existing research provides limited insights into the mechanisms through which AI creates enterprise value. Drawing on a comprehensive dataset of Chinese listed companies spanning 2010 -2022, this study investigates  how  AI  adoption  influences  enterprise  value  through  the  mediating  role  of  data  assets  and  the moderating effect of managerial capability. Our analysis reveals that AI adoption significantly enhances enterprise value both directly and indirectly through data assets, demonstrating substantial mediation effects. The findings establish that managerial capability positively moderates this relationship, indicating that firms with stronger management teams extract greater value from AI investments. Through extensive robustness checks and endogeneity  tests,  we  establish  the  causal  nature  of  these  relationships.  Three  heterogeneity  tests  reveal boundary  conditions:  the  valuation  premium  is  stronger  for  state-owned  enterprises,  disappears  in  heavily polluting industries unless AI is part of a credible green transition, and is evident only in non-asset-intensive firms  where  resource  reconfiguration  is  agile.  By  integrating  resource-based,  dynamic-capability,  and  stakeholder perspectives, the study clarifies when AI becomes a genuine source of competitive advantage. The results inform executives that data governance, leadership development, and asset or environmental restructuring must accompany AI budgets, and they guide policymakers toward incentive designs that couple digital adoption with sustainability and capital-efficiency objectives.

The evolution of AI applications has transcended traditional operational boundaries, catalyzing unprecedented changes in organizational structures and business models (Dong, Xin, Liu, & Yu, 2024). As Singh (2021) observes, AI ' s capacity for superhuman performance in specific domains has revolutionized sectors ranging from healthcare to manufacturing. This technological advancement has given rise to novel business  architectures,  particularly  in  platform-based  models  and service-oriented  frameworks,  necessitating  a  deeper  examination  of their value-creation mechanisms (Lee, Suh, Roy, & Baucus, 2019).

A critical yet underexplored aspect of AI adoption lies in its intersection  with  organizational  resources  and  capabilities.  Our  research posits that the relationship between AI adoption and enterprise value is inherently mediated by an organization ' s data assets and moderated by managerial capabilities (Dai, 2025a; Li, Zhong, & Tong, 2024; WambaTaguimdje, Wamba, Kamdjoug, & Wanko, 2020). This perspective addresses  a  significant  gap  in  current  understanding,  as  highlighted  by recent studies (Enholm, Papagiannidis, Mikalef, & Krogstie, 2022; Perifanis & Kitsios, 2023).

The synthesis of AI capabilities with organizational resources presents unique challenges and opportunities. Evidence suggests that organizations  excelling in data  resource  utilization while  fostering innovation-oriented  leadership  demonstrate  superior  outcomes  in  AI adoption (Enholm et al., 2022; Wamba-Taguimdje et al., 2020). The emergence  of  generative  AI  technologies  has  further  amplified  the importance of sophisticated decision-making frameworks, underlining the  critical  role  of  management  expertise  in  technology  deployment (Jiang, Xuan, & Zhang, 2024).

To empirically examine these relationships, we employ a methodologically rigorous approach utilizing a comprehensive dataset of Chinese listed companies spanning 2010 -2022. Our study introduces an innovative measurement framework that captures the multifaceted nature of organizational AI adoption. The primary measurement leverages natural language processing techniques, specifically a Word2vec-based dictionary methodology, to quantify AI adoption levels from corporate disclosures. This approach processes corporate annual reports through a Skip-gram model, identifying and weighting 73 AI-related terms based on  semantic  relevance.  To  ensure  measurement  robustness,  we  complement this with a patent-based approach that tracks firms ' AI-related intellectual  property  development  through  the  Chinese  Patent  Office database. This dual measurement strategy enables us to capture both the strategic  emphasis  and  technological  advancement  dimensions  of corporate  AI  initiatives  (Li  et  al.,  2024).  Drawing  from  the  CNRDS Platform,  we  construct  comprehensive  measures  for  our  mediating variable (data assets) and moderating variable (managerial capability), alongside  a  robust  set  of  firm-level  controls  to  account  for  potential confounding effects.

This research advances the theoretical discourse on AI-driven value creation  through  several  key  contributions.  First,  we  illuminate  the specific  pathways  through  which  AI  adoption  influences  enterprise value.  Second,  we  demonstrate  how  data  assets  serve  as  crucial  intermediaries in translating AI investments into tangible outcomes (Lui, Lee, & Ngai, 2022; Naqvi, 2021). Third, we establish the pivotal role of managerial capability in optimizing AI deployment effectiveness (AbouFoul, Ruiz-Alba, & L ´ opez-Tenorio, 2023).

Our  findings  yield  significant  implications  for  practitioners  and policymakers alike. For corporate leaders, we provide evidence-based guidance for optimizing AI investments through enhanced data management  and  leadership  development.  For  policymakers,  our  results inform  the  development  of  frameworks  that  foster  innovation  while addressing the ethical considerations inherent in AI adoption (Zhang, Song, & Wang, 2023). The complexities of AI integration in organizational contexts demand a nuanced understanding of its impact on performance and value creation. As enterprises navigate this technological frontier, our research provides crucial insights for developing sustainable competitive advantages while addressing the broader implications of AI adoption in the business landscape (Jiao, Lu, Ren, & Wei, 2024).

## 2. Literature review and hypothesis development

## 2.1. AI and enterprise value creation

The integration of artificial intelligence (AI) into business operations has emerged as a transformative force, fundamentally altering how organizations create and capture value. AI technologies enable firms to automate processes, enhance decision-making, and optimize resource allocation, thereby improving operational efficiency and effectiveness (Cao, 2021). The potential of AI to drive enterprise value creation is particularly evident in its ability to analyze vast amounts of data, uncover insights, and facilitate informed strategic decisions (Nurlia, Daud, & Rosadi, 2023). For instance, AI-driven analytics can identify market trends and consumer preferences, allowing organizations to tailor their offerings  and  enhance  customer  experiences  (Dai,  2025b;  Fischer, 2024).

Moreover,  the  co-creation  of  value  through  AI  is  increasingly recognized as a critical component of successful business strategies. By leveraging  AI  technologies,  firms  can  engage  customers  in  the  value creation  process,  fostering  collaboration  and  enhancing  customer satisfaction  (Kaartemo & Helkkula,  2018).  This  shift  toward  a  more interactive and participatory approach to value creation aligns with the principles of service-dominant logic, which emphasizes the importance of relationships and interactions in generating value (Swan, Peltier, & Dahl, 2024). As organizations adopt AI, they not only improve their internal processes but also create opportunities for external stakeholders to participate in value co-creation, thereby enhancing overall enterprise performance (Baabdullah, 2024).

The relationship between AI adoption and enterprise value is further supported by empirical evidence demonstrating that organizations that effectively utilize AI technologies experience significant improvements in  performance  metrics  such  as  revenue  growth,  market  share,  and customer loyalty (Ghaffar, Arshad, Siddqiue, & Nasir, 2024). However, the realization of these benefits is contingent upon the organization ' s ability to integrate AI into its existing business models and processes, highlighting the need for strategic alignment and organizational readiness (Alami et al., 2021).

In summary, AI serves as a catalyst for enterprise value creation by enhancing  operational  efficiency,  enabling  data-driven  decision-making, and fostering collaborative value co-creation with customers and stakeholders.  As  organizations  continue  to  invest  in  AI  technologies, understanding the mechanisms through which AI contributes to value creation  becomes  increasingly  important  for  both  practitioners  and researchers.

## 2.2. The mediating role of data assets

Data assets play a pivotal role in the effective implementation of AI technologies and their subsequent impact on enterprise value (Eichler, Gr ¨ oger,  Hoos,  Schwarz, & Mitschang,  2022;  Jian,  Xiaofan,  Jinlin, & Yunqing,  2023).  The  ability  to  collect,  manage,  and  analyze  data  is essential  for  organizations  seeking  to  leverage  AI  for  competitive advantage (Kashyap, Morse, Patel, & Shah, 2021). Data serves as the foundation  upon  which  AI  algorithms  operate,  and  the  quality  and quantity of data directly influence the effectiveness of AI applications (Nurlia et al., 2023). Organizations that possess robust data assets are better positioned to extract actionable insights from AI systems, leading to improved decision-making and enhanced operational performance.

Furthermore, the integration of data assets with AI technologies facilitates the development of predictive models and advanced analytics, enabling organizations  to  anticipate  market  trends  and  customer  behaviors (Sun, Xu, Yu, & Wang, 2022). This predictive capability not only enhances strategic planning but also allows firms to respond proactively to  changes  in  the  business  environment,  thereby  creating  additional value (Cao, 2021). For example, companies that utilize AI-driven analytics to optimize supply chain management can reduce costs, improve inventory turnover, and enhance customer service levels (Ghaffar et al., 2024).

The mediating role of data assets in the AI-value creation relationship is supported by research indicating that organizations with strong data management capabilities are more likely to realize the full potential of their AI investments (Nurlia et al., 2023). By effectively leveraging data,  firms  can  enhance  their  AI  applications,  leading  to  improved performance outcomes and increased enterprise value. Therefore, understanding the interplay between AI adoption and data assets is crucial for organizations aiming to optimize their value creation strategies.

## 2.3. Managerial capability as a boundary condition

Managerial capability is a critical boundary condition that influences the  effectiveness  of  AI  adoption  and  its  impact  on  enterprise  value (Helfat & Martin,  2015).  The  successful  adoption  of  AI  technologies requires  not  only  technical  expertise  but  also  strong  leadership  and strategic vision (Huang, 2024). Managers play a vital role in guiding the organization through the complexities of AI integration, ensuring that AI initiatives align with overall business objectives and are supported by appropriate resources (Kashyap et al., 2021).

Research has shown that organizations with capable management teams are better equipped to navigate the challenges associated with AI adoption, including issues related to data governance, ethical considerations,  and  change  management  (Kaartemo & Helkkula,  2018). Effective managerial practices can facilitate a culture of innovation and adaptability, enabling organizations to respond to the dynamic nature of AI technologies and market demands (Swan et al., 2024). Moreover, managers  who  prioritize  employee  training  and  development  in  AIrelated  skills  can  enhance  workforce  readiness  and  engagement, further contributing to the successful implementation of AI initiatives (Nurlia et al., 2023).

The moderating effect of managerial capability on the AI-value creation relationship is underscored by studies indicating that organizations with strong leadership and strategic alignment are more likely to achieve positive outcomes from their AI investments (Weber, Engert, Schaffer, Weking, & Krcmar, 2023). By fostering an environment that encourages  collaboration,  experimentation,  and  continuous  learning, managers can enhance the effectiveness of AI technologies and maximize their potential for value creation.

## 2.4. Hypothesis development

Based on the theoretical foundations established in the preceding sections, we propose the following hypotheses to guide our empirical investigation:

Hypothesis 1 . AI adoption demonstrates a positive association with enterprise value.

We hypothesize that the implementation of AI technologies has a direct positive effect on enterprise value. Organizations that effectively integrate AI into their operations are expected to experience improvements in performance metrics such as revenue growth, operational efficiency, and customer satisfaction.

Hypothesis 2 . Data assets mediate the relationship between AI adoption and enterprise value.

We propose that data assets mediate the relationship between AI adoption and enterprise value. Specifically, organizations with strong data management capabilities will be better positioned to leverage AI technologies, leading to enhanced decision-making and improved performance outcomes.

Hypothesis 3 . Managerial capability moderates the relationship between AI adoption and enterprise value.

We hypothesize that managerial capability moderates the relationship between AI adoption and enterprise value. Organizations with capable management teams are expected to achieve greater benefits from AI investments, as effective leadership can facilitate the successful  integration  of  AI  technologies  and  promote  a  culture  of innovation. In summary, this theoretical review highlights the intricate relationships between AI adoption, data assets, managerial capability, and  enterprise  value  creation.  By  investigating  these  dynamics,  our research aims to contribute to a deeper understanding of how organizations can optimize their AI strategies to enhance value creation.

## 3. Research design

## 3.1. Data and sample selection

Our empirical investigation examines Chinese listed companies from 2010 to 2022, comprising 22,938 firm-year observations. This extensive timeframe captures both the emergence and maturation of AI adoption across Chinese enterprises. We retrieve data from the Chinese Research Data Services (CNRDS) Platform, which provides standardized financial and operational metrics. To ensure data quality, we exclude financial institutions due to their distinct regulatory environment, ST and *ST companies, firms with missing data for key variables, and observations with extreme values at the 1st and 99th percentiles.

## 3.2.2. Dependent variable: Enterprise value

The measurement of enterprise value through Tobin ' s Q represents a critical  component  of  our  research  design,  employing  two  distinct calculation methods to ensure comprehensive value capture. Our primary measure constructs Market Value A by combining A-shares market value, B-shares market value (converted to RMB), non-tradable shares value,  and  total  liabilities.  This  approach  effectively  addresses  the complexities of China ' s split-share structure while incorporating both equity and debt components of firm value. The inclusion of non-tradable shares, valued using the equity-to-registered capital ratio, reflects the unique ownership characteristics of Chinese listed companies and provides a more complete picture of corporate worth.

In parallel, we employ an alternative calculation focused on markettraded shares, where Market Value B emphasizes actively traded equity components while maintaining consistency in liability treatment. This complementary approach reduces potential upward bias that might arise from non-tradable share valuation, offering a more conservative estimate aligned with international valuation practices. The conversion of B-shares  to  RMB  using  appropriate  exchange  rates  ensures  currency consistency across both measurements.

The dual measurement strategy strengthens our analysis by addressing potential biases inherent in single valuation methods while acknowledging the institutional characteristics of China ' s market environment. This methodological choice enhances result reliability through measurement triangulation, particularly important when examining the relationship between AI adoption and enterprise value. Both calculations normalize market value by total assets, maintaining the theoretical foundation of Tobin ' s Q as a ratio of market value to replacement cost, thereby facilitating meaningful cross-sectional comparison among firms of varying sizes.

## 3.2.3. Mediating variable: Data assets

We  measure  data  assets  through  the  proportion  of  data-related keywords  in  annual  reports,  capturing  organizations ' emphasis  on data management and utilization. This measure reflects both the quantity and strategic importance of data resources within the organization.

## 3.2.4. Moderating variable: Managerial capability

Drawing from established literature, we construct a comprehensive measure  of  managerial  capability  incorporating  management  team characteristics, educational background, industry experience, and strategic decision-making effectiveness.

3.2.4.1. Control variables. Our empirical analysis incorporates a comprehensive  set  of  control  variables  to  account  for  firm-specific characteristics that might influence enterprise value. Firm size, measured as the natural logarithm of total assets, controls for scale effects  and  operational  complexity.  Debt-Asset  ratio  (total  liabilities divided by total assets) captures financial structure and risk exposure, while revenue growth rate reflects operational momentum and market opportunities. Financial leverage, calculated as the ratio of long-term debt  to  equity,  accounts  for  capital  structure  decisions  and  financial risk. Board size measures corporate governance effectiveness through the  number  of  directors,  complemented  by  ownership  concentration (shareholding percentage of top three shareholders) to reflect governance structure and monitoring intensity. The SA index serves as a proxy for  financial  constraints,  capturing  firms ' ability  to  access  external financing.  These  controls  collectively  account  for  fundamental  firm characteristics, governance mechanisms, and financial conditions that prior literature identifies as significant determinants of enterprise value, thereby isolating the unique effects of AI adoption on firm performance.

## 4.2. Addressing endogeneity issues

The endogeneity test in Table 4 employs a two-stage least squares (2SLS) regression to establish causality between AI adoption and enterprise value. The highly significant relationship between lagged and current AI adoption in the first stage ( t = 135.526) validates our choice of instrumental variable. This strong persistence suggests that AI adoption  decisions  reflect  structured,  long-term  strategic  planning  rather than opportunistic technological investments.

The  second-stage  results  retain  statistical  significance  while  controlling for potential reverse causality, strengthening the causal interpretation  of  AI ' s  impact  on  firm  value.  The  instrumental  variable approach reveals that after accounting for endogeneity concerns, the positive relationship between AI adoption and firm value remains robust ( t = 2.894). The reliability of these findings is supported by the model ' s substantial  explanatory  power,  with  R-squared  values  of  0.782  and 0.376 for the first and second stages respectively.

Interestingly, the control variables display distinct patterns across the two stages. While firm size positively influences AI adoption in the first stage ( t = 5.907), it maintains a negative relationship with firm value in the second stage ( t = 36.604). This contrasting effect suggests that larger firms are more likely to adopt AI technologies, yet size itself may  not  translate  directly  into  higher  market  valuations.  Similarly, growth opportunities show positive associations in both stages but with varying magnitudes, indicating that high-growth firms may leverage AI adoption more effectively for value creation.

## 4.3. Robustness checks

The robustness tests employ four alternative approaches to validate our main findings. Model 1 uses an alternative measure of AI adoption based on patent counts (AI_replace), maintaining statistical significance ( t = 3.351) while demonstrating a positive relationship with firm value. Model  2  employs  an  alternative  calculation  of  enterprise  value (FV_replace), revealing a stronger positive association with AI adoption ( t = 4.136). The increased R-squared (0.419) suggests that this alternative value measure might capture additional aspects of firm performance.  Control  variables  show  notably  different  magnitudes  in  this specification, particularly for ownership concentration (lnTop3) which shifts to a strong positive association ( t = 17.597).

Model 3 introduces firm fixed effects while excluding industry fixed effects, demonstrating that the positive relationship between AI adoption  and firm  value persists ( t = 3.216). Interestingly, the debt-asset ratio  (DAR)  shows  a  positive  relationship  in  this  specification,  contrasting with its negative effect in other models, suggesting that withinfirm variation in leverage might have different implications than crosssectional differences.

Model 4 excludes COVID-19 period observations, focusing on the pre-pandemic  sample (15,623 observations).  The  stronger  coefficient significance for AI adoption ( t = 5.801) indicates that the relationship might have been partially masked by pandemic-related market disruptions. The model ' s higher R-squared (0.418) suggests better explanatory power for the pre-pandemic period.

These varied specifications collectively reinforce the robustness of our main findings, demonstrating that the positive relationship between AI adoption and enterprise value remains stable across different variable measurements, model specifications, and sample periods.

## 4.6.2. Pollution-intensity heterogeneity

Partitioning  the  sample  by  pollution  intensity  shows  that  stock markets  reward  artificial-intelligence  spending  only  when  environmental liabilities are modest. In non-heavily polluting firms the AI coefficient is 0.027 ( t = 3.732, p < 0.01), indicating that every marginal rise in the AI-adoption index translates into a clear premium in Tobin ' s Q after controlling for size, leverage, growth, and governance characteristics. By contrast, the coefficient falls to 0.023 and loses statistical significance in heavily polluting enterprises ( t = 1.307). Investors thus appear reluctant to capitalise digital-transformation stories when future cash flows are clouded by stricter emission standards, possible carbonpricing liabilities, and higher capex needs for end-of-pipe technologies.

The pattern of the controls supports this reading. Leverage exerts a softer discount among polluters ( 0.301 versus 0.481), hinting that creditors  already  price  in  regulatory  backing  or  implicit  guarantees, whereas equity holders remain wary of residual risk. Board size is positive and significant only in the high-pollution group (0.152), suggesting that  a  larger  director  pool  adds  monitoring  value  when  firms  face complex compliance landscapes. Ownership concentration turns negative  ( 0.092) for these firms,  consistent  with  the  idea  that  minority investors  demand  greater  voice  to  safeguard  against  environmental contingencies.

Growth prospects carry a slightly larger premium among polluters (0.238  versus  0.202),  implying  that  the  market  welcomes  signals  of future revenue expansion that might offset remediation costs. Still, the muted  AI  coefficient  implies  that  digital  technologies  alone  do  not reassure investors unless accompanied by credible environmental transition strategies.

The R-squared climbs marginally from 0.351 to 0.377 when moving from clean to polluting industries, showing that our covariates explain more  of  the  valuation  variance  where  regulatory  exposure  is  pronounced.  Overall,  the  evidence  suggests  that  environmental  context conditions the financial return to AI: in cleaner sectors digital tools are seen as productivity enhancers, whereas in pollution-intensive settings their value story is overshadowed by sustainability risk.

By contrast, asset-intensive enterprises are locked into lengthy depreciation cycles and rigid production  systems,  slowing  the  sensing-and-reconfiguring  routines through which digital investments normally enhance competitiveness.

## 5. Conclusion

## 5.1. Key research findings

This study employed a 2010 -2022 panel of 22,938 firm -year observations  from  Chinese  listed  companies  to  untangle  how  artificialintelligence initiatives influence corporate market value. Two complementary measurement strategies -a Word2vec-based disclosure index and  counts  of  AI-related  patent  grants -captured  both  the  strategic salience and the technological depth of each firm ' s digital commitment. After controlling for firm size, leverage, governance, industry, and year effects,  AI  adoption  displayed  a  statistically  and  economically  meaningful association with higher Tobin ' s Q. Almost one-half of this valuation premium is channelled through the build-up and exploitation of proprietary data assets, underscoring that algorithmic capabilities alone are insufficient: only when accompanied by data stewardship do they translate into credible earnings prospects.

Managerial capability strengthens this process. Firms whose leadership  teams  possess  deep  analytical  expertise  and  strategic  agility convert  AI  expenditure  into  shareholder  value  more  efficiently  than peers, corroborating the dynamic-capabilities proposition that sensing, seizing, and reconfiguring routines are human-centred. Heterogeneity analyses  sharpen  the  insight.  First,  state-owned  enterprises  (SOEs) capture a larger AI-related valuation gain than non-SOEs, suggesting that  institutional  sponsorship  and  preferential  financing  lower  the perceived risk of digital transformation. Second, AI elevates firm value in  non-heavily  polluting  sectors  but  not  in  high-pollution  industries, indicating  that  environmental  liabilities  can  offset  the  expected  efficiency gains unless digital projects are explicitly tied to sustainability outcomes. Third, the technology boosts market value only in non-assetintensive firms; in asset-heavy contexts, long depreciation cycles and rigid capital structures slow the reconfiguration of resources, muting the impact  of  predictive  analytics  and  automation.  Collectively,  these findings extend resource-based, stakeholder, and institutional perspectives  by showing that digital capability yields competitive advantage only when either structural flexibility is high or state backing cushions adjustment costs.

## 5.2. Managerial and policy implications

For  corporate  executives  and  boards.  AI  expenditures  should  be embedded in an integrated change programme that also fortifies data governance, upgrades leadership skills, and -where physical or environmental lock-ins are severe -redesigns core assets. In light-asset, lowpollution  businesses,  rolling  out  machine-learning  systems  can  be  a stand-alone efficiency lever. By contrast, capital-intensive or emissionprone firms need to pair AI with modularisation of equipment, digitaltwin architectures for plant optimisation, or circular-economy product redesign to convince investors that digital tools will affect long-run cash flows.  Boards  should  demand  evidence  that  AI  projects  advance enterprise-wide  dynamic  capabilities  rather  than  remain  confined  to isolated pilot teams.

For  investors.  The  results  highlight  the  value  of  a  conditional screening  approach.  The  same  AI  disclosure  can  imply  markedly different  cash-flow paths depending on a firm ' s  ownership structure, pollution exposure, and capital rigidity. Portfolio managers may refine ESG and digital-transformation ratings by weighting AI indicators more heavily in flexible or state-supported settings while discounting them in asset-locked  or  high-emission  contexts  unless  coupled  with  credible transition plans.

For policymakers. A one-size-fits-all subsidy for AI adoption risks misallocation.  Light-asset  sectors  may  respond  adequately  to  generic R & D tax credits or procurement preferences, whereas capital-heavy or pollution-intensive  industries  require  blended  instruments -such  as concessional finance for green retrofits, performance-based carbon allowances,  and data-infrastructure  grants -that  jointly  lower  environmental  and  digital  barriers.  Enhanced  disclosure  guidelines  for  AIenabled sustainability metrics would also help capital markets differentiate between superficial automation spending and genuine capability upgrades.

## 5.3. Study limitations

Several caveats temper the generalisability of our conclusions. First, the institutional environment of China -characterised by an active industrial  policy,  bank-dominated  finance,  and  frequent  state  shareholding -may  amplify  the  valuation  impact  of  AI  relative  to  other jurisdictions. Second, although the study deployed  instrumentalvariable, propensity-score-matching, and Heckman corrections, unobserved factors such as organizational culture or unreported pilot failures could still confound the results. Third, the AI index blends text analytics and patent counts; it may overstate dormant patents or under-capture operational machine-learning systems that firms choose not to patent. Fourth,  the  analysis  relies  on  market-based  value  metrics;  future operating-performance  gains  or  losses  remain  to  be  tracked.  Finally, annual data obscure the timing of investment waves and the gestation lags typical of large-scale digital programmes.

## 5.4. Directions for future research

Cross-country  comparative  studies  could  reveal  how  legal  origin, capital-market  maturity,  and  data-governance  regimes  moderate  the AI -value linkage. Fine-grained, project-level expenditure logs combined with  milestone  completion  data  would  allow  researchers  to  separate immediate announcement effects from longer-run cash-flow realization.

Longitudinal case studies in asset-heavy, pollution-intensive firms could unpack micro-processes by which digital twins, robotics, and predictive maintenance  interact  with  plant  modernisation  and  decarbonisation journeys.  Future  work  might  also  explore  nonlinear  or  threshold effects -whether a critical mass of AI spending must be reached before markets reassess a firm ' s growth trajectory, or whether excess investment  triggers  diminishing  returns.  Finally,  integrating  labour-force microdata would illuminate how AI-driven productivity interacts with wage structures, skill upgrading, and internal career mobility, offering a more  holistic  view  of  value  creation  that  spans  capital  and  human resources.
