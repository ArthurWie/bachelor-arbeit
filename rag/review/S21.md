## 1. Introduction

The rise of digitization, characterized by the profound use of data analytics and artificial intelligence (AI), has compelled organizations to adopt a new perspective on their IT assets and focus on a more intricate level (Benner & Tushman, 2015). Typically defined as ' the ability of machines to perform human-like cognitive tasks, ' AI is currently viewed as the most important and disruptive new technology for organizations (Benbya et al., 2020, p.  ix).  However,  the  management of AI differs significantly  from  previous  approaches  to  IT  management  (Berente et al., 2021). AI cannot be viewed merely as a technology or a collection of  technologies; rather, it represents an ever-evolving system encompassing  emerging  computational  capabilities  (Berente  et  al.,  2021). Thus, today ' s leaders must navigate both the opportunities and the obstacles that come with the pervasive adoption of AI.

In  this  study,  we  explore  how  AI-powered  capabilities  can  create value  for  organizations,  leading  to  enhanced  firm  performance  and innovation. These capabilities are crucial in establishing firms ' ability to swiftly  identify  and  respond  to  market  changes,  such  as  shifts  in customer preferences or new market trends. We refer to this concept as ' Adaptive Response to Market Changes ' (ARMC). Our emphasis is on adopting a core competencies perspective (Ravichandran et al., 2005) to effectively reflect how firms respond to market changes in two key areas: customer responsiveness and operational adjustment.

Furthermore, environmental conditions influence firms ' capabilitybuilding  processes  and  responses  to  market  demands  (Lee  et  al., 2015). However, limited research has explored how these conditions affect firms ' responses to market changes concerning AI even though they play a crucial role in shaping managerial decision-making (Pavlou & El Sawy, 2011; Wilden & Gudergan, 2015). While strategic decisions in  product  or  market  adaptation  can  be  unpredictable  in  rapidly changing  environments  (Eisenhardt & Martin,  2000;  Mitchell  et  al., 2011), our study proposes that the effects of AI-powered capabilities on ARMC depend on specific environmental conditions. Specifically, we theorize that two environmental conditions -environmental hostility and environmental dynamism -affect the degree to which AI-powered capabilities drive firms ' responses to market changes.

Focusing on this objective, we will elucidate how the interplay between AI-powered capabilities and environmental conditions can result in different levels of ARMC, which directly affects firm performance, process innovation, and product innovation. Our study seeks a deeper understanding of the circumstances under which AI-powered capabilities will likely yield benefits or drawbacks for organizations. Moreover, our  investigation  into  how  AI-powered  capabilities  facilitate  ARMC contributes  to  AI  research  beyond  its  purely  technical  aspects  (e.g., Asatiani et al., 2020). Thus, the empirical insights from this study offer practical  guidance  to  organizations,  enabling  them  to  anticipate  and prepare for the economic impacts of AI implementation.

## 2. Theoretical background

## 2.1. An overview of dynamic capabilities

Dynamic capabilities are defined as ' a  firm ' s  ability  to  integrate, build, and reconfigure internal and external competencies to address rapidly changing environments ' (Teece et al., 1997, p. 516). Dynamic capabilities are distinct from operational capabilities because they serve different  organizational  functions.  While  operational  capabilities  are essential for companies ' smooth functioning in day-to-day operations, dynamic capabilities help organizations achieve and sustain a competitive  advantage  (Lin & Kunnathur,  2019;  Teece,  2007;  Teece  et  al., 1997). Firms demonstrate dynamism in capabilities by adapting strategies to changing market conditions and creatively combining existing resources in innovative and unconventional ways (Morgan et al., 2009).

Dynamic capabilities constitute firms ' ability to (1) sense and shape opportunities and threats, (2) respond to market opportunities, and (3) maintain  competitiveness  through  enhancing,  aligning,  and  reconfiguring  firms ' intangible  and  tangible  resources  (Teece  et  al.,  1997). Although  the  primary  idea  of  dynamic  capabilities  centers  on  these sensing,  seizing,  and  reconfiguring  activities  (Teece,  2007),  scholars have proposed several other dynamic capabilities, including dynamic managerial capabilities (Helfat & Peteraf, 2015), organizational agility (Chakravarty et al., 2013; Nazir & Pinsonneault, 2012; Sambamurthy et al., 2003), market orientation (Morgan et al., 2009), and new product development (Pavlou & El Sawy, 2006).

In the context of ARMC, organizational agility emerges as the most closely  related  concept,  encompassing  companies ' ability  to  flexibly adapt to shifting market conditions and seize opportunities for sustainable growth and competitiveness. In their recent work, Kalaignanam et al. (2021) explored the commonalities and distinctions among dynamic capabilities, agility, and associated concepts. According to their findings, dynamic capabilities encompass sensemaking , which involves firms ' ability to respond to unexpected events by discerning the events unfolding and striving to establish a more structured environment, and speed ,  which represents the swiftness with which firms sense changes and initiate responses. However, dynamic capabilities lack the element of iteration. In contrast, agility includes the critical aspect of iteration , entailing the iterative refinement of decisions before their relaunch or scaling to minimize uncertainty. Thus, while agility and dynamic capabilities  overlap,  they  also  have  distinct  features,  such  as  agility emphasizing the iterative nature of decision-making to reduce uncertainty.

The dynamic capabilities framework also recognizes the importance of agility. However, it posits that the continual pursuit of agility may not always be necessary since doing so could lead to a loss of efficiency and additional  costs.  Instead,  agility  becomes  imperative  primarily  when market changes are linked to uncertainty or risks (Teece et al., 2016). Put differently, firms may not require constant agility except in situations where uncertainty is present. Consequently, when firms encounter uncertainty, they can enhance their agility by leveraging dynamic capabilities. Next, we explain ARMC from the agility and dynamic capabilities framework.

## 2.2. Adaptive response to market changes (ARMC)

We  derive  the  concept  of  ARMC  from  the  organizational  agility perspective. Scholars have offered various definitions of agility depending  on  the  context.  For  example,  in  the  marketing  field, Kalaignanam  et  al.  (2021)  conceptualized  marketing  agility  as ' the extent to which an entity rapidly iterates between making sense of the market and executing marketing decisions to adapt to the market ' (p. 36). Meanwhile, in the information systems (IS) field, Tallon and Pinsonneault (2011) conceptualized organizational agility as ' the ability to detect and respond to opportunities and threats with ease, speed, and dexterity ' (p. 464). Sambamurthy et al. (2003) also proposed that agility comprises  three  interrelated  capabilities:  customer,  partnering,  and operational agility. Moreover, Lu and Ramamurthy (2011) conceptualized  two  dimensions  of  organization  agility:  market  capitalizing  and operational adjustment agility.

Organizational  agility,  as  highlighted  by  Lu  and  Ramamurthy (2011), represents a comprehensive firm-wide capability to effectively navigate  through  unexpected  changes  in  dynamic  business  environments. Given the breadth of this concept, encompassing all its elements within a single study is challenging. Nonetheless, in our research, we focus  on  two  crucial  elements  related  to  organizational  agility  that ARMC 1 captures: customer  responsiveness and operational  adjustment / adaptiveness dimensions. 2 These two dimensions specifically focus on the two  core  competencies  found  within  organizations,  namely  market- Y. Market-access  competencies  encompass  all  activities  that  enable firms to connect with their customers and effectively identify their needs (Ravichandran et al., 2005). In the context of ARMC, customer responsiveness  represents  a  crucial  aspect  of  market-access  competencies, encompassing companies ' ability to actively engage with customers to foster  collaboration,  explore  innovative  opportunities,  and  develop competitive  strategies  in  response  to  market  changes  (Sambamurthy et  al.,  2003).  For  instance,  customer  responsiveness  could  include adapting  to  shifts  in  aggregate  customer  demand  and  customizing products  or  services  to  cater  to  individual  customer  preferences. Operational-related competencies encompass all internal business process activities that enable firms to deliver distinctive and dependable products  while  enhancing  the  speed  and  precision  of  key  operations (Chen et al., 2017). Within these competencies, operational adjustment assumes significant importance. Operational adjustment refers to firms ' ability to promptly modify their internal business processes, facilitating effective adaptation to shifts in market demands (Lu & Ramamurthy, 2011). For instance, operational adjustments could involve expanding into a new market and adjusting the range of products or services offered for sale.

## 2.3. Artificial intelligence and AI-Powered capabilities

AI is ' the frontier of computational advancements that references human intelligence in addressing ever more complex decision-making problems ' (Berente  et  al.,  2021,  p.  1435).  Currently  viewed  as  the most important and disruptive new technology for organizations (Benbya  et  al.,  2021),  AI  is  revolutionizing  managerial  practices  for analyzing performance and developing novel insights (Kaplan & Haenlein, 2019). In addition, it includes statistical machine learning, neural networks, and  natural  language  processing  (NLP)  (Davenport & Mahidhar, 2018).

Technology is widely acknowledged as a fundamental resource that organizations  can  effectively  exploit,  deploy,  and  leverage  to  their advantage  (Manis & Madhavaram,  2023).  In  light  of  the  capabilitybuilding process, the significance of investments in IT lies in its successful integration into the core activities of organizations (Doherty & Terry, 2009; Ravichandran et al., 2005; Sambamurthy et al., 2003). In this context, Information Systems (IS) serves as a crucial mechanism in establishing coherence between IT activities and firm priorities, thereby facilitating the development of comprehensive capabilities that enable seamless  cross-functional  integration  and  coordination  (Chen  et  al., 2017; Ravichandran et al., 2005). Through this lens, our focus centers on three AI-powered capabilities that are related to three important business  needs:  automating  business  processes,  gaining  insights,  and engaging with customers (Davenport & Ronanki, 2018).

These  AI-powered  capabilities  include  (1) AI-enabled  automation capability ,  also  referred  to  as  mechanical  AI;  (2) AI-enabled  analytics capability , known as cognitive insights or thinking AI; and (3) AI-enabled relational capability , also known as feeling AI or cognitive engagement (Davenport & Ronanki, 2018; Huang et al., 2019; Huang & Rust, 2021a). Although  these  three  AI  capabilities  are  interrelated,  each  supports business functions differently (Huang & Rust, 2021b). Each AI-powered capability brings unique advantages: AI-enabled automation excels in standardization,  AI-enabled  analytics  capability  is  most  effective  for personalization,  and AI-enabled relational capability proves ideal  for fostering strong relationships (Huang & Rust, 2021a, 2021b). The definition of each AI-powered capability and examples of each are summarized in Table 1.

The first AI capability -AI-enabled automation capability -is used to automate repetitive and routine tasks (Huang & Rust, 2021b; Makarius et al., 2020). AI-enabled automation is often used to improve output consistency,  standardization,  reliability,  and  precision  (Huang  et  al., 2019).  The  second  capability -AI-enabled  analytics  capability -can be described as ' analytics on steroids ' (Davenport & Ronanki, 2018, p. 4). AI with this capability generally uses machine-learning algorithms to detect patterns and interpret their meanings in decision-making processes (e.g., predict what a particular segment of customers is likely to buy and analyze warranty data to identify the safety problems in automobiles and other manufactured products) (Huang et al., 2019). Unlike traditional analytics, AI-enabled analytics involves learning and adapting systematically from data (Huang et al., 2019). An AI system can process both unstructured and structured data, as well as information generated  by  the  system  itself  to  acquire  new  or  modify  existing knowledge (Paschen et al., 2020). Lastly, AI-enabled relational capability engages with human users using NLP chatbots, intelligent agents, and machine learning (Davenport & Ronanki, 2018). AI with a relational capability allows users to directly engage with a virtual agent rather than  with  human  customer-service  agents  (Davenport & Ronanki, 2018). Such AI can learn and adapt its behaviors by learning from its interactions with human users (Huang & Rust, 2021a).

## 3. Research model and hypotheses development

## 3.1. Research model

Fig.  1  presents  our  conceptual  model.  In  proposing  our  research model, we posit that the three AI-powered capabilities act as predictors of ARMC, which measures how quickly and easily a firm can respond to opportunities  within  two  areas:  customer  responsiveness  and  operational  adjustment.  According  to  the  underlying  theory  (e.g.,  Sambamurthy et al., 2003), the first-order dimensions of  ARMC  are complementary (i.e., they interact and co-vary with each other). Thus, following guidelines provided by Tanriverdi and Venkatraman (2005), we  conceptualize  ARMC  as  a  reflective,  second-order  construct  consisting  of  two  first-order  dimensions:  customer  responsiveness  and operational adjustment. Furthermore, considering that tasks can differ in  their  degree  of  automation,  analytics,  and  relational  intelligence capability (e.g., information acquisition often involves more mechanical processes,  whereas  information  processing  and  analysis  are  more analytics-driven) (Huang et al., 2019), we perceive the three AI-powered capabilities as interrelated yet distinct constructs. Thus, we conceptualize them as reflective, first-order constructs.

Furthermore, we consider the impact of environmental conditions as we  explore  the  relationships  between  AI-powered  capabilities  and ARMC. We hypothesize that the effects of AI-powered capabilities on ARMC may vary depending on the environmental conditions. Specifically,  we  aim  to  comprehend the influence of perceived hostile and dynamic  environmental  contexts  (Mitchell  et  al.,  2011).  Finally,  we Y. Sullivan and S. Fosso Wamba establish  a  connection  between  ARMC and  our  primary  outcomes  of interest: firm performance, process innovation, and product innovation. In our investigation, we adopt an agility perspective to view firm performance as an outcome of ARMC (Chen et al., 2014; Holmqvist & Pessi, 2006; Kathuria et al., 2018). Additionally, we employ the dynamic capabilities perspective, which suggests that firms with strong sensing and adaptive  response  abilities  can  drive  innovation  and  new  product development (Hoonsopon and Puriwat, 2019; Puriwat and Hoonsopon, 2021; Teece et al., 2016). Thus, we also measure process and product innovation as our dependent variables.

## 3.2. Hypotheses development

## 3.2.1. The relationships among AI-Powered capabilities

According to Huang and Rust ' s (2021a) perspective of multiple AIs, the  advancement  of  AI  occurs  cumulatively.  In  this  context,  AI  progresses through higher levels while retaining its lower-level capabilities. From this perspective, AI-enabled automation represents the lowest and simplest level, followed by AI-enabled analytics capability, and, finally, AI-enabled relational capability. Therefore, we assert that AI-enabled automation serves as the fundamental building block for firms ' analytics  and  relational  capabilities.  AI-enabled  automation  empowers firms  to  perform  repetitive  tasks,  albeit  with  limited  learning  and adaptive capabilities (Huang et al., 2019). Considering that this type of AI  is  the  most  cost-effective  and  easiest  to  implement  (Davenport & Ronanki, 2018), we can reasonably assume that firms would typically prioritize establishing this AI capability as their initial step. Once this capability has been established, they can utilize it to enhance AI-enabled analytics  capability  through  algorithms  and  models  as  a  means  of learning, as well as AI-enabled relational capability to establish meaningful relationships with users ((Huang & Rust, 2022)).

Further,  AI-enabled  analytics  capability  can  significantly  enhance firms ' relational  capability,  leading  to  improved  business  decisionmaking, especially in the context of future collaborations with human users  (Huang & Rust,  2022).  This  result  becomes  evident  when  AIpowered  analytics  efficiently  uncover  valuable  insights  and  personalize  user  interactions  (Davenport  et  al.,  2020).  Furthermore,  as  AI continually acquires new insights and learns through its algorithms, it becomes increasingly  adept  at  recognizing  and  responding  appropriately during human interactions (Huang et al., 2019). Thus, our first three hypotheses concern the relationships among AI-powered capabilities:

H1: AI-enabled automation capability is positively associated with AI-enabled analytics capability.

H2: AI-enabled analytics capability is positively associated with AIenabled relational capability.

H3: AI-enabled automation capability is positively associated with AI-enabled relational capability.

## 3.2.2. AI-enabled automation capability and ARMC

AI-enabled  automation  capability  plays  a  vital  role  in  enhancing ARMC by automating routine tasks and enabling team members to focus on more impactful high-level activities for the business (Raju & Koch, 2019). A notable example is Cr ´ edit Agricole Technologies et Services (CA -TS),  which employs Robotic Process Automation (RPA) to automate its project validation process, resulting in faster decision-making (Deloitte, 2020). AI-enabled automation not only breaks down organizational siloes by automating data sensing and collection processes, but also delivers standardized benefits through its consistency (e.g., using robots for packaging tasks) (Huang & Rust, 2021b). By eliminating unnecessary  traditional  data  collection  methods,  automation  capability helps firms respond to market changes by reducing redundancies and inefficiencies (Huang et al., 2019).

The strategic deployment of AI-enabled automation is also considered  a  contingency-driven  decision,  empowering  organizations  to respond to market changes by automating routine and time-consuming processes, thereby reducing production costs and risks associated with delivering products/services. Thus, AI-enabled automation can improve scalability (Raju & Koch, 2019). In turn, firms can efficiently redeploy/ redirect their resources to value creating and value capturing activities (Teece et al., 2016). Therefore, we hypothesize the following:

H4: AI-enabled automation is positively associated with ARMC.

## 3.2.3. AI-enabled analytics capability and ARMC

The relationship between AI-enabled analytics capability and ARMC can be explained through the lens of sensing (Teece et al., 2016). In environments characterized by uncertainty, companies must sense and generate  digital  options  for  growth  before  the  market  logic  of  these options becomes apparent to all (Teece et al., 2016). AI functionalities, such  as  predictive  analytics,  diverse  customer  data  analysis,  and personalized  content  creation,  provide  a  range  of  digital  options  for organizations.  These  options  enhance  process  efficiency,  knowledge alternatives, and rapid response to changes. For instance, from a marketaccess  perspective,  integrating  AI  into  products  and  services  allows companies to generate and test models swiftly, enhancing the ability to explore new markets, develop innovative products, and expedite product  development  (Davenport,  2018).  From  an  operational-related standpoint,  AI  analytics  capability  offers  novel  problem-solving  approaches and supports human decision-making by overcoming inherent information-processing  limitations  (Haefner  et  al.,  2021).  Thus,  AIenabled analytics capability is crucial in facilitating ARMC, leading to the  personalization  of  products  and  services  by  supporting  decision- Y. Sullivan and S. Fosso Wamba making processes throughout the entire value chain.

According to the dynamic capability perspective, undertaking actions to proactively create hypotheses about the future implications of observed  events  and  trends  can  help  organizations  sense  and  seize market opportunities (Teece et al., 2016). For example, firms can use machine learning to make predictions and assess their quality in practice (Albrecht et al., 2021; Capatina et al., 2020; Davenport et al., 2020). They can also track users ' actions and responses in the field, offering accurate suggestions that capture users ' increasing interest over time (Hollebeek et al., 2021). Since AI analytics tools provide data-driven knowledge (Kiron & Schrage, 2019), they offer accurate and reliable information, enabling firms to flexibly respond to market opportunities that might be overlooked by humans operating alone (Haefner et al., 2021). ## 3.2.4. AI-enabled relational capability and ARMC

AI-enabled relational capability goes beyond merely detecting patterns in data and providing insights for decision-making processes; it involves  using  data  to  train  AI  systems  that  can  interact  with  users without human intervention (Davenport & Ronanki, 2018). AI ' s relational capability is used to engage users in NLP chatbots, deep learning, and  machine  learning  (Duan  et  al.,  2019).  When  employed  in  user interaction, an AI application with a relational capability offers several business benefits: it facilitates firm-initiated communications, engages with customers in a friendly manner, is unaffected by frustration or fatigue like humans are, and can easily scale up to handle a large volume of  customer  communications  (Luo  et  al.,  2019).  These  advantages empower firms to listen attentively to users and address their concerns effectively.

The relationship between relational capability and ARMC can also be explained  from  the  data  network  effects  perspective  (Gregory  et  al., 2021). A platform exhibits data network effects if the more it learns from the data it collects on users, the more valuable the platform becomes to each  user.  For  example,  Uber  uses  machine-learning  algorithms  to analyze users ' data and improve its algorithmic matching, information, and experience offered to users who engage in exchanges among riders and drivers. Uber ' s goal is to inform and engage users with push notifications and messages that provide intelligent recommendations that adapt to changing contextual and situational circumstances (Gregory et al., 2021; Rosenblat, 2018). One of the strategic roles of AI in today ' s business is to continuously improve enterprises ' products and services for each user (Gregory et al., 2021), allowing firms to cope with rapid, relentless,  and  uncertain  changes.  Therefore,  we  hypothesize  the following:

H6:  AI-enabled  relational  capability  is  positively  associated  with ARMC.

## 3.2.5. The moderating role of environmental hostility and environmental dynamism

Prior  research  has  shown  that  external  environmental  factors  are important contextual variables in influencing decision-making processes and organizational agility  (Chatterjee  et  al.,  2023;  Helfat,  2022;  Lee et al., 2015; Mitchell et al., 2011; Teece et al., 2016). These factors can significantly impact companies ' strategic choices, resource allocation, and  overall  responsiveness  to  changing  market  conditions,  thereby playing a crucial role in shaping organizations ' ability to adapt to and thrive  in  a  dynamic  business  landscape  (Helfat  et  al.,  2007).  In  this study, we seek to understand the effect of two environmental contexts (environmental hostility and environmental dynamism) in moderating the relationship between AI-powered capabilities and ARMC.

3.2.5.1. Hostile environmental context. Environmental hostility ( ¨ Ozsomer et al., 1997) indicates ' unfavorable external forces for a firm ' s business ' (Zahra & Garvis, 2000, p. 475). These unfavorable environmental  conditions  can  result  from  radical  industry  changes,  intense regulatory  burdens  placed  on  the  industry,  or  fierce  rivalry  among competitors (Chen et al., 2014; Zahra & Garvis, 2000). Hostility also results from perceived competitive-, market-, and product-related uncertainties (Dess & Beard, 1984; Zahra & Garvis, 2000). Under hostile environmental conditions, market structures are fluid, clusters of knowhow  and  technologies  are  globally  dispersed,  and  innovations  are dependent on technology combinations (Teece, 2014). Thus, environmental hostility requires firms to break their routines and continuously learn new complexities and uncertainties, leading to increased chances of survival and success in such challenging settings (Teece et al., 2016). Not surprisingly, firms need a strategic direction to ensure survival in such environments (Slevin & Covin, 1997).

Given  that  AI-enabled  automation  is  primarily  used  to  automate routine tasks (Huang et al., 2019), maximize efficiency, and minimize variability (Huang & Rust, 2021a), firms relying on this capability while operating  in  highly  hostile  environments  cannot  quickly  respond  to market  changes.  While  AI-enabled  automation  enhances  operational efficiency and reduces human error (Huang & Rust, 2021a), its limitations lie in its inability to adapt quickly to dynamic and unpredictable market conditions (Tussyadiah, 2020). Thus,

H7a: Environmental hostility negatively moderates the relationship between AI-enabled automation capability and ARMC.

Hostile  environments  are  characterized  by  severe  competitive  intensity exacerbated by price wars and customers who exhibit little loyalty  to  their  suppliers  (Slevin & Covin,  1997). 3.2.5.2. Dynamic  environmental  context. A  dynamic  environment  is ' one with a highly unpredictable and unstable rate of change and high levels of uncertainty about the state of the context, the means-ends relations, and/or the outcomes of actions ' (Mitchell et al., 2011, pp. 687688). These changes include customer and technology changes (Im & Workman, 2004; Jaworski & Kohli, 1993; Kohli & Jaworski, 1990). For example, the changes brought about by the driving force of technology have created hypercompetitive environments (Kyl ¨ aheiko & Maijanen, 2020;  Teece,  2014).  In  such  environments,  customers ' demands and competitors ' actions are unpredictable, which requires frequent changes in how businesses operate (Allal-Ch ´ erif et al., 2021).

Given that AI-enabled automation learns and adapts to only a minimal  degree,  it  is  ideal  for  service  standardization  (Huang & Rust, 2021b).  For  instance,  in  a  low  level  of  dynamism  where  customers exhibit homogeneous demand or when companies handle routine tasks, AI-enabled  automation  offers  a  convenient  and  efficient  operational solution (Huang & Rust, 2021b). Under such dynamism, AI-enabled automation will be a bottleneck. Its rigid and standardized processes may not sufficiently cater to the diverse and fluctuating demands of customers or adapt to unexpected shifts in the competitive landscape. H8a: Environmental dynamism negatively moderates the relationship between AI-enabled automation capability and ARMC.

AI-enabled analytics capability allows firms to create new information and predictions from data, even when the data are unstructured (Shrestha et al., 2019). This capability is akin to the generative-sensing capability (Teece et al., 2016) that empowers firms to proactively sense and learn from their environment. By harnessing AI-enabled analytics, organizations can gain valuable insights from vast and complex datasets, enabling them to identify emerging trends, anticipate market shifts, and uncover  patterns  that  might  have  otherwise  remained  undetected (Huang & Rust, 2021b; Teece et al., 2016). ## 3.2.7. ARMC, process Innovation, and product innovation

In  organizational  settings,  innovation  is  commonly  defined  as creating  any  new  product,  service,  or  process  for  an  organization (Tushman & Nadler, 1986). A vast majority of research supports that firm innovation is a multidimensional phenomenon, and one way to conceptualize innovation as an organizational outcome is to separate product innovation from process innovation. Product innovation is ' the novelty and meaningfulness of new products introduced to the market [in] a timely fashion ' (Wang & Ahmed, 2004, p. 304). Product innovation  is  most  often  referred  to  as  perceived  newness,  novelty,  or uniqueness of products (Wang & Ahmed, 2004). It is also defined as any emerging technology or combination of emerging technologies that are exploited to produce goods or services (Bhoovaraghavan et al., 1996).

Process innovation is ' the introduction of new production methods, management approach, and technology that can be used to improve production and management processes ' (Wang & Ahmed, 2004, p. 305). Similarly, Trantopoulos et al. (2017) characterized process innovation as a novel and efficiency-improving endeavor to reduce the production costs  of  goods  or  services.  In  some  studies,  process  innovation  is considered a sub-element of technological innovation (Wang & Ahmed, 2004). While product innovation is centered on the market and primarily driven by customer needs, process innovation is internally oriented and primarily motivated by efficiency (Damanpour & Gopalakrishnan, 2001). Regardless of the differences between product and  process  innovation,  prior  research  has  suggested  that  they  are closely related (Oke et al., 2007).

Recently, innovation has emerged as a key outcome of organizational agility, as evidenced by studies conducted by Battleson et al. (2016), Hoonsopon & Puriwat  (2019),  and  Puriwat  and  Hoonsopon  (2021). Considering this development, we contend that examining the impact of ARMC on innovation becomes particularly relevant when AI-powered capabilities  are  involved.  AI  can  assist  the  innovation  process  by improving conditions for innovation and, in turn, facilitating the ideagenerating process and overcoming information-processing constraints (Haefner et al., 2021). For example, machine learning-based methods are used to predict the most promising materials to test, providing firms with the ability to adapt. The need to change and adapt triggers creativity and the ability to innovate (Georgsdottir & Getz, 2004). Thus, we argue that AI indirectly influences firms ' innovation through ARMC.

The relationship between ARMC and innovation can be explained from  several  perspectives.  First,  from  the  creativity  perspective,  the flexibility of the existing knowledge structure has proven to be a source of  new  ideas  (Georgsdottir & Getz,  2004). In the context of technology capability, Zhou and Wu (2010) noted that ' as a firm accumulates more technological capability, its absorptive capacity should increase, which leads to more product innovations ' (p. 549). Firms with high ARMC are better at reacting to changes in market demand (Tallon & Pinsonneault, 2011). ## 4. Research methodology

This study uses a two-stage survey to collect data from IT executives and business decision-makers (e.g., C-level executives, presidents, senior vice  presidents,  senior  directors,  directors,  and  managers  or  senior managers). These key informants are likely to be most informed about their  firms ' business  and  AI  initiatives.  We  measured  AI  constructs, environmental factors, ARMC, and all control variables at one point in time (t). Then we measured firm performance, process innovation, and product innovation at the second point in time (t + 1). The first survey was conducted in November 2020, and the second was completed by the same respondents four months later. We chose the four-month gap to minimize  response  bias  and  ensure  the  accuracy  of  the  information collected about the firms ' performance. Conducting the second survey early in 2021 allowed us to capture up-to-date data and insights about firms ' performance in the previous year.

We used a longitudinal study design primarily for three reasons: (1) to achieve correspondence with the temporal ordering of constructs in our  research  model;  (2)  to  enhance  our  arguments  for  causality regarding the relationship between agility and organizational outcomes; and  (3)  to  reduce  the  threats  of  common  method  bias.  Data  were collected through an online channel with organizations as our unit of analysis. Next, we discussed our methodological choices, encompassing concerns regarding measurement selection, sampling, response rate, and evaluation of potential non-response bias, following the recommendations of Hulland et al. (2018).

## 4.1. Measures

To the extent possible, we operationalized constructs by adapting existing scales to the context of our current studies. We captured responses to all items using multi-item reflective measures on a sevenpoint Likert scale. We then validated the measurement scales in a series  of  procedures  to  ensure  content  validity,  construct  validity,  and reliability (Straub, 1989), as described later in this section. The details of measurement items can be found in Appendix A.

AI-Powered  Capabilities :  We  measured  three  AI  capabilities: AI- enabled automation , AI-enabled analytics , and AI-enabled relational capabilities . Consistent with the literature (e.g., Huang et al., 2019; Huang & Rust,  2021a), we modeled each dimension as a first-order construct. Then we developed a new scale to operationalize AI capabilities based on insights from Cheatham et al. (2020) and used three items to measure AI-enabled automation. All three items measured the extent to which AI tools and applications are used for automation. For example, AI tools and  applications  are  used  to  automate  time-consuming  activities, including claim processing, basic customer-service interaction, and inventory tracking. We measured AI-enabled analytics capability using nine items, including the use of AI to predict equipment failure, respond quickly to changing inventory levels, test nearly all potential scenarios before  making  a  decision,  predict  the  next-best  product  or  service  a customer is likely to buy, and simulate product or service delivery. To measure AI-enabled relational capability, we used four items that reflect the extent to which firms used AI to engage with their customers. These items were adapted from Lee et al. (2020).

To validate all items used in measuring AI-powered capabilities, we asked for feedback on our initial measurement items from two IS faculty members with experience in survey research methods. The feedback led us to review and modify several items. Next, the initial set of items and the predefined categories were submitted for a card-sorting test (Moore & Benbasat, 1991). Results from this test indicated that wording for some items needed additional clarity. After we conducted two rounds of card-sorting tests, the test resulted in a satisfactory classification of items into predefined categories.

ARMC :  We measured ARMC using seven items that reflected how easily  and  quickly  firms  can  address  the  opportunities  in  two  areas: customer responsiveness and operational adjustment. These items were adapted from Tallon and Pinsonneault (2011).

Environmental  Hostility  and  Dynamism :  We  measured  environmental hostility using five items developed by Chen et al. (2014). The indicators assessed the existence of unfavorable external forces in firms ' business environments (Zahra & Garvis, 2000). Environmental dynamism assessed the degree of rapid technological change, shifts in customers ' preferences, and changes in competitor actions. The scale was adapted from Lee et al. (2015).

Firm  Performance :  We  used  self-reported  items  to  measure  firm performance.  This  approach  is  consistent  with  prior  IT  capability research investigating relative performance measures (e.g., Chen et al., 2014; Queiroz et al., 2018; Ravichandran et al., 2005). We adapted five items from Queiroz et al. (2018) to measure market share, revenues, sales growth, and profitability relative to competitors.

Process and Product Innovation :  We modeled firms ' product and process innovation as reflective, first-order constructs (Damanpour & Gopalakrishnan, 2001). Product innovation captured firms ' capacity to innovate compared to all other competitors within the same industry concerning the level of novelty, utilization of cutting-edge technology, pace of product development, and number of new products or services introduced.  On  the  other  hand,  process  innovation  denotes  firms ' technological competitiveness, speed in adopting the latest technology, and novelty in technology utilization. These items were adapted from Prajogo and Ahmed (2006).

Control  Variables :  We  controlled  for  the  effect  of  organizational factors (i.e., firm location, industry sector, firm size, and firm age) on Y. Sullivan and S. Fosso Wamba firm performance and innovation following prior work on the business value of IT (see Chen et al., 2021; Kulkarni et al., 2017). Firm ' s location was a binary variable with 0 for UK companies and 1 for French companies . Industry sector was a binary variable with 0 for nonmanufacturing  firms  and  1  for  manufacturing  firms. Firm  size was measured  as  a  firm-wide  number  of  employees,  and  fi rm  age was measured as the number of years a firm has been in business.

## 5.2. Test of common method bias

In  addition  to  using  a  longitudinal  study  design,  we  performed  a series of statistical tests to alleviate common method concerns. First, we applied  Harman ' s  single-factor  test  using  exploratory  factor  analysis (Podsakoff  et  al.,  2003).  The  resulting  unrotated  component  matrix showed that the measurement items did not load on a single factor. A single factor accounted for only 38.02 % of the total variance. Second, we examined common method variance (CMV) using a marker variable technique (Lindell & Whitney, 2001) for the variables collected at one point in time (t). In this technique, a marker variable is a scale that is theoretically unrelated to at least one other scale in the questionnaire. The marker variable ' s mean correlation with other variables in the study ( rM ) is an estimate of the extent of CMV in the sample. We computed CMV-adjusted  correlations  between  the  variables  of  interest  ( r A)  by partialling out rM from the uncorrected bivariate correlation ( rU ) using the following formula r A = ( rU -rM ) / (1rM ) (Malhotra et al., 2006) and we estimated the significant difference between rU and rM using a tstatistic  (Bhattacherjee & Hikmet,  2007).  In  our  study,  we  used  respondents ' current state of mind (i.e., ' my mind feels unfocused right now ' ) as the marker variable. This variable had a mean absolute correlation of 0.07 with the items used in this study. The mean change in correlation due to the CMV-adjusted correlations was very small (0.03). These results suggest the lack of CMV bias in our sample.

## 6. Discussion

The primary objective of this study is to explore how AI-powered capabilities can add value to organizations by enhancing their ARMC. We  have  proposed  that  three  AI-powered  capabilities -AI-enabled automation,  AI-enabled  analytics,  and  AI-enabled  relational  capabilities -play pivotal roles in driving ARMC. Drawing on the perspectives of organizational agility and the dynamic capability framework, we conceptualize ARMC as firms ' capacity to swiftly recognize and adapt to market shifts, encompassing customer responsiveness and operational adjustment as their core competencies. We also examine the moderating effects of environmental conditions (environmental hostility and dynamism) on the relationships between AI-powered capabilities and ARMC.

Our findings reveal that the relationships between AI-powered capabilities and ARMC depend on environmental conditions. Specifically, the findings show that environmental hostility negatively moderates the relationship between AI-enabled automation and ARMC, as well as between  AI-enabled  relational  capability  and  ARMC.  However,  it  positively  moderates  the  relationship  between  AI-enabled  analytics  and ARMC.  Additionally,  the  findings  demonstrate  that  environmental dynamism  positively  moderates  the  relationship  between  AI-enabled relational capability and ARMC. Finally, our study establishes significant positive relationships between ARMC and both firm performance and innovation. These findings have important implications for both research and practice.

## 6.1. Implications for research

Our study makes several contributions to research on the business value  of  IT.  First,  the  results  provide  robust  empirical  evidence  supporting the idea that AI can enhance organizational responsiveness and adaptability  in  the  face  of  market  changes.  The  hype  around  AI  is causing many companies to prematurely adopt AI applications without fully grasping the true value of AI (Brock & von Wangenheim, 2019; Davenport,  2018).  However,  directing  AI  toward  the  key  business competencies can strengthen firms ' ability  to  be  adaptive,  agile,  and responsive to market changes, thereby positioning firms for sustainable growth and success in dynamic business environments.

Second,  our  findings  suggest  a  positive  association  between  AIenabled automation and ARMC. By leveraging AI-enabled automation tools, such as RPA, firms can automate repetitive and time-consuming business  processes,  resulting  in  improved  productivity,  faster  market entry for products and services, and enhanced responsiveness to market opportunities. However, our findings indicate that AI-enabled automation  is  more  suitable  for  stable  environments  with  well-defined  processes and relatively predictable changes, where firms can produce and sell homogenous products in large volumes (Lee et al., 2015). Thus, AIenabled automation efficiently streamlines production and operational processes, ensuring consistency and cost-effectiveness while minimizing human errors and improving overall efficiency.

Third, our findings also reveal that the effect of AI-enabled analytics on ARMC is significantly altered once the moderating effect of environmental hostility is added to the model. Specifically, in highly hostile environments  where  uncertainties  and  disruptions  are  prevalent,  the impact  of  AI-enabled  analytics  on  ARMC  becomes  even  more  pronounced. AI-enabled analytics can assist organizations in processing vast amounts of complex data and extracting actionable insights, allowing them to gain a deeper understanding of market dynamics and customer behavior (Huang et al., 2019). In such hostile conditions, the ability to quickly identify emerging trends and adapt business strategies accordingly  becomes  a  critical  advantage  for  firms  seeking  to  control  the inherent  risk.  Insights  enabled  by  AI  overcome  the  challenges  of obtaining  information  in  a  hostile  environment,  enabling  evidencebased decisions in complex situations (Fern ´ andez-Rovira et al., 2021). Thus, AI-enabled analytics capability allows firms to develop planned strategies in response to constant threats posed by the environment.

Fourth, our results demonstrate that environmental hostility negatively moderates the relationship between AI-enabled relational capability  and  ARMC.  AI  tools  with  relational  capability  utilize  existing conversation data to understand a wide range of customers and employ machine learning and NLP to grasp the context, continuously enhancing AI ' s responses and interactions with customers (Davenport & Ronanki, 2018). These diverse digital options enabled by AI relational capability can potentially drive ARMC both at market and operational levels as organizations become more effective in responding to customers, leading to changes in internal business processes. However, many AI systems designed for this purpose are still experimental (Huang & Rust, 2020). Thus, in highly hostile environments where the risk is too high and can be costly (Mitchell et al., 2011), the effectiveness of AI-enabled relational capability may be hampered, and organizations should carefully evaluate  its  implementation  and  consider  alternative  strategies  to enhance ARMC under such conditions.

Fifth, we found that environmental dynamism does not moderate the relationship between AI-enabled automation and ARMC, as well as between AI-enabled analytics and ARMC. However, we observed a positive and significant moderation effect of environmental dynamism on the relationship  between  AI-enabled  relational  capability  and  ARMC.  In environments characterized by high dynamism, firms often face significant unpredictability regarding their customers ' tastes and preferences (Lee et al., 2015). Consequently, they need to continuously engage in competitive actions to attract and retain customers. In this context, AIenabled  analytics  can  aid  in  establishing  a  relational  capability, which, in turn, facilitates the development of ARMC in a highly dynamic environment. By leveraging AI-enabled relational capability, organizations can adapt their customer interactions and responses in real time, enabling them to stay responsive to the ever-changing market conditions and better meet customer needs and preferences.

Lastly, our findings strongly support a positive relationship between ARMC and firm performance, process innovation, and product innovation. From the dynamic capabilities perspective, ARMC empowers firms to seize new opportunities (Teece et al., 2016). However, being highly responsive  and  adaptive  to  the  environment  can  come  with  costs. Consequently, the necessity for ARMC, and thus organizational agility, arises when substantial uncertainty and dynamism are present in the business  environment (Chakravarty et al.,  2013;  Teece  et  al.,  2016). Moreover,  AI  can  potentially  revolutionize  the  way  firms  operate, transforming  an  entire  organization ' s  rigid  mindset  into  a  more responsive  and  adaptive  approach.  This  transformation  provides  an opportunity to explore problems from multiple perspectives, paving the way for innovative and novel solutions.

## 6.2. Practical and societal implications

Our findings provide several practical implications. First, executives exploring the potential of AI can use our results to guide their AI investments. Our study shows that different functions of AI can enhance ARMC, depending on the environmental conditions. With the uncertainty  surrounding  AI,  executives  would  be  wise  to  systematically evaluate their business needs to determine which areas could benefit from AI. The three AI-powered capabilities discussed in this study may help organizations to broaden their digital options and flexibly respond to market opportunities.

Our findings suggest that not all AI capabilities hold the same significance. AI-enabled automation can aid organizations in cost reduction and standardizing routine tasks, particularly in stable environments. On the  other  hand,  AI-enabled  analytics  capability  may  enhance  firms ' ARMC by processing, evaluating, and analyzing information, proving more valuable in a hostile environment. Lastly, AI-enabled relational capability is key in fostering ARMC by actively engaging with customers and continuously learning from these interactions, making the process instrumental  for  adaptability  in  highly  dynamic  environments.  By leveraging the diverse capabilities of AI, organizations can effectively adapt to changing environments and improve their performance.

Our findings also suggest that nurturing their capacity for swift and effective responses to market shift enables organizations to seize new opportunities. This study highlights the positive impact of ARMC on innovation outcomes. Although implementing AI can be challenging, AI systems can drive ARMC, leading to both process and product innovation, as well as improved performance outcomes. Therefore, AI systems enable firms to explore novel approaches to problem-solving, providing them  with  the  flexibility  to  respond  to  threats  and  opportunities (Haefner et al., 2021). With such flexibility, firms can readily identify emerging market demands and create new products/services to meet those needs.

Our  study  indicates  that  AI  capabilities  are  transforming  the  dynamics between humans and machines, the methods of data analysis, and the decision-making process from a societal standpoint. Although AI initiatives  can  greatly  influence  customer  experience  and  workforce skills (Butner et al., 2020), how these changes will unfold in the long run is currently unknown. For instance, past research has shown that under high levels of environmental hostility and dynamism, IT resources and competencies  are  necessary  so  that  managers  can  actively  sense  opportunities for competitive actions (Lee et al., 2015). AI has transformed how  organizations  perceive  and  respond  to  such  challenges  since AI-powered capabilities provide a more flexible and adaptive approach. As AI technologies continue to advance, they are likely to play an even more  significant  role  in  shaping  the  future  of  business  and  society. ## 6.3. Limitations and future research directions

This research has some limitations that suggest directions for future work. First,  as  mentioned  earlier,  organizational  agility  is  a  comprehensive  and  multifaceted  concept.  While  we  derived  the  concept  of ARMC  from  the  broader  agility  framework,  we  encourage  future research  to  explore  the  wider  aspects  of  organizational  agility.  This expanded investigation could uncover additional dimensions and potential intersections that could enhance our understanding of how AIpowered  capabilities  contribute  to  firms ' responsiveness  and  adaptability. Further, as Kalaignanam et al. (2021) observed, sensemaking, speed,  and  iteration  define  agility.  Our  concept  of  ARMC  does  not explicitly contain iteration; however, it aligns with the crucial elements of sensemaking and speed, which are integral components of organizational agility. We call for future research to conduct a longitudinal study, explicitly observing all three aspects of agility in the study.

Second, while many researchers have defined agility as encompassing speed and flexibility (e.g., Kalaignanam et al., 2021), Prange (2021) suggests that agility is not reliant solely on speed as a defining characteristic.  Specifically,  according  to  Prange,  agility  requires  both  the resilience derived from a reliable core and the dynamism anchored in ongoing  change.  Our  conceptualization  of  ARMC  does  not  explicitly elaborate on the concept of agility as slowness. Future research should explore different aspects of agility to lead to a better understanding of how agility, including ARMC, can contribute to organizational success in varying environments.

Third, we conceptualize each AI-powered capability as a first-order construct  by  following  the  existing  concept  of  multiple  AIs  (e.g., Huang et al., 2019; Huang & Rust, 2021a). However, the relationships among AI-powered capabilities may be more complex than we have hypothesized. For instance, interaction effects could occur among AI capabilities. A larger sample size would be required to test such complex relationships.  To  examine  these  complexities  further,  exploratory research could achieve a much stronger theoretical foundation. Understanding the dependencies among a wider capability system in an organization  could  be  an  interesting  and  important  agenda  for  future research in this area, especially under different environmental conditions.

## 7. Conclusion

Our research investigates how AI-powered capabilities can add value to  organizations  through  their  capacity  to  directly  affect  how  firms identify and respond to market changes. We offer a concept of ARMC derived from organizational agility and dynamic capabilities perspectives. We also investigate the moderating role of environmental conditions in influencing the relationship between AI-powered capabilities and ARMC. Our findings indicate that the effects of AI-enabled automation, AI-enabled analytics, and AI-enabled relational capability on ARMC are contingent on two environmental conditions -environmental hostility and environmental dynamism. Our research provides a theorybased understanding of AI ' s impact on organizations. In addition, we endeavor to establish a foundational framework for future theory-driven studies to gain a comprehensive understanding of how AI benefits organizations. This understanding can contribute to improved organizational performance and foster additional competitive actions.
