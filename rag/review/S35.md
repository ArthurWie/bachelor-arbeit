## Abstract

Purpose -Despite a broad consensus on firm internationalization through digital platforms, it still appears to be a black box mechanism through which emerging market small and medium-sized enterprises (ESMEs) upgrade their global value chain (GVC) position. This study aims to investigate how artificial intelligence (AI) adoption and strategic agility coupling shape ESMEs' GVC positioning through the lens of international marketing capabilities (IMCs), differentiating international advantage into two dimensions: product innovation and brand upgrading. Furthermore, it investigates how the depth and breadth of platform embeddedness moderate this relationship.

Design/methodology/approach -A quantitative approach was employed to analyse data from 322 small and medium-sized enterprises in China utilizing cross-border platforms. The research model was tested using structural equation modelling, with data analysis conducted through SPSS 27, AMOS 24 and STATA to evaluate both the measurement and structural models.

Findings -The study demonstrates that the coupling of AI adoption and strategic agility positively influences IMCs, product innovation and brand upgrading, while IMCs serve as a mediating mechanism. Additionally, platform embedding characteristics exhibit a nonlinear, inverted U-shaped moderating effect: a medium level of embedding depth maximizes the coupling's positive impact on product innovation, whereas a moderate level of embedding breadth optimizes its positive effect on brand upgrading.

Originality/value -This study proposes a holistic framework that explores how platform affordances facilitate the identification and forecasting of international market information and resource redeployment within platform ecosystems, driving product and brand value creation. It advances the dynamic capabilities hierarchy in international business research by integrating digital platforms and the ESMEs perspective, illustrating how higher-order strategic agility shapes embedded ESMEs' lower-order IMCs, enhancing international competitive advantage. Additionally, it examines AI-enabled marketing capabilities through a broader lens of international advantage.

Keywords Artificial intelligence adoption, Strategic agility, International marketing capabilities, Global value chains, Cross-border platforms, ESMEs

Despite the growing recognition of platforms' importance in firms' internationalization, research has primarily focused on factors driving firms' internationalization via digital platforms, emphasizing firm-specific resources, capabilities, platform types, industry conditions and country-specific contexts (Da Rocha et al ., 2024; Gong et al ., 2024). However, while many ESMEs are advancing internationally without achieving global status, significant gaps remain in understanding how they engage with cross-border platforms, specifically in leveraging AI and strategic agility to drive product innovation and brand upgrading, despite the recognized importance of GVC upgrading. Moreover, we suggest that the magnitude of this change may hinge on ESMEs' embedding characteristics. The emergence of ESMEs' internationalization through crossborder platforms introduces two characteristics: embedding breadth and depth. As a result, these embedding differences may lead to differential quality and quantity of information and resources accessible to ESMEs through digital platforms. Accordingly, this study seeks to answer the following research questions to illuminate the dynamic interplay between ESMEs and cross-border platforms and to maximize competitive gains:

How does the coupling of AI adoption and strategic agility within a cross-border platform enable GVC upgrading for ESMEs?

What role do the IMCs of ESMEs play in this process?

How do platform embedding breadth and depth separately moderate the effect of coupling AI adoption with strategic agility on product innovation and brand upgrading?

To investigate the above research question, we performed a large-scale survey of Chinese small and medium-sized enterprises. China's cross-border e-commerce unleashes its potential in 'selling globally', reaching 2.63 trillion RMB in 2024, a 10.8% year-on-year increase.

This study makes four key contributions. First , our research offers novel insights into the role of digital platforms in international business by proposing a novel coupling framework linking AI adoption and strategic agility (e.g. Jean et al ., 2020; Da Rocha et al ., 2024). This coupling framework extends prior work that has typically examined AI and strategic agility separately. ## 2. Theoretical background

## 2.1 Coupling AI adoption and strategic agility

AI adoption is defined as the incorporation of technology into an organization's identity, integrating machines' ability to identify, interpret, infer and learn from data to create flexible and accurate decisions (Mikalef and Gupta, 2021; Dahlke et al ., 2024). In the context of ESMEs' internationalization via digital platforms, cross-border platforms' investment in AI infrastructure enables ESMEs to overcome traditional infrastructure constraints (Luo and Bu, 2018; Wei and Pardo, 2022). For example, Ahi et al . (2022) highlight that advanced technologies have important implications for firms that govern the structure of their value chains through digital platform networks. By adopting AI, platforms can generate, analyse and act upon real-time data at unprecedented speed and scale (Massa et al ., 2023; Liu et al ., 2025). Thus, the cross-border platform not only provides ESMEs with access to foreign networks for International Marketing Review

[S. 5]

global expansion but also offers a range of advantages through AI adoption, supporting their international competitiveness (Hossain et al ., 2022; Manis and Madhavaram, 2023; Dahlke et al ., 2024). For example, platforms' AI adoption allows more value to be created by offering cognitive analyses of global consumer behaviour, mining global user profiles through online footprints and user feedback, facilitating real-time multilingual communication and streamlining logistics and payment systems to its participants (Wei and Pardo, 2022). Accordingly, ESMEs embedded in the platform constantly reshape their international competitive advantages by mitigating challenges, such as language barriers, temporal and spatial limitations in gathering international market insights, cross-border payment issues and logistical complexities.

Cross-border platforms are profoundly influenced by AI, with agile platforms better positioned to leverage these AI technologies for rapid market responsiveness and operational efficiency and develop customer-centric solutions (Kano, 2018; Ahammad et al ., 2021; Ahi et al ., 2022; Abdalla et al ., 2025). Grounded in the dynamic capability perspective, strategic agility is a high-order dynamic capability, which refers to the platform's ability to adapt and reconfigure its resources to respond effectively to AI-driven international market insights (Teece et al ., 1997; Winter, 2003; Ahammad et al ., 2021). AI adoption supports this strategic agility by reducing information asymmetry, enabling predictive insights and facilitating decentralized decision-making (Liu et al ., 2025). In particular, ESMEs in weaker institutional environments, institutional pressures and limited competitive advantages constrain agility (Luo and Bu, 2018; Abdalla et al ., 2025), underscoring how the platform's strategic agility mitigates inherent resource limitations (Ahammad et al ., 2021; Zahoor and Lew, 2023). According to a study by Shams et al . (2021), strategic agility is a key intangible asset in international business. Similarly, Fourn � e et al . (2014) describe it as a meta-capability that allows companies to swiftly identify local opportunities, favour global complementarities and promote sustainability. Furthermore, Abdalla et al . (2025) argue that by embracing agility, platforms can integrate emerging technologies. Strategic agility also supports the global brand development identity by enabling firms to respond quickly to user feedback and strengthen their reputation (Tallon and Pinsonneault, 2011; Demir et al ., 2021; Mancuso et al ., 2024). Notwithstanding its recognized importance, there remains a notable gap in understanding how ESMEs in weak institutional settings leverage cross-border platforms' strategic agility to advance in GVCs.

As global attention to environmental, social and governance standards continues to grow, underscoring the need for the ESME to explore strategies for achieving global sustainable development (Luo and Bu, 2018; UN, 2015). Given their resource limitations, exposure to international competitive pressures and institutional pressures often complicate this process. The majority of studies have focused on how digital platforms enable SMEs to acquire foreign knowledge, develop international opportunities, reduce export-related costs and lower entry barriers, thereby influencing the scope and performance of internationalization (e.g. Jean et al ., 2020; Da Rocha et al ., 2024). While existing research confirms the role of AI adoption in enhancing performance, innovation and competitive advantage (e.g. Gama and Magistretti, 2023; Raisch and Krakowski, 2021), little is known about how AI adoption on platforms evolves in international business. Furthermore, prior research emphasizes the standalone effects of AI adoption or strategic agility and few studies examine how these constructs are coupled within a unified framework. This theoretical gap is particularly salient given the complex nature of ESMEs navigating complex GVC upgrading, where agility must be complemented by robust informational infrastructures.

## 2.2 International marketing capabilities (IMCs)

IMCs are defined as a firm's ability to deliver products that meet the needs of targeted market segments and to deploy marketing resources effectively in turbulent foreign markets (Eng and Spickett-Jones, 2009; Fang and Zou, 2009; Zahoor and Lew, 2023). IMCs are conceptually linked to shifts in international marketing strategies (Fang and Zou, 2009). Accordingly, for resource-limited ESMEs, cultivating robust IMCs is critical for securing competitive positions in GVCs. Since AI analytics provide data-driven knowledge, they offer accurate and reliable market information, enabling firms to flexibly respond to international market opportunities that might be overlooked by humans. AI-enabled cross-border platforms thus enhance the ESME's ability to marketing research, strategy and action stages -key tenets of the IMCs framework (Huang and Rust, 2021). For example, at the marketing research stage, AI can be effectively employed for data collection, in-depth market analysis and customer understanding. Progressing to the marketing strategy phase, AI facilitates precise segmentation recognition, targeting and positioning. At the marketing action stage, AI contributes to standardization, personalization and relationalization. This structured application of AI across different marketing stages underscores its pivotal role in optimizing marketing efficacy and strategic alignment. Despite emerging research on AI-enabled marketing capabilities (Huang and Rust, 2021; Manis and Madhavaram, 2023; Zahoor and Lew, 2023), international marketing scholarship has inadequately examined their role in global international contexts.

[S. 6]

From a hierarchical view of dynamic capability, successful international development necessitates higher-order dynamic capabilities for adapting to the global market environment and adjusting resource bases. AI operations, in practice, function synergistically with other resources to enhance ESMEs' capabilities, including diversified marketing tools that optimize market capabilities (Dahlke et al ., 2024). IMCs serve as vital lower-order operational capabilities that support various aspects of core marketing activities (Winter, 2003). In this setting, ordinary capabilities are the ESME's international marketing routines (operationalized in product or brand), and we study the processes through which such routines change, affected by platforms' coupling technical infrastructure and higher-order dynamic capabilities. In this regard, ESMEs need to utilize platforms' higher-order dynamic capabilities for developing lower-order IMCs that address market-specific demands, such as market conditions, consumer preferences, cultural nuances, regulatory environments and distribution channels embedded in market practices, thereby effectively delivering value in GVCs (Teece et al ., 2016; Ahammad et al ., 2021; Schulze and Brusoni, 2022). Platforms exhibiting superior strategic agility can co-create value with participants, highlighting the importance of AI adoption for ESMEs to identify market demands and emerging opportunities (Pfajfar et al ., 2024b). Thereby, the coupling of AI adoption and strategic agility allows ESMEs to extend, modify or create ordinary capabilities, allowing them to respond faster to shifting market demands and capitalize on emerging opportunities (Winter, 2003). Accordingly, as lower-order operational capabilities, IMCs translate customer knowledge into tactical marketing applications, enabling product innovation and brand development.

Empirical studies have consistently revealed a well-supported relationship between IMCs and firms' international performance outcomes, such as financial metrics, export results and innovation performance (e.g. Eng and Spickett-Jones, 2009; Chatterjee et al ., 2022). However, the results can vary across different contexts and samples. Nonetheless, the international marketing literature still lacks more granular advantages research, including new product innovation and global brand upgrading (Pfajfar et al ., 2024b). Furthermore, research has typically examined IMCs in isolation without adequately accounting for their mediating roles. This lack of integration into the interaction perspective between digital platforms and ESMEs limits the explanatory power of existing theories in explaining international competitive advantage heterogeneity among ESMEs via cross-border platforms. Addressing these questions could help build a richer theoretical understanding of how coupling AI adoption and strategic agility enables ESMEs to upgrade their GVC position.

Rapid shifts in technological innovation, market upgrading, GVC restructuring, and geopolitical tensions are causing traditional emerging economies small and medium-sized International Marketing Review

ESMEs now face both opportunities and challenges in upgrading their GVC to reshape their international advantages. GVCs consist of interconnected processes in the production, distribution and consumption of goods and services worldwide; thus, product innovation and brand upgrading are critical for enhancing value chain positioning (Caputo et al ., 2022; G € olgeci et al ., 2023). Unlike SMEs in developed economies, ESMEs often suffer from institutional voids and limited resources (Ko et al ., 2021; Singh et al ., 2022), which can trap them in a 'low-end lock-in', resulting in low-profit margins within GVCs (Adomako et al ., 2019). The development of cross-border platforms should not only foster growth points and new momentum for foreign trade but also foster independent brands and innovative development momentum for enterprises. Consequently, ESMEs compete not based emphatically on the institutional and policy environment and resources shortage, etc. in which they may be disadvantageous, but on the creative utilization and combination of internal and external resources of the cross-border platforms in which they are embedded to enhance their international value-added activities, such as producing highquality, branded products.

As one of the largest cross-border e-commerce markets, China's cross-border e-commerce exports exceeded 1.9 trillion yuan in 2024, marking a 15.2% increase and underscoring the sector's pivotal role in emerging economies and global trade growth. The dynamic nature of the Chinese market presents a valuable context for investigating how cross-border platforms contribute to supporting the potential of product innovation and brand upgrades for ESMEs. However, current research predominantly focuses on the impact of digital platforms on international performance and scope, with the literature largely centred on firms from developed countries. This narrow focus offers limited insights into how ESMEs can leverage their limited resources and weak formal institutions to strengthen their international standing (Da Rocha et al ., 2024). We believe that AI adoption and strategic agility coupling models have great potential to explain why and how some ESMEs without possessing traditionally defined monopolistic advantages such as global market power, brand images, organizational reputation, customer loyalty and proprietary technologies can upgrade their position in the GVC. Addressing this gap holds both theoretical and practical significance in understanding how cross-border platforms can reposition ESMEs from low-end participants to key innovators and brand leaders (G € olgeci et al ., 2023).

## 3. Hypothesis and conceptual model development

3.1 Coupling of AI adoption and strategic agility for international competitive advantage We propose that coupling AI adoption with strategic agility creates significant opportunities for ESMEs to achieve international competitive advantage. AI adoption includes routines for data collection and analysis, supporting data-driven decision-making and crafting innovative scenarios (Verganti et al ., 2020; Garbuio and Lin, 2021). Specifically, AI's capabilities for sensing, interpreting and evaluating market data can act as powerful tools for product creativity and brand enhancement through automation and augmentation (Raisch and Krakowski, 2021; Ameen et al ., 2024). For example, generative AI technologies like Open AI's system facilitate the expansion of both problem and solution spaces, driving product and brand advantages in alignment with design thinking principles.

Product innovation has long been a primary avenue for establishing international competitive advantage, particularly by developing or enhancing products that offer unique benefits and superior quality compared to those of competitors (Cui and Wu, 2017; HurtadoTorres et al ., 2018). Cross-border digital platforms deploy AI to provide real-time data across the value chains; this facilitates collaboration among participants, shortens experimentation cycles and speeds up international market entry (Mariani and Nambisan, 2021; Gama and Magistretti, 2023; Del Giudice et al. , 2023). AI on these platforms also helps aggregate and identify diverse consumer demands, enhancing the accuracy of market predictions and empowering ESMEs to bridge cultural and geographical divides, thereby fueling product innovation (Garbuio and Lin, 2021; Verganti et al ., 2020). Meanwhile, brand upgrading refers to the ability of export firms to achieve higher customer cognition, satisfaction and loyalty toward their products compared to foreign competitors (Lang et al ., 2023; Zahoor et al ., 2024). When brands operate across different cultures, managing value perceptions becomes more complex (Spyropoulou et al ., 2011). Here, AI assists ESMEs by supporting targeted advertising and automatically personalised content that resonates with specific cultural and institutional contexts. This approach facilitates the precise communication of brand image, improves the international exposure and dynamic stakeholders' interactions, reduces the difficulty of cross-cultural content adaptation and ultimately increases international brand recognition and customer loyalty (Spyropoulou et al ., 2011; Mandler et al ., 2021). For example, AI-driven chatbots offer real-time engagement across time zones, which Mandler et al . (2021) argue builds trust and credibility, strengthening brands' market positions abroad.

[S. 8]

Strategic agility is particularly crucial for ESMEs' internationalization efforts due to the variable institutional support and rapid, unpredictable changes in global markets (Adomako et al ., 2019; Ahammad et al ., 2021). It entails the timely sensing of opportunities, rapid decision-making and adaptive strategic direction adjustments, which are essential for managing disruptive innovations and navigating unpredictable market conditions (Fourn � e et al ., 2014; Zahoor et al ., 2024). Given that ESMEs often lack strong brand recognition or cutting-edge technology, we argue that platforms' strategic agility allows these ESMEs to leverage platform resources to reconfigure their existing resource base, enabling swift responses to evolving market trends (Ahammad et al ., 2021). This flexibility empowers ESMEs to bridge competitive gaps, particularly by swiftly aligning resources with international market demands through digital platforms to enable new product development and enhance brand recognition (Ameen et al ., 2024). The literature has addressed the role of strategic agility in international business or international performance (e.g. Fourn � e et al ., 2014; Demir et al ., 2021), though mainly focusing on its isolated effects rather than examining its potential synergy with AI. AI supports this agility by enabling automated and data-driven decision-making processes, which are particularly valuable for product innovation and brand positioning (Raisch and Krakowski, 2021; Gama and Magistretti, 2023). Recent studies comparing optimal profits with and without AI have found that manufacturers' optimal profits in both modes consistently increase with platform power when AI is integrated (Xu et al ., 2023). Despite these advancements, limited research has explored the combined impact of AI adoption and strategic agility on driving product innovation and brand advantage. Thus, we formulate the following hypothesis:

H1. The coupling of AI adoption and strategic agility has a significant positive effect on (a) product innovation advantage and (b) brand upgrading advantage.

## 3.2 Direct and mediating effects of IMCs

IMCs refer to the ability of ESMEs to reconfigure marketing resources, effectively identify foreign marketing needs, introduce new products to meet these needs and deliver value in foreign markets, ultimately helping ESMEs create value in the export market (Papa et al ., 2022; Zahoor and Lew, 2023). Innovation performance has been extensively observed by studies on a sample of SMEs operating in various foreign markets; evidently, SMEs with IMCs are becoming more innovative in international markets (Pfajfar et al ., 2024b). For example, early on, Eng and Spickett-Jones (2009) indicate that marketing capabilities help Chinese companies manufacture upgrades in international value chains. A similar mechanism recently revealed indicates that the focal marketing capabilities developed by born-global firms help in addressing a global market gap or being first to market with radical, patented product innovations (Zahoor and Lew, 2023; Pfajfar et al ., 2024b). Thus, we maintain that IMCs can engender product innovation advantages for ESMEs.

Moreover, ESMEs that possess IMCs can realise brand advantage, transitioning from original equipment manufacturing to original design manufacturing and, ultimately, to ownbrand manufacturing (Eng and Spickett-Jones, 2009). In the area of international branding, IMCs encompass specific categories, such as marketing communications, brand management, market information acquisition, interpretation and dissemination, all of which are expected to influence foreign customers' mental responses towards the brand (Chatterjee et al ., 2022; Pfajfar et al ., 2024b). For example, market and process knowledge, along with export market experience, enables firms to effectively adapt their brands to local characteristics (Liu et al. , 2025; Zahoor et al ., 2024). Notably, managing customers' brand perceptions is particularly complex, requiring brands to tailor their positioning to fit local market characteristics. Spyropoulou et al . (2011) indicate that an exporting firm's communication capability, through its market sensing and linking customer attitudes and behaviour, significantly enhances its branding advantage. Collectively, ESMEs with superior IMCs may strengthen their brands' global positioning. On these grounds, we hypothesise:

H2. IMCs have a significant positive effect on (a) product innovation advantage and (b) brand upgrading advantage.

Drawing further on the hierarchical view of dynamic capability (Winter, 2003), we aim to elucidate that the coupling of AI adoption and strategic agility directly influences IMCs, which, in turn, mediate the impact of this coupling on product innovation and brand upgrading advantages. According to Winter (2003), ordinary capabilities enable short-term operational success, while dynamic capabilities modify, extend or create these ordinary capabilities. Previous research argues that higher-order dynamic capabilities, like strategic agility, do not directly enhance competitive advantage but do so by shaping lower-order operational capabilities (Fourn � e et al ., 2014; Ahammad et al ., 2021). In this framework, strategic agility acts as a higher-order dynamic capability, coupling with AI adoption to facilitate the development of lower-order IMCs, which then strengthen ESMEs' international competitive advantage.

The coupling of AI adoption with strategic agility offers significant potential for enhancing IMCs. AI can reinforce IMCs by enabling strategic agility to combine or integrate with customers, partners, global supply chain and other resources, regardless of geographical boundaries (Hossain et al ., 2022; Manis and Madhavaram, 2023). AI adoption are also becoming standard for cross-border platforms, marketing functions and activities that can benefit from AI, due to its ability to recognize patterns from data (e.g. text mining, speech recognition, facial recognition) (Huang and Rust, 2021). Specifically, AI can collect and synthesise vast amounts of global user data from various international markets, while advanced cognitive computing transforms tacit knowledge into segmentation insights, enabling ESMEs to uncover latent market positioning and gain a more comprehensive understanding of market dynamics within their industry (Manis and Madhavaram, 2023). For instance, Alibaba International Station's AI-powered risk control agent can swiftly identify market risks in response to rapidly evolving global trade policies and product standards. Notably, when cross-border platforms demonstrate strategic flexibility, they can flexibly reallocate and reconfigure global resources and competencies (Zahoor et al ., 2024). In this regard, ESMEs enable leveraging platforms' powerful resource advantages, such as intelligent supply chains, to expand market coverage and enhance IMCs, allowing them to quickly adapt to varying market conditions across different countries and regions (Zahoor and Lew, 2023). Thus, although, being resource-constrained, ESMEs may not have the market resources needed to engage in global value-creation activities, the cross-border platform's agility enables them to a realignment of any available resources and the exploitation of opportunities presented by changing global market conditions (Zahoor et al ., 2024). Accordingly, the benefits of AI adoption, coupled with their agile nature, can enable ESMEs to attain market orientation, marketing planning and new product development advantages suited to improving IMCs (Manis and Madhavaram, 2023).

[S. 10]

Mainstream studies in the international business domain have extensively discussed the relevance of strategic agility and proposed its primary aspects for international performance and success (Ahammad et al ., 2021; Demir et al ., 2021; Zahoor et al ., 2024). Still, more studies are needed to connect marketing to agile strategies, particularly in the context of ESMEs, distinguishing the different underlying capabilities that lead to international advantage (Shams et al ., 2021). More importantly, despite the emergence of AI-enabled marketing capabilities (Manis and Madhavaram, 2023), limited attention has been paid to what impact combining AI adoption and strategic agility may have on IMCs. Therefore, it is expected that AI adoption, when coupled with strategic agility, will enhance IMCs, we hypothesize:

H3. Coupling AI adoption with strategic agility has a significant positive effect on IMCs.

In addition to the direct effects, we anticipate that IMCs mediate the effects of coupling AI adoption and strategic agility on product innovation and brand upgrading advantage in our proposed conceptual model. An enterprise that effectively integrates its IMCs is better positioned to leverage the AI and strategic agility of cross-border platforms to enhance creativity in new products and brand development. It is more likely to generate added value in GVCs because it can fulfill marketing-related needs. IMCs facilitate customer knowledge acquisition, price adjustments, product and brand development and the strategic adaptation of marketing tactics, enabling ESMEs to target foreign customers with differentiated offerings (Zahoor and Lew, 2023).

Product innovation is mainly reflected in tangible goods R&D and production and service innovation and process (Cui and Wu, 2017). Product innovation decisions typically involve utilizing AI-enabled marketing capabilities to determine the optimal product attributes for development and employing test markets to decide whether and to what degree the product will be accepted (Huang and Rust, 2021). For instance, AI can assist ESMEs in analysing foreign user feedback from social and e-commerce platforms to identify new product features or design directions; AI can also support ESMEs in simulation testing, thereby reducing R&D costs. One especially important research area concerns how well AI-driven algorithms forecast demand for really new markets, develop innovative products and expedite the overall product development process (Davenport et al ., 2020). Furthermore, the strategic flexibility offered by platforms allows ESMEs to adapt more effectively to AI-driven predictions (Shams et al ., 2021). For example, flexible global supply chain models enable ESMEs to adjust production plans based on market feedback swiftly, accelerating new product iteration.

Moreover, the absence of brand effects not only diminishes the added value of products but also constrains the long-term international competitiveness of ESMEs. Building strong brands requires ESMEs to employ positioning and marketing mix strategies to shape foreign customers' perceptions (Pfajfar et al ., 2024b). AI-driven insights can tailor advertisements for foreign markets by classifying customers and evaluating potential advertisements based on their effectiveness in eliciting desired responses, thereby enhancing brand influence (Manis and Madhavaram, 2023). Besides, coupling AI adoption and strategic agility provides a foundation for IMCs to achieve effective allocation of advertising resources and manage cross-border customer relationships, thereby mitigating the constraints imposed by ESMEs' limited investments in marketing and brand building. IMCs facilitate brand differentiation, drive innovative branding strategies and align with international customers' preferences, thereby enhancing brand image and perceived value (Zahoor et al ., 2024). This enhanced positioning elevates ESMEs in GVCs by fostering customer loyalty and enabling premium pricing (Eng and Spickett-Jones, 2009). Thus, we propose the following hypotheses:

H4. IMCs positively mediate the effects of coupling AI adoption and strategic agility on (a) product innovation advantage and (b) brand upgrading advantage.

## 3.3 Moderating effects of ESMEs' platform embedding characteristics

We refer to embedding breadth as the number of digital platforms in which ESMEs participate, encompassing various types, such as e-commerce, social and innovation platforms (Kobarg International Marketing Review

et al ., 2019). Below, we will discuss how embedding breadth is associated with the effects of coupling AI adoption and strategic agility on ESMEs' international advantage, specifically in terms of product innovation and brand upgrading.

More specifically, when embedding breadth shifts from low to medium, ESMEs can access a wider variety of data through diverse platforms, enhancing their potential for creative recombination, complementary alignment within digital ecosystems and differentiated platform positioning, ultimately facilitating product innovation and brand upgrading (Hurtado-Torres et al ., 2018). In such conditions, the coupling of AI adoption and strategic agility enables the integration and analysis of diverse international market information, resulting in knowledge spillovers and unique product innovation advantages derived from heterogeneous resources, distributed innovation and cooperation among participants (Li et al ., 2019; Hagedoorn et al ., 2018). For example, interaction and reach platforms mostly comprise social media, facilitating product exchanges and providing access to essential data on prices, location and reputation. Additionally, different platforms allow ESMEs to tailor branding strategies based on platform positioning and target demographics, ultimately advancing brand equity (Liu et al., 2025). For example, AI-driven rankings and review scores on e-commerce platforms can communicate product quality to foreign consumers, while social platforms facilitate interactions that build brand trust (Cutolo and Kenney, 2021). Consequently, increasing embedding breadth amplifies the effects of coupling AI adoption and strategic agility on ESMEs' international advantages.

By contrast, when embedding breadth shifts from medium to high, ESMEs may encounter a 'learning trap', increase in information processing, resource dispersion and attention allocation challenges (Hagedoorn et al ., 2018; Kobarg et al ., 2019). As platform types increase, the degree of complexity of the portfolio and the difficulty of information processing, coordination and integration across platforms also escalate (Mariani and Nambisan, 2021; Li et al ., 2022). In such conditions, even with the adoption of AI and high strategic agility, the limited attention ESMEs can allocate across multiple platforms may restrict resources for innovation activities (Kobarg et al ., 2019). Additionally, various platforms can lead to inconsistent brand messaging, hindering consumers' ability to form a clear brand identity and negatively impacting brand perception (Hagedoorn et al ., 2018). For example, Li et al . (2022) discover that multinational enterprises with a more diversified digital platform attention scope experience constrained international sales. In short, initially, ESMEs benefit from embedded variety in digital platforms; however, excessive diversification can diminish these advantages due to rising coordination costs and declining information quality (Hurtado-Torres et al ., 2018; Ko et al ., 2021). Combining the discussions above, we formally propose that:

H5. The breadth of platform embedding has an inverted U-shaped moderating effect on the positive relationship between coupling AI adoption and strategic agility and (a) product innovation advantage and (b) brand upgrading advantage.

Embedding depth reflects the resource investment ESMEs dedicate to cross-border digital platform activities and the intensity of their interactions with platform owners and participants (Li et al ., 2019; Kobarg et al ., 2019). We also anticipate that embedding depth moderates the effect of coupling AI adoption and strategic agility on international advantage.

More precisely, as embedding depth shifts from low to medium, ESMEs invest targeted resources and relationships into digital platforms, fostering close collaboration and tacit knowledge transfer (Li et al. , 2019). Dahlke et al. (2024) argue that the effectiveness of direct transmission of AI knowledge relies on the strength of links and the depth of shared knowledge among partners. By deeply engaging with digital platforms, ESMEs can establish AI-based organisational learning routines and extract AI-driven international marketing insights (Manis and Madhavaram, 2023). This engagement promotes resource transfer and integration, allowing ESMEs to leverage platform agility to shorten R&D cycles (Hagedoorn et al ., 2018). In addition, AI-powered, strategically agile organisations establish access mechanisms and bolster brand credibility. ESMEs grow alongside these platforms, resulting in a bundling of their brand images. The global influence of large digital platforms enhances ESMEs' international visibility, particularly when consumers are cautious about new brands, as platform credibility fosters trust (Dahlke et al ., 2024). Furthermore, frequent interactions with global consumers also sustain a strong brand community atmosphere (Spyropoulou et al. , 2011). Hence, increased embedding depth positively moderates the effect of coupling AI adoption and strategic agility on product innovation and brand upgrading.

[S. 12]

However, this positive moderating effect may diminish as embedding depth rises. A higher embedding depth is similar to a fairly closed system for AI adopters that may limit ESMEs' abilities to value and process distant and novel information (Hagedoorn et al ., 2018). This may lead to a 'familiarity trap', where knowledge integration restricts the flow of new insights and reduces the novelty of the platform's knowledge base (Cutolo and Kenney, 2021). Embedding depth can also result in relational inertia, making ESMEs rigid in their platform type preferences. Over-dependence on specific platforms restricts strategic flexibility and hinders resource reallocation for relevant product innovation (Hagedoorn et al ., 2018). In addition, ESMEs employing a single-platform strategy may encounter power imbalances, as platform control limits their ability to implement independent branding strategies. Furthermore, a lack of marketing channel diversification can impede effective brand promotion (Cutolo and Kenney, 2021). Considering the discussion above, we postulate that varying levels of platform embedding depth affect ESMEs' ability to develop new products and upgrade brands in the global markets:

H6. The depth of platform embedding has an inverted U-shaped moderating effect on the positive relationship between coupling AI adoption and strategic agility and (a) product innovation advantage and (b) brand upgrading advantage.

## 4.2 Variables and measurement

In this study, all measurement instruments for the research model constructs were adapted from prior studies. A seven-point Likert scale (1 5 strongly disagree, 7 5 strongly agree) was employed for all items.

Dependent variables. The coupling of AI adoption and strategic agility is conceptualized and developed as a formative-formative type of second-order construct, reflected by the AI

[S. 15]

## 1264

adoption and strategic agility first-order construct, which was previously validated by Ameen et al . (2024). AI adoption items were taken from Hossain et al . (2022), which evaluate the extent to which digital platforms actively utilize AI to interpret international data correctly and derive insights. Following Hossain et al . (2022), we used a four-item scale to measure AI adoption on the platform, asking ESME managers how AI helps design products and services, manage and optimize supply chains and reduce shipping costs and transit time, accurate predictions of inventory demand and accelerates the strategic decision process. Strategic agility was measured through eight statements using a framework adapted from Tallon and Pinsonneault (2011), which emphasizes the speed and flexibility in leveraging AI-driven learnings to achieve specific goals and tasks on the platform. The items for strategic agility, such as respond to changes in consumer demand, customize products to suit an individual customer, introduce new product or pricing schedules in response to changes in competitors, expand into new international markets and adopt new technologies to produce better (see Table 4).

Mediating variable. We employed a five-item IMCs scale, adapted from Yang et al. (2019) and validated by Zahoor and Lew (2023), which covers product modification according to the export market, switching from the current export market, modifying existing products and resources to meet export market needs and switching the uses and applications of resources.

Dependent variables. While product innovation and brand upgrading advantages were measured using scales from Cui and Wu (2017) and Spyropoulou et al . (2011), respectively. The product innovation advantage was evaluated using four indicators of innovativeness, specifically the degree to which a new product is novel to the industry and introduces original concepts. And the brand upgrading advantage was assessed using a four-item scale to evaluate the brand position of an ESMEs' offering in the export market, focusing on brand image, awareness, 'share of mind' and personality, relative to its major competitor.

Moderating variables. Platform embedding characteristics were divided into two dimensions: breadth, which refers to the extent to which firms engage with multiple digital platforms and depth, reflecting the intensity of collaboration with these platforms (Kobarg et al ., 2019; Mariani and Nambisan, 2021). To assess platform embedding breadth and depth, we modified scales from Xiao et al . (2020), originally designed for international networks of ESMEs and combined them with measures from Kobarg et al . (2019) to evaluate managers' perceptions of their company's platform embeddedness in terms of breadth and depth. Breadth and depth were calculated by the scores of the three and four items per construct, respectively. The key items are listed in Table 4.

Control variables. Additionally, to mitigate potential biases and keep them in line with prior literature, we incorporated relevant control variables. Respondents provided information on both firm-level and individual-level characteristics. Specifically, firm size, firm age, ownership type, industry category, international sales and profit were included in the research model. Furthermore, we controlled for individual factors such as gender, age, education level, position and tenure.

## 5.1 Measurement model

We assessed the reliability, content validity, convergent validity and discriminant validity of the measurement model using several statistical methods. For reliability testing, we used Cronbach's alpha and composite reliability (CR). The Cronbach's alpha values for the constructs ranged from 0.799 to 0.889, while the CR values varied from 0.801 to 0.890 (see Table 4). All the metrics exceeded the recommended threshold of 0.7, indicating good reliability (Hair et al ., 2010). Next, we examined the factor loadings, which, ideally, should be greater than 0.7, with a minimum acceptable threshold of 0.6 (Fornell and Larcker, 1981). The factor loadings for the remaining items ranged from 0.673 to 0.876, with all exceeding the minimum threshold of 0.65.

CFA was validated in two stages: (1) assessing goodness-of-fit indices and (2) assessing construct validity (Hair et al ., 2010). The results revealed that the data fit the model well ( χ 2 /df 5 1.361; goodness of fit index (GFI) 5 0.900; comparative fit index (CFI) 5 0.967; TuckerLewis index (TLI) 5 0.964; incremental fit index (IFI) 5 0.968; root mean square error of approximation (RMSEA) 5 0.034). The χ 2 /df exceeded the minimum requirement of 1:3, while the values for GFI, IFI, TLI and CFI all surpassed the recommended threshold of ≥ 0.90. RMSEA also meet the criteria of <0.05 (see Table 3).

In the second stage, we validated content validity, convergent and discriminant validity. First, the measurement items are all derived from mature scales published in authoritative journals and have been verified, thus ensuring the measurement tool's content validity. Second, the average variance extracted (AVE) values for all constructs ranged from 0.503 to 0.708, surpassing the 0.50 threshold, which indicates adequate convergent validity (see Table 4). Third, to assess discriminant validity, we utilized the heterotrait-monotrait (HTMT) ratio method. We found that the square roots of the AVEs (shown on the diagonal of the matrix) were greater than the correlations between constructs, thereby confirming discriminant validity (Fornell and Larcker, 1981) (see Table 5).

## 5.3 Testing for response and non-response

We followed Armstrong and Overton's (1977) procedure to test for non-response bias by comparing the first and last 25% of responses across key variables. We conducted t -tests on diverse organizational characteristics, such as industry type and firm size, to compare the two groups. The t -test results indicated no statistically significant differences between the two subsamples in terms of industry type ( t 5 � 0.362, p 5 0.719) and firm size ( t 5 � 1.271, p 5 0.208), indicating that no systematic non-response bias was present for the data collected.

## 5.4 Common method bias

To address potential common method bias (CMB) from single-informant data, we implemented several preventive approaches. During data collection, we included redundant questions in the questionnaire to minimise simultaneity bias and the effects of common method variance (CMV) (Chang et al. , 2010). Additionally, respondents were kept unaware of the study's objectives to reduce evaluation concerns and social desirability bias and maintain the integrity of their responses. To further assess CMB, we conducted Harman's single-factor test, which revealed that a single factor accounted for only 33.08% of the total variance -well below the 50% threshold (Podsakoff et al. , 2003), indicating minimal CMB risk. Moreover, the results of the single latent factor analysis ( χ 2 /df 5 6.151; RMSEA 5 0.127; GFI 5 0.571; CFI 5 0.516; TLI 5 0.483; IFI 5 0.519) differed from those of the six-factor confirmatory model ( χ 2 /df 5 1.361; RMSEA 5 0.034; GFI 5 0.900; CFI 5 0.967; TLI 5 0.964; IFI 5 0.968).

We employ Kock's (2015) full collinearity test to complement the previously suggested collinearity test. According to Kock and Lynn (2012), collinearity is typically indicated when the variance inflation factor (VIF) exceeds 3.3, suggesting the presence of CMV. In our analysis, all VIF values remain below this threshold (VIF<1.101), confirming that any potential effects of CMV are negligible. In addition, to establish the validity and reliability of the higher-order construct of coupling of AI adoption and strategic agility, we first assessed the weights of the formative lower-order constructs on their corresponding higher-order constructs (comprising two first-order constructs). All weights were significant and positively associated with their assigned higher-order construct. Next, we examined potential multicollinearity among the firstorder constructs. All VIF values for the first-order constructs were below the 3.3 threshold, indicating that multicollinearity was not a concern within our sample (see Table 9).

## 6. Discussion

## 6.1 Discussion of results

Digital technologies have significantly influenced international business, and SMEs have increasingly leveraged digital platforms to expand internationally in recent years (Li et al ., 2019). These platforms, often multi-sided or two-sided, connect foreign customers with independent SME providers. Beyond becoming important conduits for international transactions, cross-border platforms serve as vital marketing channels for resourceconstrained ESMEs, enabling them to secure a favourable global position in both product and brand. Our study investigates the role of AI adoption and strategic agility of platforms in promoting the international competitive advantage of ESMEs. We found that the coupling of AI adoption and strategic agility allows ESMEs to identify target foreign international opportunities and access market-specific insights and resources for product innovation and brand upgrading. Moreover, our results indicate that IMCs mediate the relationship between this coupling and two key international advantages. ESMEs can develop their IMCs through digital platforms to fulfil foreign market customers' expressed and latent needs, thus promoting international competitiveness.

More importantly, the results highlight that platform embedding characteristics are crucial moderating factors. Specifically, embedding breadth has an inverted U-shaped moderating effect on the relationship between the coupling of AI with strategic agility and brand upgrading advantage. That is, embedding breadth improves the effect of coupling AI with strategic agility on brand upgrading up to an optimal point (at a medium level). We also find that embedding depth has an inverted U-shaped moderating effect on the relationship between the coupling of AI with strategic agility and product innovation advantage. In other words, the effect of embedding depth on product innovation advantage becomes stronger when the depth of enterprise embeddedness in the platform is from low to medium, while when the depth of platform embeddedness is from medium to high, the impact of coupling on brand upgrading advantages weakens. These findings imply that while network engagement is beneficial, excessive breadth or depth does not always yield superior performance.

## 6.2 Theoretical implications

Building on research examining internationalization through digital platforms, this study's objective was to try to understand if and under what conditions cross-border platforms can enhance ESMEs' GVC positioning. To address this question, we consider cross-border platforms from the standpoint of key resources and capabilities that offer ESMEs competitive advantages. This study makes four significant theoretical contributions.

First , our study is one of the first to explore the coupling of AI adoption and strategic agility within digital platform reshaping product innovation and brand upgrading, providing insights for ESMEs to overcome the 'low-end lock-in' in GVCs and achieving global sustainable development goals. To advance the theoretical understanding of firms' internationalization from emerging markets through digital platforms, we present a second-order aggregate concept examining the coupled effects of AI adoption and strategic agility. Specifically, this study extends existing research on digitalization and SME internationalization, responding to calls for deeper exploration of the intersection between digital platforms and internationalization (Kim and Cavusgil, 2020; Luo, 2021). While existing research has explored key internationalization outcomes arising from the use of digital platforms, such as cost-effective market entry, international speed, scope and performance (Coviello et al ., 2017; Li et al ., 2019; Gong et al ., 2024), it also highlights the risk of a 'virtuality trap' for platform, raising concerns about the ESMEs' sustainable competitive advantage (Da Rocha et al ., 2024). Our research further examines the higher-level impact of digital platforms on internationalization efforts by introducing the distinctive process of AI adoption coupled with strategic agility, which enables ESMEs despite lacking strategic assets like proprietary technologies, global brands and market power, to real-time sense international market shifts and creatively leverage resources from cross-border channels and cooperative networks to upgrade their value chain position in global competition.

[S. 26]

Second , the study extends the strategic agility literature by examining how digital platforms utilize agility not just as a high-level dynamic capability, but also as a strategic response to AIenabled predictive insights and decision-making. While existing research has emphasized internal processes and strategies that enable agility, such as decision-making flexibility, adaptive leadership, exploitation and exploration strategies (Ahammad et al ., 2021; Shams et al ., 2021), this study advances understanding by demonstrating how AI adoption necessitates the development of agile capabilities. AI-enabled platforms enhance ESME's ability to sense environmental shifts, reconfigure resources and respond with agility -key tenets of the dynamic capability framework (Teece et al. , 1997, 2016). While Strategic agility, defined as a firm's ability to rapidly adapt strategies in response to external changes, traditionally emphasizes the resultant impact on the firm's international performance (Ahammad et al ., 2021). The study demonstrates how synthesizing vast data and identifying actionable signals enhances strategic foresight and responsiveness in rapidly evolving demand patterns and customer needs. This contribution clarifies how combining agility with AI enables ESMEs to create and capture value in global environments marked by uncertainty, institutional inefficiency and market volatility.

Third , following the dynamic capability hierarchy view, the findings evidence the existence of a robust mechanism of the IMCs in the relationships between the coupling of AI adoption and strategy and international competitive advantage (Zollo and Winter, 2002; Winter, 2003; Schulze and Brusoni, 2022). By providing empirical insight into higher-order dynamic capabilities that drive 'regular' operational capabilities for GVC upgrading, this research offers a significant opportunity to advance the dynamic interaction relationship between digital platforms and firms (Eng and Spickett-Jones, 2009). Previous research has examined the antecedents of digital platform use in internationalization, focusing on internal factors such as firm resources and capabilities, as well as organizational factors, including marketing and international orientation (e.g. Li et al ., 2019; Da Rocha et al ., 2024; Gong et al ., 2024). We concentrate on IMCs as specific operational capabilities, such as global rebranding and new product innovation, instead of only one general IMC, where IMCs reflect the processes ESMEs learn and implement to handle strategic marketing changes.

Additionally, this study also responds to Pfajfar et al . (2024b), who advocate for the application of IMCs within intricate GVCs by highlighting the critical role of robust IMCs in enabling product innovation and brand upgrading within the context of emerging economies (Eng and Spickett-Jones, 2009). Existing IMC research has predominantly focused on developed economies (e.g. Zahoor and Lew, 2023); studies in emerging economies provide insights by exploring the direct relationship between focal capabilities and company adaptiveness, export performance and financial performance (Pfajfar et al ., 2024a). Our findings extend AI-driven marketing capabilities to international business research, particularly when a company enters advanced internationalization but still has not attained global corporate status.

Finally , our study examines the nonlinear moderating role of platform embedding breadth and depth in the direct relationship between coupling AI adoption and strategic agility and two distinct outcomes: product innovation advantage and brand upgrading advantage. existing research has pointed out that the boundary conditions of internationalization were mostly focused on macro-level contingencies, such as institutional pressures, foreign market uncertainty and country-specific environmental factors (e.g. Adomako et al ., 2019; Ko et al ., 2021). However, little is known about how platform embedding characteristics shape the effectiveness of AI adoption and strategic agility. This nuanced understanding provides a more refined model for theoretical predictions and enriches the academic discourse on the embedding breadth and depth and its role in shaping ESMEs' product innovation and brand upgrading advantage.

We observed embedding depth has an inverted U-shaped moderating effect on the positive relationship between coupling AI adoption and strategic agility and product innovation advantage; however, it does not moderate the relationship between coupling AI adoption with strategic agility and brand upgrading. The findings suggest that greater depth in platform embeddedness facilitates the transfer of tacit knowledge generated by AI, thus reducing the risk of product innovation and enhancing the exploratory level for innovation. However, overreliance on specific platforms limits access to diverse market information, creating a 'familiarity trap' that hinders the reallocation of resources necessary for product innovation. This pattern is consistent with Kobarg et al.' s (2019) observation of a similar inverted U-shaped relationship between collaboration depth and radical innovation. Similarly, the impact of embedding breadth on brand upgrading follows an inverted U-shape. ESMEs embedded across multiple platforms enable SMEs to harness platform-specific differences, tailor brand strategies and deliver targeted brand messages underpinned by platform positioning and user preferences, driving overall brand exposure. However, excessive embedding risks a 'learning trap', where limited attention allocation hinders the integration of fragmented multi-platform information, potentially leading to inconsistent brand messages received by users, thus damaging the brand image. This finding extends the research of Xiao et al. (2020), who highlighted the significance of international network breadth in enhancing global performance. Our findings cautioned that an excessive network scope may dilute brand focus, potentially undermining brand equity.

## 6.3 Practical implications

The findings of this study carry several crucial implications for managers striving to GVC upgrade via digital platforms. First , owners of cross-border platforms should recognize AI adoption and strategic agility as critical drivers of participants' product innovation and brand upgrading. AI adoption significantly enhances the platform's potential for identifying international market opportunities by collecting, integrating, analyzing and leveraging user and international market knowledge. This enables efficient and effective redeployment of their resources to value-creating and value-capturing higher-yield activities as AI-driven insight warrants. However, AI adoption can be costly and needs to be married to a sound strategy and agility. AI provides direction and goals for agility, while agility supports the effective implementation of AI-driven insight. Without considering such a coupling relationship, the value and necessity of AI adoption cannot be properly presented. Therefore, incorporating strategic agility throughout AI adoption processes is crucial, ensuring a smooth creation and protecting the value of new digital tools.

Second , IMC is a key mechanism through which the coupling of AI adoption and strategic agility affects product innovation and brand strategies. ESMEs should build efficient capabilities to cater to international customers' requirements, develop cross-functional marketing processes specific to foreign markets, innovate routines and appeal to local clients while maintaining global brand identity. This approach enables them to effectively translate market-specific insights gained through digital platforms into actionable global product and brand strategies. For example, this can be achieved through platform data assetization, intelligent marketing systems and modular product platforms. Consequently, ESMEs should optimize their IMCs to develop a set of dynamic marketing skills and competencies through responsive and efficient cross-functional market processes to adapt to the changing global market environments, competing products and distribution channels and customer needs.

[S. 28]

Third, managers should carefully consider product attributes and brand positioning needs when selecting cross-border platforms that align with their target foreign markets. In particular, striking a balance between platform embedding breadth and depth is essential. Platforms are called 'two-sided markets' or 'multi-sided markets', equipped with mature marketing tools and global resource networks to expand ESMEs' international marketing channels and product international visibility through search and promotion functions. However, excessive diversification across multiple platforms complicates market information management, while over-reliance on a single platform constrains strategic autonomy. To optimize international competitiveness, our results indicate that when embedding breadth at a medium level, coupling AI with strategic agility significantly benefits brand upgrading. This finding suggests that ESMEs aiming to enhance their brand advantages should appropriately maintain cooperative relationships with multiple cross-border platforms. If they prefer to pursue product innovation advantages, ESMEs should prioritize establishing close collaborative relationships over-diversification, as economies of scope provide limited applicability in platform-based new product development context.

## 6.4 Limitations and future research

While this study provides an initial step in understanding how AI coupling with strategic agility should be organized within platforms and how it drives international value creation, this research has certain limitations that offer avenues for future research. Our findings are based on data from SMEs in China. While this region provides a relevant and timely context due to its proactive stance on digital platform use in internationalization, the results may not be fully generalizable to other geographical settings or international models. Future studies could test the robustness of our framework and compare cross-national contexts to assess how regulatory, cultural and infrastructural variations influence the effects of AI-enabled strategic agility and SMEs' international competitive advantage.

In addition, while we recognized platform embedding characteristics by studying the moderating role of embedding breadth and depth, other characteristics of digital platforms may also affect their outcomes in internationalization. This suggests fruitful opportunities to examine additional platform characteristics, particularly typological variations such as sales, interaction and innovation platforms operating in domestic versus foreign contexts. Specifically, future work should therefore investigate how SMEs strategically deploy distinct platform types to achieve specific objectives, particularly investigating how interaction platforms drive brand upgrading while sales platforms facilitate product innovation.

Finally, self-reported data from SME managers regarding platform AI adoption and agility perceptions risks introducing bias. Future studies should incorporate secondary data for objective measures to strengthen the robustness and reliability of the results. Addressing this limitation would advance understanding of how cross-border platforms enhance SMEs' postentry competitive advantage across diverse contexts and conditions. Furthermore, the evolving nature of AI technologies introduces temporal limitations, as current platform tools and implementation strategies may soon become obsolete. Future scholars might investigate longitudinal studies to track the evolution of AI infrastructures and their strategic integration over time, assessing whether their impacts on marketing and innovation remain stable or fluctuate in response to technological advancements and market developments.

We address the underexplored coupling role of AI adoption and strategic agility, and its impact on product innovation and brand upgrading. These effects International Marketing Review
