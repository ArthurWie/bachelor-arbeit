## 1. Introduction

In the late 1980s, American scholar Strassman surveyed 292 enterprises and found a strange phenomenon. There was no obvious correlation between the IT investment of these enterprises and the return on investment (ROI). Robert Solow, an economist who won the Nobel Prize in 1987, called this phenomenon the "productivity paradox": "Computers everywhere except in the productivity statistics." The central argument is  that  despite  the  enormous  advances made  by firms  in  the field  of science and technology, technological progress has not been translated into corresponding productivity gains throughout the economic system [1].  According  to  Solow,  there  are  three  broad  reasons  for  this  phenomenon. One is the delayed effect, which means that technological innovations  usually  take  some  time  to  fully  affect  productivity.  The second is the problem of resource allocation. If there are problems in resource allocation by firms or Governments, this may affect the application  and  diffusion  of  new  technologies,  thus  limiting  productivity gains [2]. Thirdly, there is the issue of measurement. In emerging industries and high-technology areas, traditional productivity measurement methods may be difficult and inaccurate, resulting in a failure to scientifically reflect the benefits of technological progress. Some viewpoints suggest that IT investment seems to fail to significantly stimulate enterprise productivity in some cases. Therefore, revealing the impact of AI on firm productivity is an important topic to analyze whether Solow ' s paradox can be valid in the digital age. Based on panel data of 3235 listed companies in China from 2007 to 2021, we comprehensively discuss the impact and mechanism of AI on firm productivity using fixed-effects model, systematic GMM model, and mediated-effects model. Key findings include: AI significantly improves firm  productivity,  especially  in  state-controlled,  internationally  minded,  and  innovative  firms.  Mitigating  information asymmetry is a key channel, while specialized division of labor and independent green innovation are potential ones. Supply chain digital transformation policies enhance the productivity effect of AI, and AI shows green  development  benefits.  Additionally,  the  dynamic  decomposition  effect  shows  that  the  productivityenhancing effect of AI is slowing down in the long run. This research provides important insights into understanding AI ' s role in the digital age and holds significance for firms and policymakers.

Building on this foundation, Madan and Ashok [6] further explored issues related to the adoption and proliferation of AI in public administration through a systematic literature review. The study points out that the  implementation of AI involves technological, organizational, and environmental factors and that there are tensions between automation and augmentation, facilitation and autonomy, and data accessibility and security and privacy. Strategic use is where managers need to think about choosing the right place in the business model architecture for  AI  to  leverage  the  network  effect  of  data  to  attract  customers, improve  service,  prevent  customer  migration  and  achieve  sustained performance excellence [12]. Therefore,  the  first  key  research  question  and  innovation  of  this study is  to  explore  the  impact  of  AI  development  on  productivity  of Chinese firms at the empirical level. The scope of this paper is the panel data formed by A-share listed companies in China from 2007 to 2021, and the focus is to explore the extent to which AI can affect firm productivity from a firm perspective. Why do we say this is the first point of innovation? This is because past research has focused on the impact of information and communication technology (ICT) on productivity at the macro-provincial or municipal level. For example, Wang and Guo [14] explored the positive effects of ICT on green total factor productivity in local cities  and the negative productivity impacts on neighboring regions, using 282 cities in China as the scope of the study. Erumban [15] demonstrated  the  proved  impact  of  ICT  on  labor  productivity,  with lower transaction costs as the path, using a study sample of more than 100 countries around the world from 1997 to 2017.Compared to AI, ICT is  an  earlier  and  macroscopic  concept  that  covers  both  information technology  and  communication  technology  [16,17].  Different  from previous  studies,  this  study  constructs  a  thesaurus  based  on  crawler technology by crawling the words related to "artificial intelligence" in the annual reports of listed companies, and then summarizes the word frequencies and takes the logarithm to get the AI level of the companies. Further, we calculated the firm ' s productivity level using four methods such  as  Ordinary  Least  Squares  (OLS),  Fixed  Effects  (FE),  Luhnberg Productivity Indicator (LP), and Optimal Productivity Indicator (OP). On this basis, this study for the first time empirically focuses the research perspective from macro-cities and countries to enterprises, proving the positive effect of AI on enterprise productivity.

Second, what factors actually play a mediating effect between AI and firm productivity? How do China ' s current digitalization and environmental policies affect the relationship between the two? The solution to these two key questions is the second innovation of this study. This is because previous studies have focused on the role of factors such as human capital, operating costs, and digital transformation as channels between the two [18,19]. The mediating role of the above categories of factors  is  not  an  intuitive  effect  of  AI,  and  its  transmission  role  is doubtful. Instead, we focus on exploring the prominent role of AI in mitigating  firms ' information  asymmetry  based  on  AI ' s  information properties. At the same time, we consider that AI may be able to promote social  specialization  and  technological  innovation,  which  are  two channels we are expanding. Importantly, the key to boosting firm productivity is the efficient operation and integration of supply chains [20]. Therefore, we analyze the moderating role of supply chain pilot policies on  the  productivity  effects  of  AI.  Of  course,  if  firms  are  affected  by environmental regulations, their  productivity enhancement strategies are bound to suffer [21]. Therefore, low-carbon pilot policies are also taken into account by us. In addition, we discuss in detail the heterogeneous effects of AI on firm productivity moderated by the nature of the firm, the international perspective of the leader, and the sense of innovation,  enriching  the  heterogeneity  analysis  perspectives  of  existing studies.

In addition, there may be a temporal transmission effect of historical information infrastructure developments on current AI technology advances.  Unfortunately,  this  potential  moderating  effect  has  not  been explored. Therefore, this study aims to address this issue and presents it as the third innovation. Specifically, we innovatively consider the differential impact of historical information infrastructure development on the productivity effect of AI. We use the Ming Dynasty stagecoach as a proxy variable for the historical impact, which is justified by the fact that the stagecoach, as a major transportation route and information transit station,  facilitated  the  transfer  of  information  and  the  circulation  of knowledge, and inspired people to think about intelligent management [22]. In addition, differences in the level of productivity of the firms themselves  can  also  interfere  with  the  impact  of  AI.  Therefore,  we further discuss the differential impact of AI on firms ' productivity at different  productivity  levels  using  a  panel  quantile  regression  model (PQRM) [23]. Importantly, the impact of AI on productivity cannot be static. When discussing the impact of ICT or digital technology, previous studies tend to focus only on the static impact of both [24,25]. Instead, we further analyze the dynamic impact of AI on productivity based on a panel vector autoregressive (PVAR) model.

Firm productivity is affected by a variety of factors. Enterprise productivity refers to the efficiency and ability of an enterprise to produce goods or provide services over a given period of time [34]. It is usually measured in terms of the ratio of outputs to inputs, such as the number of products produced per unit of time and the value created per unit of resources. An increase in productivity of an enterprise means that the enterprise  can  utilize  its  resources  more  efficiently  and  obtain  more output with less inputs,  thus  improving  its  competitiveness  and  economic efficiency. Specific measurement indicators can vary according to different industries and production characteristics [35]. Policy factors are one of the factors that most directly affect firm productivity and are most  frequently  discussed  in  academia.  Zhang  et  al.  [36]  use  fiscal subsidies and tax incentives as two proxy variables for policy analysis to explore the productivity effects of the two types of policies on Chinese industrial firms. The results show that both types of policies have positive productivity effects, with tax incentives clearly having a stronger stimulus effect. The impact of cleaner production on firms ' production performance  is  also  evident.  Cleaner  production  practices  by  firms enhance productivity through increased demand for highly skilled labor, technological  advances  and  equipment  upgrades  [37].  The  role  of environmental regulation is also not negligible. Wang et al. [4] used China ' s environmental tax as a representative of environmental regulation, and used the DID model to assess the role of environmental tax reform on firm productivity. The results found that environmental taxes hinder substantial innovation of firms and are not conducive to productivity enhancement.

[3], based on panel data from 201 cities in China, demonstrate the positive impact of digital  finance on carbon productivity, with human capital and marketization  as  important  channels. ## 3. Theoretical analysis and hypothesis formulation

First  of  all,  we  would  like  to  discuss  the  reasons  why  AI  works directly  on  enterprise  production  and  improves its  productivity from multiple perspectives. Overall, AI plays an important role in improving enterprise productivity and can help enterprises achieve higher levels of automation, intelligence and personalized production, thereby increasing competitiveness and gaining greater market share [47].

Specifically, AI can directly empower enterprise production in five ways. First, AI can promote the automation and intelligent upgrading of enterprise production.AI technology can be used to automate repetitive tasks and processes, thereby reducing the need for human resources and lowering the error rate. Through intelligent production processes, enterprises can manage resources and time more efficiently [48]. Second, AI has powerful data analysis and prediction functions. AI can analyze large amounts of data and extract valuable information to help enterprises  make  more  accurate  predictions  and  decisions.  By  analyzing market trends, customer demand and supply chain data, companies can better  plan  production  schedules  and  resource  allocation  to  improve productivity [49]. Third, AI can help companies with quality control and preventive maintenance.AI technology can monitor key parameters in the production process in real time and identify potential problems or anomalies in a timely manner. Through real-time monitoring and predictive  maintenance,  companies  can  reduce  equipment  downtime, improve productivity, and ensure that product quality meets standards [50]. Fourth, AI can provide personalized production and customization services.AI can realize personalized production according to customer needs and preferences, thus improving the market competitiveness of products.  Through  the  intelligent  production  system,  enterprises  can adjust their production lines more flexibly, respond quickly to market changes, and meet the personalized needs of customers [51]. Fifth, AI has human resource optimization capabilities. ai technology can help enterprises optimize the allocation and management of human resources and improve employee efficiency and satisfaction. Through automation and intelligent tools, enterprises can reduce the investment of human resources in low value-added tasks, thus allowing employees to focus more  on  innovation  and  high-value  work  [4].  In  summary,  through literature  analysis  and  theoretical  elaboration,  AI  can  promote  automation  and  intelligent  upgrading  of  enterprise  production,  provide powerful data analysis and prediction functions, facilitate quality control and preventive maintenance, realize personalized production and customized  services,  and  optimize  human  resources,  thus  directly enhancing  enterprise  productivity.  In  short,  Hypothesis  1  can  be formulated and confirmed pending empirical analysis in Section 5.

## H1 . AI has a direct and positive impact on firm productivity.

It is important that we consider the main channels through which AI improves productivity. The first and primary channel is to mitigate information  asymmetry.  Information  asymmetry  is  a  market  in  which there  is  an  information  discrepancy  between  buyers  and  sellers  that results  in  unequal  or  inefficient  transactions [52]. Given AI ' s  unique advantage  in  processing  massive  amounts  of  information,  it  is  irreplaceable in alleviating enterprise information asymmetry. First, AI can help enterprises predict market demand more accurately by analyzing big  data  to  discover  potential  market  trends  and  consumer  behavior patterns. This prediction capability can reduce the uncertainty of market information and lower the degree of information asymmetry for enterprises [53]. Second, AI can personalize marketing and services based on customers ' historical behaviors and preferences, improving the match between products and services. This personalization capability can increase  customers ' trust  and  loyalty  to  the  company  and  reduce  the impact  of  information  asymmetry  [54].  Third,  AI  can  monitor  the market and customer feedback in real time and adjust production plans and marketing strategies in a timely manner. This real-time monitoring and feedback mechanism can reduce the lag in the transmission of information  and  reduce  the  degree  of  information  asymmetry  [55]. Finally,  AI  can  optimize  supply  chain  management  and  improve  the efficiency and reliability of production and distribution. By monitoring and forecasting all aspects of the supply chain in real time, AI can reduce information  asymmetry  caused  by  supply  chain  problems  [56].  In summary, AI improves productivity by alleviating information asymmetry,  specifically  by  analyzing  big  data  to  more  accurately  predict market demand, personalize marketing and services, monitor market and customer feedback in real time, and optimize supply chain management. Thus, hypothesis 2a can be formulated.

H2a . AI improves firm productivity by reducing information asymmetry.

## 4.2. Variables ' definition

## 4.2.1. Dependent variable

The  explanatory  variable  of  this  study  is  firm  productivity  (TFP) [62]. Total Factor Productivity (TFP) is an important indicator of the overall  productivity  of  a  firm,  and  there  are  various  methods  for calculating TFP, including Ordinary Least Squares (OLS), Fixed Effects (FE), Luenberger Productivity Indicator (LP), Optimal Productivity Indicator (OP) and four other common methods. This study uses the FE method for estimation. The FE model is based on panel data, where the same set of firms are observed over a period of time and their production data are collected. The FE model assumes the existence of fixed, but unobserved, characteristics of each firm, which may affect the firm ' s productivity. Thus, the FE model models individual differences within firms  through  fixed  effects  terms,  thus  providing  more  accurate  productivity  estimates.  Importantly,  because  the  FE  model  controls  for within-firm fixed effects, it is able to reduce the impact of omitted variable bias. This contributes to the accuracy and robustness of the estimates.  In  addition,  the  FE  model  retains  the  effect  of  time-invariant factors on the comparisons and therefore better explains the relationship between productivity changes within firms and changes in the external environment.

## 4.2.3. Control variables

Based on previous studies [63], the following control variables were selected in this study to help exclude other factors from confounding the relationship of the variables, thus improving the accuracy and explanatory power of the model. Firm size ( AST ) is expressed as the logarithm of total assets. Return on Assets ( ROA ) is the ratio of net income to total assets. Cash reserve ( CASH ) is funds plus trading financial assets as a percentage of total assets. The gearing ratio ( TL )  is  expressed  as  the share of liabilities as a percentage of total assets. Total Asset Growth Rate ( TAGR ) is a measure of the percentage increase in the total asset size of a business or organization over time. Shareholding concentration ( TOP )  is  expressed  as  the  percentage  of  shares  held  by  the  largest shareholder. Segregation of shares ( SEP ) is the percentage of shares held by the controller.

## 4.2.4. Intermediary variables

Based on the analysis of Hypothesis 2, we test the intrinsic mechanism of AI acting on firm productivity in this section. The mechanism variables for this study are shown below.

Information Asymmetry (ASY). The IA indicators in this study are based on the liquidity ratio LR [64], the illiquidity ratio ILL [65], and the yield inversion indicator GAM [66], and are obtained by principal component analysis. The selection of the liquidity ratio LR, the illiquidity ratio ILL and the yield inversion measure GAM as  indicators  of  information  asymmetry  is  a  common  practice based on relevant academic research [67].These indicators can reflect  the  trading  activity,  liquidity  situation  and  abnormal changes in returns in the market, thus indirectly reflecting the degree of information asymmetry. Through principal component analysis,  the  information  from  these  indicators  can  be  synthesized to provide a more comprehensive measure of information asymmetry.

Specialized division of labor (SD).SD is measured using the valueadded method to indicate the extent to which an enterprise influences the division of labor in the industrial chain. The calculation method is shown in Eq. (5). The value-added method is used  to  measure  the  division  of  specialization  because  it  can reflect an enterprise ' s ability to process and transform resources in the production process, thereby reflecting its position in the industrial chain and the extent of its influence on the division of labor. This method has been widely used in research in related fields and has a certain degree of academic rationality [68].

Green Innovation (GI). GI is represented by the number of green patents independently filed by firms in the year. The number of green patents independently filed by enterprises is used to indicate green innovation because green patents can directly reflect the  innovative  achievements  of  enterprises  in  environmental

protection and sustainable development [69]. Green innovation is  of  great  significance  to  the  sustainable  development  and competitiveness of enterprises, so it is reasonable to choose the number of green patents as an indicator [16].

## 5.3. Robustness testing

We use two ways to robust the findings, the first is to change the dimensionality of the fixed effects. Column (1) in Table 4 shows the estimation  results  after  adding  city  fixed  effects.  Controlling  for  city fixed effects removes interference from specific influences that may exist across  cities.  For  example,  factors  such  as  the  level  of  economic development,  industrial  structure,  and  market  size  of  different  cities may have an impact on the business conditions of firms, and by controlling  for  city  fixed  effects,  these  region-specific  effects  can  be excluded to more accurately assess the impact of AI on firm productivity. Column (2) in Table 4 presents the estimated results of the interaction effects controlling for industry and year. Controlling for industry and year interaction effects reduces omitted variable bias due to unobserved industry-  and  time-specific  factors,  which  helps  to  improve  model credibility and makes the findings more robust and reliable. It can be seen that the estimated results of the fixed effects model using different dimensions pass the test, and the coefficients and significance of the effect  of  AI  on  productivity  do  not  change  significantly.  Further,  we consider the possible endogeneity problem of the model. The endogeneity problem is a common challenge in business research, where certain variables may be correlated with the error term, leading to biased OLS estimates.  GMM  can  solve  the  endogeneity  problem  by  instrumental variables, which effectively controls the bias caused by endogeneity and improves the accuracy and consistency of estimation. Since firms ' productivity is likely to be affected by the previous period, we propose to reestimate the effect of AI on productivity by using the system generalized moments estimation model (SYS-GMM). This is because the SYS-GMM model is able to  estimate  the  parameters  of  the  dynamic  panel  data model more accurately  and  reduce  the  bias  of  parameter  estimation compared to the traditional panel data estimation methods. By introducing instrumental variables and controlling time series correlation, the SYS-GMM model is able to improve the accuracy and robustness of parameter estimation, making the results of the study more credible. Column (3) reports the estimation results for SYS-GMM, and our findings still hold after overcoming the endogeneity problem.

## 6.4. Differential productivity effects

Consider that the firm productivity base is also an important factor influencing  the  developmental  effects  of  AI.  Therefore,  we  further employ a panel quantile regression model to explore the regression results for productivity located at different quantiles. Columns (1) -(4) in Table 10 represent the effect of AI on productivity at the 25 %, 50 %, 75 %, and 95 % quartiles, respectively. It can be seen that the productivityenhancing effect of AI development across firms does not differ significantly below the 75 % quartile. The productivity enhancement effect of AI is significantly larger when firms ' productivity is distributed above 75 %. This suggests that firms with a high productivity base tend to be larger, with more production lines, customer bases and market shares. These firms can achieve greater economic gains through scale effects after the introduction of AI technology, resulting in larger productivity gains. In addition, this group of enterprises may be in a more competitive market environment where they need to continuously improve their productivity to remain competitive. As a result, high-productivity organizations are more inclined to adopt the latest technologies, such as AI technologies, to increase productivity and achieve market share growth.

## 6.5. Dynamic impact decomposition

Panel Vector Autoregression (PVAR) models take into account both time-series and cross-sectional features in panel data and are better able to capture the dynamic relationship between AI and productivity. By introducing lagged terms in the direction of the time series, the PVAR model can explore both short- and long-run dynamic effects between the variables [83]. We first determine the lag order to be first order by the Akaike  Information  Criterion  (AIC),  Bayesian  Information  Criterion (BIC) and Modified Akaike Information Criterion (HQIC) together. Then, Fig. 5 reports the dynamic impact of AI on productivity. The impulse response results show that the instantaneous shock of AI positively affects TFP and reaches its maximum in period 2, then gradually falls back by period 10. Our findings validate the initial effect of technological innovation. At the initial stage of AI technology introduction, firms may invest  significant  resources  in  technological  innovation  and  transformation, leading to rapid productivity gains. The application of new technologies may improve productivity, optimize resource allocation, and enable firms to achieve higher levels of productivity in the short term. Over time, AI technology is gradually adopted and popularized by other enterprises, and the marginal effect of the technology gradually diminishes [84]. At the same time, the impact of AI on TFP may gradually diminish as market competition intensifies and the differentiation advantage between firms gradually shrinks.

## 7.1. Academic implications

This study sheds light on the impact of AI on firm productivity and its mechanisms at the micro level, providing new empirical evidence for research in the field of AI. Unlike previous studies that focus on the macro-level impact of ICT and digital technologies, we fill a gap in the existing  literature  by  focusing  on  firm-level  AI  applications.  For example, while Pan et al. [85] show that digital technology has a positive impact on firms ' total factor productivity, our study enriches this area by further refining the specific role of AI on firms ' productivity.

By verifying the direct productivity-enhancing effect of AI on firms and mechanisms such as information asymmetry mitigation, this study expands the  understanding of  the  intrinsic  mechanisms  by  which  AI affects  firm  performance.  This  is  similar  to  Alesina  and  Zhuravskaya [61] ' s study, which suggests that certain mechanisms play a dominant role  in  a  particular  phenomenon,  and  our  finding  that  information asymmetry is the main channel through which AI enhances firm productivity provides an important theoretical foundation for subsequent research.

The findings in this study on the moderating role of supply chain innovation policies and low-carbon pilot city policies on the productivity effect of AI, as well as the green development effect of AI, complement other studies. For example, Wei et al. [80] confirm the role of supply  chain  digital  transformation  in  enhancing  firms ' innovation performance,  and  our  study  further  reveals  the  moderating  effect  of supply chain innovation policies in the relationship between AI and firm productivity. In addition, Cheng et al. [40] found that AI enhances firms ' environmental  protection  capabilities,  and  our  study  specifically  addresses the facilitating effect of AI on firms ' green innovation, especially the significant impact on joint green innovation, which provides a new perspective for research in the field of green innovation.

## 7.2. Practitioner implications

For enterprises, the results of this study provide clear guidance for their  application  of  AI  technology.  Enterprises  should  recognize  the important role of AI in improving productivity and realizing automated and  intelligent  production,  and  actively  introduce  AI  technology.  In particular, state-owned enterprises and enterprises with an international Enterprises should focus on the alleviation of information asymmetry when applying AI. Through the data analysis and forecasting functions of AI, enterprises can more accurately grasp market demand and predict trends, so as to optimize production plans and marketing strategies [86]. At the same time, enterprises should also pay attention to the application of AI in quality control, personalized production and human resources optimization, giving full play to the potential of AI.

Policies formulated by governments have far-reaching impacts on enterprise production. Enterprises should pay close attention to supply chain innovation policies and actively participate in supply chain digital transformation in order to fully utilize the productivity-enhancing effects of AI. Meanwhile, for the low-carbon pilot city policy, enterprises need to respond reasonably to minimize the negative impact on AI applications while meeting low-carbon requirements. For example, Luo et al. [81] found that there are certain deviations in the implementation of low-carbon pilot city policies at the enterprise level, and that enterprises  should  strengthen  communication  with  the  government  and jointly seek solutions in order to realize the benign interaction between low-carbon development and AI application.

## 7.3. Limitations

This study is mainly based on the data of listed companies in China for analysis, and the generalizability of the findings may be somewhat limited. There are differences in AI application levels and policy environments among enterprises in different countries and regions. In the future, we can consider expanding the scope of the study to include data from enterprises in more countries and regions to verify the generalizability of the findings.

In  the study, AI is mainly measured by the frequency of relevant terms in annual reports, which may not be able to accurately reflect the development level of enterprise AI. the development of AI is a dynamic process, which involves the continuous updating of technology and the Z. Sun et al.

expansion of application scenarios, and in the future, we can explore more accurate measurement methods, such as adopting the indicators to directly assess the level of application of enterprise AI technology.

The  factors  affecting  enterprise  productivity  are  numerous  and complex, and although this study has considered some major factors, there may still be other factors that have not been included, such as the management level of the enterprise and enterprise culture. These factors may have some impact on the results of the study, and future research can further expand the selection of variables to reveal the relationship between AI and enterprise productivity more comprehensively.

## 7.4. Future research agenda

Examine the impact of the convergence of AI with other emerging technologies (e.g., IoT, big data, etc.) on enterprise productivity. The convergence of these emerging technologies brings new opportunities and challenges for businesses, and future research could focus on the synergies and interactions between them, as well as trends in different policy environments.

Focus on the ethical and social issues brought about by AI [26]. The development of AI may lead to the restructuring of employment, the protection of data privacy and other issues, and future research needs to explore these issues in depth and propose corresponding solution strategies in order to realize the sustainable development of AI.

Future  research  could  focus  on  the  absorptive  capacity  of  firms adopting AI. Specifically, exploring the dynamic relationship between firms ' absorptive capacity and the development of AI technology is an important research agenda. As AI technology continues to evolve, the absorptive capacity of firms will need to improve, and future research could focus on how firms can adjust and optimize their absorptive capacity in time to adapt to changes in the technology.

## 8. Conclusions and policy implications

AI  technology  has  become  an  important  engine  driving  social development and change, and exploring its micro-enabling role in enterprise production can help countries seize the opportunities of the AI era and realize sustainable development. This study comprehensively analyzes the heterogeneous effects and internal mechanisms of AI on the productivity of Chinese firms through a variety of research methods, and draws a number of important conclusions.

First,  the  innovative  finding  of  this  study  that  AI  is  effective  in increasing firm productivity and that this finding remains robust after changing  the  research  methodology  provides  strong  evidence  of  the positive role of AI in firm production. Second, the study innovatively points out that the nature of enterprise ownership, international vision and innovation consciousness of leaders moderates the impact of AI on productivity, which is manifested in the fact that AI has a greater impact on the productivity of state-owned enterprises, and enterprises whose leaders have strong international vision and innovation consciousness, which  provides  new  perspectives  on  enterprise  management  and decision-making. Again, this study finds that information asymmetry is the main mechanism by which AI enhances firm productivity, and that specialization and green innovation also show potential channel effects, a finding that expands the understanding of the mechanisms by which AI works. In addition, the fact that supply chain innovation policies positively moderated the impact of AI on productivity, while low-carbon pilot  city  policies  had  the  opposite  effect,  provides  a  valuable  reference  for  policymakers.  At  the  same  time,  AI  has  a  significant  green development  effect,  which  can  significantly  promote  the  joint  green innovation capability of enterprises, especially the promotion of progressive green innovation is more obvious, which reflects the important role of AI in sustainable development. In addition, the finding that historical infrastructure development has a significant modernization effect, and that AI development in areas where historical Ming Dynasty stagecoaches were distributed increased firm productivity more significantly, reveals an interesting link between history and modern technological development. Finally, there are differences in the impact of AI on firms with different productivity bases, with a greater positive impact on firms with productivity at the 75th percentile and above, which provides a basis for individualized growth strategies. In addition, the positive impact of AI on productivity shows a "first rise and then fall" boosting effect,  which provides new insights into the pattern of AI ' s impact.

In  summary,  these  innovative  findings  of  this  study  provide  a comprehensive and unique perspective for a deeper understanding of the role of AI in enterprise production, which will help countries and enterprises better grasp the opportunities of the AI era and realize sustainable development. Based on the above findings, we can summarize the following four policy implications:

First, the government can formulate policies to promote enterprises to accelerate the pace of digital transformation and smart manufacturing, and encourage them to apply AI technology to improve productivity and product quality. Second, the government can incentivize private and Sino-foreign joint ventures to increase investment and application  of  AI  technology  through  policy  instruments  such  as  tax incentives,  financial  support,  and  innovation  incentives.  Finally,  the government can encourage enterprises and leaders to develop a sense of innovation and an international outlook through education and training and support for scientific research projects. This includes encouraging enterprises to cooperate with leading international AI firms to absorb advanced technology and management experience, thereby improving their competitiveness and innovation.

Consider the role of information asymmetry as a mechanism in AI and productivity enhancement. The government can establish a trust mechanism and certification system for AI technology, certify and supervise AI products and services that meet the standards, improve enterprises ' confidence in AI technology, and promote its application and productivity enhancement. Supply chain innovation policy has a positive  regulating  effect,  and  the  government  and  enterprises  should strengthen  cooperation  to  jointly  promote  supply  chain  innovation, realize supply chain digital transformation and intelligent upgrading, and improve the efficiency and competitiveness of the whole industrial chain. Low-carbon pilot policies may lead to limited application of AI technology, increased cost pressure and difficulties in technology integration. Governments and enterprises can increase support and investment in AI technology, formulate more flexible low-carbon policies, and promote technology integration and innovation to realize the positive interaction  between low-carbon  development and AI technology application.

AI technology can promote cross-border cooperation and knowledge sharing among enterprises, especially in the field of green technology innovation. Enterprises can share data, algorithms and models to jointly develop AI applications that solve environmental problems and promote joint green technology innovation. At the same time, the government can promote the formation of an industrial ecosystem for green technological innovation and foster cooperation and innovation among enterprises  upstream  and  downstream  of  the  industrial  chain.  The construction of post stations in the Ming Dynasty helped the government to collect and transmit information from all over the world, providing support for decision-making. Similarly, in modern society, data collection and sharing are crucial for the development of AI technology. The government and enterprises  can  establish  a  data-sharing  platform  to facilitate the sharing of data resources among all parties to support the application and development of AI technology.

Firms  with  high  productivity  levels  are  likely  to  focus  more  on optimal allocation of resources and efficiency gains, and are therefore more willing to invest in AI technologies to smarten and automate their production processes. When formulating relevant policies, the government can focus on the needs and characteristics of this part of the enterprises, and formulate targeted support policies to provide them with more support and encouragement, so as to promote the wide application Z.
