## ABSTRACT

This  study  examines  the  use  of  Generative  AI  (GAI)  in  the  hospitality industry,  particularly  in  hotels  and  resorts.  It  investigates  the  implications of GAI towards the competitive advantage, firm performance, and organisation's  performance.  Employing  the  Technological-OrganizationalEnvironmental (TOE) framework, this study further explains how narcissism  moderates  the  relationship  between  the  factors  that  assist  in adopting  GAI  and  GAI's  moderating  effect  on  the  overall  competitive positioning  and  organisational  outcomes.  The  study  uses  longitudinal analysis  to  shed  some light  about  the  influences  related  to  GAI  adoption, including  the  level  of  technological  readiness  in  the  organisation,  as  well as  certain  individual  traits.  The  managers  and  owners  will  leverage  these insights,  addressing  how  GAI  enhances  operational  efficiency,  customer experience, and competitiveness in the hospitality industry context. This study combines the Technology-Organization-Environment (TOE) framework  with  the  personality  trait  of  narcissism  to  analyse  the  factors driving Generative AI adoption in the hospitality sector on different levels. Using  a  longitudinal  approach,  it  provides  new  understanding  of  the impact of leadership characteristics on strategic technologies and management  decisions,  underscoring  the  value  of  leveraging  GAI  for sustained competitive edge and enhanced organisational efficiency.

## Statement of novelty

This study offers a novel contribution by being one of the first to empirically examine the adoption of Generative AI (GAI) within the hospitality industry through a longitudinal lens, integrating the Technology-Organization-Environment (T-O-E) framework alongside leadership traits such as narcissism.  While  most  prior  research  has  focused  on  static  models  or  single-point  analyses  of  AI adoption,  this  study  uniquely  captures  how  organisational  readiness,  technological  capability, and environmental pressures evolve over time and influence competitive advantage and firm performance. By including psychological dimensions of leadership and using data from two different timeframes, the research provides a comprehensive and dynamic understanding of GAI adoption, offering both theoretical enrichment and actionable insights for industry stakeholders navigating digital transformation.

As the emergence of technology is dictating consumer habits and the standards set within the industry,  it  is  essential  to  consider  what  the  rationale  is  behind  the  hospitality  businesses'  usage of generative AI in order to remain a competition (Dwivedi et al., 2024; Wong et al., 2023b). Investigating  the  factors  influencing  risk  decision-making  regarding  generative  AI  adoption  implementation,  such  as  organisational  readiness,  technological  capabilities,  and  environmental  pressures, would help enhance the understanding of the risks and rewards of integrating AI in the hospitality sector  (Dwivedi  et  al.,  2024;  Majid  et  al.,  2024).  Understanding  this  information  will  support  the effective  formulation  of  strategies,  allocation  of  resources,  and  innovations,  which  will  be  crucial in pursuing long-term growth and success in a dynamic digital landscape (Hsu et al., 2024).

While  many  shareholders  seem  enthusiastic  about  using  generative  AI  technologies,  very  few studies have directly addressed the factors upon which the hotels and resorts intend to embrace such  key  innovations  (Law  et  al.,  2023).  What  I  see  in  most  studies  today  are  explorations  into advanced AI technologies and their broader popularity as opposed to an increasing body of work on  generative  AI.  Few  studies  investigate  the  role  of  organisational  and  technological  contexts and environmental factors in developing the intention of hospitality stakeholders to invest in generative AI (Prasad Agrawal, 2024; Ivanov et al., 2024). On top of that, there seems to be little research that has sought to assess the implications of generative AI adoption, especially in terms of firm productivity, such as revenues, cost, and customer satisfaction, in the context of the hospitality industry.

The  Technology-Organization-Environment  (TOE)  framework  demonstrates  all  the  elements  of attention when trying to understand the degree of acceptance of the hotels or resorts towards generative AI technologies (Wang et al., 2016). Many studies, including the TOE framework, anomalously state that some factors impede the organisation's will to adopt change, such as generative AI adoption (Dwivedi et al., 2024). In the hospitality sector, the adoption of Generative AI (GAI) stems from the  interrelation  of  technology,  organisation,  and  an  individual's  level  of  involvement.  Grasping these factors is vital in revealing the response of hotels and resorts towards digital transformation. This work takes a systematic approach to identify the guiding factors of GAI adoption, facilitating further exploration of the strategy, operation, and behaviour which motivate successful implementation.

This study leverages several pivotal constructs to understand the Generative AI adoption within the hospitality industry. It describes that organisational readiness is the level of a hotel or a resort's possession of the requisite resources, culture, and support frameworks for the efficient implementation and adoption of generative AI technologies (AL-Khatib, 2024; Darwish et al., 2025). Technological  capability  explains  the  propensity  of  an  organisation  to  obtain,  integrate,  and  effectively leverage  sophisticated  innovations  like  generative  AI  in  improving  operational  processes  and service  delivery  (Singh  et  al.,  2024).  Firm  performance  illustrates  the  holistic  refractive  decline  in the  efficacy  of  a  hotel  or  resort  towards  key  operational  metrics  like  increased  revenue,  more efficient  cost  structure,  heightened  customer  satisfaction,  and  favourable  market  positioning (Chatterjee et al., 2021a; Li et al., 2024). Narcissism defines a concept that is best known as a selfcentred,  self-admiring,  and  conceited  personality  trait  that  influences  decision-making  and  risktaking within organisations (Tandon et al., 2023).

Even  though  several  personality  factors  affect  the  adoption  of  technology,  this  research  deals with  narcissism  because  it  relates  to  leadership  and  risk-taking  behaviour,  as  well  as  innovation adoption  in  organisations.  Narcissistic  people  have  an  inflated  self-image,  a  strong  desire  to  be recognised, and a tendency to make overly bold decisions (Campbell & Foster, 2011; Emmons, 1987). Such traits can have a pronounced impact on a person's response to disruptive innovations like Generative AI (GAI). Unlike openness to experience, which entails a willingness to learn and accept new ideas (McCrae & Costa, 1997), narcissism is an actively driving force of behaviour through a desire to reach goals, gain, and control especially in a leader's context (Rosenthal & Pittinsky, 2006). Often narcissistic  managers  are  overconfident in  making organisational decisions, actively seeking out  new technologies,  and  striving  to  compete  on  differentiation-factors  that  are  directly  aligned  with GAI's  promise of  strategic  transformation  (Chatterjee  &  Hambrick,  2007).  In  addition,  higher  innovation and digital leadership transformation through narcissism have been reported, especially in the  context  of  high  energy  and  highly  visible  domains  such  as  hospitality  (Engelen  et  al.,  2016; Zhang et al., 2015). Thus, in the context of the Technology-Organization-Environment (TOE) framework, this research treats narcissism as a moderating variable in an attempt to reveal how personality traits  at  the  individual  level  interrelate  with  organisational  and  technological  factors  to  produce certain  outcomes  with  respect  to  adoption-a  gap  that  continues  to  persist  within  the  literature on AI adoption.

Individual  differences,  especially  personality  traits,  have  been  known  to  influence  decisionmaking,  particularly  regarding  new  technologies  such  as  GAI.  For  instance,  an  individual  with  a high  level  of  narcissism  is  likely  to  show  higher  self-confidence,  a  higher  tendency  to  take  risks, and a greater need for recognition and esteem. Therefore, from the above reasons, the relationship between narcissism and features such as perceived ease of use, perceived competence, and competitive  pressures  should  be  assessed  to  understand  the  outcomes  of  GAI  adoption.  Apart  from the TOE framework, narcissism is used as an essential moderator in the existing context.

Hence, the research seek to answer the following research questions:

RQ1. To what extent does the TOE framework influence the intention to adopt Generative AI?

RQ2. Is the relationship between intention to adopt GAI and competitive advantage and firm performance moderated by narcissism (high vs low)?

This research provides several important insights into the existing body of literature regarding technology adoption and organisational behaviour in the context of the hospitality industry. To begin with, it improves understanding of Generative AI (GAI) adoption by applying the Technology-Organization-Environment  (TOE)  framework  in  conjunction  with  the  individual-level  trait  of  narcissism, which has not been given sufficient attention, thereby providing a new multi-level lens. The study sheds  important  light  on  the  interplay  of  a  firm's  competitive  advantage  and  performance  with GAI  adoption  vis-a -vis  organisational  readiness,  technology  capability,  and  environmental  forces as  they  combine  with  certain  traits  of  the  decision-makers'  personality.  Second,  the  study  puts forth  the  concept  of  narcissism  as  an  important  moderating  factor,  empirically  validating  its  role in  strategic  decisions  around  innovation and  digital  advancement. Finally,  the  use  of  longitudinal design allows the research to capture shifts in the dynamics over time, thus contributing depth to theory  while  helping  managers  in  planning  how  to  optimally  employ  GAI  to  boost  operational efficiency, enhance customer interactions, and achieve organisational goals.

## 2. Background literature

## 2.1. Generative AI in hotels & resorts

Generative Artificial Intelligence (GAI) can potentially significantly impact the hospitality sector. (Dogru et al., 2025). It is a technology expected to innovate processes (Wong et al., 2023a; Koo et  al.,  2023).  'Generative  AI'  as  a  technology,  along  with  supporting  machine  learning  algorithms and data analytics, enables organisations to enhance many of their operations, including guest  experience  and  resource  allocation  (Dwivedi  et  al.,  2024;  Ali  et  al.,  2023).  The  scope  of application of Generative AI also encompasses several dimensions in the context of the hospitality industry (Kim et al., 2023; Wong et al., 2023a). Some of these include automated check-in systems, virtual concierge, dynamic pricing, and predictive maintenance of the building (Zhang et  al.,  2024).  Each  of  these  innovations  contributes  to  greater  efficiency  and  savings  for  operational  processes  and  improvement  in  guest  experience  (Wang,  2025).  They  offer  specific services,  proactively  cater  to  guest  requirements,  and  integrate  with  each  phase  of  the guest  experience  (Wong  et  al.,  2023a).  On  top  of  that,  Generative  AI  has  also  been  used  to fuel  innovation,  competition,  and  sustainability  of  hotels  and  resorts  worldwide  (Wong  et  al., 2023a; Kim  et  al.,  2024).

## 2.2. T-O-E framework

According to research conducted by Prasad Agrawal (2024), the TOE framework helps organisations better understand the technology adoption process. It concerns the interaction of technological, organisational,  and  environmental  determinants  of  the  adoption  process  (Chatterjee  et  al., 2021a).  For  example,  technological  factors  deal  with  the  attributes  of  the  particular  innovation, such as its degree of difficulty, fit with existing systems, and benefits (Polisetty et al., 2024). Organisational  factors  provide  understanding  regarding  the  domestic  aspects  of  firms,  including  their structures,  assets,  and  attributes  (wael  AL-khatib,  2023).  These  may  include  leadership,  emphasis on the new idea or intervention, or organisational willingness and ability to adopt new ideas (Chatterjee et al., 2021a). Environmental factors are core aspects that deal with an organisation's outer environment, including external  market  scenarios,  legal  theories,  and  competitive  forces  (Awa  & Ojiabo, 2016). In examining these three dimensions, the relevance of substantial resources to implementing the TOE framework is explicated further (Collado-Agudo et al., 2023). This becomes important for businesses that wish to harness the potential benefits of technology adoption and improve their performance.

## 2.3. Narcissism

Narcissism is a tendency that can be encountered among individuals who are self-centred and show grandiosity, consider themselves to be deserving of something, and are always seeking praise and appreciation (Christou et al., 2020). People in their working environment always have narcissistic personalities and will place themselves and their goals before their work goals (Tan & Yang, 2021). This could make one engage in organisational politics, such as self-promotion, deceptive practices, and showing less concern for workmates (Canavan, 2017). There are some necessary aspects within the leadership  where  their  confidence  and  determination  are  positive  (Yousaf  et  al.,  2023).  However, excessive  narcissism  has  been  suggested  to  be  detrimental  to  team  effectiveness,  decisionmaking as well as organisational culture (Kim et al., 2018). Self-focused leaders  will create  a  toxic culture  where  structures  such  as  power  dividers,  favouritism,  and  blame  will  be  the  norms, leading to a decline in workforce satisfaction and effectiveness (Yousaf et al., 2023). Moreover, narcissism impedes managers from effectively dealing with other management aspects, such as fostering  inventiveness  within  teams,  working  well  with  partners,  and  other  factors  related  to  the organisation's longevity (Woosnam et al., 2024).

[S. 6]

The  research  examines  the  impact  of  narcissism  on  emerging  technology  preferences  such  as generative AI. Organisational inertia and company size are fundamental, but narcissism is more discomforting. Individuals with a high level of narcissism also tend to be risk-taking, inventive, and goaloriented, as categorised by Dwivedi et al. (2024). These traits create a possibility for such individuals to be more receptive to changes such as GAI, which can eventually lead to a reduction in the resistance to change within the organisation (Wang, 2025). The link between narcissism and a high desire for recognition supports GAI's possible competitive advantage for hotels. Thus, by including narcissism as a moderator in the TOE paradigm, I may better understand how personality traits interact with disruptive technologies like GAI.

## 3. Development of hypotheses

## 3.1. Perceived ease of use

Perceived ease of use captures how individuals perceive a particular technology as effortless and convenient (Ozturk et al., 2016). Suppose decision-makers view GAI systems as user-friendly, intuitive, and easy to integrate into existing processes. In that case, they are more likely to develop positive attitudes toward GAI adoption (Kar et al., 2023). Perceived ease of use helps to overcome barriers associated with GAI (Ivanov et al., 2024). This, in turn, increases managers' intention to adopt GAI (Dwivedi et al., 2024). Furthermore, companies that perceive GAI as easy to use are more likely to invest  resources  and  prioritise  implementing  GAI  initiatives  (Gupta  &  Yang,  2024).  Gupta  et  al. (2024) note that this increases the organisation's readiness and leads to the desired implementation and use of GAI technologies. This leads us to put forward the following:

H1: Perceived ease of use is positively related to the Intention to adopt GAI

## 3.2. Organisational competency

In addition to the strategies a firm wishes to use, organisational competency introduces the collective understanding management of a firm, skills as well as capabilities towards maximising resource utilisation and opportunity exploitation (Yang et al., 2024). Companies with solid organisational competency can comprehend the pros and cons of GAI-sensitive technologies (Dogru et al., 2025). They are  also  more  competent  in  managing  the  appropriate  use  of  these  technologies  (Dwivedi  et  al., 2024). There is commensurate potential for understanding organisational control with the adoption of GAI (Dogru et al., 2025) to pursue the strategic objectives of the organisation, to be more competitive, and to improve performance (Wang, 2025).

A firm's competency is vital in shaping the decisions of hotels and resorts to adopt generative AI (Law et al., 2023). Competency is the strategic ability to see and pursue a new opportunity (Kim et al., 2023).  Large  hotel  chains  understand  how  valuable  GAI  will  be  to  their  functioning  and  want  to adopt it to bolster their advanced AI systems (Kim et al., 2023), thus improving the customer experience (Law et al., 2023). Therefore, I incorporate the idea that a positive connection exists between organisational competency and the will to adopt Generative AI. Therefore, I hypothesise:

H2: Organizational competency is positively related to the Intention to adopt GAI

## 3.3. Competitor pressure

Competitor pressure, derived from the concern of rival organisations and market forces, provides strong reasons for organisations to adopt new technologies (Merhi & Harfouche, 2024). Numerous researchers have explored the links between perceived competitive pressure and firms' inclination to adopt technology (Tyler et al., 2020). As a result, competitor pressure will positively affect business organisations' intentions to use Generative AI (Prasad Agrawal, 2024). When one of the hotels starts

to use modern technologies, it sets a new standard, making it difficult for other hotels to ignore such technologies (Buhalis et al.,  2024;  Dwivedi  et  al.,  2024;  Nguyen  et  al.,  2022).  This  collective  rivalry forms  an  environmental  construct  within  the  Technology-Organization-Environment  (TOE)  framework (Van Huy et al., 2024). Consumers' market dynamics push Stare Jeans in GAI to retain competitiveness (Buhalis et al., 2024). Hence, I propose:

H3: Competitor pressure is positively related to the Intention to adopt GAI

## 3.4. Intention to adopt generative AI

Prior studies have revealed the fact that the willingness of organisations to adopt new technologies such as GAI is predicated on the ability to be competitive (Kar et al., 2023). Focusing on GAI allows organisations to be more efficient and effective in service delivery and offer customers better quality goods and services (Kanbach et al., 2024). In addition, GAI allows companies to analyse data and use them to  predict  trends,  adjust  offerings,  and  react  to  changing  customers'  demands  quickly  (Kar et al.,  2023).  Hence, it can be argued that organisations with aggressive GAI orientation will more likely  gain  and  sustain  a  competitive  advantage as innovation and market responsiveness leaders (Dwivedi et al., 2024). Thus, I hypothesise:

H4: Intention to adopt GAI is positively related to competitive advantage

According to previous research, the desire of the companies to acquire GAI reveals their proactive attitude, especially in adopting new and rapid technologies to enhance the organisation's capabilities and achieve its strategic objectives (Dwivedi et al., 2024). It can be added that GAI deployment can  be  beneficial  to  a  company's  performance  (Kanbach  et  al.,  2024),  making  better  use  of  its resources, cutting down on expenses, growing its intake, and improving the level of contentment among clients (Hollebeek et al., 2024). Also, implementing GAI is helpful because it leads to competitive gains, which contribute toward improving financial performance and market scope (Kar et al., 2023). Thus, I hypothesise:

H5: Intention to adopt GAI is positively related to firm performance

## 3.5. Competitive advantage and firm performance

Competitive advantage is such a factor that supports a company's performance above its competitors. Chadwick & Flinchbaugh (2021) note that such advantages facilitate superior operational and profitability  performances.  Businesses  that  enjoy  competitive  strategies  that  the  present  market cannot  surpass  stand  the  chance  of  fierce  market  rivalry  and  earn  more  returns  and  profits (López-Gamero  et  al.,  2009).  Competitive  advantages  also  help  tackle  oppression  from  external and circumstantial change within the industry (Yong Kim & Oh, 2004). Therefore, more competitive advantages are expected, such as better-paying policies, growing revenue, and more market share (Kumar et al., 2011). Hence, I propose:

H6: Competitive advantage is positively related to firm performance

## 3.6. Narcissism as a moderator

Narcissism, characterised by a self-absorbed and high need for adoration and validation (Pizam et al., 2022), could impact the leader's attitudes and motivations toward the efforts of GAI adoption. It is important to investigate how competitive advantage is leveraged to achieve firm performance in the presence of narcissistic leaders (Shirokova et al., 2024). Such leaders are more prone to acquiring GAI for prestige enhancement rather than in pursuit of winning in the market only (Budhwar et al., 2023). Moreover, those higher up the hierarchy and narcissistic may act in a manner that undermines the efficacious adoption of GAI (Liang et al., 2024). Such behaviours include the abuse of catchphrases and  catchword  plans  used  to  achieve  short-term  results  instead  of  pursuing  a  long-term  plan, especially those made by superiors (Liang et al., 2024). High levels of narcissism may dilute the positive relationship between the intention to adopt GAI and competitive advantage and firm performance (Goli & Khan, 2022). Thus, I formulate the following hypothesis:

H7a: Narcissism will moderate the association between the intention to adopt GAI and competitive advantage

H7b: Narcissism will moderate the association between the intention to adopt GAI and firm performance

H7c: Narcissism will moderate the association between competitive advantage and firm performance

## 4. Methodology

Utilising a longitudinal research design allowed us to understand better how adopting generative AI influences competitive advantage and firm performance. This study employs a longitudinal research design which allows tracking of generative AI's (GAI) impact on competitive advantage and firm performance over time. Longitudinal research is more suited for this study as it captures the technological  and  organisational  adoption  dynamics  along  with  changing  perceptions  in  management  as opposed  to  snapshot  analyses  from  cross-sectional  studies.  GAI,  an  emerging  technology,  is expected  to  have  gradual  impacts  on  organisations  as  they  adapt,  develop  new  competencies, and  strategically  evolve  over  time.  Collecting  data  during  two  separate  time  intervals-prior  to and  following  a  major  adoption  milestone-enables  the  study  to  demonstrate  progress,  identify causal  links,  and  evaluate  enduring  effects  on  performance  outcomes.  These  insights  help improve accuracy while deepening understanding on the impacts of GAI in the hospitality industry. The initial period was from November 2022 to February 2023, and the subsequent was from December 2023 to February 2024. The participants were managers (senior and middle-level employees) of hotels and resorts in India.

Participants were selected based on specific screening criteria.

Are you a manager (senior, middle, or junior level) in a hotel or resort?

## 5. Analysis

I  used  Structural  Equation  Modeling  (SEM)  with  Smart  PLS  for  this  study.  Using  a  three-stage approach, the study analysed the model's performance in different firm age contexts, specifically less than or equal to 10 years and more than 10 years. This allows for a comparison of the estimated path coefficients. Our analysis covers the evaluation of measurement models, structural models, and multi-group analysis. Before conducting the main analysis, I performed preliminary checks to ensure the reliability of the results. These checks included assessing normality and common method bias.

Before conducting hypothesis testing, it was crucial to evaluate the existence of multicollinearity. Based on the criteria established by Petter et al. (2007), the study assessed all VIF values to determine the presence of severe multicollinearity. A VIF of ≤ 3.3 (in both the time frame, t1 & t2) indicates an absence of such multicollinearity. With all VIF values below the threshold set by Petter et al. (2007), there were no significant multicollinearity issues, allowing the study to move forward with hypothesis testing confidently. ## 7. Theoretical implications

First of all, with the help of the T-O-E framework, this article examines the adoption of Generative AI (Agrawal, 2024). It studies the intricate relationship among the technological aspects (Polisetty et al., 2024), the structural factors within the organisation (Chatterjee et al., 2021a), and the context of use (Awa  &  Ojiabo,  2016)  through  the  lens  of  an  adoption  process.  The  longitudinal  method  helps researchers  grasp  the  changing  nature  of  the  competitive  edge  in  tourism  and  hospitality.  This method  helps  provide  a  clearer  picture  of  GAI's  roles in  strategic  positioning  and  market performance.

Second, the paper has value for the hospitality industry by highlighting the competitive usage of Generative AI, particularly strategic fit and change (Merhi & Harfouche, 2024). This paper also demonstrates how organisational aspects play out in GAI use in different contexts. It examines how perceived  usefulness,  organisational  resources,  and  external  competition  pressure  influence  this process (Kar et al., 2023). This study also focuses on the intra-organisational factors in the contexts of innovation projects and their results, enriching technology adoption scholarship. Using the TOE model, this construct enables one to examine different technological developments, organisational resources,  and  environmental  aspects  that  determine  the  competitive  advantage  the  hotels  and resorts achieve as they adopt GAI technology.

Third, this research contributes to the scholarly conversation by incorporating psychological traits at the individual level-narcissism in particular-into the structurally oriented TOE framework, providing a blended sociological and psychological perspective on technology adoption in the hospitality  sector.  By  looking  into  the  effects  of  narcissistic  leadership  on  the  relationship  between  the adoption of GAI and the performance of a firm, the study integrates personality-driven innovative motivations  with  strategic  decision-making.  In  demonstrating  these  relationships,  the  study  not only  reveals  the  strategic  and  operational  significance  of  GAI,  but  also  illuminates  the  ways  in which  leadership  traits  can  enhance  or  distort  its  adoption.  Thus,  the  study  contributes  to  the body of literature on the integration of technology and leadership by showing that the successful adoption of GAI and the impact on performance in a firm relies not only on external and internal conditions  of  appropriateness,  but  the  cognitive  structures  and  motivating  factors  of  the  firm's decision-makers. Having this perspective sheds light on the wide-reaching impacts of digital transformation on highly serviced sectors like tourism and hospitality.

## 8. Practical implications

Managers  and  owners  of  organisations  in  the  hospitality  industry  must  appreciate  the  value  of embracing  Generative  AI  (GAI).  First,  with  the  Zamindari  and  the  advent  of  GAI  technologies, there  is  an  opportunity  to  reduce  operational  efficiencies,  improve  customer  relations,  and  add more advantages over other players. Second, it is necessary to achieve this in practice by creating an environment that promotes innovative thinking and continuous education, which is necessary to deploy  GAI  effectively.  In  order  to  maximise  the  returns  on  GAI  tools  adoption,  managers  and owners need to ensure that every employee gets enough training and development. Third, managers and owners must ensure that organisational readiness and change management are factors considered when implementing GAI technologies.

Communication is one of the most critical areas of management research and practice; defining clear roles, responsibilities, channels, and processes, cooperation between the departments, including  collaboration  between non-adjacent departments, and potential organisational change resistance  from  the  employees  is  essential. Fourth, it is  crucial  for  managers  and  owners  that competitive intelligence is sought and explored in terms of customers, their orientations, and technology developments to seize and make favourable circumstances. Trusted consultative technology providers and industry networks may provide support and further guidance on the strategy development and adoption phases. Soon, through the effective use and integration of the GAI tools, managers and owners will improve their operational effectiveness, improve customer satisfaction, and prepare  their  hotels  and  resorts  for  successful  operations  in  the  highly  competitive  environment and the calendar market.

Moreover, alignment of strategic objectives as well as evaluation from the market requires the constant  active  attention  from  the  managers  and  the  owners  regarding  the  outcomes  of  GAI execution.  Performance  metrics  such  as  customer  satisfaction,  the  ratio  of  bookings  to  inquiries, operational savings, and employee productivity will measure GAI's performance to the company's goals. In addition, building feedback and agility can enhance process ownership among employees, thus encouraging AI process refinement which leads to innovation and improvement. Also, managers must monitor the growing concerns of trust issues on the brand especially in the discipline of AI ethics, data privacy, and law because these domains are critical to customer loyalty and corporate image. Addressing these issues, the generals in hospitality can use marketing strategies to ensure GAI investments are not only short gain but  also  sustain  adaptability  and  competitiveness  in  the healthcare industry's aging digital environment.

## 9. Limitations and future research

Though the findings of  this  research  indeed  point  towards  generating  valuable  insights  for  practice  and  theory  alike,  the  applicability  of  these  results  is  affected  by  certain  assumptions  and other  aspects  unique  to  the  study.  Therefore,  the  ability  to  transfer  these  conclusions  to  other hospitality  environments  or  industries  will  be  limited.  Also,  since  most  GAI  technologies  are complex,  changing  rapidly,  and  becoming  more  advanced,  it  is  prudent  to  continuously  investigate  their  effects  on  organisational  performance,  customer  satisfaction,  or  employee  relations. Subsequent  studies  may  consider  organisational culture and  leadership, market  conditions, how these mediate GAI adoption and implementation, and their subsequent impact on competitive  advantage.

## 10. Conclusion

The  advancing  technologies  of  Generative  AI  (GAI)  significantly  transforms  the  means  by  which hotels  and  resorts  compete and innovate in the context of preserving consumer digital attention and  experience.  From  a  narcissism  standpoint,  especially  integrated  with  a  TOE  approach,  this study offers a detailed understanding of GAI adoption's multi-faceted influences. The longitudinal and cross-sectional multi-group approach provides insights into the stages of organisational preparedness, technological readiness, environmental conditions, and personality traits of decision makers which greatly influence the application of GAI by the firm and its realisation on the competition and performance  of  the  firm.  The  refinement  of  the  TOE  framework  concerning  the  contribution strengthens  the  literature  on  hospitality  innovation  by  integrating  social  cognitive  factors,  thus enhancing the understanding of innovation adoption behaviours in hospitality business.

[S. 17]

This research may be of practical use to hospitality managers, business owners, and government decision-makers. It was observed that organisational preparations with regard to structures, technologies, and leaders' mindsets, as well as their personality traits, are significant for the successful adoption of GAI. It was noted that high narcissism can be detrimental but, if contained, can also foster rapid and bold strides towards innovation. This highlights the dual governance needed to maintain confidence on one hand and avoid excessive exposure to needless risk on the other. Consequently, hospitality firms are urged to analyse both the human and technological dimensions when devising strategies for digital transformation. With GAI's internal and external drivers comprehensive stakeholder  efforts  can  be  channelled  towards  strategically  sculpted  interventions  aimed  to  heighten guest and operational interface while reinforcing agile edge advantage within a transforming services environment.
