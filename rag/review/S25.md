## A B S T R A C T

The growing focus on sustainability and technological innovation has encouraged small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) to adopt artificial intelligence (AI) capabilities and a green entrepreneurial orientation as central drivers of sustainable performance. This research investigates the contribution of AI capabilities in promoting green innovations and creativity in SMEs, which collectively lead to long-term sustainability. The study is based on the Resource-Based View (RBV) theory, which offers a theoretical framework for investigating how AI-based competencies and green entrepreneurial strategies can promote SME performance. The research model comprises infrastructure, business integration, and a proactive attitude influencing AI capabilities, affecting SME creativity and green innovations. Moreover, green risk-taking, innovativeness, and proactiveness significantly contribute to green entrepreneurial orientation, affecting SME creativity and green innovations, ultimately resulting in sustainable  performance.  A  quantitative  research  design  was  applied,  utilizing  survey  data  gathered  from  SME managers and business owners functioning in the manufacturing and service sectors in Saudi Arabia. 250 SMEs were examined using ' Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) ' analysis to validate the proposed hypotheses and evaluate these relationships. The results indicate that AI capabilities significantly impact SME creativity and green innovations. Moreover, green entrepreneurial orientation positively influences SME creativity and green innovations, which in turn facilitate sustainable performance. This research identifies the importance of SMEs allocating resources to AI infrastructure, proactive business strategies, and entrepreneurial risk-taking to foster green  innovation  and  sustainability.  Moreover,  policymakers  must  facilitate  AI-based  green  initiatives  by implementing incentives and regulatory policies to promote sustainable development. By integrating AI capabilities and green entrepreneurship, SMEs can achieve a competitive advantage while achieving world sustainability goals.

Moreover,  the  Resource-Based  View  (RBV)  provides  theoretical support for how firms acquire competitive advantage (Barney, 1991). RBV postulates that the ability of a firm to integrate internal resources and capabilities, such as knowledge, technology, human capital, and financial resources -plays a significant role in enhancing performance (Bertram, 2016; Madhani, 2010; Wang & Zhang, 2025). RBV has been widely applied, with research highlighting the utilization of dynamic capabilities  to  enhance  inter-organizational  relationships  and  sustain competitive advantage (Madhani, 2010; Royer & Simon, 2024; Wang & Zhang, 2025). This  study  aims  to  address  critical  research  gaps  by  empirically examining the interrelationships among artificial intelligence (AI) capabilities, green entrepreneurial orientation (GEO), SME creativity, and green innovations, and how these constructs collectively influence the sustainable performance of small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) in the context of Saudi Arabia. As an emerging economy experiencing accelerated digital transformation and heightened environmental consciousness, Saudi Arabia presents a compelling setting to explore how technologically driven and sustainability-oriented strategies shape SME resilience  and  competitiveness.  A  quantitative  research  design  is employed to carefully evaluate the proposed theoretical model and test the hypothesized relations among the latent constructs. The findings of this study are expected to contribute to existing literature by providing empirical evidence on the synergistic effects of AI and GEO on green innovation,  thereby  providing  practical  insights  for  SMEs  aiming  to improve their sustainability performance. The study ' s  primary objectives are as follows.

To analyze the impact of AI capabilities on SME creativity and green innovations.

To examine how green entrepreneurial orientation influences SME creativity and innovation within SMEs.

To  assess  the  overall  effect  of  these  constructs  on  sustainable performance.

The findings of this study contribute to the ongoing discourse on sustainable business practices in emerging economies by highlighting how AI capabilities and green entrepreneurial orientation jointly foster SME creativity, drive green innovations, and enhance sustainable performance.  Specifically,  the  research  introduces  SME  creativity  as  a mediating capability and extends the Resource-Based View (RBV) to the digital-sustainability interface, offering new insights into how intangible and technological resources shape competitive advantage and long-term resilience.

## 2. Theoretical background and hypothesis development

The  Resource-Based  View  (RBV)  theory  serves  as  the  theoretical foundation of this study. It is used to examine how Artificial Intelligence (AI) capabilities and Green Entrepreneurial Orientation (GEO) shape the sustainable performance of small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) in Saudi Arabia ' s manufacturing and service sectors (Estensoro et al., 2022) (see Fig. 3 showing proposed model). RBV posits that firms achieve sustainable competitive advantage by leveraging internal strategic resources  that  are  valuable,  rare,  inimitable,  and  non-substitutable (VRIN) (Newbert, 2008; Seddon, 2014). These tangible and intangible strategic resources enable firms to create and sustain superior performance over competitors (Jiao et al., 2020; Wang & Zhang, 2025). In this study, AI capabilities and GEO are conceptualized as strategic resources, whose alignment and deployment can promote creative and innovative outcomes,  ultimately  leading  to  enhanced  sustainable  performance (Estensoro et al., 2022).

AI capabilities are increasingly acknowledged as valuable intangible resources under the RBV framework because they facilitate knowledge creation,  improve  decision-making  processes,  and  enable  efficient operational  performance  (Chen  et  al.,  2022;  Wang  et  al.,  2025).  As technological advancements in AI continue to evolve, SMEs have the opportunity  to  utilize  AI  tools  for  various  organizational  functions. These capabilities are not only scarce but also difficult to imitate without substantial investment and organizational orientation, thereby fulfilling the VRIN criteria of RBV (Chen et al., 2022). Within this theoretical frame, Sj ¨ odin et al. (2023) mentioned that three constructs are essential in  shaping  AI  capabilities:  AI  infrastructure,  business  spanning,  and proactive  stance.  AI  infrastructure  refers  to  the  foundational  technological  systems,  platforms,  and  architecture  necessary  to  support  AI deployment in business activities (Mikalef et al., 2023). A robust infrastructure enables seamless integration of intelligent tools and enhances the organization ' s ability to leverage big data, machine learning, and other AI techniques to support core operations and analytics (Mikalef & Gupta, 2021). On the other hand, business spanning denotes how AI technologies are integrated across different departments and functions within the firm (Mikalef et al., 2023). This cross-functional integration promotes  knowledge  sharing,  interdepartmental  collaboration,  and cohesive decision-making, thereby contributing to the development of comprehensive AI capabilities.

Furthermore, a proactive stance captures the organization ' s strategic readiness and willingness to embrace technological change. Firms with a proactive approach to digital transformation tend to invest in AI earlier, experiment with emerging technologies, and develop adaptive strategies that support long-term growth (Yusuf et al., 2024). Together, these three constructs shape the overall AI capabilities of SMEs, positioning them to respond  creatively  and  innovatively  to  market  and  environmental challenges (Chen et al., 2022).

In  parallel,  Green  Entrepreneurial  Orientation  (GEO)  is  another strategic  resource  viewed  through  the  RBV  lens  (Rong  et  al.,  2025). Rong et al. (2025) stated that GEO embodies the environmental consciousness  of  a  firm ' s  entrepreneurial  posture  and  includes  green risk-taking, innovativeness, and proactiveness. It represents an W.N. Alwakid and N.A. Dahri intangible,  firm-specific  capability  that  proactively  enables  SMEs  to respond  to  ecological  challenges  and  sustainability  demands  (Bhatti et al., 2023). Green risk-taking refers to the willingness of firms to invest in  novel  and  uncertain  green  projects,  such  as  adopting  eco-friendly technologies or engaging in sustainable supply chain practices (Lumpkin & Dess, 1996), even in the face of financial or technological uncertainty (Rong et al., 2025). Moreover, Rong et al. (2025) stated that Green  innovativeness  involves  generating  new  ideas  and  developing green  products,  processes,  or  services  that  minimize  environmental impact while enhancing market competitiveness. Green proactiveness reflects  the  firm ' s  forward-looking  behavior  in  anticipating  environmental issues and responding through sustainable initiatives even before regulatory  pressures  arise  (Miller,  1983).  These  dimensions  of  GEO equip firms to act strategically in alignment with environmental values, fostering an  organizational  culture that prioritizes ecological responsibility, long-term thinking, and adaptability (Miller, 1983). GEO, when well-integrated with other strategic resources, enhances a firm ' s capacity to meet both market and sustainability goals simultaneously (Rong et al., 2025).

## 2.6. Green proactiveness → green entrepreneurial orientation

Green proactiveness refers to a company ' s ability to anticipate, react to, and integrate sustainability in its core strategies before the exacerbation of environmental issues (Saether et al., 2021). Buysse, Kristel, and Alain Verbeke (Buysse & Verbeke, 2003) mentioned that proactiveness  allows  companies  to be  in  front  of  regulatory  requirements, market demand, and customer preference for green alternatives. Specifically, this research examined how SME creativity and green innovation mediate these relationships. ## 3.1. Sampling strategy and rationale

A two-stage sampling method was adopted to ensure a representative and statistically robust sample. In the first stage, a preliminary list of 500 SMEs operating within the manufacturing and service sectors in Saudi Arabia was compiled from publicly available industry directories and SME databases. In the second stage, 270 SMEs were randomly selected using simple random sampling techniques across both online and offline channels and that have used AI technologies. Following data cleaning, screening for missing values, and checking for inconsistencies, 250 valid responses were retained for final analysis.

The  sample  size  of  250  exceeds  the  minimum  requirements  for Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) analysis. Tinsley & Tinsley, (1987) recommend 5 -10 respondents per indicator variable, implying a minimum sample of 205 -410 for the 50 indicators used in this study. Similarly, Li and Lomax (2017) advocate a sample range of 100 -500 based on model complexity. Moreover, Reinartz et al. (2009) also affirm that a minimum of 100 responses is acceptable for PLS-SEM. Therefore, the sample size of 250 meets all recommended thresholds, ensuring validity and reliability of the results.

## 3.2. Instrument development and construct items

The survey instrument  comprised  50 items  on  a  five-point  Likert scale ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). This scale was selected for its ease of comprehension and widespread use in social science research (Bouranta et al., 2009; Dahri, Yahaya, Al-Rahmi, et al., 2024; Soormo et al., 2024) and its proven ability to reduce respondent fatigue and enhance participation (Taherdoost, 2019; Tanujaya et al., 2022). All measurement items were adopted and adapted from previously validated scales: AI Capabilities were measured using dimensions such as infrastructure, business spanning, and proactive stance, adapted from studies by Mikalef et al. (2023), Mikalef and Gupta (2021), Chen et al. (2022) and Imjai et al. (2024). Green Entrepreneurial Orientation (GEO)  was  assessed  using  items  related  to  green  risk-taking,  green innovativeness, and green proactiveness, based on the work of (Bhatti et al., 2023; Borah et al., 2023; Xiao et al., 2023). SME Creativity and Green  Innovation  (mediating  variables)  were  measured  using  scales adapted from (Bhatti et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2022; Xiao et al., 2023). Sustainable  Performance  (dependent  variable)  was  evaluated  using multidimensional economic, social, and environmental measures adapted from sustainable enterprise performance models (Bhatti et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2022; Mikalef & Gupta, 2021). A complete list of items and sources is provided in Appendix A, ensuring methodological transparency and replicability.

## 3.3. Validity, reliability, and pilot study

The  survey  instrument  was  reviewed  by  three  domain  experts specializing in AI, sustainable entrepreneurship, and innovation management to ensure content validity. Their feedback was incorporated to improve language clarity and eliminate ambiguous items. A pilot study W.N. Alwakid and N.A. Dahri was conducted with 70 SME managers to test the reliability and internal consistency  of  the  instrument.  The  Cronbach ' s  alpha  values  for  all constructs exceeded the threshold of 0.80, indicating excellent internal consistency  (Nunnally & Bernstein,  1994).  Results  from  the  pilot informed minor revisions to sentence structures but confirmed the instrument ' s adequacy for the full-scale study.

## 3.5. Data analysis: Structural Equation Modeling (SEM)

The  study  employed  Partial  Least  Squares  Structural  Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) using SmartPLS 4.0 to analyze the proposed conceptual  model.  PLS-SEM  was  chosen  for  its  robustness  in  handling complex models and its suitability for exploratory studies and theory development,  particularly  under  non-normal  data  distributions  and moderate  sample  sizes  (Hair  et  al.,  2014,  2019a).  A  two-stage  SEM analysis was conducted: First, Measurement Model Assessment: Convergent validity was evaluated using factor loadings ( > 0.70), composite  reliability  (CR > 0.70),  and  average  variance  extracted  (AVE > 0.50) as per Fornell & Larcker (Fornell & Larcker, 1981a). Discriminant validity was assessed through the ' Fornell-Larcker criterion and the Heterotrait-Monotrait (HTMT) ratio ' (Henseler et al., 2015), confirming construct  uniqueness.  Reliability  was  ensured  with  Cronbach ' s  alpha values > 0.70  for  all  constructs.  Multicollinearity  was  checked  via ' Variance  Inflation  Factor  (VIF) ' ,  with  all  values  below  the  recommended threshold of 3.0, indicating no multicollinearity issues (Sarstedt et al., 2021). Second, Structural Model Assessment: Hypotheses were tested using the bootstrapping method with five thousand resamples to determine  path  coefficient  significance.  Coefficient  of  determination (R 2 ) values were examined, with values > 0.30 indicating moderate and > 0.50 indicating high explanatory power (Hair et al., 2017). Effect size (f 2 )  was  calculated  to  evaluate  the  impact  of  predictor  variables  on dependent variables, categorized as small ( > 0.02), medium ( > 0.15), or large ( > 0.35) following Cohen ' s guidelines (Cohen, 1988).

## 3.6. Common method bias (CMB)

Several procedures were employed to assess and mitigate the risk of common method bias (CMB): Procedural remedies included clear item wording,  assured  anonymity,  and  psychological  separation  of  items. Harman ' s  Single  Factor  Test  was  conducted  using  exploratory  factor analysis. The first factor accounted for 43 % of the total variance, below the 50 % threshold, suggesting that CMB was not a significant concern (Dahri et al., 2025; Podsakoff et al., 2003). Variance Inflation Factor (VIF) values ranged between 1.38 and 2.50, well below the critical value of 3.0, indicating the absence of multicollinearity and CMB (Hair, Hult, et al., 2021; Kock, 2015).

## 5. Discussion

This  study  examines  the  role  of  Artificial  Intelligence  capabilities (AIC)  and  Green  Entrepreneurial  Orientation  (GEO)  in  Green  Innovations (GRI), SME Creativity (GSC), and Sustainable Performance (GSP) in small and medium enterprises (SMEs). The findings revealed that AIC and green entrepreneurial activities have significantly contributed  to  green  innovations,  creativity,  and  sustainable  performance in SMEs. The findings also contribute to digital transformation and  sustainability  literature,  demonstrating  how  AI-based  and  green entrepreneurial strategies can revolutionize business processes.

## 5.1. The role of AI capabilities in developing SME creativity, green innovations, and sustainable performance

The study examined how AI abilities propel green innovations, SME creativity, and sustainability. The performance of AI relies on organizational determinants like infrastructure (p = 0.000), business spanning (p = 0.020), and a proactive attitude (p = 0.000). Pretz-Andersson et al. (2024) emphasized that strong infrastructure provides the technology for AI, enabling SMEs to automate, predict, and optimize resources. The business spanning variable captures external collaboration and market reach and is critical for AI adoption. Carayannis et al. (2025) highlight that SMEs with wide networks are likely to invest in AI to improve efficiency, streamline processes, and improve competitiveness. Moreover, a proactive attitude captures a firm ' s inclination to embrace tech advancements,  which  is  critical  for  AI  success.  Firms  that  embrace  AI strategies early are well placed to propel sustainable growth and attain long-term competitiveness.

Evidence also supports the massive effect of AI capabilities on SME creativity (p = 0.000) and green innovations (p = 0.000). AI technologies  drive  innovation  in  problem-solving,  enabling  SMEs  to  change business models, generate sustainable products, and attain sustainable production. Han & Trimi, (2022) contribute to real-time automation and analytics to aid SMEs in optimizing resources, driving innovation, and creativity. Raman et al. (2024) also mentioned that AI supports green innovations  by  facilitating  green  strategies,  waste  reduction,  and energy-efficient production. Moreover, the findings of Madanaguli et al. (2024) highlighted how AI-enhanced process automation drives operational efficiency and sustainability across sectors, especially in developing economies. However, while their study focused on manufacturing or  circular  business  models  (CBM),  the  current  study  extends  these findings into SME-driven innovation ecosystems. In contrast to traditional  RBV-based interpretations that emphasize static resources, our findings  support  a  more  active  view  where  AI  functions  as  a  transformative enabler of innovation and creativity, consistent with recent studies by Arroyabe et al. (2024), who found AI-driven capabilities to be essential for fostering digital ambidexterity in SMEs. Unlike earlier work by Chen et al. (2022), which viewed AI primarily through cost-efficiency lenses,  our  results  suggest  AI  adoption  contributes  significantly  to environmental  stewardship  through  innovation.  These  findings  are supported  by  earlier  studies  on  AI ' s  role  in  sustainability-inspired innovation.  More  recent  empirical  work  by  (Elia  et  al.,  2020)  and Arroyabe  et  al.  (2024) also  supports  the  notion  that  AI  capabilities generate economic and ecological value when embedded in organizational  learning  systems.  However,  these  studies  often  neglect  the moderating role of firm-level characteristics such as proactive culture or external collaboration -elements our study underscores as essential for AI ' s effectiveness.

These findings highlight SME investment in digital infrastructure to ensure the adoption of AI and innovation. The government must introduce training schemes and financial support to allow the integration of AI, making SMEs competitive in an AI-driven economy. Chatterjee et al. (2024) similarly emphasized that public-private partnership models are crucial  to  addressing  digital  capability  gaps  in  SMEs,  particularly  in emerging economies, such as Saudi Arabia. Adopting AI capabilities can enable SMEs to attain  economic  growth  while  supporting  global  sustainability  goals, making SMEs leaders in the digital economy.

## 5.4. Theoretical implications

This study advances the theoretical discourse on the Resource-Based View (RBV) by refining its application to AI capabilities, green entrepreneurial  orientation  (GEO),  and  sustainable  performance  in  SMEs. Traditionally,  RBV  posits  that  firms  achieve  sustained  competitive advantage  by  strategically  deploying  valuable,  rare,  inimitable,  and non-substitutable  (VRIN)  resources  (Barney,  1991;  Wang & Zhang, 2025). Our findings extend this theory by positioning AI-based competencies -such as intelligent infrastructure, predictive analytics, and decision-support systems -as critical VRIN assets that enable SMEs to develop eco-innovative capabilities and foster creativity. These intangible resources do not merely support operational efficiency but actively drive  sustainability-oriented  innovation,  a  refinement  of  the  RBV ' s conceptualization of dynamic capabilities (Wang & Zhang, 2025).

Moreover, the study validates GEO, green risk-taking, innovativeness, and proactiveness as an entrepreneurial capability that aligns with RBV ' s emphasis on internal resource orchestration. These components of GEO act as strategic enablers that shape firm behavior towards sustainability,  particularly  through  creative  responses  to  environmental challenges and the pursuit of green innovations. The robust linkages between  GEO,  SME  creativity,  and  green  innovations  reaffirm  that unique entrepreneurial orientations are valuable, inimitable, and deeply embedded in organizational culture, thus fulfilling the VRIN criteria. Importantly, this research offers a theoretical contribution by incorporating  creativity  and  green  innovation  as  mediating  capabilities  that bridge core strategic resources and sustainable performance outcomes. This mediation extends the RBV by illustrating how innovation pathways transform resource configurations into a competitive advantage. This insight responds to calls for a more process-oriented RBV framework in the context of sustainability and emerging technologies (Wang & Zhang,  2025;  Xiao  et  al.,  2023).  Finally,  by  applying  RBV  in  the context of emerging markets, particularly in the Saudi SME sector, this study  contributes  to  contextualizing  and  localizing  RBV  theory  in non-Western economies. It proposes a revised theoretical model that integrates AI, green orientation, and sustainability imperatives, offering a comprehensive view of how resource-based capabilities manifest in practice. Future research should further unpack these strong resource interactions across industry contexts and temporal dimensions.

SMEs ' managers should prioritize AI infrastructure and decision mechanisms to initiate green innovations. Automation and predictive analytics through AI  can  increase  green  process  automation  and  resource  efficiency  to enhance sustainable performance (Wang & Zhang, 2024). Policymakers  should  support  such  endeavors  by  issuing  AI adoption incentives, R & D financing for sustainability, and pro-environmental policies. Finally, the research embodies SME creativity towards sustainability. Firms should establish innovative cultures through employee training, digitalization, and cross-industry networks. ## 6. Conclusion

This  study  explored  how  AI  capability  and  green  entrepreneurial orientation  affect  SME  creativity,  green  innovations,  and  sustainable performance in Saudi SMEs. Based on the Resource-Based View (RBV), it explored how AI capability, driven by infrastructure, scope of business, and proactiveness, was integrated with green entrepreneurial orientation  as  green  risk-taking,  innovativeness,  and  proactiveness  to  drive green innovations and creativity, resulting in sustainable performance. These dynamics are critical to achieving long-term business resilience and green sustainability.

We  used  a  quantitative  approach  based  on  Structural  Equation Modeling (SEM) of survey data from Saudi SMEs in the manufacturing and services industries. Findings showed that AI capability significantly impacted green innovations (p = 0.000) and SME creativity (p = 0.000), and how AI tools improve decision-making, resource allocation,  and innovation processes. Moreover, green entrepreneurial orientation was significantly related to SME creativity (p = 0.000) and green innovations (p = 0.000), validating the hypothesis that firms that take risks and are proactive achieve better environmental performance. Furthermore, SME creativity  (p = 0.000)  and  green  innovations  (p = 0.000)  were confirmed as key drivers  of  sustainable  performance,  justifying  their mediating  role  in  establishing  a  connection  between  AI  capabilities, green entrepreneurial orientation, and long-term business sustainability. The study ' s hypotheses were confirmed, showing that.

AI capabilities are positively moderated by infrastructure, business spanning, and a proactive stance.

AI capabilities have a significant positive effect on SME creativity and green innovations.

Green entrepreneurial orientation, facilitated by green risk-taking, innovativeness,  and  proactiveness,  generates  SME  creativity  and green innovations.

SME green innovation and creativity are mediating mechanisms of prime importance, positively impacting sustainable performance.

At the theoretical level, this study enriches CCDT in verifying that AIenabled capabilities are core capabilities influencing sustainable innovation,  and RBV is enriched by revealing how green entrepreneurial resources provide sustainability competitive advantages in markets. On a practical level, the research highlights the need for SMEs to embrace AI-enabled  strategies  and  green  entrepreneurship  into  their  business models. AI infrastructure investments, sustainability-driven R & D, and state-sponsored incentives can improve SME resilience and long-term performance.

## 6.1. Study limitations and future research directions

The  study ' s  findings  are  invaluable  contributions;  the  study  has several limitations that need to be highlighted. First, generalizability is an issue. The study focused on Saudi Arabian SMEs in the manufacturing and service industries, which may restrict the findings ' generalizability to  other  industries and nations with dissimilar regulatory, economic, and  technological  contexts.  Future  research  needs  to  address  cross-

country  or  sector-based  analysis  to  enhance  the  external  validity  of the  findings.  Second,  the  use  of  self-reported  survey  data  presents certain limitations. While surveys effectively capture managerial perceptions and strategic orientations, they are subject to potential biases such as social desirability bias, recall bias, and standard method variance. Respondents may unintentionally overstate their firm ' s capabilities or sustainability practices, which could affect the accuracy of the findings. Future research could mitigate these concerns by employing multi-source data, including objective performance metrics or triangulating survey results with interviews or archival data.

Additionally, expanding the sample to include SMEs from different regions  and  industries  would  improve  generalizability  and  provide  a broader view of the phenomena under investigation. Theoretically, the study used the Resource-Based View (RBV) to describe the effect of AI capabilities and green entrepreneurial orientation. Whilst these frameworks were helpful in perceptions, future research may use alternative theories to better account for AI adoption and sustainability practices in SMEs. Methodologically, the study ' s use of a cross-sectional approach restricts its ability to identify causality. Future research should use a longitudinal approach to monitor how AI capabilities and green entrepreneurial  orientation  affect  SME  creativity,  green  innovations,  and sustainable performance over time.

Furthermore, qualitative findings through case studies or expert interviews could supplement the quantitative approach, providing a more subtle understanding of the underlying mechanisms. Finally, analytical innovation could add even more to the worth of the study. Since SEM has robust statistical verification, using artificial neural networks (ANN) or machine learning techniques could enhance prediction further and unveil subtle non-linear interactions. Future research could further explore such advanced analytical techniques to elucidate the dynamics of AIenabled sustainability strategy in SMEs.
