## 1. Introduction

The hospitality industry stands at a pivotal juncture, melding traditional service values with the transformative potential of artificial intelligence (AI). As AI emerges as a crucial force, it drives unprecedented enhancements in service quality, operational efficiency, and personalized guest (Bulchand-Gidumal et al., 2023; Guo et al., 2024). However, the integration of AI demands a delicate balance of technology readiness and  continuous  adaptation  to  evolving  market  demands -areas  that remain  critically  underexplored  (Parvez,  2020;  Warner  and  W ¨ ager, 2019).

Recent  developments  underscore  the  urgency  of  addressing  this balance. For example, Marriott International has been at the forefront of implementing AI-driven chatbots to handle customer inquiries, significantly  enhancing  response  times  and  customer  satisfaction  (Al-Hyari et al., 2023). ## A B S T R A C T

This study explores the relationship between technology readiness, AI adoption, dynamic capabilities, sustainable competitive advantage, and technological turbulence, assessing their collective impact on value creation within the Malaysian hospitality industry. Employing the Resource-Based View, Dynamic Capabilities Framework, and Contingency Theory, the research analyzes these strategic elements ' roles in enhancing organizational performance. Using stratified purposive sampling, data was collected from 336 respondence. Structural equation modeling was utilized to analyze the relationships among the constructs. The results reveal that technology readiness and AI adoption positively influence value creation, with AI adoption playing a direct role in enhancing organizational  capabilities  and  efficiencies.  Interestingly,  dynamic  capabilities  exhibited  a  complex  impact, initially  impeding value creation due to transitional challenges, but ultimately proving crucial in navigating technological turbulence. Sustainable competitive advantage emerged as a significant mediator, translating AI adoption and dynamic capabilities into enhanced value creation. However, the moderating effects of technological turbulence were selective, significantly influencing only the relationship between dynamic capabilities and value creation. These findings underscore the nuanced interplay between strategic resources and environmental factors in driving value creation in a technologically evolving industry.

These  examples  illustrate  how  AI  is  not  only  meeting  the  growing expectation for hyper-personalization but also establishing new benchmarks for service excellence in the industry. As reported by The Wall Street  Journal and CNBC in  2023,  such  AI-driven  innovations  are increasingly  becoming  the  standard  in  the  competitive  hospitality market (Captain, 2023; Siu, 2023).

Moreover, advancements in machine learning, a key subset of AI, have  significantly  improved  data  quality  and  algorithmic  accuracy (Okafor et al., 2020; Whang et al., 2023). Despite these technological strides, the sector faces significant challenges in applying AI to enhance customer interactions and streamline operations (Bassano et al., 2020). For  instance,  the  ethical  implications  of  AI  deployment,  particularly regarding data privacy, have become a focal point of concern. Reports from The Deloitte reveal growing unease over AI ' s role in guest monitoring and data usage, sparking debates on how hospitality businesses can  responsibly  integrate  these  technologies  without  compromising customer trust (Leila, 2024). For instance, the use of facial recognition technology in some hotels has raised questions about consent and data protection, prompting calls for clearer regulations and more transparent practices (Solarova et al., 2023).

Additionally, AI ' s potential to foster inclusive experiences and create new value -for  instance,  improving  service  accessibility  for  disabled guests -highlights its broader implications (Navarro et al., 2014; Vuong and  Tran,  2021).  AI-driven  solutions,  such  as  voice-activated  room controls and tailored in-room services for guests with disabilities, are increasingly being adopted by hotels (Buhalis and Moldavska, 2021a). These innovations address a critical need for inclusivity in the industry. However, the unpredictable shifts characteristic of today ' s technological landscape complicates strategic AI deployment, blending preparedness with the necessity for agility (Arias-P ´ erez and Cepeda-Cardona, 2022). The competitive pressures resulting from technological disruption, as highlighted by Harvard Business Review and TechCrunch in 2024, further emphasize the need for businesses to swiftly adapt to maintain market relevance (Calder et al., 2024; Wiggers, 2024).

Research indicates that while hospitality businesses are poised  to leverage their dynamic capabilities for AI adoption, there is a significant gap in harnessing these capabilities to fully engage stakeholders and enhance the guest experience comprehensively (Ghesh et al., 2023). The true challenge lies in utilizing AI not merely as a tool for operational efficiency (Rana et al., 2022) but as a strategic asset to actively generate value for users -a pathway crucial for achieving sustainable competitive advantage  (Jiang  and  Wen,  2020;  Kot  and  Leszczy ´ nski,  2022).  This challenge is particularly pertinent as companies face mounting competitive pressures and the need for innovation to stay ahead of new market entrants rapidly embracing AI technologies.

To address these gaps, this study employs a quantitative approach using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) to investigate  how  Malaysian  hospitality  businesses  can  leverage  their dynamic capabilities for effective AI adoption. Specifically, the research seeks to answer the following questions:

How do technology readiness and dynamic capabilities influence AI adoption  in  Malaysia ' s  hospitality  industry,  particularly  in  the context of technological turbulence?

How  does  AI  adoption  contribute  to  value  co-creation  between hospitality businesses and their customers, and how is this process influenced by the businesses ' sustainable competitive advantage?

What is the mediating role of sustainable competitive advantage in the  impact  of  technology  readiness,  dynamic  capabilities,  and  AI adoption on value creation, and how does technological turbulence modify these relationships?

The anticipated outcomes of this study are set to challenge existing paradigms and extend the understanding of how dynamic capabilities are exploited in an AI-enhanced environment. The findings aim to provide hospitality businesses with a strategic blueprint for deploying AI, not  merely  to  optimize  operational  efficiency  but  also  to  enrich  the customer experience, thus redefining industry standards. Additionally, this research will inform policymakers and educators, guiding regulatory frameworks and academic curricula to prepare industry leaders for a future increasingly dominated by AI.

By  examining  these  dynamics  through  a  multifaceted  theoretical lens, the research offers fresh insights into technology ' s role in fostering value creation and securing competitive advantage, significantly enriching the academic discourse on technology management in volatile environments. Moreover, exploring emerging AI technologies and their potential future roles in hospitality underscores the study ' s relevance in guiding long-term strategic planning and innovation.

## 2. Literature review

2.1. Integration of AI adoption, dynamic capabilities, and value cocreation in organizational performance

The  existing  literature  offers  extensive  insights  into  AI  adoption, dynamic  capabilities,  value  co-creation,  and  sustainable  competitive advantage, particularly within the hospitality, tourism, and SME sectors. The literature reveals several critical factors driving AI adoption across various industries. For instance, Islam et al. (2023) identify perceived ease  of  use,  perceived  usefulness,  technology  competency,  top  management support, and competitive pressure as significant influencers of AI  adoption  in  Malaysia ' s  tourism  industry,  with  market  turbulence playing a moderating role. Similarly, Lada et al. (2023) emphasize the importance of top management commitment and organizational readiness for AI adoption among Malaysian SMEs, noting that competitive pressure and employee adaptability have lesser impacts (see Table 1).

Dynamic  capabilities  have  also  been  recognized  as  crucial  for enhancing operational sustainability and firm performance, particularly in environments characterized by technological turbulence. Naz et al. (2023) demonstrate that big data analytics and AI capabilities significantly enhance hotel performance in Malaysia, moderated by environmental  dynamism.  Chatterjee  et  al.  (2021) further  explore  how absorptive capacity and dynamic capabilities impact the adoption of ubiquitous customer relationship management (UCRM) systems, which, in turn, positively influence operational sustainability and firm performance.

AI ' s role in facilitating value co-creation is another significant theme in the literature. Carvalho and Alves (2022) provide a systematic review of  customer  value  co-creation  in  the  hospitality  industry,  identifying customer behaviors and technological resources as key drivers. Chandra and Rahman (2024) develop a framework integrating AI characteristics with customer behavior, showing how AI influences customer decision-making  processes.  Paschen  et  al.  (2020) contribute  to  this discussion by examining the complex interactions between human and non-human actors in AI-enabled value co-creation.

Moreover,  the  impact  of  technological  disruptions,  including  Industry  4.0  and  the  Metaverse,  on  the  hospitality  industry  has  been widely recognized. Buhalis et al. (2019), Buhalis et al. (2022), Buhalis and Moldavska (2021b) discuss the challenges and opportunities posed by these disruptions, emphasizing the need for strategic reengineering to maintain  sustainable  competitive  advantage.  Ed-Dafali  et  al.  (2023) examine how Industry 4.0 readiness mediates the relationship between strategic orientations and sustainable competitive advantage for SMEs in emerging markets.

This  study  makes  a  significant  contribution  by  integrating  these diverse streams of literature to offer a comprehensive model that examines the interrelated impact of AI adoption, dynamic capabilities, and value co-creation on organizational performance and sustainability in Malaysia ' s hospitality and SME sectors. Unlike previous research that often  treated  these  variables  in  isolation,  this  study  investigates  the interplay between AI adoption and dynamic capabilities, explores the moderating role of market turbulence and competitive pressure, and focuses on AI-facilitated value co-creation. By doing so, this research provides novel insights into how these elements collectively drive performance and strategic advantage in a rapidly evolving technological landscape, particularly within the unique context of Malaysia.

Central to this view is the assertion that AI, as a strategic technological resource, can significantly enhance performance and sustainability when properly managed amidst technological turbulence and with effective leadership support (Chatterjee et al., 2021; Zahra, 2021).

The Dynamic Capabilities Framework complements RBV by stressing the need for ongoing adaptation and innovation in response to rapid technological  changes  (Dhanu  et  al.,  2024;  Giannoukou,  2024).  This framework highlights the necessity for businesses to adapt their models swiftly and effectively in response to environmental changes such as market  fluctuations  and  crises,  ensuring  competitive  advantage  and continuous innovation (Campo et al., 2014; Cheah et al., 2018; Linde et al., 2021). Meanwhile, Contingency Theory posits that optimal strategies are contingent upon fluctuating internal and external circumstances, making  it  crucial  for  organizations  to  navigate  disruptions  and  enhance service innovation in the hospitality sector (Buhalis and Sinarta, 2019; Gaur et al., 2021; Senbeto and Hon, 2021). This integration is particularly critical in navigating the complexities of market conditions  and  organizational  readiness,  which  are  key  for  achieving  sustainable success in a competitive industry.

Collectively, these theoretical perspectives provide a robust framework  (refer  to  Fig.  1)  for  understanding  how  leveraging  advanced technologies  and  strategic  adaptability  are  essential  for  maintaining competitiveness and fostering value creation in a dynamic and rapidly evolving sector. ## 2.3. Hypothesis development

Damerji and Salimi (2021) and Sudaryanto et al. (2023) established that perceived ease of use and usefulness significantly affect AI adoption,  though  Sudaryanto  noted  that  technology  readiness  might  not directly impact AI adoption. Further supporting this, Palade and Carutasu (2023) and AlSheibani et al. (2018) suggested that AI readiness fits well within broader digitization frameworks, while Flavi ´ an et al. (2021) and Godoe and Johansen (2012) underscored the crucial role of technology readiness, particularly through customers ' technological optimism and discomfort. J ¨ ohnk et al. (2021) identified key AI readiness factors such as assets, capabilities, and commitment.

Regarding technology readiness and value creation, Kearney et al. (2018) and  White  et  al.  (2017) found  that  technology  readiness  enhances value creation behaviors for both enterprise and users, supported by Kearney ' s emphasis on digital technology satisfaction. Fujihara et al. (2022) and Lin (2007) noted that human values and technology readiness influence online purchase intentions and perceived e-service value, respectively.  Similarly,  Lee  et  al.  (2009) linked  personal  values  and technology readiness to service quality and customer loyalty, with Tuyet and  Tuan  (2020) and  Yieh  et  al.  (2012) confirming  the  impact  on perceived value and customer satisfaction.

These findings lead to the formulation of the following hypotheses:

H1 . .  Technology  readiness  positively  influences  AI  adoption  within organizations.

[S. 6]

H2 . . Technology readiness positively impacts value creation for both enterprises and users.

AI  adoption  significantly  influences  value  creation  across  various sectors (Bassano et al., 2020; Haftor et al., 2024; Kot and Leszczy ´ nski, 2022; Payne et al., 2021; Washizaki, 2020; Y. Zhu et al., 2022). In the UAE,  organizational  decisions  are  shaped  by  the  accessibility  of  AI technologies, with perceived risks associated with job displacement and creation  being  notable  factors  (Alzaabi  and  Shuhaiber,  2022).  In healthcare,  the  value  creation  facilitated  by  AI  hinges  on  digital self-efficacy and the quality of relational services, with shared decision-making as a pivotal component (Swan et al., 2023). Perifanis and  Kitsios  (2023) suggests  that  integrating  AI  into  business  and  IT strategies  offers  potential  for  developing  new  business  models  and gaining competitive advantages. In the luxury sector, AI applications such as chatbots contribute to value creation by enhancing customer engagement (Bassano et al., 2020). Moreover, the appeal of intelligent personal assistants is driven by their usefulness, enjoyment, technical features, and cost benefits, which collectively influence their perceived value and adoption (White et al., 2017). AI virtual humans create value by fulfilling social and respectful needs through interactive experiences (Xu, 2023).

Dynamic capabilities also play a crucial role in value creation, as evidenced by multiple studies. Rashidirad and Salimian (2020a) and Dyduch et al. (2021) identified that sensing, learning, integrating, and coordinating  capabilities  are  essential  for  SMEs  to  create  value.  Yu (2021) found that dynamic capabilities positively moderate the impact of enterprise digitalization on financial performance. X. Sun and Zhang (2021) and  Madhala  et  al.  (2020) highlighted  the  importance  of resource and technology integration capabilities, along with analytics, in  steering  clear  of  value  creation  pitfalls  and  achieving  data-driven outcomes  for  both  enterprise  and  users.  Innovation  and  effective decision-making capabilities are key to enhancing value creation and competitive advantage, as noted by Ayegba and Lin (2020) and Mehralian et al. (2022). Additionally, Sasmoko et al. (2019) emphasized the critical role of digital leadership in boosting innovation and dynamic capabilities.

These insights substantiate the following hypotheses:

H3 . . AI adoption will positively influence value creation.

H4 . . Dynamic capabilities will positively influence value creation.

Numerous studies have  investigated  the  link  between  sustainable competitive advantage (SCA) and value creation. Guptam and Benson (2011) found that sustainable companies are not only competitive but also excel financially. Hariastuti et al. (2021) identified key drivers of sustainable value creation, such as environmental and social factors, and their impact on competitive advantages. Bhattacharjee (2018) explored how disruptive value creation affects competitive forces, while Rodriguez et al. (2002) proposed a dynamic firm perspective incorporating sustainable development. Laszlo et al. (2006) stressed the importance of stakeholder  integration  in  decision-making  to  enhance  competitive advantage. Empirical findings by Hahn et al. (2007) in the manufacturing sector and a demand-based view of SCA by Adner and Zemsky (2004) further validate the significant relationship between SCA and value creation, which is echoed by Thai and Mai (2023) who linked competitive advantage to sustainable growth.

H5 . .  Sustainable  competitive  advantage  will  mediate  the  positive  relationship between technology readiness and value creation.

The mediation role of SCA in the relationship between technology readiness  and  value  creation  has  been  supported  by  various  studies. Talebnejad (2012) emphasized the importance of managerial skills in IT for creating and sustaining SCA, which is crucial for value creation as suggested by Adner and Zemsky (2004). Guptam and Benson (2011) and Rodriguez et al. (2002) highlighted that sustainable companies, often adept  at  leveraging  technology,  excel  competitively  and  financially, with  sustainability  enhancing  SCA  and  competitiveness.  The  digital economy further leverages technology for sustainability-driven competitive advantage (Nurova and Freze, 2021).

H6 . . Sustainable Competitive Advantage mediates the influence of technology readiness on Value Creation.

The role of AI in value creation is intricate and influenced by multiple factors. Studies like Perifanis and Kitsios (2023) and Khan et al. (2024) indicate AI ' s significant impact on business value across areas such as decision-making and supply chain optimization. Yet, its influence  is  mediated  by  factors  like  AI  availability  and  perceived  risks (Alzaabi and Shuhaiber, 2022; Govindan, 2024). The interplay of AI and sustainability,  enhancing  green  innovation  and  Sustainable  Development Goals (Mhlanga, 2021; Wang, 2023), suggests SCA ' s  mediating role,  supported  by  strategic  frameworks  like  those  proposed  by  Bari et  al.  (2024) which  advocate  for  SCA  through  sustainable  dynamic capabilities.

H7 . .  Sustainable  Competitive  Advantage  mediates  the  influence  of  AI adoption on Value Creation.

Furthermore,  dynamic  capabilities  (DC)  are  pivotal  in  sustaining competitive performance and advantage, as highlighted by Mikalef and Pateli  (2017) and Acosta-Prado and Tafur-Mendoza (2022), who underline  the  essential  role  of  IT-enabled  dynamic  capabilities.  Correia et al. (2020) suggests that dynamic capabilities mediate the relationship between  market  orientation  and  competitive  advantages,  affecting business performance and linking DC directly to value creation through SCA.

H8 . .  Sustainable  Competitive  Advantage  mediates  the  influence  of  Dynamic Capability on Value Creation.

Low et al. (2021) and Chng et al. (2021) highlight the significant moderating role of technological turbulence, where Lee emphasizes its impact  on  internal  market  orientation  processes,  and  Ch ' ng  demonstrates its positive influence on eco-organizational innovation and social performance. The dynamic nature of technological turbulence implies that firms operating in turbulent environments need to be more adaptive and flexible to maintain a competitive edge. This aligns with Cheah et al. (2018) and Matraves and Rondi (2005) findings that industry turbulence can  enhance  a  firm ' s  Sustainable  Competitive  Advantage  (SCA)  by forcing  companies  to  continuously  innovate  and  differentiate  their products.

The role of digitalization and disruptive value creation in sustaining competitive advantages under technological turbulence is further supported by Knudsen et al. (2021) and Bhattacharjee (2018). They suggest that firms that successfully navigate digital transformation in turbulent environments  can  leverage  these  disruptions  to create sustained competitive  advantages.  Furthermore,  strategic  responses  such  as maneuverability and flexibility, as discussed by Mariam et al. (2023) and Celuch and Kasouf (2014), illustrate how firms can innovate business models and enhance customer value despite -or because of -turbulent conditions.

Dayioglu and Kusku (2022) and Wiesel (2022) provide additional insights  into  the  strengthening  effect  of  technological  turbulence  on aligning organizational commitment with value creation activities and on customer-based corporate valuation. Margarida et al. (2018) explore how  technological  turbulence  influences  the  relationship  between organizational  innovation  intensity  and  export  performance,  particularly  through  the  mediating  role  of  marketing  capabilities.  These  insights underscore the critical role that technological turbulence plays in shaping firm strategies and outcomes.

Finally,  studies  by  Autry  et  al.  (2010) and  Hamdi  et  al.  (2016) highlight how technological turbulence moderates key organizational processes, such as technology acceptance and innovation speed, further emphasizing its pivotal role in different organizational contexts.

## 3. Research methodology

The choice of Malaysia as the focus for this study on AI adoption and value creation in the hospitality industry is motivated by its vibrant hospitality sector, burgeoning AI ecosystem, and strategic significance in  the  Asia-Pacific  region.  Malaysia ' s  commitment  to  technological innovation, supported by a significant rise in AI startups, provides a dynamic backdrop for exploring AI ' s integration across diverse hospitality settings. This setting allows for a nuanced examination of how advanced  technologies  can  enhance  service  delivery  and  operational efficiency.

## 3.1. Sampling

Utilizing a stratified purposive sampling approach, the research is designed to capture diverse perspectives across various industry management levels, focusing on prominent hospitality companies and AI startups in Malaysia. The data collection period of December 2023 to February 2024 was strategically chosen to align with the year-end and new-year business planning phases, when organizations typically make or  evaluate  decisions  on  technological  adoption,  including  AI.  This timing  also  coincided  with  a  post-peak  tourism  season  in  Malaysia, ensuring better availability of key industry stakeholders.

Out of the initial group, 179 organizations showcased extensive AI exposure within their operations, a criterion crucial for ensuring the data accurately reflected deep-seated experiences and perspectives on AI ' s implementation and impact. Ultimately, 84 organizations, encompassing both startups and established entities, participated and returned completed  questionnaires  from  four  designated  respondents  each, totalling 336 individual responses. The respondent stratification within each  organization  included  Top-Level  Management,  Middle  Management, Operational Management, and Technical Specialists . Prior to the main survey, a pilot test involving 20 selected respondents with significant AI exposure was conducted to validate the questionnaire ' s  reliability and clarity.

## 3.2. Measurement items development

Dynamic  Capability  is  conceptualized  based  on  the  pioneering frameworks of Teece, Pisano (1994), and further refined by Eisenhardt and Martin (2000). This construct encompasses three core dimensions: a.  Sensing-  detecting  and  perceiving  opportunities  and  threats,  b. Seizing- mobilizing resources to address these opportunities and threats, and c. Transforming- the ongoing adaptation and realignment of the organization (Ma et al., 2021). Value creation is framed within the bifurcates into Enterprise Value (organizational benefits from collaborative endeavors) and User Value (the personalized satisfaction and value derived by the users) (Chuang and Lin, 2015). Technology Readiness, as outlined by Shehadeh et al. (2022), evaluates an organization ' s infrastructure, cultural orientation, and strategic inclination towards embracing emerging technologies like AI, analytics, and cloud computing, highlighting the proactive adoption of digital innovations for business enhancements. Artificial Intelligence Adoption, guided by Chatterjee et al. (2023), probes into the extent and efficacy of AI technology utilization within hospitality operations, emphasizing AI ' s role in automating processes and augmenting decision-making with superior computational intelligence. Sustainable Competitive Advantage, drawing from Kim et al. (2011), investigates the long-term strategic assets that facilitate superior organizational performance. This construct focuses on leveraging AI and digital technologies for innovation, market development, and resolving industry challenges. Technological Turbulence, also based on Kim et al. (2011), captures the dynamic and often disruptive  nature  of  technological  evolution  within  the  industry, examining  its  influence  on  strategic  innovation  and  organizational responsiveness to technological breakthroughs.

## 3.3. Data analysis technique

In  this  study,  the  data  analysis  method  utilized  IBM  SPSS  for descriptive  and  demographic  statistics  to  understand  the  sample ' s characteristics, and SmartPLS v 4.0.8.1 software for structural equation modeling (SEM), aligning with Hair et al. (2022) recommendations. This two-stage  SEM  approach  began  with  establishing  the  reliability  and validity of all constructs, including the intricate dimensions of Value creation  and  Dynamic  Capability  as  higher-order  constructs.  It  then rigorously examined the relationships among these constructs within the Malaysian  hospitality  industry ' s  context  of  AI  adoption,  ensuring  a thorough investigation of their multidimensional roles and impacts.

## 4. Data analysis

The demographic analysis from this study on AI adoption and value creation for both enterprise and users within the Malaysian hospitality industry reveals a predominantly young and well-educated workforce, with the majority aged between 25 and 44 years and holding at least a Bachelor ' s degree. This suggests a workforce that is likely adaptable and receptive to new technologies, including AI. The sample shows a higher proportion of male participants but also significant female representation,  indicating  diversity  in  the  industry ' s  approach  to  technology adoption.  With  an  even  distribution  across  various  organizational roles -from top-level management to technical specialists -and a strong inclination towards AI startups (88 % of respondents), the data points to a dynamic sector keen on innovation. Additionally, the wide range of experience in the hospitality industry, predominantly less than 10 years, coupled with a substantial engagement in AI projects (86 % of organizations  involved  to  some  extent),  underscores  a  forward-looking  industry  prepared  for  digital  transformation.  This  blend  of  youthful enthusiasm,  educational  sophistication,  and  balanced  insight  across management levels presents a fertile ground for exploring the transformative  potential  of  AI  in  enhancing  operational  efficiencies  and creating value within the hospitality sector.

## 4.3. Discussion and hypothesis testing

The findings of this study provide a nuanced understanding of how technology  readiness  (TR),  AI  adoption  (ARA),  dynamic  capabilities (DC), sustainable competitive advantage (SCA), and technological turbulence  (TT)  interact  to  influence  value  creation  (VC)  within  the Malaysian hospitality industry. Each hypothesis tested contributes to a broader understanding of the complex dynamics at play (see Table 7).

Firstly, the strong positive influence of technology readiness on AI adoption ( β = 0.561, p < 0.001) confirms the foundational role that technological preparedness plays in the successful implementation of AI. This finding aligns with prior studies by Damerji and Salimi (2021) and Godoe and Johansen (2012), which emphasize that organizations with a higher degree of technological readiness are better positioned to adopt and  integrate  AI  technologies  effectively.  As  highlighted  by  Parvez, (2020), Warner and W ¨ ager (2019), many hospitality firms have not yet reached full AI maturity, largely because they are still developing the technology infrastructure and human capital necessary to support AI systems. This underscores that technology readiness is not just a preliminary step but an ongoing condition necessary for achieving true AI maturity. It also resonates with the motivation raised earlier -where the lack of full-scale AI maturity was identified as a central challenge -and reinforces the notion that investments in technology infrastructure and skills development are critical precursors to AI adoption.

However, the study presents a counterintuitive finding in Hypothesis 2, where technology readiness negatively impacts value creation ( β = 0.275, p < 0.001). This result suggests that the process of becoming technologically ready may initially create disruptions that negatively affect value creation (Alam et al., 2023; K. Zhu et al., 2004), likely due to the transitional challenges involved in aligning new technologies with existing business processes and customer expectations. This contrasts with the general assumption that technology readiness inherently boosts value creation, indicating that the benefits of technology readiness may be  more  complex and delayed than previously understood (Li et al., 2023). This finding shed light on why organizations may struggle to realize  immediate  value  from  their  AI  initiatives.  While  readiness  is necessary,  it  does  not  guarantee  instantaneous  performance  gains. Instead, it suggests that the early stages of AI deployment -marked by adjustments in infrastructure and workforce training -are crucial yet often turbulent phases that must be managed carefully to eventually achieve the value promised by AI.

Interestingly,  dynamic  capabilities,  which  are  often  viewed  as essential for sustaining competitive advantage and driving value creation, showed a negative relationship with value creation ( β = 0.385, p < 0.001). Dynamic capabilities are not instant panaceas; they require iterative learning and adaptation before they translate into measurable value.

Sustainable competitive advantage (SCA) was identified as a crucial mediator in the relationship between AI adoption and value creation ( β = 0.063, p = 0.002), as well as between dynamic capabilities and value creation ( β = 0.131, p = 0.001). These results underscore the importance of SCA as a bridge between advanced technological adoption and tangible value creation outcomes. This aligns with research by Guptam and  Benson  (2011) and  Hariastuti  et  al.  (2021),  who  argue  that sustainability-focused  competitive  strategies  enhance  organizational performance and value creation. However, the expected mediation of SCA between technology readiness  and  value  creation  (H6)  was  not supported ( β = 0.008, p = 0.574), suggesting that simply being technologically ready does not automatically lead to value creation through SCA. In line with the initial motivations, this implies that while SCA can enhance the payoff from AI adoption and dynamic capabilities, it does not automatically translate the mere existence of technological infrastructure  and know-how into added value. Organizations must enact strategic and managerial interventions to harness the benefits of their readiness fully.

Regarding technological turbulence (TT), the findings were mixed. While  TT  significantly  moderated  the  relationship  between  dynamic capabilities  and  value  creation  (H12:  path  coefficient = 0.140, p = 0.025) -highlighting  the  importance  of  adaptability  in  turbulent environments -it did not significantly moderate the impact of SCA, TR, or ARA on VC (H9: p = 0.214; H10: p = 0.845; H11: p = 0.062). These non-significant findings in H9 to H11 offer limited theoretical contribution, as they do not substantively alter our understanding of how SCA, TR, or ARA functions under turbulent technological conditions (Ahmed et al., 2022; Senathirajah et al., 2024). Instead, they reinforce the idea that  turbulence ' s  effects  are  highly  context-dependent  and  may  not uniformly influence all strategic or capability-driven pathways. Still, the result for H12 supports the notion that when faced with rapid technological changes, the ability to reconfigure and reposition through dynamic capabilities becomes particularly valuable. This insight resonates with literature suggesting that firms equipped with strong adaptive capacities can better leverage turbulent conditions to innovate and create value (Lee and Yoo, 2021).

[S. 11]

Literature on technological turbulence supports the notion that it can either hinder or enhance strategic business processes and value creation. For instance, Lee and Yoo (2021) have demonstrated how technological turbulence can positively impact internal market orientation processes and drive eco-organizational innovation and social performance. These findings align with the observed moderation in H12, suggesting that dynamic capabilities are particularly crucial in adapting and thriving in turbulent environments. Conversely, works by Cheah et al. (2018) and Matraves  and  Rondi  (2005) indicate  that  industry  turbulence  can enhance a firm ' s sustainable competitive advantage and affect the sustainability of product differentiation, although these effects were not evident here.

Overall, the study ' s  evidence clarifies the conditions under which technology readiness translates into effective AI adoption and, eventually, value. In doing so, it addresses the earlier lack of clarity about how technology preparedness and strategic capabilities interplay in delivering tangible benefits. Collectively, the findings encourage hospitality firms  to  invest  not  only  in  cutting-edge  technologies  but  also  in  the managerial acumen, organizational structures, and long-term strategic planning  necessary  to  navigate  the  path  to  AI  maturity  and  achieve sustained competitive advantages.

## 5. Implications of this study

## 5.1. Theoretical implications

This study, grounded in the Resource-Based View (RBV), Dynamic Capabilities  Framework,  and  Contingency  Theory,  offers  significant theoretical contributions to understanding AI adoption and its impact on value  creation  in  the  hospitality  industry.  The  findings  confirm  that Technology Readiness and AI Adoption are pivotal in enhancing Value Creation, aligning with the RBV ' s assertion that well-managed, unique resources lead to superior organizational performance (Guo et al., 2024; Bulchand-Gidumal et al., 2023). However, the study extends this theory by  highlighting  that  the  mere  possession  of  technological  assets  is insufficient. Instead, the strategic integration and effective utilization of these  resources are  crucial for  realizing  their  potential  benefits.  This nuanced understanding adds depth to the RBV by emphasizing that resources must be aligned with organizational strategies to drive value creation,  a  challenge  also  identified  in  earlier  studies  (Parvez,  2020; Warner and W ¨ ager, 2019).

Moreover, the results underscore that organizations are yet to fully realize AI maturity largely because such maturity is contingent upon high levels of technology readiness. As introduced in Section 1, even leading industry players like Marriott and Hilton, despite their early and visible  investments in AI (Al-Hyari et al., 2023; Takyar, 2024), have faced ongoing hurdles in seamlessly integrating these technologies. This finding substantiates the introductory arguments that highlight the gap between current technology readiness and the long-term, stable integration of AI-driven solutions necessary to achieve full AI maturity and sustainable competitive advantage.

Through the lens of the Dynamic Capabilities Framework, the study reveals the complexities organizations face when adapting to technological changes and market opportunities (Arias-P ´ erez and Cepeda-Cardona,  2022).  While  Dynamic  Capabilities  were  found  to moderate  certain  relationships,  the  initial  negative  impact  on  value creation suggests that organizations encounter significant challenges in synchronizing these capabilities with strategic objectives amid technological turbulence. These difficulties echo the Introduction ' s discussion on  how  unpredictable  shifts  in  technology  landscapes  demand  both preparedness and agility (Calder et al., 2024; Wiggers, 2024). Thus, the present research enriches the theoretical conversation by illustrating the dual-edged nature of dynamic capabilities, resonating with the complex scenario initially outlined in the Introduction.

Finally, Contingency Theory helps elucidate the importance of fitting internal resources and capabilities to external environmental conditions. As discussed earlier (Leila, 2024; Solarova et al., 2023), the ability to navigate  ethical  considerations,  data  privacy,  and  customer  expectations hinges on aligning organizational strategies with environmental conditions.  The  study  confirms  that  organizational  performance  is contingent upon  this strategic fit, reinforcing the Introduction ' s emphasis on the interplay between technology readiness, environmental turbulence, and stakeholder engagement.

## 5.2. Managerial insights

From a managerial perspective, the study ' s findings emphasize the importance of not only adopting advanced technologies such as AI but also ensuring that the organization is prepared to integrate and leverage these technologies effectively. As noted in the Introduction, many firms are  at  a  critical  juncture,  attempting  to  move  beyond  basic  implementation  toward  full-scale  AI  maturity  (Parvez,  2020;  Warner  and W ¨ ager, 2019). Managers must recognize that technology readiness -encompassing robust infrastructure, skilled personnel, and supportive organizational culture -is a foundational  prerequisite for achieving this maturity and reaping the associated benefits of enhanced customer service, operational efficiency, and tailored guest experiences (Bulchand-Gidumal et al., 2023; Guo et al., 2024).

Dynamic capabilities emerge as crucial managerial levers. Managers should  invest  in  real-time  data  technologies,  continuous  learning mechanisms,  and  agile  decision-making  processes,  aligning  with  the Introduction ' s observations that market turbulence and rapid technological evolution demand organizational adaptability (Arias-P ´ erez and Cepeda-Cardona, 2022; Calder et al., 2024). By focusing on dynamic capabilities,  leaders  can  navigate  the  transitional ' growing pains ' of implementing AI solutions, ultimately improving value creation as the organization ' s technology readiness matures.

Furthermore, as suggested in the Introduction, the concept of sustainable competitive advantage extends beyond regulatory compliance to involve strategic differentiation and building long-term stakeholder trust  (Leila,  2024; Solarova et al., 2023). Managers should view sustainability as an integral component of their strategic resource configuration. By embedding ethical considerations, data privacy safeguards, and inclusivity measures (Buhalis and Moldavska, 2021a; Vuong and Tran, 2021) into AI-driven initiatives, they not only differentiate their offerings but also cultivate a strong corporate reputation over time.

In operationalizing these strategies, ongoing monitoring and evaluation through key performance indicators (KPIs) and metrics will help managers  gauge  their  progress  toward  AI  maturity.  This  iterative approach, foreshadowed by the complexity and ethical considerations raised in the Introduction, ensures that organizations can adapt their strategies  in  real-time,  maintaining  alignment  with  evolving  market conditions and technological opportunities.

## 5.3. Policy implications

At the policy level, the study ' s findings corroborate the arguments presented  in  the  Introduction  about  the  urgent  need  for  supportive policy frameworks that encourage AI adoption within the hospitality industry  (Captain,  2023;  Siu,  2023).  Policymakers  should  provide incentives -such as tax breaks, grants, or favorable loans -that enhance technology  readiness.  By  doing  so,  they  effectively  bridge  the  gap between the availability of advanced technologies and their strategic, value-generating application.

[S. 12]

The Introduction highlighted that the ethical, data privacy, and inclusivity  challenges  associated  with  AI  deployment  require  careful regulation and capability-building (Leila, 2024; Solarova et al., 2023). Policymakers are thus encouraged to support digital literacy programs, workforce training, and educational curricula that equip employees at all levels with the necessary skills. Such measures ensure that businesses can  evolve  from  basic  AI  implementation  toward  achieving  true  AI maturity, as initially emphasized in the study ' s motivation.

Finally, as technological turbulence intensifies, policies promoting scenario planning, risk management, and resilience strategies become indispensable. The Introduction ' s reference to industry disruptions and the unpredictability of technological change (Calder et al., 2024; Wiggers, 2024) resonates here. By fostering an environment where organizations can flexibly adapt to emerging challenges, policymakers help ensure  that  firms  sustain  their  competitive  advantages  and  drive long-term value creation in an ever-shifting technological landscape.

## 6. Conclusion and future research direction

This study has rigorously examined the complex interrelationships among technology readiness,  AI  adoption,  dynamic  capabilities,  sustainable  competitive  advantage,  and  technological  turbulence  within the Malaysian hospitality industry. Through the theoretical lenses of the Resource-Based  View,  Dynamic  Capabilities  Framework,  and  Contingency Theory, this research has elucidated the pivotal roles these strategic elements play in leveraging technological assets for competitive advantage and enhanced organizational performance.

The  findings  from  this  investigation  affirm  that  both  technology readiness and AI adoption are crucial in bolstering organizational capabilities  and efficiencies. Yet, the efficacy of these technological interventions  transcends  mere  adoption,  hinging  significantly  on  their strategic integration and alignment with broader organizational strategies  and  prevailing  market conditions. This is  evidenced by the substantial roles of dynamic capabilities and the moderating influence of technological  turbulence.  Furthermore,  the  research  underscores  the imperative of sustainable competitive advantage in translating technological and strategic capabilities into palpable value creation, emphasizing the integration of sustainability within organizational strategy.

Looking  to  the  future,  this  research  opens  several  pathways  for further inquiry. Initially, subsequent studies might explore the longitudinal impacts of AI adoption and technology readiness on value creation, particularly examining how these effects evolve as organizations mature in integrating these technologies within their operational frameworks. Additionally, there is a pertinent need to delineate the specific types of AI technologies that yield significant benefits across various domains of hospitality management, ranging from customer service enhancements to operational efficiency improvements.

Another fertile area for future research involves the exploration of cultural  and organizational  variables  that  influence  the  interplay  between dynamic capabilities and technological turbulence. Insights into how  different  organizational  cultures  and  structures  assimilate  and capitalize on technological changes could inform more effective strategies for technological innovation management.

Moreover, given the escalating importance of sustainability in the global business environment, further investigations could focus on how environmental and social governance influences the strategic deployment of technology in fostering competitive and sustainable advantages. Probing into the nexus between sustainability practices, technological innovation,  and  customer  satisfaction  could  unveil  comprehensive strategies  for  value  creation  not  only  in  the  hospitality  industry  but across various sectors.

In conclusion, while this study contributes significant insights into the strategic management of technology in the hospitality industry, the dynamic  and  rapidly  evolving  nature  of  technology  and  market conditions necessitates ongoing scholarly exploration. Future research should endeavor to expand upon the findings of this study, exploring innovative dimensions and theoretical frameworks that can assist organizations in navigating the complexities of integrating technology and strategy to achieve sustainable growth and competitive edge.
