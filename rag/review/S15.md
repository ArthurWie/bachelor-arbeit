## A B S T R A C T

Drawing on the dynamic capability view and resource-based view, this study investigates the relationship among artificial intelligence (AI)-based partner relationship management, firm performance, and sustainable competitiveness concerning the climate management of business-to-business (B2B) firms. Throughout this investigation, the study additionally explored the impact of several other dimensions, including information communication technology capability, firm fit, and technological readiness, as fundamental requirements for the implementation of AI-based partner relationship management. The present study also investigates the relationship between firm partner engagement and firm information processing systems for climate management of B2B firms and their impact  on  achieving  firm  performance  and  sustainable  competitiveness.  A  mixed-methods  approach  was employed  that  involves  in-depth  interviews  with  senior  managers  from  a  diverse  set  of  B2B  firms,  and  the proposed hypothesis model was evaluated by analysing the collected B2B data of 142 responses using partial least squares structural equation modelling. The findings of the study show that information communication technology capability and technological readiness play a significant role in improving the performance of AIbased  partner  relationship  management.  Furthermore,  the  implementation  of  AI-based  partner  relationship management  in  B2B  firms  would  help  partner  engagement  and  information  processing  systems  for  climate management, leading to sustainable firm competitiveness. The outcomes of this study offer many implications for managers and practitioners in the B2B sector and suggest potential avenues for future research.

## 2. Theoretical foundation and hypothesis development

## 2.1. Dynamic capability view (DCV) theory

This research incorporates the dynamic capability view (DCV) theory (Teece, Pisano, & Shuen, 1997) to determine what influence the uptake of an AIPRM solution throughout a sales channel has, as well as the effects of this uptake. According to Teece (2014), a dynamic capability is conceptualised along three dimensions: ' sensing ability, seizing ability, and transforming/reconfiguring ability ' . Therefore, businesses may use AIPRM as an external asset or an opportunity to meet ever-changing market demands by adopting a ' Partner  Relationships  Management ' solution (Chatterjee et al., 2023). However, this goal may not be met without external resources. Companies should also be able to assess the value of new possibilities and resources swiftly, reorganising and coordinating internal and external competencies to meet the challenges of changing business contexts. After absorbing new possibilities, businesses must make the most of them. For businesses to successfully integrate AIPRM throughout their sales channels, they must first cultivate many competencies (i.e., ' sensing, seizing,  and transforming or reconfiguring ' ) in their collaborators (Schreyogg, G. and Kliesch-Eberl, M., 2007). Accordingly,  the  partner  relationship  management  operations  of  a company's partners' end-customers can be aided by AI, and the company,  in  turn,  can  offer  its  partners  real-time,  personalised  services based on the information it gleans from the partner relationship management operations activities using AI (Chatterjee et al., 2023). As a result,  collaborators  will  be  able  to  better  connect  with  their  target audience.  One  way  to  do  this  is  to  partner  with  suppliers  to  better discover,  evolve,  and  co-create  worth  for  end  customers.  This  is  the capacity  for  perception  (Chatterjee  et  al.,  2023).  Collaborators  also require the capacity to gather and use the resources necessary to meet emerging demand in the marketplace quickly and effectively. Finally, businesses should learn to pool their resources in new ways to adapt to shifting markets (Fainshmidt, Pezeshkan, Lance Frazier, Nair, & Markowski, 2016).

## 2.2. Resource-based view (RBV) theory

The resource-based view (RBV) is a management concept for identifying  the  strategic  assets  needed  by  businesses  to  gain  a  market advantage (Barney, 1991). The RBV emphasises a company's assets to identify the skills and knowledge that may boost performance and lead to  competitive  advantage  (Akter,  Wamba,  Gunasekaran,  Dubey, & Childe,  2016).  Combining  many  organisations'  potentially  static  and diverse resources is foundational to the concept of competitive advantage in B2B relationship administration (Barney, 1991). The primary focus of RBV theory is on how well businesses can manage their resources while working together in a B2B setting (Kozlenkova, Samaha, & Palmatier, 2014). Different organisations function differently because they have different strengths and ways of handling resources (Short, 2014). The RBV suggests that businesses should share information and ideas to strengthen their B2B ties (Short, 2014). Employees from diverse companies  with  B2B  ties  benefit  from  an  open  exchange  of  ideas, knowledge, and experience (Crick & Crick, 2020). Improving B2B connections is possible with appropriate AIPRM deployment and utilisation (Libai et al., 2020). As a bonus, this will make it easier to realise the full capability  of  the  technology  deployed  inside  the  business  (Mikalef, Boura, Lekakos, & Krogstie, 2019). Corporate performance and advancement in a B2B scenario, as measured by RBV, is contingent on the company's inventory of internal resources, such as material capital, personnel capital, and organisation capital (Barney, 1991).

## 2.3. Hypothesis formulation and model development

To get an edge in the market, companies must combine their information  and  communication  technology  capabilities  with  other  resources, both  material and immaterial (Akter, Gunasekaran, Wamba, Babu, & Hani, 2020). Building new capabilities requires several steps, including  information  collection,  information  security,  information dissemination, and data-driven decision-making. In addition, companies should reorganise and adapt their resources to maximise potential gains during times of uncertainty (Sunday & Vera, 2018). This is crucial for establishing information and communication technology and paving the way for the growth of AIPRM capability (Chatterjee, Rana, et al., 2021). Furthermore, the incorporation of big data analytics and AI functionality into B2B firms has been made possible by advancements in information and  communication  technology  capabilities  (Chung,  Kingshott,  MacDonald, & Putranta, 2021). In addition, the establishment of AIPRM capabilities necessitates the creation of a proficient and flexible information technology planning procedure through cooperative efforts with industry associates. It is crucial to have sufficient information technology infrastructure to provide seamless operations and services (Rahman et al., 2023). Since AIPRM is conceived of as a dynamic capacity, it has its roots in DCV. This is because it is based on the alteration of routines, such as the development of information and communication technology. To thrive in today's fast-paced business environment, companies must be able to rapidly and efficiently realign their distinctive assets and competencies to seize opportunities and satisfy customer needs. Additionally,  B2B  firms  must  provide  an  environment  that  encourages  their industrial collaborators to experiment with novel uses of information and communication technology (Lu & Ramamurthy, 2011). Hence, we hypothesise:

For companies to successfully adopt new Industry 4.0 technologies, their  technological  readiness  is  crucial  (Telukdarie,  Buhulaiga,  Bag, Gupta, & Luo, 2018). According to Chatterjee (2019), AI is essential for supporting digital partner relationship management activities inside the company. AIPRM is conceptualised as a dynamic capability because it is based on shifting routines, such as demonstrating innovation in technological  readiness  and  working  towards  increasing  information  and communication technology competence, both of which have their origins in DCV. ' Dynamics 365 for customer insights ' is one example of a system with such intelligence. Data sets from customers may be analysed using this technology for prediction purposes. Chatterjee (2019) identify many firms, including ' Zoho, Sugar PRM, and Sales Force ' , that provide AIPRM technologies. To further expand information and communication  technology  capabilities,  technological  readiness  necessitates  the contribution of several essential resources (Gupta & George, 2016). In  addition,  Chatterjee (2019) note that organisations can experience difficulties  with infrastructure when implementing AIPRM technologies, difficulties that may be solved with a ready plan and good training. ## H3 . Firm fit has a positive relationship with implementing AIPRM in B2B firms.

Collaboration among enterprises has the potential to provide much more favourable outcomes in addressing the issue of climate change compared to the unilateral monitoring of emissions only driven by a firm's  self-interest  (Dahlmann & Roehrich,  2019).  In  addition,  businesses may have relational motivations for aggressively pursuing legitimacy and conformity with stakeholder standards (Paulraj et al., 2017). Business  strategies,  activities,  and  business  settings  in  which  supply chain involvement is carried out are all intertwined with a company's motivations  for  implementing  carbon  reduction  initiatives,  whether ethical, interpersonal, or functional (Lintukangas, Arminen, K ¨ ahk ¨ onen, & Karttunen, 2022). Data availability in Industry 4.0 fuels AI-driven applications  such  as  partner  relationship  management  and  enables affordable, segmented service delivery at scale (Chatterjee, Rana, et al., 2021). According to Bag, Gupta, Kumar, and Sivarajah (2021), AI systems driven by large data sets may help businesses better understand their clients, employees, and competitors. As a result, AIPRM systems may aid in vetting high-value clients, forecasting future business, and allocating resources accordingly. Since B2B partners (i.e., supplier and buyer companies) may wish to remain affiliated and in frequent contact to  seek  out  prospective  business  possibilities,  this  will  help  to  boost relationship performance. According to Chatterjee, Rana, et al. (2021), partner relationship management quality and satisfaction greatly affect workers' opinions and propensities to use AIPRM solutions. The RBV holds  that  a  company's  competitive  edge  and  long-term  viability improve  when  it  has  access  to  scarce,  high-value  resources  that  are difficult for rivals to replicate (Short, 2014). Everyone benefits when companies collaborate to improve supply chain capabilities by investing time, money, and expertise (GEP, 2021). Interfirm competitive advantage is based on specialised resources, knowledge-sharing procedures, complementary endowments and competencies, and sound governance practices that firms develop and cultivate via their interactions with one another (Rahman et al., 2023). According to Chung, Kingshott, MacDonald  and  Putranta  (2021),  the  capacity  for  mass  customisation  is positively associated with the analytical intelligence provided by big data,  improving  partner  relationship  management  performance.  This will  eventually  improve  partners'  engagement  with  end  customers. Improving  collaborators'  capacities  for  value  identification,  development, and cocreation with suppliers may lead to better, more individualised services for end consumers (Chatterjee et al., 2021, b). This is the capacity for perception. However, there is a lack of literature on how AIPRM capabilities relate to B2B firms' partner engagement, especially for climate management. Thus, we propose the following hypothesis:

## H4 . AIPRM capabilities positively affect B2B firms' partner engagement in climate management.

Since the transition from competitive to cooperative B2B partnerships is hampered by information asymmetry, businesses require facilitation skills to sustain collaborative B2B connections (Cherbib, Chebbi, Yahiaoui, Thrassou, & Sakka, 2021). These enabling skills, also known as competencies of the company, include information technology-based applications  and  technologies.  In  a  B2B  setting,  workers  have  been observed  to  use  various  methods  for  strengthening  ties  with  their counterparts at other companies. An AIPRM system may help improve these methods (Chatterjee et al., 2020). If these methods are determined to be ineffective, companies may find it challenging to continue working together effectively. As a result, B2B interactions are impeded (Demlehner, Schoemer, & Laumer, 2021). Climate management is possible through  firms'  information  processing  by  monitoring  environmental standards, and it is believed that B2B information processing systems may be enhanced by installing an AIPRM platform for administering B2B relationships (Lacka, Chan, & Wang, 2020). Furthermore, AI-facilitated collaborative administration should incorporate the capacity to merge assets to innovate and adapt to shifting market circumstances, therefore enhancing the efficacy of information sharing (Fainshmidt et al., 2016). From  the  DCV  viewpoint,  AIPRM  implementation  across  the  sales network develops various capabilities (i.e., sensing, seizing, and transforming or reconfiguring) of partners for adopting AIPRM systems based on  effective  information  sharing  (Rahman  et  al.,  2023).  This  is  the capability of converting or resetting based on DCV. Accordingly, we can hypothesise as follows:

## H5 . AIPRM capabilities positively affect B2B firms' information processing systems for climate management.

Researchers examining firm partnerships argue that, as a result of resource and capability constraints, the acquisition of external resources significantly influences firm performance (Chuang, Dahlin, Thomson, Lai, & Yang, 2015). Furthermore, they increasingly assert that exceptional performance is not solely dependent on the success of individual partnerships but rather on the collective impact of the entire portfolio of collaborations (Wassmer, 2008). Furthermore, environmental communication strategies aim to demonstrate to the public and the media that environmental problems are given significant attention by management. By doing so, it fosters strong and cooperative connections between the business and its stakeholders (Nair & Menon, 2008). Therefore, a strong environmentally friendly corporate image serves as a distinct indication of a company's dedication to the environment for its primary A. Samadhiya et al.

This  reputation  can  effectively  enhance  sales among environmentally conscious customers, providing the firm with a competitive edge over its rivals in the market (Martín-de Castro et al., 2015). B2B partnership channels provide access to outside assets that are complementary to an organisation's needs, and the alliances formed between vendors and suppliers to provide these resources directly affect an enterprise's success (Cainelli, De Marchi, & Grandinetti, 2015). Utilising cutting-edge technology to engage with their suppliers and consumers,  businesses  can  take  advantage  of  dynamic  capabilities  by continually developing, modifying, and redesigning internal and external competencies, supporting partnership management and reaching  firm  performance  (Rahman  et  al.,  2023). Based on these considerations, the following hypothesis is proposed:

## H6 . B2B firms' partner engagement in climate management positively affects firm performance.

In this context, ' process ' refers to the unique set of inputs, activities, participants, and outputs that may be measured. In the B2B sphere, data processing systems are a vital resource (Dwivedi, Ismagilova, Rana, & Raman, 2021). In addition to facilitating the identification of particular areas that obstruct information flow among the organisations engaged in B2B relationship management, an information processing system gives a calibrated  measure  of  variability  for  the  output  of  a  given  process (Farrokhi, Shirazi, Hajli, & Tajvdi, 2020). Information processing systems are capable of generating valuable data about climate management. These data encompass various aspects, such as partner environmental performance, emissions data, energy usage, and compliance with sustainability standards. DCV aligns with this model, indicating that the corporate setting is dynamic. To adapt to changing market conditions, businesses must invest and allocate assets (Makadok, 2001). While information technology capabilities may help businesses execute competitive objectives while considering shifting market circumstances, these capabilities are also dynamic, as they can combine and transform the firm's available resources in a novel way to achieve firm performance (Eriksson, 2014). Organisations with well-functioning information technology are expected to gain positive B2B relationship management results after installing an AIPRM solution (Dwivedi et al., 2021). Consistent with RBV's central tenet, which states that improved performance results from enterprises pooling resources and information to  create  a  more  cohesive supply chain, this strategy emphasises the need  for  both  parties  to  invest  in  the  development  of  its  members (Rahman et al., 2023). The above discussion leads to the formulation of the following hypothesis:

H7 . B2B firms'  information  processing  systems  for  climate  management positively affect firm performance.

Organisational performance is evaluated in terms of the net results the organisation produces. Financial and promotional factors, including business share, return on investment, growth in sales, and net revenue, are all taken into account (Kreye & Perunovic, 2020). In addition, the degree of satisfaction is used as a reliable indicator of the efficiency of an organisation  (Keramati,  Mehrabi, & Mojir,  2010).  The  subjective assessment of an organisation's ability to successfully manage its B2B relationships is correlated with the performance of organisations functioning in a B2B setting (Sin, Tse, & Yim, 2005). Organisational success is  largely  determined  by  the  means  through  which  it  accumulates

' valuable, inimitable, rare, and nonsubstitutable ' resources; as stated, RBV theory is a useful tool for determining these means (Chatterjee, Rana, et al., 2021). It is believed that these capabilities affect an organisation's ability to maintain its sustainable competitive advantage. By accomplishing these goals, organisations are seen as gaining a competitive edge over other existing contemporaneous organisations that are already operating in the market (Rahman et al., 2023). The extent to which the performance of an organisation operating in a B2B setting is capable of achieving higher advantages in terms of social well-being in comparison to the performance of other organisations operating under circumstances that are comparable to those conditions is the definition of sustainable competitive advantage (Chatterjee, Rana, et al., 2021). ## 3. Research methodology

## 3.1. Context, hypothesis validation, and questionnaire design

The recommended three-stage study design was employed in this research (Schilke, 2014). Fig. 2 depicts the research process followed for this study.

The research consisted of in-depth interviews with those who are working in B2B firms. During the first section of the interview, questions were asked regarding various topics related to their corporate climate action  capabilities,  from  the  perspective  of  firm  fit,  information  and communication technology capabilities, and firms' technological readiness for AIPRM. We also asked them to explain the role of AIPRM in firm partner engagement and firm information processing for climate management and its cascading effect on firms' performance and longevity based on taking actions oriented towards climate change. The second component  of  the  study  follows  naturally,  critically  examining  the study's hypotheses through interviews with business leaders about the importance  of  various  firm  capabilities  for  adopting  and  effectively implementing AIPRM, which ultimately results in sustainable competitive  advantage.  There  was  widespread  agreement  that  organisations' capabilities can facilitate effective AIPRM implementation and progress towards firm performance and sustainable competitiveness. We have confidence  in  our  hypotheses  since  the  academic  literature  supports them before moving on to the third and last section of the pilot phase interview. In this section, the comments on the clarity of the questions and their challenges in responding were collected, having filled out a draft version of the questionnaire to be used in future survey research. Several questions were either reworded or removed as a direct consequence of this procedure. Ultimately, we acquired data related to the constructs  of  our  research  from  the  relevant  organisations  and  performed analysis on that data.

Given these considerations, we are certain that these managers will offer an accurate and relevant assessment of the aims of the current study. We finally received a total of 142 responses from B2B firms for further data analysis.

A lack of discriminant validity is not consistently detected by these methods (Fornell and Larcker criterion), as shown in a simulation study by  Henseler,  Ringle,  and  Sarstedt  (2015).  To  evaluate  discriminant validity,  Henseler  et  al. In  addition,  they  use  a  Monte  Carlo simulation  analysis  to  show  that  the  novel  method  outperforms  the Fornell-Larcker criterion and the evaluation of (partial) cross-loadings. Therefore, we  additionally evaluated discriminant validity using HTMT standards (as shown in Table 5). The consistency of our model is confirmed by both the Cronbach's alpha and composite reliability scores obtained.  Construct  validity was  established  after verification of adequate  levels  of ' content,  convergent,  discriminant ' validity  and reliability (O'Leary-Kelly, Vokurka, & R., 1998). These analyses informed the conclusion that the constructs were adequately measured, allowing us to move on to hypothesis testing.

## 4.3. Structural model assessment

We evaluated the structural model by assessing the squared multiple correlations ( R 2 ) of the endogenous variables, Cohen's f square of the exogenous  variables,  the  impact  of  path  coefficients,  the  predictive relevance (Stone-Geisser's Q 2 value) of the exogenous variable, and the evaluation  of  unobserved  variation  (Schoenherr,  2016).  All  of  these factors were taken into consideration. Table 4 shows the R 2 values of each variable explained by the model. The model explains 70.2% of the variance in AIPRM, 42.4% of the variance in B2B firms' information processing for climate management (FIP), 61.1% of the variance in B2B firms' partner engagement for climate management (FPE), 61% of the variance in B2B firm performance (FP), and 60.3% of the variance in B2B firm sustainable competitiveness (SC).

In addition, we calculated the impact of the exogenous variable using Cohen's f 2 . This measure is used to assess the relative impact of a predictor construct on an endogenous construct in terms of its explanatory power.  This  can  be  done  by  comparing  the  two  constructs.  For  this research, the range of the effect size is from 0.089 to 1.570, covering a wide spectrum of small to large effect sizes of various exogenous variables (Hair, Hult, Ringle, & Sarstedt, 2016). The Q 2 value proposed by Stone-Geisser was also evaluated. Predictive relevance, or Q 2 , is used to evaluate a model's usefulness for making predictions. In calculating Q 2 , we  validate  the  endogenous  components'  predictive  ability.  If Q 2 is greater than zero, this means that our reconstructed values are accurate and that the model is useful for making predictions (Geisser, 1974). The Q 2 values for this investigation are greater than 0 (as shown in Table 4), indicating that the model has a high predictive value.

The 'fi nite mixture PLS module ' was used to evaluate unobserved heterogeneity.  The  results  for  the  entropy  statistic  were  consistently substantially above 0.7 for a variety of different numbers of segments. Following the findings for the Bayesian and Akaike information criteria, the  two-segment solution  was  shown  to  have  the  maximum  positive support. Based on these findings, it seems that unobserved heterogeneity does not play a significant role here. In PLS-SEM, an overall measure of how well a model fits the data has been established and is called the goodness of fit. When researchers are examining the PLS-SEM findings of various data groups using the same PLS path model, Henseler and Sarstedt (2013) demonstrate that the goodness of fit may be beneficial for a PLS multigroup analysis. The standardised root mean square residual (SRMR) is a goodness of fit metric for PLS-SEM that was introduced by Henseler et al. (2014). This measure may be used to avoid model misspecification.  Hu  and  Bentler  (1999)  indicate  that  an  optimal  SRMR value is less than 0.08 or 0.10. The SRMR value for the present research is 0.039, which is less than 0.08, indicating a good model fit.

## 7. Conclusion

This study investigates the external factors that affect the effective adoption of AIPRM skills and how such capabilities influence firm performance and businesses' ability to maintain a competitive advantage over the long term when the firm's intended activities are climate-based. This  research  has  shown  that  dynamic  capabilities  are  crucial  to enabling businesses to embrace and effectively utilise AIPRM capabilities in the context of climate measures for competitive advantage, and this has been demonstrated through an examination of information and communication technology, firm technological readiness, firm fit, firm partner  engagement  for  climate  management,  and  firm  information processing  for  climate  management.  The  research  has  also  identified AIPRM as a critical resource that can help cultivate the nimble expertise needed to deliver firm partner engagement and firm information processing effectively for managing climate in B2B firms. The study has also investigated  the  interrelationships  between  these  factors,  which  are classified according to the RBV and DCV, as well as how they might improve firm performance and lead to enduring competitive advantage. This research has made important contributions to understanding the function of AIPRM in B2B firms by presenting a statistically oriented discussion, consequences, and future proposals based on the findings.

The outcomes of this research highlight the relevance of AIPRM as a strategic  tool  that  may  assist  B2B  firms  in  reacting  to  a  commercial environment that is becoming increasingly dynamic and competitive. The necessity of companies adopting a dynamic capabilities viewpoint, which allows them to adapt and react to changing market conditions successfully,  is  underlined  here.  This  helps  companies  adapt  and respond  to  changing  market  situations  effectively.  The  research  also highlights the potential for AIPRM to ease firms' adoption of environmentally friendly practices and bestow a competitive advantage in the marketplace. This study offers significant insights that may aid businesses in utilising the full potential of AIPRM to encourage development and  prosperity  despite  the  obstacles  introduced  by  an  ever-evolving business  environment. These insights can be used to guide organisations in exploiting AIPRM.

## 7.1. Limitations and future research directions

In  terms  of  potential  limitations,  we  may  acknowledge  that  the current research focuses only on the external elements that influence adoption and implementation; no consideration is given here to the internal  aspects  that  may  affect  a  company's  capacity  to  build  AIPRM capabilities. Additionally, this research does not consider possible barriers  to  adopting  and  implementing  AIPRM  capabilities,  such  as employee aversion to change, organisational culture, or costs associated with  these  factors.  Because  of  the  elapsed  time  between  the  period covered in this study and the present day, The potential advantages of deploying  AIPRM  capabilities  are  given  precedence  in  this  research above any possible restrictions or constraints that may occur in practice.
