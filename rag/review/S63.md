## 1. Introduction

Climate change is reshaping the global economic landscape at an unprecedented pace, pushing industrial enterprises-the primary sources of  carbon  emissions-to  the  forefront  of  sustainable  transformation (Voosen, 2025). Global policy trends are rapidly shifting environmental performance  from  voluntary  corporate  social  responsibility  (CSR)  to mandatory  compliance  requirements,  such  as  China ' s ' dual  carbon ' targets  and  the  European  Union ' s  Corporate Sustainability  Reporting Directive  (CSRD)  (Hern ´ andez  et  al.,  2024;  Bohnsack  et  al.,  2022). However, traditional emission reduction methods such as energy substitution and process optimization have encountered significant technical bottlenecks. The rate of energy efficiency improvement lags far behind the level required to achieve carbon neutrality goals (Chu et al., 2024). In the context of digital transformation, the driving mechanism of Artificial Intelligence capabilities (AIC) on corporate sustainability performance (CSP) and its organizational boundary requirements have emerged as a key topic in advancing digital sustainability, both in theory and practice. This study is based on dynamic capabilities theory (DCT) and sustainable development theory, and reveals the micro-mechanisms and boundary conditions of AIC driving CSP. Unlike AI technologies, AIC represents a coherent set of routines integrating tangible resources, such as technology and data, and intangible  resources,  such  as  coordination  and  organizational  capabilities, that enable an enterprise to perform specific value-creating activities in a  repeatable  and  reliable  manner  (Kumar  et  al.,  2025b;  Mikalef  and Gupta, 2021).

Building on this, and drawing primarily from Sj ¨ odin et al. ' s (2021, 2023) framework,  AIC  can  be  deconstructed  into  three  hierarchical sub-capabilities that represent progressive levels of AI sophistication: perceptive, predictive, and prescriptive capabilities. In general terms, perceptive  capabilities  focus  on  sensing  and  interpreting  data  in real-time, utilizing algorithms to monitor environments, processes, and systems for immediate insights without forecasting or decision-making elements. This foundational layer enables organizations to gather and analyze current states accurately and efficiently. Predictive capabilities build upon perceptive ones by employing machine learning and statistical models to anticipate future trends, risks, or opportunities based on historical and real-time data patterns. This dimension shifts from mere observation  to  foresight,  allowing  firms  to  prepare  for  potential  scenarios.  Finally,  prescriptive  capabilities  extend  further  by  not  only predicting outcomes but also recommending optimal actions or strategies through advanced optimization techniques and simulation, providing decision-makers with actionable guidance to achieve desired results. These sub-capabilities, as conceptualized by Sj ¨ odin et al., form a coevolutionary  framework  where  they  interact  and  scale  together  to drive innovation and value creation in various business contexts, such as product-service  systems  and  circular  business  models  (Sj ¨ odin  et  al., 2021, 2023). This general framework lays a foundational structure for examining AIC ' s relationship with CSP, allowing us to explore how these dimensions contribute to sustainability outcomes. Furthermore, studies have introduced AIC as enabling firms to continuously sense opportunities  across  markets  and  technologies,  thereby  driving  performance (Sullivan and Wamba, 2024). However, the role of AIC in enhancing CSP has not been analyzed with empirical evidence. The dynamic capabilities theory (DCT) has been widely used to explain the relationship between digital technology capabilities and corporate sustainable innovation (Heredia et al., 2022), yet it has not been applied to elucidate the AIC-CSP linkage. To fill this gap, this study conceptualizes AIC as dynamic capabilities to investigate how they enhance CSP through rapid information acquisition and opportunity sensing.

Even though AIC can be used to sense opportunities for enhancing CSP, the pathway may not be straightforward and likely depends on its sub-capabilities, an aspect unaddressed in prior research. According to DCT ' s  process  view  of ' sensing-seizing-reconfiguring, ' opportunities sensed by AIC must be effectively seized and reconfigured to realize CSP gains (Teece et al., 1997). In this vein, dynamic capabilities like AIC not only influence CSP at a higher level but also exhibit differential effects through their sub-capabilities (Shang et al., 2020). Specifically, when linked to sustainability performance, perceptive capabilities can help businesses improve operational efficiency by enabling real-time monitoring of resource use and environmental impacts, as seen in cases like Sany Heavy Industry, which achieved an 8 % efficiency increase and 10 % fuel reduction through such applications (Wenhui et al., 2024). Predictive capabilities, by forecasting trends and mitigating risks, support proactive sustainability strategies, for instance, allowing firms like CATL to reduce production time by 86.5 % and energy consumption by 79.6 % (Wang et al., 2022). Prescriptive capabilities further enhance CSP by offering optimized recommendations for process adjustments that balance economic, environmental, and social goals, exemplified by Haier ' s process optimizations that boosted overall resource efficiency (Steiber and Alvarez, 2024). Collectively, these sub-capabilities underscore AIC ' s potential  to  elevate  CSP  in  manufacturing  firms.  However,  existing studies have not empirically examined the layered effects of AIC and its perceptive, predictive, and prescriptive dimensions on CSP.

Additionally, this study also examines potential boundary conditions that could either enhance or diminish the impact of AIC on CSP. The manufacturing industry plays a crucial role in the adjustment of China ' s economic  structure  (Pan  and  Zhao,  2019;  Shen  et  al.,  2020),  which serves as the empirical context for our research. Traditional manufacturing  firms  encounter  even  greater difficulties  and  uncertainties as a result of the industrial sustainable transformation process (Liu et al., 2021, 2023; Yu and Chen, 2025). For manufacturing firms  to  achieve  sustainable  development,  it  is  imperative  that  they reinforce  their  AIC,  foster  innovation,  and  improve  their  intrinsic development. Xia and Lou (2018) proposed that traditional manufacturing firms rely on linear value chains, highly standardized processes, and locked-in strategic paths, while Industry 4.0 manufacturing firms are characterized by open value networks, agile decision-making structures, and dynamic capabilities investments. AIC may pose a disruptive threat to traditional manufacturing firms, but it may be a catalyst for innovation for Industry 4.0 manufacturing firms. Unfortunately, the industry kinds of organizations-such as traditional manufacturing firms and Industry 4.0 manufacturing firms-have rarely been regarded as significant variables in the literature that currently exists.  Therefore,  it  is  necessary  to  directly  explore  the  influence  of manufacturing firm type on relevant variables, to enhance our differentiated understanding of the influence mechanism of AIC on CSP. In this study, we will further explore whether there is heterogeneity in the effect of AIC on CSP among different types of enterprises (traditional manufacturing firms and Industry 4.0 manufacturing firms).

There  thus  exists  a  gap  in  the  research  concerning:  (1)  The  first empirical  test  of  the  overall  AIC  and  its  perceptive,  predictive,  and prescriptive capabilities on the stratified influence mechanism of CSP; (2) Revealing the boundary role of enterprise types in the path of AIC influencing  CSP.  To  reach  the  research  goal,  the  following  research questions:

Research Question 1 . How does AIC affect CSP? What is the specific impact of the three sub-capabilities of AIC on CSP?

Research  Question  2 .  Is  there  significant  heterogeneity  in  the impact of AIC on CSP across different types of industrial firms?

Based on dynamic capabilities theory and sustainable development theory, this study constructs the ' AIC → CSP ' analytical framework, and introduces the type of enterprise as a boundary condition. To address the above research question, this study uses data from a questionnaire survey of 292 individuals from 120 Chinese manufacturing firms to develop a partial least squares structural equation model (PLS-SEM).

The rest of the sections of this study are organized as follows. In Section  2,  a  theoretical  review  and  hypothesis  formulation  are  conducted. In Section 3, the research design, methodology, and process are highlighted. In Section 4, the results of the empirical analysis are presented. In Section 5, the findings are discussed in detail, focusing on analyzing the theoretical and practical results of the study. Section 6 summarizes  the  research  contributions,  practical  implications,  and limitations.

## 2. Theory and hypothesis development

## 2.1. Dynamic capabilities theory

Dynamic capabilities theory (DCT) emerged as a lens to explain how firms achieve competitive advantage under conditions of uncertainty, by enabling them to integrate, build, and reconfigure internal and external competences to address rapidly changing environments (Teece et al., 1997; Eisenhardt and Martin, 2000). Teece et al. (1997) proposed DCT for fast aligning with the changing external business environment, and dynamic capabilities can be split into three categories: (1) Sensing opportunities,  (2)  seizing  chances,  and  (3)  reconfiguring  possibilities. Specifically, sensing opportunities involves scanning activities; seizing opportunities involves agile investment in commercialization activities;

reconfiguring  opportunities  involves  breaking  the  unfavorable  path dependencies that hinder sustainable development (Bashir et al., 2022). For instance, Huawei corporation has identified a new opportunity in low-light photography for mobile phones. To effectively take advantage of this opportunity, Huawei reallocates both internal and external resources. As a result, Huawei launches the P series, which represents a strategic  response  to  this  opportunity.  This  illustrates  the  process perspective of DCT, demonstrating how firms leverage dynamic capabilities to sustain competitive advantage amid uncertainty.

Research on digital capabilities and innovation has recently made extensive use of DCT (Sullivan and Wamba, 2024; Heredia et al., 2022). In  particular,  Sullivan  and  Wamba  (2024) investigated  how  three AI-powered skills may respond to market shifts and grasp opportunities, leading  to  innovation  and  improved  business  performance.  Heredia et al. (2022) proposed a model to explain the effect of digital capabilities on firm performance through the PLS-SEM method in the ' new normal ' context  from  a  firm-level  perspective.  Previous  studies  have  demonstrated  that  DCT  provides  a  coherent  framework  for  analyzing  the relationship between AIC and CSP. Consequently, we explain how AIC influences CSP by using the process perspectives of DCT, reinforcing that this  study ' s  contribution  is  theory-driven  by  extending  DCT  from traditional  competitive  advantage  to  digital  sustainability  under  uncertain conditions.

According to the DCT process view, AIC can be regarded as a highlevel  capability  of  an  enterprise,  as  it  facilitates  the  creation  of  new knowledge and information while also acquiring existing information through various channels (Krakowski et al., 2023). Regarding this, we are  reiterating  the  findings  of  Hercheui  and  Ranjith  (2020),  who discovered that the spread of AIC in manufacturing has an impact on organizations ' dynamic skills in terms of the firm ' s ability. Specifically, the sensing capability within dynamic capabilities enables companies to accurately discern market demand, technological trends, and potential risks, thereby identifying opportunities in advance. The seizing capability in dynamic capabilities continuously tracks changes in the environment  and  the  operational  status  of  the  enterprise,  and  promptly detects deviations. The reconfiguring capability in dynamic capabilities can  flexibly  adjust  resource  allocation  and  organizational  structure based on feedback from the first two. The three elements work together to help companies respond quickly to the market, optimize operations, strengthen core competitiveness, and ultimately achieve steady growth in sustainable performance. Similarly, the impact of AIC and its three sub-capabilities on CSP also follows the DCT process. By identifying and predicting wasteful processes and procedures in manufacturing, CSP can be seen as an opportunity to reconfigure and take on environmental and social  responsibilities  (Wang  et  al.,  2025;  Madanaguli  et  al.,  2024). Based on the above analysis, it is reasonable for this study to use DCT to explain the research model, as it provides a robust theoretical justification  for  linking  AIC  to  CSP  in  uncertain  digital  transformation contexts.

## 2.2. Corporate sustainability performance

Corporate sustainability performance (CSP) is conceptually anchored in the Triple Bottom Line framework, which posits that sustainable  business  outcomes  must  concurrently  advance  economic viability, environmental stewardship, and social responsibility (Elkington, 1997; Schaltegger and Wagner, 2011). In this study, CSP is operationally defined as a multidimensional construct comprising three interdependent pillars: first, economic sustainability reflects enhanced operational efficiency through resource optimization and cost reduction (Kumar et al., 2025b), alongside export market development that fosters long-term  profitability  (Kumar  et  al.,  2025b);  second,  environmental sustainability  encompasses tangible  outcomes such as pollution mitigation,  waste  reduction,  and  the  facilitation  of  green  innovations enabled by technological advancements (George et al., 2020; Hussain et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024); third, social sustainability manifests in strengthened  stakeholder  trust,  supply  chain  reliability  (Olan  et  al., 2024), thereby addressing societal expectations beyond profit maximization. This conceptualization aligns with contemporary sustainability metrics,  including  the  EU  Taxonomy  for  Sustainable  Activities  and empirical validations in managerial decision-making contexts (Khaled et al., 2021).

The theoretical linkage between CSP and Artificial Intelligence Capabilities (AIC) is fundamentally rooted in Dynamic Capabilities Theory (DCT),  which  posits  that  firms  leverage  high-order  capabilities  to reconfigure  resources  and  adapt  to  turbulent  environments  (Teece, 2007; Mikalef and Gupta, 2021). Here, AIC functions as a meta-capability that integrates tangible resources (e.g., AI infrastructure, data ecosystems) with intangible assets (e.g., organizational routines, human-machine collaboration skills) to transform sustainability challenges into opportunities (Mikalef and Gupta, 2021). Empirical evidence suggests that distinct sub-capabilities within AIC drive specific CSP dimensions:  perceptive  capabilities  enhance  decision-making  rationality, optimizing resource allocation across economic and environmental  spheres  (George  et  al.,  2020);  predictive  capabilities  enable proactive  pollution  reduction  and  risk  mitigation,  directly  elevating environmental performance (Wang et al., 2024; Hussain et al., 2024); while prescriptive capabilities -though ineffective in this study ' s context -hypothetically could advance social sustainability through ethical AI governance (Mancuso et al., 2025). Notably, the AIC-CSP relationship exhibits  contingency  effects,  as  Industry  4.0  manufacturing  firms harness AIC ' s adaptive potential more effectively for social and environmental innovation compared to traditional counterparts (Schwaeke et al., 2025), underscoring the need for boundary conditions in theoretical models.

Critically, this study acknowledges the paradoxical tensions identified in recent literature, where AI-driven sustainability initiatives may inadvertently  generate  value  destruction  alongside  value  creation (Mancuso et al., 2025). For instance, energy-intensive AI computations could negate environmental gains, while opaque algorithmic decision-making might erode stakeholder trust. Such tensions contextualize the non-linear relationship between AIC and CSP, particularly regarding  prescriptive  capabilities ' limited  efficacy  in  this  empirical setting. By grounding the AIC-CSP nexus in DCT and Triple Bottom Line Theory -while  integrating  empirical  validation  from  manufacturing contexts (Kumar et al., 2025b) and critical perspectives on AI ' s  dual impact (Mancuso et al., 2025) -this section establishes a robust theoretical foundation for hypotheses development. The clear operationalization  of  CSP  and  its  mechanistic  linkages  to  AIC ' s  hierarchical capabilities  enhance  internal  consistency,  ensuring  that  subsequent analyses  align  with  established  sustainability  literature  and  dynamic capability paradigms.

## 2.3. An overview of AIC and CSP

The theory of corporate sustainability emphasizes balancing social and environmental responsibilities while creating economic value, with the goal of long-term survival. It integrates economic, social, and environmental bottom lines, requiring companies to balance short-term interests with long-term development and achieve a win-win situation for all  parties  through innovation and responsible management (Hussain et al., 2018). CSP takes a broader view of economic, social, and environmental value and shifts the focus from customers and shareholders to multiple  stakeholders  (Shang  et  al.,  2020;  Schwaeke  et  al.,  2025). Although scholars have a wide range of interests, the field of CSP remains in its infancy. Additionally, some scholars explore the drivers of CSP.  For  instance,  scholars  theoretically  argue  that  creating  shared value (Jadoon et al., 2021), dynamic capabilities (Alkaraan et al., 2024), stakeholder management (Agrawal et al., 2025), blockchain (Xu et al., 2025), and digitalization (Zhang et al., 2023) are significant drivers of CSP. Recently, some scholars regard AI technology as the driver of CSP (Zhong and Song, 2025; Wang et al., 2024). However, these studies are C. Renfei et al.

Furthermore, scholars taking the  capability  perspective  hold  that  AIC,  rather  than  AI  technology, performs  well  in  guaranteeing  sustainable  competitive  advantages (Mikalef and Gupta, 2021), which should also be true for CSP.

As  for  AIC,  it  is  defined  as  the  organization ' s  ability  to  utilize cognitive computing technology to enhance strategic decision-making in various  functional  areas  (Sullivan  and  Wamba,  2024).  From  the perspective of dynamic capabilities, AIC can be divided into perceptive, predictive, and prescriptive capabilities (Sj ¨ odin et al., 2023). From the perspective of data application, AIC can be divided into data management, algorithm development, and the democratization of AI applications (Sj ¨ odin et al., 2021). As the  core  digital  capabilities,  AIC  enables  enterprises  to  promote  sustainable development goals (SDGs) through the effective deployment of digital resources and solutions. The interest in AIC can be attributed to its  potential  to  revolutionize  various  aspects  of  organizational  management, leading to sustainable development, but existing research is mainly  qualitative  (Di  Vaio  et  al.,  2020;  Sj ¨ odin  et  al.,  2023).  Some quantitative studies on AIC investigate its impact on economic performance. For example, based on the DCT, Sullivan and Wamba (2024) find that  AIC  can  promote  firm  performance  and  innovation  through  the response to market changes. Based on the resource-based theory of the firm, Mikalef and Gupta (2021) develop and validate a survey instrument  to  measure  an  organization ' s  AI  capability,  demonstrating  its positive  impact  on  organizational  creativity  and  performance.  This provides theoretical and practical implications for understanding and developing AI capabilities in organizations, highlighting the importance of complementary resources beyond just technology. However, there is no empirical research that explores the impact of AIC on CSP, as well as its inner mechanisms and boundary conditions. In summary, this study explores the relationship between AIC and CSP from the perspective of capability, which enriches the driving factors of CSP. Compared with previous studies, this study broadens the research perspective on the relationship  between  AI  and  CSP  from  technology  to  capability,  and incorporates the context of sustainability.

## 2.4. The relationship between AIC and CSP

According to DCT, we contend  that  AIC  can  sense  market  opportunities,  internal  resource optimization opportunities, and revenue growth opportunities by integrating inter-firm, internal, and external information, which is conducive  to  CSP  (Nishant  and  Kennedy,  2020;  Sj ¨ odin  et  al.,  2023). Kumar et al. (2025b) examined how AI capabilities significantly influence export market development and exploration, enabling companies to improve their economic performance. Olan et al. (2024) pointed out the supporting role of explainable AIC in enterprise supply chain decision support, and that data mining and machine learning methods can help improve the automated decision-making process in supply chains. (2023), perceptive capabilities enable manufacturers to unlock and create new corporate value by sensing product operational profiles through sensors, developing insights into customer intelligence. Predictive capabilities allow companies to employ AI to convert from trends in prior data to predict future  events  and  behaviors,  increasing  enterprise  value  through improved  resource  utilization.  Prescriptive  capabilities  mainly  assist manufacturers in using AI to gain prescriptive insights for optimizing operations at the system level, identifying the ideal actions to maximize sustainable  value.  AIC  (Sj ¨ odin  et  al.,  2023;  Olan  et  al.,  2024)  has garnered  a  lot  of  interest  from  academics  and  consistently  enables businesses to enhance sustainable performance through resource allocation optimization, innovation, operational efficiency, and decision-making science. Three sub-capabilities within AIC can enhance CSP: slowing, narrowing, and guiding. The specific details are as follows:

The perceptive capabilities of AIC refer to AI using algorithms to monitor product performance in real time and analyze massive amounts of data, enabling companies to instantly identify and resolve issues and improve  operational  efficiency.  They  can  help  manufacturing  firms effectively extend the resource use cycle by prolonging the service life of products and their components, thereby slowing down the consumption of industrial raw materials (Sj ¨ odin et al., 2023). For instance, AI can use information from a wide range of connected sensors inside or close to the machines to sense their surroundings (Ferreira and Goncalves, 2022). The potential to delve deeper into data is presented by AI perception, which eventually yields insights into production intelligence. In addition,  AI  enables  firms  to  evaluate  vast  amounts  of  data  and  uncover hidden patterns that would be challenging to uncover through conventional research, increasing operational transparency (Xia et al., 2023). Specifically,  manufacturing  firms  can  use  perceptive  capabilities  to monitor product usage and performance in real time, and analyze historical  data  and  behavioral  patterns.  Implement  preventive  maintenance  measures  before  potential  problems  arise,  thereby  effectively extending the service life of products and minimizing resource waste. Manufacturing firms can rely on slowing mechanisms built on perceptive capabilities to capture anomalies in resource flows within production systems. They can transform wasteful practices such as material redundancy  and  energy  spillover  into  economic  and  environmental performance.

The  predictive  capabilities  refer  to  AI  using  historical  data  and pattern recognition technology to predict future trends and outcomes. Through automated recommendations and decision support, enterprises can identify and avoid potential risks in advance and optimize resource allocation. They enable manufacturing firms to use AI to proactively analyze multidimensional data collected, predict anomalies and normal variations, and achieve resource savings and efficiency improvements. Predictive methods analyze trends in historical data and convert the learning outcomes of AI into predictions of future events and behaviors (Rousopoulou et al., 2022). This enables AI to infer the probability and trends of future events and provide actionable insights for operational planning, such as determining when to perform maintenance shutdowns and  developing  route  planning  (Cao  et  al.,  2022).  Early  warning  of breakdown and resource waste risks is another important advantage of predictive capabilities, enabling businesses to take preventative measures  to  achieve  productivity  and  sustainability  performance  (Sj ¨ odin et al., 2023). Therefore, the application of predictive capabilities allows for greater effectiveness through lowering time and material resource waste, boosting productivity, and saving costs. Manufacturing firms can rely on narrowing mechanisms based on predictive capabilities to help them  effectively  improve  the  efficiency  of  material  and  energy  use. Production parameters are automatically adjusted and more energy-efficient operating strategies are proposed, thereby improving sustainability performance.

The prescriptive capabilities refer to AI providing actionable guidance to businesses based on predictive results, enabling them to remain flexible and efficient amid market changes by formulating strategies and adjusting business processes. They help manufacturers build solutions that  maximize  sustainable  value  through  millions  of  simulations  to identify  ideal  actions,  thereby  further  optimizing  product  operations and usage (Sj ¨ odin et al.,  2023). Specifically, companies can use prescriptive  capabilities  to  track  the  entire  process  of  product  use  and retirement in real time, accurately analyze customer return patterns, and  simplify  resource  recovery  processes  to  reduce  resource  waste (Venkatesh et al., 2024). In addition, manufacturers can use prescriptive capabilities  to  optimize  industrial  product  design  (e.g.,  increasing product disassembly) and promote material reuse (Jackson et al., 2024). Ultimately, a set of prescriptive and systematic implementation standards and programs will be formed, thereby fundamentally improving industrial  production  processes.  Manufacturing  firms  can  utilize  prescriptive  capabilities  to  guide  the  implementation  of  optimal  system optimization and operation plans, leveraging massive amounts of data and advanced computing capabilities. Based on the analysis above, this study proposes the following hypotheses:

Hypothesis 1 . AIC has a positive effect on CSP.

Hypothesis 1a . Perceptive capabilities have a positive effect on CSP.

Hypothesis 1b . Predictive capabilities have a positive effect on CSP.

Hypothesis 1c . Prescriptive capabilities have a positive effect on CSP.

## 2.5. The role of type of manufacturing enterprise

While many studies have examined the effects of firm ownership (Liu et al., 2025) and size (Liu and Wang, 2025), few have investigated the impact of firm industry type. According to this study, it is imperative to have discussions about the several kinds of manufacturing companies that are currently in operation, including both traditional and Industry 4.0 manufacturing firms. The relationship between AIC and CSP may differ between the two types of firms. Industry 4.0 manufacturing firms, aligned  with  the  logics  of  the  fourth  industrial  revolution,  integrate cyber-physical systems, Internet of Things (IoT), artificial intelligence (AI), big data analytics, and cloud computing to enable smart, interconnected  production  ecosystems  (Frank  et  al.,  2019;  Ghobakhloo, 2020). Traditional manufacturing, as the ' foundation industry ' of the national economy, is a core component of the industrial system, covering basic industries such as textiles, machinery, chemicals, metallurgy, and building materials (Zhang et al., 2023). Despite the wave of digitalization and green transformation, traditional manufacturing firms continue to  support  the  stable  development  of  China ' s  economy  with  their enormous scale, complete industrial chains, and extensive employment capacity. Typical production characteristics of traditional manufacturing firms include standardized production processes coexisting  with  labor-intensive  operations,  high  energy  consumption  and high emissions, long supply chains that rely on physical infrastructure, and  slow  technological  renewal  and  iteration  (Liu  et  al.,  2023).  For example, industrial energy consumption accounts for more than 65 % of total energy consumption in China, with traditional manufacturing accounting for a large proportion of this. Most traditional manufacturing enterprises are still in the Industrial 2.0 to 3.0 stages, with technological upgrades limited by a shortage of funds and talent. Through technologies  such  as  5G,  industrial  Internet,  and  big  data,  some  traditional manufacturing firms are moving toward full-chain intelligence, gradually  transforming  into  Industry  4.0  manufacturing  firms  (Tian  et  al., 2023a). ## 3. Study design and model evaluation

## 3.1. Study design

This study aims to test the proposed theoretical model and hypotheses, particularly the hierarchical empowerment mechanism of AIC and its  boundary  conditions  affected  by  enterprise  type.  To  achieve  this research  objective,  we  choose  to  use  partial  least  squares  structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM) as the core data analysis method. This method  is  selected  based  on  the  following  key  considerations  and applicability: (1) This research model is exploratory in nature, aiming to reveal  the  complex  path  relationship  between  AIC  and  its  subcapabilities and CSP, and to examine the role of enterprise type. PLSSEM is particularly suitable for such exploratory research,  especially when theoretical models are still under development or the structural relationships between variables are not yet fully understood (Sarstedt et al., 2019; Hair et al., 2021). Compared with covariance-based structural  equation  modeling  (CB-SEM),  PLS-SEM  has  more  relaxed  assumptions  regarding  data  distribution  and  is  more  adaptable  to  the non-normal data distribution that may exist in this study. (2) The final effective  sample  size  obtained  in  this  study  is  relatively  limited. PLS-SEM has significant advantages in handling small to medium sample sizes (Hair et al., 2021). PLS-SEM enables effective estimation of path coefficients in complex models even when the sample size is relatively limited (Soumya et al., 2022). This study has a medium-sized effective sample, comprising 120 manufacturing enterprises aggregated at the corporate level, derived from 292 independent questionnaires. According to Hair et al. (2021), the minimum sample size required for PLS-SEM is typically no less than 10 times the maximum number of paths pointing to any latent variable in the model. In this study, the effective sample size far exceeds this minimum requirement, ensuring the robustness and validity of the model estimates. (3) PLS-SEM can simultaneously handle C. Renfei et al.

## 3.2. Data collection

To obtain the sample data needed for this study, we conducted a questionnaire  survey  from  April  to  September  2024  with  300  senior managers from 120 local Chinese manufacturing companies by adopting the teaching platform of MBA and EMBA at Dalian University of Technology.  This  platform  ensures  sample  breadth  and  diversity,  while participants ' industry  expertise  allows  the  data  to  accurately  reflect AIC ' s role in driving CSP, providing a robust foundation for analysis. Given the focus on AIC in manufacturing enterprises, we selected firms based on China ' s manufacturing industry standards. The 120 manufacturing enterprises surveyed are widely distributed in the central and  eastern  regions  of  China,  covering  important  cities  such  as Shanghai, Dalian, Hangzhou, Shenzhen, Xi ' an, and Beijing. The above cities  have  strong  economic  strength,  scientific  and  technological innovation capability, and policy support, and their manufacturing enterprises  cover  a  wide  range  of  areas  and  face  the  urgent  need  for transformation and upgrading, which provides rich practice cases for exploring  the  innovative  path  of  combining  AIC  with  CSP.  The manufacturing sectors of these companies cover a wide range of areas such as automobile manufacturing, electronics manufacturing, textile and  garment  processing,  and  steel  manufacturing,  all  of  which  are closely  related  to  and  in  line  with  the  direction  of  development  of emerging strategic  industries  advocated  by ' Made in China (2025) ' . During  the  preparation  of  the  scales  and  questionnaires,  this  study specifically invited five senior experts in the field, who thoroughly discussed and meticulously adjusted the reference scales to ensure professionalism.  In  addition,  50  CEOs  in  MBA  programs  were  additionally invited to independently review the questionnaire content, and their valuable comments further ensured the high relevance and consistency of the scale content with the design of this study. To mitigate common methodological bias brought on by the selfassessment method, this study employs a two-stage approach to data investigation. Questionnaires were distributed to multiple senior managers per firm (averaging 2 -3 per enterprise) to capture diverse perspectives and enhance data reliability at the firm level (Podsakoff et al., 2003).  Responses  from  the  same  firm  were  aggregated  by  averaging scores across items, transforming individual-level data into firm-level constructs for analysis, as the study focuses on enterprise capabilities and performance. No weighting was applied, as all respondents were equivalently  qualified  senior  managers.  Invalid  questionnaires  (e.g., incomplete,  patterned  responses)  were  excluded.  The  first  phase  for collecting data on AIC. The second stage is used to collect CSP, and the questionnaire for this stage is distributed only to those who responded to the completed questionnaires from the first stage. This study considers questionnaires that are filled out incorrectly, omit information, contain substantial  portions  of  the  same  response,  or  do  not  meet  the  requirements for completion to be invalid. In this study, all questionnaires are coded to make it easier to match the two survey stages. The questionnaires for this study are distributed either face-to-face or by e-mail. The Bartlett ' s test of sphericity was significant ( p < 0.001), and KMO values were higher than 0.8. Therefore, the questionnaire for this study can be formalized. 300 questionnaires were distributed as part of the formal survey ' s first phase, and 295 valid questionnaires were returned. In the first stage, 295 questionnaires were distributed to participants, and in the second stage, 295 of them were returned. 292 valid surveys were received after invalid ones were removed. The advantages of the partial least squares structural equation modeling (PLS) model in small sample studies are demonstrated by the fact that 292 sample sizes satisfy the model ' s requirements for sample size.

## 3.4. Measures

In  this  study,  the  scales  applied  were  obtained  using  two  main acquisition  methods.  First,  direct  reference  was  made  to  proven  and mature scales from existing studies, which cover important dimensions such as CSP. Then, based on the in-depth understanding and analysis of the existing scales in the previous studies, the scales are modified and supplemented in the key areas of AIC to ensure that they are closer to the actual needs of this study and have higher applicability.

AIC  has  great  potential  to  promote  sustainable  development  by reducing and eliminating traditional inefficiencies at the system level. The construct on AIC was adopted from Sj ¨ odin et al. (2023), who based their  framework on case studies of multiple observations of complex organizational processes. The study divided AIC into three sub-dimensions treated as reflective latent constructs in the PLS-SEM model: perceptive capabilities, predictive capabilities, and prescriptive capabilities.  Perceptive  capabilities  are  the  continuous  monitoring  of device performance to identify hidden patterns and anomalies. Predictive capabilities are the abilities to anticipate and respond to environmental changes from multidimensional data to enable efficient resource utilization.  Prescriptive  capabilities  involve  running  simulations  to determine  the  ideal  actions  to  maximize  sustainable  value.  To  operationalize  these,  the  scale  items  were  adapted  and  integrated  with manufacturers ' production  processes  during  initial  testing  to  reflect comprehensive firm-level digital practices. For example, a perceptive capability item might be: ' Continuously monitoring equipment performance ' (coded on a five-point Likert scale). Responses to these items were transformed by averaging scores across multiple respondents per firm to form the latent variable scores for each sub-dimension.

CSP was measured using three dimensions, namely economic performance, environmental performance, and social performance, mainly referring to the categorization of CSP by Shang et al. (2020). Economic performance emphasizes the profitability of a company by working with its  stakeholders.  Environmental  performance  focuses  on  eliminating adverse environmental impacts from production processes and building an  environmentally  friendly  corporate  image.  Social  performance  is satisfying  the  interests  of  all  parties  by  improving  the  health  of  employees or the community. The scale contains a total of 18 observed variables related to corporate profit, streamlining processes, handling waste, and improving employee health. ## 4.1. Assessment of structural model

The underlying hypotheses are tested using the full sample, without distinguishing  between  traditional  and  Industry  4.0  manufacturing firms. The path coefficients and hypothesis testing results in the structural model are shown in Table 5 and Fig. 2. The empirical results show that all three model paths are significant at the p-value less than 0.01 level ( p < 0.01), except for the relationship between prescriptive capabilities and CSP (H1c), which is not significant. Thus, three hypotheses in this study are supported, which are H1, H1a, and H1b.

Hypothesis 1 proposes a positive relationship between AIC and CSP. The results in Table 5 show that AIC has a positive effect on CSP ( β = 0.49; p < 0.001). Indeed, the AIC of firms may positively influence CSP by optimizing operational processes and enhancing efficient resource allocation, providing explanatory power for these results. In hypotheses 1a and 1b, the coefficients β of the impact of perceptive capabilities and predictive capabilities on CSP reach 0.47 ( p < 0.01) and 0.43 ( p < 0.01), respectively, which are significant at p-values below 0.01. In hypothesis 1c, the coefficient of the impact of the firm ' s prescriptive capabilities on CSP is 0.28, with a p-value higher than 0.05, which does not prove the significance  of  the  relationship.  Possible  explanations  are  that  prescriptive capabilities are higher-order capabilities based on massive data analysis and decision simulation, and that their formation and development  are  constrained  by  the  operational  processes  of  industrial manufacturing. Given that manufacturing firms do not yet have welldeveloped prescriptive capabilities, they may not be able to realize the role of prescriptive capabilities on CSP in the short term.

## 4.3. Effects of control variables

Among  the  three  control  variables,  firm  size  is  significantly  and positively correlated with AIC ( β = 0.31; p < 0.01), which suggests that firm scale not only empowers AIC through capital and technology reserves,  but  also  amplifies  technology  dividends  through  ecological location advantages. This supports the development of the resource base view (Barney, 1991) in the digital sphere. Firm age shows a positive effect on CSP ( β = 0.17; p < 0.05). Industry concentration is significantly positively correlated with CSP ( β = 0.19; p < 0.05), which reveals a divergence of sustainable transformation paths in monopolistic markets. In highly concentrated industries, head players rely on market dominance  to  internalize  AIC  and  achieve  sustainable  premiums  through technology exclusivity. Small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), on the other hand, have fallen into a ' sustainable depression ' due to limited ecological  collaboration.  In  AI-driven  sustainable  transformation,  industry  structure  reconfigures  the  road  to  value  capture  through competitive norms, while enterprise scale establishes the groundwork for technology adoption.

## 5. Discussion

## 5.1. Effect of AIC on CSP

The empirical findings indicate that AIC has a significant positive driving effect on CSP overall (H1 is established), which confirms the theoretical expectation of treating AIC as dynamic capabilities. That is, AIC can empower sustainable development of enterprises through information integration and opportunity perception, which is consistent with the views of Wang et al. (2025), Jiao et al. (2024), and Kumar et al. (2025b). However, an in-depth analysis of the hierarchical effects of AIC sub-capabilities reveals a more complex internal mechanism: perceptive capabilities and predictive capabilities, as the foundation of technology empowerment, have been proven to effectively and directly improve CSP (H1a and H1b are valid). This result is consistent with the classic logic of ' instrumental rationality dominates short-term gains ' in technology empowerment theory (Wu and Fan, 2025; Phillips, 2019). Specifically,  solving  specific  problems  through  hardware  performance upgrades  and  data  training  efficiency  can  quickly  translate  into observable  and  sustainable  performance  gains  (Ghobakhloo  et  al., 2023).

Contrary to prior studies (Sj ¨ odin et al., 2023), our data shows the relationship between prescriptive capabilities and CSP is not significant. This  discrepancy  may  stem  from  the  phased  disconnection  and C. Renfei et al.

divergence of paths between AI technology and AIC accumulation in the digital  transformation  process  of  manufacturing  firms.  Specifically, compared with prescriptive capabilities, perceptive capabilities eliminate  the ' information  black  box ' through  real-time  data  feedback, directly reducing the decision-making lag costs of manufacturing firms. With the continuous accumulation of data collected by manufacturing firms, sustainable performance will be driven in a stable manner. Predictive capabilities are based on inferences about future trends based on perceptual data, forming a positive cycle with sustainable performance. However,  prescriptive  capabilities,  as  high-level  capabilities,  require breaking through organizational inertia and interest barriers (Li et al., 2022b, 2024). In addition, the benefits of prescriptive capabilities are highly  indirect  and  long-term,  and  are  easily  disrupted  by  external variables. For instance, an automotive manufacturer deployed AI-driven process optimization algorithms, but the approval system and departmental barriers resulted in a disconnect between the algorithmic models and production requirements. This echoes Teece et al. (1997) assertion that the value of high-level dynamic capabilities may be constrained by organizational foundations. This may hinder the impact of higher-order prescriptive  capabilities  on  sustainable  performance.  This  discovery offers a dynamic, multi-layered viewpoint for studying AIC. That is, the short-term focus should be on the instrumental value of perceptive and predictive  capabilities  (e.g.,  equipment  monitoring  to  reduce  consumption), while the long-term goal should be to use organizational reengineering to unlock the systemic potential of prescriptive capabilities.

## 5.2. AIC value transformation mechanisms for different types of manufacturing firms

This study introduces enterprise type as a boundary condition, profoundly revealing the impact of different types of manufacturing firms on AIC value transformation, and providing empirical evidence for understanding the context dependence of AIC empowering CSP. Empirical results show that in traditional manufacturing firms, the promotional effect  of  AIC  on  CSP  is  significantly  weakened,  and  its  hierarchical empowerment effect exhibits characteristics of ' marginal diminishing returns. ' Although perceptive and predictive capabilities remain effective, their effects are far less than those of Industry 4.0 manufacturing firms, while prescriptive capabilities are insignificant and even exhibit negative  values  in  the  opposite  direction.  This  deeply  reflects  the inherent  constraints  of  traditional  manufacturing  models  in  terms  of management  processes  (Tian  et  al.,  2023b),  departmental  barriers (Werning and Spinler, 2020), and path dependence (Brekke et al., 2024). These  constraints  can  be  further  understood  through  the  lens  of absorptive  capacity  theory  (Cohen  and  Levinthal,  1990;  Zahra  and George,  2002),  where  traditional  manufacturing  firms ' limited  prior experience with digital technologies hampers their ability to recognize, assimilate, and apply AIC innovations, thus restricting its transformation into sustainable outcomes. For example, an automotive manufacturer invested 70 % of its AI budget in production line optimization, but the multi-level approval system resulted in a technology iteration cycle of up to 18 months, ultimately rendering the effects of low-carbon process design insignificant. That is, AIC has limitations as dynamic capabilities in the context of traditional manufacturing firms.

Conversely, Industry 4.0 manufacturing firms, with their open value networks (Awan et al., 2021), flat  organizations  (Iram et  al.,  2024), data-driven  and  agile  decision-making  organizational  characteristics (Khin and Kee, 2022), provide a natural ' soil ' for the release of AIC value  (especially  prescriptive  capabilities).  However,  this  superiority extends beyond mere organizational openness or ecosystem participation. Industry 4.0 firms often possess pre-existing capabilities to handle digital  technologies,  which significantly  enhance  their  absorptive  capacity for AI (Cohen and Levinthal, 1990). This prior knowledge base allows  them  to  more  effectively  integrate  AIC  into  core  processes, amplifying its impact on sustainability by facilitating the assimilation of external AI innovations and their internal reconfiguration for CSP gains (Zahra and George, 2002). In this context, AIC not only plays a significant  role  in  promoting  CSP,  but  also  enables  each  sub-capability  to effectively  fulfill  its  intended  function.  For  instance,  Industry  4.0 manufacturing firms can deeply embed sustainable goals such as energy consumption monitoring and carbon footprint tracking into the underlying design of AI systems, and achieve real-time routing optimization and dynamic resource allocation through cross-system data flows, ultimately improving sustainable performance (Wenhui et al., 2024; Wang et  al.,  2022;  Steiber  and  Alvarez,  2024).  Therefore,  the  fundamental difference between the two types of enterprises lies not only in the depth of  technology  application,  but  also  in  whether  the  organizational infrastructure can provide the necessary ' adaptability ' for AIC (especially prescriptive capabilities). This adaptability is theoretically nuanced by absorptive capacity, where Industry 4.0 firms ' enhanced ability to absorb AI knowledge acts as a multiplier for dynamic capabilities, distinguishing them from traditional counterparts. This enables it to transform from a passive response tool into a strategic lever that proactively  drives  systematic  and  sustainable  change.  This  finding greatly enriches our understanding of the boundary conditions for the value conversion of digital capabilities, highlighting the central role of organizational structure and management models in unlocking the full potential of AIC.

## 6. Conclusion

## 6.1. Theoretical implications

This study empirically tests the hierarchical influence mechanism of AIC and its sub-capabilities on CSP, significantly expanding the existing  technology-dominated  perspective  on  the  relationship between AI and sustainability. It takes the lead in deconstructing and verifying AIC under the dynamic capabilities theory (DCT) framework, revealing its complexity as high-order dynamic capabilities driving CSP. Previous studies have mostly focused on technical functionality, broadly assuming that AI promotes sustainability by improving decision-making rationality and resource efficiency, but lacking empirical analysis of the composition of AI C and its differentiated pathways. Based on DCT, this study not only confirms that AIC has a positive impact on  CSP  overall,  but  more  importantly,  through  hierarchical analysis, it reveals that ' perceptive capabilities ' and ' predictive capabilities ' as basic technical enabling layers can directly and effectively improve CSP, while the role of ' prescriptive capabilities ' as a high-level system optimization layer has not met expectations. This empirical finding reveals that AIC-enabled CSP is not a homogeneous ' black box ' process, but rather follows the logic of ' instrumental rationality first, value rationality second. ' Of these, instrumental rationality emphasizes the efficiency of behavioral  means,  with  the  goal  of  maximizing  efficiency.  In contrast, value rationality emphasizes  the social value of behavior, that is, the long-term value of behavior to the public interest and moral norms (Wu and Fan, 2025; Phillips, 2019). This finding not only challenges Sj ¨ odin et al. (2023) ' s assumption that AIC sub-capabilities are equally effective contributing factors, but more importantly, it bridges a key explanatory gap in dynamic capabilities theory in organizational contexts: the potential value of higher-order dynamic capabilities (such as prescriptive capabilities) may be offset by organizational infrastructure  rigidity.  Therefore,  this  study  uses  hierarchical empirical testing to not only deepen the research perspective on AI and sustainability from ' technology application ' to ' capability building, ' but also explores the boundaries and conditions of AIC at  different  levels.  This  provides  a  more  organizationally  relevant, hierarchical theoretical foundation for dynamic capabilities

This study reveals that the type of enterprise is a boundary condition  that  significantly  affects  the  efficiency  of  the  transformation  from  AIC  to  CSP,  providing  important  contextual deepening  and  boundary  expansion  for  the  application  of  dynamic capabilities theory (DCT) in the digital context. Empirical results show that the value realization of AIC does not exist in isolation,  but  is  deeply  embedded  in  specific  organizational structures  and  operating  models.  Traditional  manufacturing firms are constrained by their linear value chains, rigid processes, departmental barriers, and historical path dependence. Even if they  invest  resources  in  developing  AIC,  its  effectiveness  is greatly  weakened  by  organizational ' organizational  inertia, ' making it difficult for AIC to effectively transform into CSP. This echoes and deepens the classic argument in organizational theory that ' organizational inertia ' hinders  change  (Hannan  and Freeman, 1984; Lyu et al., 2016). On the contrary, Industry 4.0 manufacturing firms rely on their open value networks, flat organizations, agile decision-making, and data-driven characteristics  to  provide  a  natural ' fertile  ground ' for  the  effective operation of AIC (especially high-level prescriptive capabilities), thereby significantly amplifying the positive impact of AIC on CSP. This finding not only confirms the key role of ' organizational adaptability ' in the realization of capability value in dynamic  capabilities theory (Teece et al., 2016),  but  more importantly, it clearly identifies and empirically validates ' enterprise  type ' as  a  core  contextual  variable  and  theoretical boundary for understanding differences in the effectiveness of digital capabilities (such as AIC). This fills a theoretical gap in previous research, which has not sufficiently addressed the heterogeneity of organizational infrastructure and its far-reaching implications  when  exploring  the  relationship  between  digital capabilities  and  sustainable  performance.  It  provides  a  key theoretical lens and organizational-level explanatory mechanism for  future  research to understand ' why the same digital capabilities produce vastly different results in different companies. '

## 6.2. Practical and managerial implications

This study finds that AIC is key enablers for industry to move towards sustainability, and calls for more managers of manufacturing companies engaged in digital  transformation  to  focus  on  and  capitalize  on  that potential. Primarily, companies realizing sustainable performance need to rely on AIC that facilitates sustainability opportunity perception and event prediction throughout the accelerated commercialization process. Although  managers  usually  view  sustainability  as  a  transformational direction and give importance to AI technologies, this study shows that AIC has a positive impact on the sustainable performance of industrial firms. By emphasizing strategic innovation and change in the age of AI, AIC helps generate sustained performance. Thus, managers should view the development of AIC as an opportunity to improve CSP. Traditional manufacturing enterprises should focus on the modular embedding of perceptive  and  predictive  capabilities,  and  direct  AI  algorithms  into local  optimization  scenarios  such  as  energy  consumption  monitoring and dynamic scheduling of supply chains. Industry 4.0 manufacturing firms need to strengthen the eco-level synergy of their prescriptive capabilities, but they need to be wary of the hidden environmental costs of arithmetic expansion. AI investment must be closely linked to organizational transformation for both kinds of businesses. By separating their extremely complicated business segments, traditional companies create mechanisms for agile development.

Then, managers need to recognize that AIC is a key enabling factor in driving manufacturing companies to improve CSP, but its value is not uniformly released and is highly dependent on the type of enterprise. First, managers of manufacturing enterprises should recognize that the improvement of AIC requires a layered focus strategy. Perceptive capabilities and predictive capabilities, as the technical foundation layer, can directly and quickly bring about significant and sustainable performance  improvements  by  improving  operational  efficiency,  and should be the focus of current investment. Companies can prioritize the application of AI in scenarios dominated by ' instrumental rationality, ' such as energy consumption monitoring, dynamic supply chain scheduling, and predictive maintenance, to obtain short-term visible benefits. Next, managers need to accept that prescriptive capabilities won ' t work on their own in the short term. Since these are high-level capabilities, they require a lot of organizational inertia and cross-departmental coordination,  so  their  value  is  often  indirect  and  long-term.  Therefore, companies should not expect to see immediate improvements in sustainable performance simply by deploying complex AI decision-making systems. Instead, they should view this as a long-term capacity-building goal  and  recognize  that  realizing  its  full  potential  will  require  deep organizational  process  reengineering  and  management  model  transformation.  Finally,  and  most  importantly,  companies  must  deeply recognize that their own type is the core boundary condition for AIC value  transformation.  Traditional  manufacturing  companies  are  constrained  by  data  silos,  rigid  processes,  and  organizational  structures, resulting in significantly lower efficiency in the transformation of their AIC to CSP compared to Industry 4.0 manufacturing firms. In summary, manufacturing enterprises need to formulate differentiated AIC development paths and organizational support strategies based on their own characteristics  to  maximize  the  enabling  value  of  AI  technology  for sustainable transformation.

## 6.3. Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the industry coverage and geographical distribution of the sample may limit the generalizability of the findings. The study data are mainly from Chinese manufacturing firms,  especially  focusing  on  the  dichotomous  comparison  between traditional  and  Industry  4.0  manufacturing  firms.  While  this  enables companies to analyze the transformation process in depth in the Chinese context, it may limit the universality of the conclusions in other regions of the world, other industries (such as the service industry), or different types  of  companies  (such  as  multinational  companies).  In  addition, companies may be at different stages of digital transformation in reality, and a simple dichotomy may not fully capture the complex mix of forms in between. Future research needs to include more granular indicators of corporate digital maturity. Furthermore, the research model does not sufficiently  analyze  dynamics  and  time  effects.  This  study  is  mainly based on cross-sectional data and uses static analysis methods. However, the construction and improvement of AIC and its impact on CSP are likely to be a dynamic process that may even involve lag effects. The lack of tracking and analysis of the time lag between capacity development and performance impact is a shortcoming of this model in explaining long-term dynamic relationships. Finally, the assessment of digital capabilities is relatively narrow. The study focuses on the direct impact of AIC on CSP and the boundaries of their enterprise types. However, the sustainable transformation of enterprises in reality may be driven by the synergistic combination of multiple digital capabilities (such as big data analysis capabilities, IoT capabilities, and digital platform capabilities), which may be complementary or substitutable. This study has not yet explored the interaction between AIC and other key digital capabilities and their comprehensive impact on CSP, which limits the depth of understanding of the overall digital empowerment of enterprises.
