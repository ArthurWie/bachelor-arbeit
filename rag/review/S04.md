## 1. Introduction

Customer relationship management (CRM) is considered a strategic macro  process.  It  aims  to  ensure  building  and  sustaining  a  profitmaximization  portfolio  concerning  customer  relationship  with  the firms (Saura, Palos-Sanchez, & Blanco-Gonz ´ alez, 2019). Many studies have been published on CRM in the B2C context, but explicit research on CRM in the B2B relationship context is still at a rudimentary stage (Irsic, 2017).  B2B  CRM  activities  help  to  analyze  the  customers ' needs  by examining  the  multifarious  customer  data  (Kim,  Park,  Dubinsky, & Chaiy, 2012). Some of the huge-sized databases for such B2B relationship  management  are  Matrix  Marketing,  Ampliz  Salesbuddy,  Easy Leadz, Leadspace, and so on (Ampliz, 2020). Firms use these B2B databases for accurate and faster targeting opportunities and cross-channel integration, and for effective collaboration with vendors and partners. Analysis of such a huge volume of customer data is difficult for humans. Therefore, appropriately using technology like artificial intelligence (AI) is  essential (Josiassen, Assaf, & Cvelbar, 2014; Wang & Feng, 2012). This invites the need to study AI-integrated CRM systems in the B2B context  (Harrigan,  Soutar,  Choudhury, & Lowe,  2015;  San-Martína, Jim ´ eneza, & Lopez, 2016). ## 2. Literature review

A huge volume of customer data can be effectively analyzed without requiring  human interference by using an AI-integrated CRM system (Ferraris, Santoro, & Bresciani, 2017; Heide, Wathne, & Rokkan, 2007). In the B2B context, firms can use inputs from analyzing customer data to strengthen the CRM system (Herhausen, Mio ˇ cevi ˇ c, Morgan, & Kleijnen, 2020;  Kumar,  Shankar, & Alijohani,  2019;  Al-Omoush,  Sim ´ on-Moya, Atwah Al-ma ' aitah, & Sendra-García, 2021). B2B relationship marketing is interpreted as 'the marketing of goods and services to commercial enterprises, governments, and other non-profit organizations for use in goods and services they produce to sell to other industrial customers ' (Chaston, 1999). A CRM system analyzes the volume of customer data, which a firm can use to effectively prioritize, customize, and efficiently segment those data (Alshawi, Missi, & Irani, 2011; Dalla Pozza, Goetz, & Sahut, 2018; Lacka, Chan, & Wang, 2020; Suoniemi et al., 2021). AI is useful for the CRM system to perform these activities in the B2B context (Chatterjee, Ghosh, Chaudhuri, & Chaudhuri, 2020b; Verma & Verma, 2013). When analyzing customer data through an AI-embedded CRM system, a firm ' s decision-making process is automated and more accurate,  impacting its operational efficiency (Assimakopoulos, Papaioannou,  Sarmaniotis, & Georgiadis,  2015;  Greenberg,  2010;  Maxwell, Jeffrey, & L ´ evesque, 2011). The use of AI-integrated CRM system in a firm impacts its performance provided there is strong leadership support (Inyang, Agnihotri, & Munoz, 2018; Thakur et al., 2016; Rana et al.,

2021). Several studies (Chatterjee, 2015, 2019; Lipiainen, 2015; Mustak,  2014)  have  observed  that  AI  enhances  the  efficiency  of  a  CRM system. Researchers (Ahearne et al., 2012; Nguyen & Waring, 2013) have also observed that applying new technology improves the business model  through  better  communication  activities,  information  control system and management design, thus improving CRM activities. Studies have revealed that applications of AI-integrated CRM systems impact firm performance (Coltman et al., 2011; Lee et al., 2014; Sreenivasulu, 2019; Piccolo et al., 2021). From the study of literature, it was observed that the CRM system effectively helps firms to improve their relationships  with  their  customers  (Kumar  et  al.,  2019).  Studies  also  have documented that firms with AI-embedded CRM are able to prioritize, customize,  and  segment  the  collected  data  of  the  customers,  which eventually improves firm performance (Lipiainen, 2015; Lacka et al., 2020). However, from studying literature, it appears that no exhaustive study has investigated the impact of adopting AI-embedded CRM in a B2B context under the moderating influence of leadership support and technology  turbulence.  This  has  given  rise  to  the  need  to  study  this aspect.

## 3. Theoretical background and development of hypotheses

## 3.1. Theoretical background

In the context of AI-CRM adoption for B2B relationship management, the  firms  need  to  emphasize  how  to  utilize  the  available  resources, especially  various  information  of  customers  (Agnihotri  et  al.,  2016; Chatterjee et al., 2020b; Davenport et al., 2020). In analyzing customer data with AI technology, the CRM system for B2B relationship management should improve firm performance. This requires firms to make best use of available resources. In this scenario, the management of the firm needs to involve its employees in improving the B2B relationships. Employees should learn how the available resources can help them make the B2B relationships more fruitful, effective, and dynamic (Fainshmidt et al., 2016). Here, it is deemed to be essential to nurture the resourcebased view (RBV) theory (Kor & Mahoney, 2003) and dynamic capability view (DCV) (Teece et al., 1997). Both these theories posit that, to achieve better performance in the B2B relationship context with AI-CRM technology,  firms  need  to  develop  and  codevelop  their  appropriate sensing abilities. Therefore, they have to identify and assess the available and applicable technological opportunities to adequately mobilize the  appropriate  resources  in  dynamic  B2B  relationship  management (Fainshmidt et al., 2016). Be it mentioned here that RBV is considered to present a managerial framework for determining the strategic resources a firm can exploit towards achieving sustainable competitive advantage. RBV focuses on firms ' internal resources that are valuable, rare, inimitable, and not substitutable (VRIN). Proper utilization of these resources helps them to gain sustainable competitive advantage. In the context of B2B relationships, firms need to effectively deploy their VRIN resources to  harness  those  resources  between  cross-functional  units,  alliance partners, and vendors. However, in the dynamic business environment, when customer demands are found to be changing rapidly, all firms involved in a B2B relationship should respond to those changing needs and  articulate  dynamic  strategies  amongst  each  other  to  gain  joint success. This is the concept of DCV theory, which posits that firms need to possess the ability to integrate, build, and reconfigure internal and external resources to adapt to a rapidly changing business environment (Teece, 2012). Again, employees will pervasively and sometimes openly resist using any new system in a firm, as observed in terms of status quo bias (SQB) theory (Samuelson & Zeckhauser, 1988). The arrival of AI has also brought the emergence of the idea of technology turbulence. This is related with the concept that users of a new technology like AI or a new system commonly become inhibitors of it. They raise several excuses, such as they anticipate problems cropping up in the new technology, they doubt the user-friendliness of the new technology or the new system, and sometimes they are concerned about their readiness to use the S. ## 3.2. Development of hypotheses and conceptual model

From studying literature and theories, we were able to identify the factors that would impact the firms ' performance. In this section, we establish  the  relationship  among  these  factors  by  formulating  some hypotheses with consideration of two moderators 'technological turbulence ' and  'leadership  support ' .  These  will  help  to  develop  a  model conceptually.

## 3.2.1. AI-CRM adoption for B2B relationship management (ACRM)

Firms  that  use  AI-CRM  in  the  B2B  relationship  management  to analyze the customer data will be able to reduce the overall administrative costs for better marketing campaigns (Ascarza et al., 2018; Lipiainen, 2015). Firms that work together and use a CRM system in the B2B context will fetch better results by accurately taking appropriate decisions as to how to proceed with the analyzed customer data (Saura et al., 2019). But analyzing a huge volume of customer data manually is problematic,  and  here  applications  of  AI  come  to  rescue  (Chatterjee et al., 2019). Thus, adopting AI-CRM for B2B relationship management could impact the decision-making system in an automated form that influences the operational quality and efficiency of the firms involved in B2B relationship (Mustak, 2014).

## Automated decision making (ADM)

To improve the B2B relationship, the firms need to establish a close relationship with their customers and partners. The firms should collect various  data  on  customers,  partners,  and  clients  to  process  through different CRM functionalities, to become useful and accurate information. The firms should then address their needs as and when required (Irsic, 2017). Managing, analyzing, and utilizing such huge volumes of data manually may not be possible, and the firm may fail to respond to the  customers ' need  in  an  efficient  and  timely  way.  The  manual decision-making process can also lead to employees ' making erroneous decisions  in  managing  their  firms ' B2B  relationship  with  their  customers, partners, and other clients. Thus, it is necessary to efficiently use such huge volume of customer data to arrive at an appropriate decision at  the  right  time  (Verma & Verma,  2013).  Any  kind  of  intelligent automation of a CRM system will help the employees better manage their B2B relationships. AI embedded with a CRM system can automate the decision-making process. This will also help to improve the satisfaction level of the employees involved in the B2B relationship (Alshare & Lane, 2011). It is also perceived that different stakeholders involved in B2B relationship management, such as managers and clients, will also experience improved levels of satisfaction. The above discussions help to formulate the following hypothesis.

H1:  AI-integrated  CRM  system  enables  automated  decision  making (ADM), which in turn helps to improve the B2B relationship satisfaction (BRS).

Firms need to innovate in multiple ways to improve their B2B relationships  with  customers,  and  an  AI-integrated  CRM  system  can complement those efforts. The firms collect customer data on their requirements, likings, dislikings (Li & Nguyen, 2016), and so on. Most of the time, the firms use different tools and processes to collect different kinds of customer data but managing a huge volume of data and using it at the right time in the B2B context is a challenge (Nguyen & Mutum, 2012).  Firms  collectively,  synchronistical,  and  collaboratively  synchronistical analyze customer data to devise the next course of actions (Lano,  2015).  Most  firms  follow  these  usual  operational  procedures using a CRM tool to improve their B2B relationships. The more the firms can  correctly  analyze  the  data  and  can  use  the  information  at  an appropriate time, the better they will be at maintaining B2B relationships. Adopting an AI-integrated CRM system may help in maintaining better relationships with firms ' increased operational efficiency (Molinillo & Japutra,  2017).  This  is  because  the  automated  system  can accurately manage and analyze huge volume customers without human involvement (De Luca, Herhausen, Troilo, & Rossi, 2020; Graca, Barry, & Doney, 2015; Rosenberg, 2018; Rust, 2020). As the firm ' s operational efficiency increases with the adoption of an AI-integrated CRM system, its B2B relationship will be positively impacted, which will increase the level  of  satisfaction  of  the  people  involved  in  B2B  relationship  management. This leads us to hypothesize as follows.

H2: AI-integrated CRM system helps to improve the operational efficiency (OPE) of the firms, which in turn improves the B2B relationship satisfaction (BRS).

The concept of AI-CRM adoption for B2B relationship management is considered an effective  system to manage the firms involved in B2B relationships. This might also influence the firms ' performance (Saura et  al.,  2019).  Firm  performance is  conceptualized as how a firm can achieve  its  target  in  a  more  efficient  way  (Li,  Ragu-Nathan,  RaguNathan, & Rao, 2006). Moreover, firm performance has a subjective measure, which corresponds to how well the firm manages its B2B relationships and how they influence its performance (Sin, Tse, & Yim, 2005). The firm performance can also be influenced by how well the firms ' B2B  relationships  are  measured,  and  the  CRM  tool  plays  an important  part  in  it.  The  relationship  can  be  improved  if  the  firms ' management  can  analyze  customer  information  and  needs  quickly through proper segmentation and prioritization to arrive at an accurate decision quickly (Nunan, Sibai, Schivinski, & Christodoulides, 2018). This process takes less time and is more cost-effective when an automated system using AI technology is used to analyze the huge volume of customer data (Pandey & Mookerjee, 2018). Hence, a firm appropriately applying an AI-integrated CRM system towards managing the quality of B2B relationships can improve its performance. The AI-integrated CRM system can also automate a few processes such as understanding customers ' requirements, their complaints, likings, and disliking. This also helps the firms to remove some of their manual tasks and to speed up the decision-making  process, finally  improving  the  firm  performance (Inyang et al., 2018; Rodriguez & Honeycutt, 2011). The above discussions help to formulate the following hypothesis.

H3:  AI-CRM  Adoption  for  B2B  relationship  management  (ACRM) significantly and positively improves the firm performance (FP).

## 3.2.3. Moderating effects of technology turbulence (TT)

If the relationship between two variables is not fixed, a third variable impacting on the relationship may strengthen the relationship, weaken the relationship, or even change the direction of the relationship. This third  variable  is  called  the  moderating  variable.  Technology ' s  rapid change and unpredictability is conceptualized as technology turbulence (Song et al., 2005). Because technology changes rapidly, the firms need to efficiently manage such changes. While incrementally upgrading an existing application, the firm might experience several issues. This is associated with the concept of technology turbulence (Ullah, Iqbal, & Shams, 2020). In the present study, technology turbulence in the B2B context is conceptualized as a situation concerning the emergence of AI technology and its integration with existing CRM applications (Santos, Borini, & Pereira, 2020). Business relations may undergo rapid changes when firms incrementally upgrade or introduce technology, especially if it changes the market orientation, impacting B2B relationships (Powell, Noble, Noble, & Han, 2018). It is possible to integrate AI technology with a firm ' s existing CRM system to improve and strengthen the B2B relationship. The employees who will use the AI-integrated CRM system in maintaining B2B relationships must be trained properly, otherwise, they  could  resist  using  it.  This  is  also  supported  by  SQB  theory (Samuelson, & Zeckhauser, 1988). This is the concept of technology turbulence,  and  it  is  perceived  to  negatively  affect  B2B  relationship satisfaction with its two predictors covering H1 and H2. These inputs lead us to formulate the following hypotheses.

H5:  Technology  turbulence  (TT)  has  moderating  impacts  in  the  relationship between automated decision making (ADM) and B2B relationship satisfaction (BRS) covering H1.

H6:  Technology  turbulence  (TT)  has  moderating  impacts  in  the  relationship  between  operational  efficiency  (OPE)  of  the  firms  and  B2B relationship satisfaction (BRS) covering H2.

## 3.2.4. Moderating effects of leadership support (LS)

The use of AI technology with the existing CRM system to manage B2B relationships is considered a new idea. The AI-CRM application for B2B relationship management would impact on the firm ' s performance (Rossomme, 2003). But initially, its introduction could face resistance from the employees who are uncomfortable using the new system. It is in conformity with SQB theory. This is the concept of technology turbulence, as has already been discussed. In this situation, for firms to adopt the  AI-integrated  CRM  system,  employees  need  appropriate  training (Chatterjee  et  al.,  2020a;  Lin,  Yip,  Ho, & Sambasivan,  2020;  Rafiki, Hidayat, & Al Abdul Razzaq, 2019). Top management needs to actively support the adoption of the new system (Thakur et al., 2016). Leadership support and reinforcement will help align the employees to the firm ' s strategy, influencing them to use the new system by removing any initial block to adopting the new system (Luo, Tong, Fang, & Qu, 2019; Sheth & Kellstadt, 2020; Vermeer, Araujo, Bernritter, Van Noort, 2019). Leadership can also support employees to overcome their initial unwillingness to use the new system. Thus, it is perceived that leadership support will have moderating effects towards the firm performance when using an AI-integrated CRM system which lead to hypothesize as follows.

H7: Leadership support (LS) acts as a moderator to impact the relationship between the B2B relationship satisfaction (BRS) and the firm performance (FP) covering H4.

## 4. Research methodology

To validate the proposed conceptual model, the partial least squares (PLS) structural equation modeling (SEM) technique was preferred. In terms of the observation of Hair, Howard, and Nitzle (2020), we note that the PLS-SEM technique is considered the most popular composite method for estimating structural equation modeling (SEM). This process comprises two parts. First is the research model in which we estimate the loadings of the items, and the average variance extracted, composite reliability, and Cronbach ' s α of the constructs. The second part of this process is to estimate the predictive relevance of the model and to assess the model fit required for testing the hypotheses by SEM. This technique can estimate a complex model to provide robust results (Wetzels et al., 2009).  The  PLS-SEM  approach  was  conducted  with  SmartPLS  3.2.3 software, and it considered non-parametric bootstrapping of 5000 replications to assess the path coefficients between the different linkages (Ringle  et  al.,  2015;  Akter  et  al.,  2017).  The  PLS-SEM  method  can analyze  data  that  are  not  normally  distributed,  which  the  CB-SEM technique cannot do (Rigdon et al., 2017; Willaby et al., 2015; Hair, Sarstedt, Ringle, & Gudergan, 2018). With PLS-SEM, we used a standard, 5-point Likert scale to quantify the responses from respondents in a survey that had a structured questionnaire.

## 5.3. Multigroup analysis (MGA)

To verify the effects of the two moderators, TT and LS, on the linkages H1 and H2 (for TT) and on the linkage H4 (for LS), MGA was undertaken with the help of an accelerated and bias corrected bootstrapping  approach  with  consideration  of  5000  resamples.  The moderator TT has been divided into two categories, which are High TT and Low TT, whereas the other moderator LS has been divided into two categories, which are Strong LS and Weak LS. For conducting MGA, a three-step process was undertaken (Matthews, 2017). In the first step, two categorical variables were selected. The variables are the groups which are large enough for statistical power. In the second step, the process of measurement invariance of composite models (MICOM) was followed (Henseler, Ringle, & Sarstedt, 2016) to determine if the measurement models have the same attributes under different conditions (Henseler,  Ringle, & Sarstedt,  2015).  This  step  is  critical,  and  it  is required to be addressed in the MGA process. The MICOM process helps to compare the group parameters and to identify whether there is any measurement invariance, partial measurement invariance, or any full measurement invariance (Henseler et al., 2016). In this study, for two categorical variables, the mean  original  differences fell  between boundaries of 2.5% and 97.5%. Also, the original variance difference fell between the 2.5% to 97.5% range. Thus, full invariance is observed. Here, it should be mentioned that if the values of the mean or original variance difference come between the range of 2.5% and 97.5%, then according to the MICOM approach, it cannot further proceed with the MGA. In the final step, the results of statistical tests to compare the multigroup  have  been  assessed.  The  SEM  technique  has  several  approaches that can be used for comparing the path coefficients (p-values) of the group. In this case, Hair, Sarstedt, Ringle, and Gudergan ' s (2017) permutation  approach  has  been  followed.  The  PLS  algorithm  and bootstrapping (5000 resamples) for each group, that is, for TT and for LS, has been computed separately. If the p-value output difference is < 0.10, it means that there is a significant difference between these groups. Thus, the p-value difference for the two categories of the moderator TT on ADM → BRS is 0.02; on OPE → BRS, it is 0.03; and for the moderator LS on BRS → FP, it is 0.02. All the differences are < 0.10, which makes them significant. Hence, while conducting the MGA analysis, the stepby-step  procedure  was  followed  including  the  MICOM  process. ## 6. Implications of the study

## 6.1. Theoretical contribution

This  study  makes  several  theoretical  contributions  to  the  extent literature.  Several  studies  have  analyzed  how  an  AI-integrated  CRM system effects firm performance (Chatterjee et al., 2019a; Chatterjee et al., 2020b). However, this is the first to have analyzed the effects an AI-integrated CRM system has on firm performance in the B2B context. This is also the first study to consider the effects of both technology turbulence and leadership support, as moderators, on firm performance when using an AI-CRM system in the B2B context. Many studies have used RBV theory (Kor & Mahoney, 2003), DCV (Teece et al., 1997), and SQB theory (Samuelson, & Zeckhauser, 1988), but ours is the first to use these  theories  to  examine  firm  performance  when  applying  an  AIintegrated CRM system for B2B relationship management.

The present study claims to have provided empirical support for the notion that neither RBV theory nor DCV theory alone can address all the aspects to interpret a firm ' s capability, which is why the present study had to import the idea of SQB theory. By combining RBV, DCV, and SQB, we claim that the present study has been able to successfully interpret the different aspects of AI-CRM in the B2B relational scenario under the moderating effects of leadership support and technology turbulence. We believe this is a special theoretical contribution of this study. Moreover, a study by Ullah et al. (2020) investigated how a firm adopting CRM could impact its performance with consideration of the moderating role of technology turbulence. The concept of Ullah et al. ' s (2020) study has been extended in this present study to investigate how both technology turbulence and leadership support, as two moderators, influence an AIintegrated  CRM  system ' s  impact  on  firm  performance  in  the  B2B context. This is claimed to have added value to extant literature. A study of  Chang  et  al.  (2010)  demonstrated  that,  in  the  context  of  Korean companies, the application of CRM technology brings positive outcomes by strengthening marketing ability. The concept of that study has been extended in the present study to document how CRM tools embedded with AI technology could yield better firm performance. This has added value to the extant literature. Moreover, we could have chosen a standard adoption model to analyze how adopting an AI-CRM system for B2B relationship management impacts firm performance. Instead, we chose some better-suited factors in developing the theoretical model, which we claim to be successful, because the proposed theoretical model achieved a high predictive power of 73%. This is also claimed to be a unique theoretical contribution of this study.

The model has achieved success, as its predictive power is as high  as  73%.  We  have  focused  on  improving  the  B2B  relationship management in the dynamic environment by using AI-integrated CRM systems to enhance firm performance. The model shows that AI-CRM adoption for B2B relationship management, along with its other two components, ADM and OPE, has considerable impact on firm performance. Adopting AI-CRM for B2B relationship management alone can hardly improve the firm performance to the desired extent compared to that  derived  from  additional  consideration  of  automated  decision making and operational efficiency. However, whatever may be the value of  the  proposed  model,  it  cannot  be  said  that  it  is  free  from  all limitations.

Appendix Appendix A1. Measurement items with sources

The present study is based on the inputs of the respondents of Indian firms. Hence, the results so derived are construed to be India-specific. The findings may not be equally applicable to other countries. Hence, the results lack generalizability. Also, the survey in the present study was conducted using inputs of 312 usable respondents, which seems to be an inadequate number to represent the entire society. The findings of the present study on how AI-CRM could impact B2B relationships were based on the analysis of data from respondents representing manufacturing and service firms only. This study did not consider representation of other types of firms, and thus, it is also a defect in its generalizability.  The  predictive  power  of  the  model  is  73%.  In  this context, the present study appears to be silent on what steps have been taken to improve its explanative power. The present study is based on the findings from analyzing cross-sectional data, which creates problems in  establishing  accurate  causality  between  the  constructs.  These  are deemed to be salient limitations of this study.

To overcome these defects and to extend this study, we suggest that future researchers take some steps to rectify the limitations. The inputs of respondents spread across the globe could be considered to portray generalized results. Also, a survey could consider inputs from more respondents to represent the entire society. To project generalizable results,  a  future  study  should  include  representation  from  agricultural firms  and  conglomerates  as  well  as  from  service  and  manufacturing firms. To enhance the explanative power of the model, other relevant boundary  conditions  could  be  examined  to  assess  if  they  could strengthen the model. To remove the defects arising from utilizing crosssectional data, a longitudinal study could be undertaken with econometric  analysis,  which  could  remove  the  causality  and  endogeneity defects.
