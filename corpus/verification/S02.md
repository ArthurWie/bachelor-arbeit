# S02 — Baabdullah, Alalwan, Slade, Raman & Khatatneh (2021), Industrial Marketing Management 98, 255–270

## Verdict: CONFIRM

Full-text audit (all 14 PDF pages = printed pp. 255–270; printed page = PDF page + 254). Every coded value checks out against the paper; no crucial omissions found under the binding case law.

**What was checked:**

- **method / sample:** Survey with structured questionnaire, n = 392 B2B SME respondents (CEOs, CIOs, managers, owners), CB-SEM in AMOS (two-stage, maximum likelihood). "The conceptual model was tested using structural equation modelling of survey data collected from B2B SMEs (n = 392)." (p. 255, abstract). Questionnaire distributed July–September 2020; 83.1% of sampled firms have 1–25 employees (p. 262, Table 3).
- **country_region:** Sample is Saudi Arabia ("acceptance of AI practices by B2B SMEs in Saudi Arabia", p. 255) — author affiliations span Saudi Arabia/Jordan/UK/India/Qatar, sample country correctly coded.
- **industry:** wholesaling merchandising, manufacturing, construction, service, agriculture, IT/social media (p. 262, Table 3) — "cross-industry (B2B SMEs)" holds.
- **ai_measure:** latent construct "SMEs' AI practices" (P.AI1–4, acceptance of AI applications, Ghobakhloo & Ching 2019 scale) — matches "survey construct (acceptance of AI practices)".
- **outcome_construct = performance:** Two perceptual outcome scales, financial performance (market share, sales volume, growth) and non-financial performance (strategic advantage over competitors, client retention, customer satisfaction). The item N.F1 "AI applications will help us to gain strategic advantages over our competitors" (p. 268, Appendix) sits inside the non-financial performance scale — there is no distinct CA construct with its own hypothesis and validation, so per case law rule 1 this stays `performance`. Competitive-advantage talk in the discussion (Demirkan & Delen citation, p. 266) is framing only.
- **effect_direction = positive:** "H5a P.AI → FIN 0.709 ... ***" and "H5b P.AI → N.F 0.681 ... ***" (p. 265, Table 9); R² financial = 0.50, non-financial = 0.48 (p. 264). Both performance paths significant positive; no moderators, mediators, thresholds, or subgroups are tested on the AI→performance link, and no null/negative performance side-finding exists. Direction is a clean positive.
- **conditions:** All antecedent results verified against Table 9 (p. 265): awareness γ = 0.452*** (strongest antecedent), technology roadmapping γ = 0.228***, attitude γ = 0.188***, infrastructure γ = 0.167***; technicality γ = 0.002 (p = .937) and professional expertise γ = 0.043 (p = .242) not significant — exactly as coded, including the two nulls.
- **quality_notes:** "this study is cross-sectional, where both antecedents and consequences of AI practices were tested at one point in time" (p. 267 [printed 266–267 limitations section]); all measures single-informant perceptual Likert scales — matches "Perceptual self-report, cross-sectional".

**Non-flags (checked, deliberately not coded):** the model's other consequence paths — service experience (0.768***), customer engagement (0.759***), joint planning (0.669***), joint problem solving (0.784***) — are additional DVs outside the performance/CA outcome scope of the coding scheme, not conditions on the performance link. Common-method-bias check (Harman single factor, 42.8%) is a robustness note, not a condition.
