# S01 — Wamba-Taguimdje, Fosso Wamba, Kala Kamdjoug & Tchatchouang Wanko (2020), BPMJ 26(7), 1893–1924

**Verdict: CONFIRM** (one cosmetic page-reference note in quality_notes, no substantive flag)

## What was checked

Full cover-to-cover read of the extracted text (all 32 PDF pages, printed pagination 1893–1924 verified against running footers: PDF p. 1 = printed 1893, PDF p. 20 = printed 1912).

- **method = case study**: correct. "we adopted a qualitative approach based on the analysis of 500 case studies" (p. 1922); archival analysis of vendor-published mini-cases, three-phase design (conception, refinement, assessment; pp. 1903–1905). No second evidence strand — not mixed.
- **sample**: correct. Exactly "five hundred (500) mini-case studies published by approved and world-renowned AI solution providers" (p. 1904); providers listed in Appendix 3 (IBM, Amazon/AWS, Cloudera, Conversica, Logz.io, Microsoft, Cognitivescale, Universal Robots, Nvidia et al., p. 1923). "~500" is accurate.
- **country_region = multi-country / industry = cross-industry**: correct. Table A2 (p. 1922) lists 30+ industries; illustrative cases span UAE (ADNOC) and USA (UHS New York); providers and clients are global.
- **ai_measure**: correct — AI observed as vendor-reported transformation projects, "types of AI" catalogued in Table A2.
- **outcome_construct = performance**: correct under case law rule 1. No distinct CA construct is measured; CA appears only as framing ("developing and optimizing their strategic and competitive advantages", p. 1893) and "competitiveness" appears merely as one KPI word in a list of process indicators (p. 1902) — exactly the adjudicated S01 precedent.
- **performance_measure**: correct. "improve on performance at both the organizational (financial, marketing and administrative) and process levels" (p. 1893); qualitative verbatim-coding of case excerpts.
- **effect_direction = conditional**: holds. The model routes all AI effects through process-level effects (Propositions 1–6, no direct AICAP→organizational path), and the authors' headline claim is mechanism-bound: "organizations achieve performance through AI capabilities only when they use their features/technologies to reconfigure their processes" (p. 1893). All six propositions "Supported" (Table 2, p. 1904), but only via the process channel — conditional per rule 2.
- **conditions**: correct and complete under rule 4. Process reconfiguration as the binding mechanism (p. 1893) and the complement bundle: "multiple key elements must be brought together to ensure the success of AI: data, talent mix, domain knowledge, key decisions, external partnerships and scalable infrastructure" (p. 1894). ("Key decisions" is omitted from the row's paraphrase — negligible.) The supplier-dependence observation in sec. 6.1 (AIPE/AIIF "highly supplier-dependent", p. 1919) is mechanism narrative, not a coded moderator. No quantified thresholds, no subgroups with sign flips, no null side-findings exist in the paper.
- **key_finding**: faithful paraphrase of the abstract's "only when ... reconfigure their processes" claim.
- **quality_notes**: the bias caveat is real and verbatim: "there may have been an element of bias in the data contained in the cases, such as exaggerated claims or even restrictions on published data" (printed p. 1912, PDF p. 20). Note: the row cites "(p. 20)", which is the PDF page; the printed journal page is 1912 (journal pagination runs 1893–1924, so "p. 20" cannot be a printed page). Cosmetic — fix only if quality_notes ever gets cited with a page number.
