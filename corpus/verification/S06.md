Three flags below.

## Flag 1 — conditions: quantified threshold missing (≥25% adoption intensity)

- **Field:** conditions
- **Current:** "AI-intensity threshold (low-level adoption: no effect); …"
- **Proposed addition:** name the threshold value: effect turns positive and significant only at adoption intensity ≥25% (Table 5: 25–50% = 0.675***, 50%+ = 0.839***; testing/0–5%/5–25% all n.s.). Case-law rule 4: always record quantified thresholds.
- **Evidence (p. 2):** "At low levels of adoption, whether firms are simply testing the technology or are at adoption levels below 25%, we do not find performance benefits from AI adoption."
- **Evidence (p. 7):** "the firm's average annual revenue growth rate generally increases with higher levels of AI adoption, and the relationship becomes significant at higher levels of adoption, namely, 25%–50% or 50% or more."

## Flag 2 — conditions: complementary tech understated ("amplify" → effect exists ONLY in that subgroup) and quantified interaction magnitudes missing

- **Field:** conditions
- **Current:** "complementary technology investments (cloud, database) amplify; internal/exclusive R&D strategy amplifies"
- **Proposed:** "…complementary technology investments (database, cloud computing): significant AI–growth relationship occurs ONLY among adopters of these technologies (no significant effect at any AI level without them, Table 6); interaction +63.7 pp (Table 7). Internal R&D strategy: effect significant only in the internal-R&D subsample (external-collaboration estimates positive but imprecise, Table 8); interaction +39.9 pp (Table 9)."
- **Evidence (p. 7):** "The pattern between AI-adoption intensity and revenue growth in Table 5 occurs only among firms that adopt these technologies."
- **Evidence (pp. 7–8):** "the interaction term is positive at 0.637 with a standard error of 0.175. Revenue growth is 63.7 percentage points higher among firms that additionally invest in these complementary technologies than at firms that intensively adopt AI technology (at 25% or higher) without investing in database and cloud computing."
- **Evidence (p. 9):** "a positive association also exists at firms that pursue external R&D collaboration, but the estimates are not precise." — and: "The table shows revenue growth that is 39.9 percentage points higher among firms adopting an internal R&D strategy."

## Flag 3 — theoretical_lens: "no grand theory" is wrong; GPT framing is explicit

- **Field:** theoretical_lens
- **Current:** "technology complementarity (no grand theory)"
- **Proposed:** "GPT (general-purpose technology) / delayed productivity gains (J-curve) + technology complementarity"
- **Rationale:** H1b is derived directly from the GPT delayed-productivity literature; the discussion positions the contribution inside the GPT/J-curve stream. GPT is also one of the thesis's four theory lenses (supervisor feedback #4), so this row should be findable under that lens in the synthesis.
- **Evidence (p. 1):** "In fact, AI is widely accepted as the newest general-purpose technology (GPT) (Goldfarb et al., 2020; Trajtenberg, 2019)"
- **Evidence (p. 12):** "Second, our finding contributes to the rich literature that discusses the delayed productivity benefits of new technologies (i.e., J-Curve effect) and the importance of complementarities when adopting new technologies… We show that similar patterns emerge for AI, the newest GPT, and that the delayed productivity gains from AI can be moderated by complementary technologies and R&D strategy."
